L2 vs L1 Timestamp Manipulation — Attack Surface Analysis

Aspect 4 of the DeepSeek independent adversarial review of the permissionless based-preconfirmation redesign (PR #22034, branch claude/taiko-preconfirmation-redesign-i0c4ls, normative doc v14). Review date 2026-08-21. Companion reports: index · soundness · attacks · auction sims · economics · doc consistency · model checker.

Bottom line: the redesign decouples the L2 clock from L1 inclusion time and replaces it with an epoch-schedule window [T_N, T_N+E). That buys precomputability and sender-independence (I1/I7) at a real, bounded cost: forward-stamping freedom (+383 s) that does not exist today, a ratchet-able anchor-staleness window, and a wall-clock lag band of ≈33–51 min in degraded modes. Several of the obvious attacks are provably closed by the design (forced-inclusion time-travel; L1-builder influence on the default anchor). Two load-bearing rules are under-specified: the origin of T_N itself, and the client-side acceptance rule for future-stamped blocks. Nothing found here is fatal; everything found here is consensus-critical.

4.2 The new attack surface: forward-stamping (F1)

HIGH (consensus-exactness, not exploitable for theft)

Because the bound is the epoch window and not the including L1 block, a seat holder (or anarchy proposer) can stamp blocks up to 383 s in the future relative to real time — something today's ts ≤ L1_ts rule makes impossible. Consequences:

  • Client acceptance divergence. Execution clients reject blocks stamped beyond a small tolerance into the future relative to the local clock (geth-class clients use an allowedFutureBlockTime-style rule; taiko-geth is not vendored in this repo so its exact tolerance is unverifiable here). A block stamped +300 s is valid per protocol but rejected per node. The normative doc names no replacement rule — today the objective ts ≤ L1_ts bound made future stamps impossible; the redesign removes it and the client-side rule is unpinned. This is a consensus-exactness gap adjacent to §13-S.5: either a tolerance must be pinned as a consensus rule (identical in circuit, Go client, Rust client), or the design must state that holders may not stamp ahead of their local clock (a soft, non-objective rule).
  • L2 apps comparing clocks. Contracts that compare block.timestamp against L1-sourced timestamps (bridge-relayed oracle prices with L1 timestamps, cross-chain deadlines) will observe an L2 clock up to ≈5.6 min ahead of the shallowest legal anchor (max ts = T_N + 383 s; shallowest anchor allowed at commit time is slot(T_N) + Γc + E − D_anchor = slot(T_N) + 4 ≈ T_N + 48 s); conversely the anchored L1 state can lag the L2 clock by up to ≈11+ min (freshness_ceiling) — round-3 corrected sign (the L2 clock is ahead of the anchor, not behind it).
  • Self-limiting in practice. A rational holder cannot exploit this without delaying its own blocks (nodes reject future-stamped blocks until wall time catches up), so the practical attack is the opposite direction (4.3).

4.3 Backward / freeze window (F2)

MEDIUM — bounded, and smaller than today for on-time chains

A content-epoch holder may stamp the epoch's blocks anywhere in [T_N, T_N+E): all at the start (clock runs ~6.4 min slow for the rest of the epoch) or stretched. Numerically the redesign tightens today's backward window for an on-time chain: 383 s vs today's 1536 s (Hoodi) / 6144 s (Mainnet). It is a TWAP/oracle-time manipulation vector bounded by E, same class as Ethereum's own ±seconds-per-slot freedom but ~30× larger in span. Under lag (4.5) the backward displacement grows to the lag band — the honest bound the doc already states for anarchy.

4.4 Forced-inclusion time-travel — provably closed (positive result)

VERIFIED CLOSED

Concern: under chain lag, a forced transaction executes at a timestamp earlier than its submission (deadline circumvention: submit after deadline D, execute at ts < D). Analysis: an item submitted at wall time w is due at d = w + F_delay and belongs to the snapshot of the epoch containing d; its earliest stamped timestamp is T_N ≥ d − E = w + F_delay − E. With the design's consensus constraint F_delay ≥ E + F_margin (r9-C1), T_N − w ≥ F_margin > 0 — forced items always execute at a timestamp strictly after their submission, regardless of lag:

F_delaymin(T_N − w)backward execution possible?

This is a genuine, non-obvious consequence of the r9-C1 constraint and deserves credit. Residual: the same guarantee does not cover discretionary traffic — a holder can stamp a normal user's tx up to 383 s before the wall-clock time the user signed it (4.3), and under lag the whole chain executes ~33–51 min in the past (4.5), which is the doc's stated "lag band".

L2 contracts using block.timestamp as a proxy for real time (expiry clocks, TWAP oracles, freshness checks on L1-synced prices) misbehave in these modes — timestamps are 33–51 min stale, then jump forward ~1 epoch per sealed epoch. The doc states the anarchy band honestly; it does not state the recovery-mode figure or the jump-forward dynamics, and it nowhere states that time-based L2 applications degrade during the band — an operational caveat for the ecosystem, not a protocol bug.

4.6 Anchor gaming: the staleness ratchet (F3)

MEDIUM — bounded lever, ratchet-able

The EBC-committed anchor must be ≥ D_anchor (32 slots) deep and within freshness_ceiling of T_N. The setter invariant only enforces freshness_ceiling ≥ D_anchor + Γc + κ + R + margin = 55 slots + margin (≈11 min minimum) — governance may set it higher. The holder therefore chooses, per epoch, which ~[11 min, 6.4 min]-old L1 state the L2 sees, and the advancement rule does not stop a ratchet: anchoring at max staleness each epoch advances the anchor at exactly wall speed, so the holder can keep every L1→L2 signal (bridge message, L1 oracle update, forced-removal) ~freshness_ceiling (≥11 min) stale indefinitely, per signal. Default (holderless) epochs fix staleness at ≥ 32 slots ≈ 6.4 min (a few slots more across empty L1 slots) — deterministic, better, and the doc never states that number (round-3 precision fix). Today's MAX_ANCHOR_OFFSET caps staleness at 25.6/102.4 min, so the redesign improves the worst case only if governance keeps the ceiling at its legal minimum; nothing in the setter invariant does that automatically. Recommend: an explicit upper bound on freshness_ceiling in the setter (e.g. ≤ 2·D_anchor + Γc + κ + R + margin) so the gaming window stays ~one epoch.

4.7 Robustness against L1-side timestamp manipulation (positive result)

VERIFIED ROBUST
  • The default anchor F(N) is defined over L1 slots, not L1 block timestamps: L1 proposers cannot move it by skewing their block timestamps. ✓
  • The decision D+κ and the AC's commitment-inclusion proof are slot-indexed (EIP-4788 beacon roots). ✓ (Precision note: §3.1's generic "presence at D+κ" should be pinned to inclusion in a canonical block with slot ≤ slot(D+κ) — the doc says this explicitly for slices/AC (§5.2) but not for the EBC itself.)
  • All derivation inputs are EBC-committed content or epoch-schedule quantities; no L1 inclusion-time observation (I1). ✓
  • L1 contracts measuring deadlines (T_exp, seal deadlines) via block.timestamp inherit the usual few-seconds L1 proposer timestamp skew — negligible, and common to every L1 deadline contract.

4.8 Undefined: the origin of the epoch clock T_N (F4)

HIGH (spec gap)

Every bound in the design is epoch-relative, yet the doc never defines where T_N comes from (presumably T_N = T_0 + N·E with T_0 fixed at deployment; Appendix C calls E "epoch schedule (known before T_N)" but nothing states the schedule). Load-bearing consequences of the choice: (a) slot alignment — E = 384 s = 32 L1 slots, but if T_0 is an arbitrary deployment timestamp, epochs are not slot-aligned and every slot-mapping (slot(T_N), F(N), D+κ) needs a rounding rule; (b) the mapping from the epoch schedule (wall clock) to L1 slots must be L1-genesis-relative, not local-clock-relative, or clients fork; (c) whether lag ever re-bases the schedule. Must be pinned in Phase A (§13-S.3 adjacent).

4.9 Recommendations (timestamp/anchor)

  1. Pin a consensus rule for the client-side future-block acceptance (or an objective replacement: e.g. forbid ts > max(T_N, L1_ts_of_EBC − Γc·12)-style bound that preserves precomputability).
  2. Add the missing setter upper bound on freshness_ceiling to cap the anchor-ratchet window.
  3. Define T_N's origin and the slot-mapping rounding rule normatively.
  4. State the recovery-mode lag (51.2 min) and the time-jump dynamics next to the anarchy band (33.4 min).
  5. Document that time-sensitive L2 apps degrade during lag bands (ecosystem guidance).
