Adversarial Attack Catalog — Malicious-Advisor Pass

Aspect 2 of the DeepSeek independent adversarial review of PR #22034 (v14 design). Review date 2026-08-21. Companions: index · soundness · auction sims · L2 vs L1 timestamps · economics · doc consistency · model checker.

Adversarial summary: the design has clearly been hardened by genuinely adversarial rounds; most classic attacks (front-run rewards, one-shot burning, empty-front-running, seal/cancel races, poke-censorship exits, recall-then-deliver double-spend) are closed, several with elegant mechanisms (I8 precommitted payees, the two-sided cutoff, the terminal FAILED bridge mark). The remaining open surface concentrates in four places: (1) watchtower-dependent deterrence (preconf-vs-record equivocation — the only theft class that needs an accuser); (2) L1-builder-cartel censorship corridors (7-slot tail slice / EBC / AC suppression — priced, not eliminated); (3) the anchor and timestamp levers (staleness ratchet, freeze/future windows — aspect 4); (4) unspecified auction mechanics (grief-bid funding forfeit — aspect 3). As a malicious advisor I would not attack the liveness structure at all — it is the strongest part. I would attack the soft layer: preconf trust, censorship pricing, and governance-estimated bonds.

2.5 Timestamp / anchor manipulation (summary)

Forward-stamp (+383 s) freedom with unpinned client rule; freeze window (383 s); anchor staleness ratchet (≥11 min at minimal ceiling); lag band 33–51 min; forced-inclusion time-travel provably impossible; L1-side slot-robustness verified. Full analysis: aspect 4.

2.6 Auction / governance attacks (summary)

Grief-bid-then-refuse-funding (forfeit unspecified — v3 text absent); Sybil standby self-promotion at reserve; T_max incumbency persistence (57→100% renewal as advantage grows); reserve-floor rent capture with a single bidder; attested-outage self-labeling excluded (independent attestation only). Full analysis: aspect 3 and aspect 5.

2.7 What a sophisticated adversary would actually do

  1. Exploit the watchtower gap: equivocate via preconf-vs-record (not double-EBC) in epochs where MEV spikes (CEX-DEX gaps, liquidations), betting no accuser acts within the horizon; sizing at p=1 means positive EV at any p<1. The double-EBC variant self-materializes, so the adversary avoids it.
  2. Bid the reserve, censor selectively, look busy: stuff K_empty with self-dealing blobs, serve only own MEV-friendly flow, extract the rest via the forced queue's delayed service. Bounded by T_max but renewable.
  3. Race the anarchy lane as a censor when the forced-fee subsidy makes racing cheap (net ≈ +0.02 to −0.01 ETH/epoch) — keeps discretionary content out at near-zero cost until a real bidder appears.
  4. Use the anchor ratchet to keep L1→L2 signals (oracle updates, bond/removal signals) ~11+ min stale while appearing to serve, delaying competitors' reactions and bridge ingestion.
  5. Avoid everything structural: the liveness machinery, the bridge handshake (post-fix), the one-shot, and the nullifiers are all closed; attacking them costs the attacker, not the protocol.

Every closure above was re-derived from the normative text and, where load-bearing, cross-checked against the deployed contracts (Bridge.sol:199-240, 247-344, 483-485; SignalService.sol:220-237; Inbox.sol:613) and the model checker's invariants.
