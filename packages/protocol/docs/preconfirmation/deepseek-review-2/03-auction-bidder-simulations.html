Auction Simulations — 0, 1, and Multiple Competing Bidders

Aspect 3 of the DeepSeek independent adversarial review of PR #22034 (v14 design). Review date 2026-08-21. Companions: index · soundness · attacks · L2 vs L1 timestamps · economics · doc consistency · model checker.

Verdict: the auction behaves sensibly in all three regimes — but the PR cannot be audited for auction soundness beyond that, because the auction mechanics are "as v3" and the v3 text is not in the PR (§4 is a one-paragraph delta). The one structural gap this exposes: the funding-forfeit rule for a bidder that wins and then fails to fund is nowhere specified, and without one, a zero-cost grief-bid can displace a serving incumbent every T_max (or at will, depending on the unspecified transition rule). Simulations below use stated assumptions.

3.1 Method & assumptions

Event simulation in simulation/auction_sim.py (stdlib-only, seeded, reproducible: python3 auction_sim.py --seed 42). Assumptions, stated because the v3 text is absent: ascending-bid auction with ≥10% increments and a reserve floor; the winner's per-epoch payment is its bid (ETH fee rate); tenures of T_max = 14 days (~3,150 epochs); per-epoch seat value ~ N(0.20, 0.12) ETH (illustrative); the incumbent observes realized value (information edge), challengers estimate with 18% noise (winner's-curse); an overbidding winner quits after q = 2 epochs (churn).

  • 1 bidder = permanent reserve-floor monopoly: the seat sells for 5% of its mean value (0.010 vs 0.20) and the monopolist keeps ~95% of the rent, renewing every T_max at the reserve. This is the design's explicitly "accepted economic property" (§4) — but it means the reserve floor is the entire price of the sequencing monopoly when competition is absent, so the reserve must be set ≈ the expected seat value. That requires exactly the same unmeasurable MEV estimate as L_safety (economics §5.3) — the two sizing problems are one problem.
  • Competition dissipates rent (price → true value as N grows) and induces churn: with noise, winners overbid 25–50% of the time and quit after q epochs, so the chain oscillates between service and anarchy windows. Under the stated assumptions the equilibrium price converges ≈ the mean value (0.20) — protocol revenue scales ~linearly with N (378 → 7,265 ETH/tenure). Service-gap frequency: 4–6 of 12 tenures ended in an early quit (N2–N3), i.e. roughly one q-epoch service gap every 2 tenures (~4 weeks) plus re-auction latency — a preconf-service reliability cost the design's "overbidding winner quits after q epochs" rule accepts but never quantifies.

READOUT
  • With symmetric racers the lane self-extinguishes when unprofitable — no discretionary content, forced-only + empty cadence only. That is the design's fallback working as intended, but it means anarchy service quality depends on a positive-sum race, which in turn requires MEV or forced-fee income to exceed proof cost. On a quiet chain: no anarchy content, ever.
  • The censor race is cheap. A determined censor pays ~proof cost per epoch and recovers the forced-fee share (it must include the forced prefix — §9.2/§11.8): net marginal censorship cost ≈ +0.02 ETH/epoch under partial forced-fee coverage, falling to ≈ −0.01 (net-subsidized) when forced fees over-cover the work (round-3 corrected figures; the sim emits exactly these two endpoints). Discretionary anarchy content is thus suppressible at near-zero cost by any actor willing to out-race everyone — the priced service-level residual r10-4 names, quantified.
  • Bidding dominates racing whenever the seat has real value: seat value 0.20 − reserve 0.01 = +0.19 vs expected race profit −0.01 — the lane does not cannibalize the auction (r10-8 confirmed under these parameters).
  • Heterogeneous racers concentrate income, not power (round-3 addition): with prover skill shares 0.6/0.25/0.15 the fastest prover wins ~61% of races — the §11.3 texture, income capped at indexed cost, no censorship lever (declining an epoch hands it to anyone else or to the empty resolution).

3.4 Auction-adjacent gaps the PR leaves open (new findings)

HIGH — unspecified mechanics with adversarial leverage
  1. No funding-forfeit rule. "Funding rule as automatic quit notice" (§4) is the entire specification of what happens when the winning bidder cannot or will not post its bond/deposit/fee. If failing to fund merely quits, a griefer can displace a serving incumbent with a phantom high bid at ~zero cost, repeatedly (each cycle: incumbent churn + q-epoch transition + service interruption). The design must specify: forfeiture of a bid deposit, or binding-funding-at-bid, or a bid bond that is slashed on failure to fund. This is the most consequential missing sentence in the PR's auction.
  2. Seat-fee destination not restated. The owner decision (status-quo.md §6.1) sends the per-epoch ETH fee to the treasury/DAO — making the auction protocol revenue — but the proposal's §4 never restates it, so the normative record depends on a companion file (economics §5.1).
  3. No transition-service rule. During the q-epoch transition after an outbid/termination, who sequences the still-assigned epochs and the gap is "as v3" (absent). An angry exiting incumbent plus an unfunded successor yields a service hole whose length is unpinned.
  4. No bootstrap rule. At deployment (or after all tenures terminate) the chain starts in anarchy until the first auction clears — presumably intended, never stated (§10 "as v3").
  5. No bid-retraction / collusion treatment. Standard auction-hygiene items (bid bonds against retraction, minimum bid duration, anti-collusion between the incumbent and standby bidders) are not addressed even at the "existence" level.

3.5 What the simulations do NOT cover (honesty)

  • Strategic bid timing (sniping at T_max re-auctions) — needs the unspecified auction clock rules.
  • Standby-bond auto-promotion dynamics (highest standby promoted on termination) — underspecified; a Sybil standby at reserve could inherit the seat at a below-market price if promotion doesn't re-run the auction (only T_max expiry re-auctions; K_empty/fault terminations promote the standby). Flagged, not simulated.
  • ETH/TAIKO price dynamics — the split bond's liveness tranche erodes with TAIKO price (economics §5.4).
