# 6.2 — review notes

**Non-normative.** Nothing here can be conformed to or failed against. This is what decomposing [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) section 6.2 into citable rules turned up: questions the text does not settle, and sentences whose normative status was ambiguous. Each item says what would close it.

Section 6.2 matters disproportionately: its ten numbered items are the contract between the two guests, and the verifier cannot check them (PROOF-6.2-2), so an ambiguity here becomes a silently divergent prover rather than a failed transaction.

## Normativity boundary

Under the convention in [../README.md](../README.md), a statement with an ID is normative and everything else is not. These sentences in 6.2 carry no ID, deliberately — confirming each is a judgement call worth an explicit decision:

| Sentence | Why it has no ID | Verdict proposed |
|---|---|---|
| "The guest must prove:" | a lead-in that frames items 1–10; each item carries its own ID | keep un-numbered |
| "The blob payload is bound to `blobHashes`:" | a lead-in for item 9's three sub-bullets | keep un-numbered |
| "(3.4 rule 3 uses its l1BlockNumber)" | cross-reference identifying which field of the origin record is used | keep un-numbered; the obligation is PROOF-6.2-14 |
| "which needs two guests" | consequence of the two-sub-proof composition; folded into PROOF-6.2-4's title and vectors | keep un-numbered, or fold explicitly |
| "namely an empty block" | the value the singleton build must produce; folded into PROOF-6.2-26 | keep un-numbered |
| "even though it contributes no final difficulty or fees" | consequence clause of PROOF-6.2-28, not a separate requirement | keep un-numbered |
| "with the FI cursor advancing by 0 or 1 per block under section 7" | part of the cut rule; folded into PROOF-6.2-41 | keep un-numbered |
| `<summary>What the guest must prove (10 items)</summary>` | a summary line; IDs are never placed there | keep un-numbered |

## Q6.2-1

**Nothing on chain ties the verifier to the pinned code hash and image set**

**Text.** "The contract only calls its `verifyProof` interface and does not check the implementation; production deployments must use the same `ZkRequiredVerifier` as mainnet (Unzen)" … "The verifier address, code hash and accepted image set are pinned in the deployment manifest and checked as a launch gate".

**Why it matters.** The ten items are enforced by the guest image, not by `land`: the verifier is opaque and its implementation is deliberately unchecked. The only control named is an off-chain launch gate against a manifest. So the protocol's guarantee reduces to "the right verifier was deployed and the right images were in its accepted set at launch", which no on-chain state attests. A deployment that skips or misreads the gate still settles; a node cannot detect the mismatch from chain state. This is the trust root for every other rule in 6.2.

**Would close it.** state whether the Inbox constructor asserts the verifier's code hash (and reverts on mismatch), or name who verifies the manifest at launch and what evidence they must publish.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-2

**Is the sub-proof combination list exhaustive?**

**Text.** "exactly two sub-proofs, at least one of them ZK (SGX + RISC0/SP1, or RISC0 + SP1), which needs two guests."

**Why it matters.** The predicate ("two, at least one ZK") admits combinations the parenthetical does not list — RISC0 + RISC0 and SP1 + SP1 are both all-ZK — while SGX + SGX fails the predicate and is also unlisted. If the parenthetical is exhaustive, RISC0 + RISC0 is invalid even though it satisfies the words; if it is illustrative, a verifier configured for only the three listed pairs wrongly rejects a valid proof. "Which needs two guests" hints at two distinct guest programs, which RISC0 + RISC0 does not provide, but that is an inference.

**Would close it.** state the allowed combinations as a set (or state that the predicate is exactly "two sub-proofs, at least one ZK, from two distinct guest programs" and the parenthetical is illustrative).

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-3

**"Settlement stops" is not classified**

**Text.** "If one backend fails, another combination can be used; if no valid combination can be assembled, settlement stops."

**Why it matters.** Section 8 distinguishes a liveness pause (automatic, lossless) from a safety halt (manual, needs investigation). "Settlement stops" is neither term. The operator response differs sharply: a pause resolves when the backend returns; a halt needs a decision. The section 8 list of causes does not mention verifier availability, so this consequence sits outside the halt taxonomy it will be operated under. It is also unstated who detects "no valid combination" and how that is signalled.

**Would close it.** classify the stop under section 8 (pause or halt), name the detector, and say whether governance recovery is the only way out.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-4

**"record" in item 2 is undefined**

**Text.** "Derive signing and pending committees using 4.3's unified rule, authenticating snapshot, entries, power, record and H+2 activation."

**Why it matters.** "Record" is one of five authenticated inputs, but 6.2 never says which record: the registry's committee record, a landing record, or the record hash 4.3 commits to. The guest must know exactly which stored value to open and against which root. H+2 activation is named but its height arithmetic lives in 4.3; a guest that reads the wrong record or applies activation one block early produces a different committee and therefore a different proof.

**Would close it.** name the record and its commitment, and cite the 4.3 clause that fixes H+2 for this item.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-5

**The consequence of needing more than L+2 headers**

**Text.** "The ordinary path follows authenticated EL parentHash to the required D(t), requiring at most L+2 historical headers." … "L+2 excludes the separate L1-ancestry and recovery-material budget."

**Why it matters.** "Requiring at most" reads as a bound, but 6.2 never says what happens when a valid chain needs one more header — for example a longer gap before `D(t)`. Is the proof invalid, is the block unprovable (so the batch stalls), or is `L+2` only an input to sizing? The second sentence makes the total proof input open-ended by excluding other budgets, which weakens any reading of `L+2` as a hard cap. Two guests can diverge exactly at the boundary, which is the worst place to diverge.

**Would close it.** state whether exceeding `L+2` makes the proof invalid, and whether the bound is checked by the guest or only planned for operationally.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-6

**Which chain id signs a commit**

**Text.** "Each block's commit carries more than 2/3 of the voting power … secp256k1eth (keccak256 then `ecrecover`, low-s), under this generation's `chain_id`".

**Why it matters.** 7.2 uses the L2 chain id for transaction eligibility, and 8.4 says "chain_id uses the new generation", so the two notions are distinct. But 6.2 says only "this generation's chain_id" without defining its derivation, its storage, or whether it changes at recovery. A guest that signs or verifies under the L2 id while the other uses a generation-scoped id rejects every block of the new generation — or, worse, accepts signatures replayed from the old domain.

**Would close it.** define the commit-signature domain per generation, its derivation, and where it is authenticated.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-7

**Where the shared zk-gas rules are pinned**

**Text.** "Nodes, the EL and both guests use the same pinned zk-gas rules/parameters and validate the entire committed transaction list under 3.4".

**Why it matters.** "Pinned" has no referent in 6.2. Section 11 assigns the zk-gas limit and meter to "the client per chain id (with the pinned EL version)", but 6.2's ten items do not name the EL version, the image, or the config field that carries the parameters. Two guests built from the same source can still meter differently if the pin is not identical, and the over-limit case (PROOF-6.2-23, -27) is exactly where that divergence becomes a different block.

**Would close it.** name the pin (EL version and/or config-hash field) and say where the guest reads it.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-8

**"The execution part" is undefined**

**Text.** "execution runs from `parentHash` to the last block's hash and stateRoot; the execution part equals the blob payload byte for byte."

**Why it matters.** "Execution part" could be the committed transaction list, the EL block bodies, or the execution input encoding. "Byte for byte" presupposes a canonical encoding that 6.2 does not name; if the guest re-encodes transactions, RLP canonicity is already handled by 7.2, so the comparison may be over a decode-and-re-encode cycle rather than raw bytes. Each reading makes a different set of payloads conform.

**Would close it.** define the execution part and its encoding, or state that equality is over the decoded transaction list rather than raw bytes.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-9

**The required outcome for an over-limit payload**

**Text.** "do not normalize an over-limit payload into a shorter prefix and prove that instead."

**Why it matters.** The prohibition is clear; the required alternative is not. If the committed payload exceeds the zk limit, does the guest fail (so the block cannot be proven and the chain stalls), or is the block skipped as empty under 7.2's zk-limit rule? Item 5 describes the empty singleton result as a legal skip, but that is the FI singleton build, not the whole committed payload. A guest that chooses "unprovable" and one that chooses "skip" produce different chains from the same data.

**Would close it.** state the outcome for an over-limit committed payload and cross-reference the rule that governs it.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-10

**Skip legality depends on an external implementation**

**Text.** "skipping an eligible transaction reproduces, on the parent, the same singleton build result as `alethia`, namely an empty block."

**Why it matters.** This is the same defect as [7.2's Q1](../review/7.2.md#q1), restated as a guest obligation: the normative content is another system's behaviour, no version or image is pinned, and a change on `alethia`'s side silently changes what the guests must prove. Because the ten items are enforced by the guest image, this is the one item whose meaning is not fixed by this document at all.

**Would close it.** pin the given-list build semantics by version/image alongside the EL rule version, or replace the reference with a self-contained predicate for "the singleton build is empty".

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-11

**What "part of the proof computation" requires**

**Text.** "its discarded trial work is still part of the proof computation even though it contributes no final difficulty or fees."

**Why it matters.** This sentence forbids dropping the trial work, but does not say what the proof must show about it. Does the discarded work consume zk gas, affect the block's zk-gas accounting, or only need to be reproducible by the guest? If it consumes budget, the limit interacts with PROOF-6.2-22; if it is only reproducible, there is no observable. A guest author cannot tell which is checked, and the two choices produce different proofs for the same block.

**Would close it.** state the observable consequence of the trial work (budget accounting, a committed value, or nothing beyond determinism).

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-12

**The KZG challenge derivation is not fixed**

**Text.** "derive the challenge point by hashing the blob together with `C`, evaluate the blob at that point itself, and verify the KZG opening of `C` at that point."

**Why it matters.** "Hashing" leaves the hash function, domain separation, input framing (blob bytes then `C`, or a length-prefixed concatenation) and the field-element mapping unspecified. Two guests can derive different challenges from the same blob and commitment and then disagree about the same opening, which makes the equivalence check non-deterministic across implementations. This is precisely the kind of detail that a shared vector set needs fixed.

**Would close it.** specify the derivation exactly (hash, framing, reduction to the scalar field) or point to the appendix clause that does.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-13

**"Inherits p's anchor" is not defined**

**Text.** "Only when p+1 inherits p's anchor does the guest take that anchor's raw header and Inbox proofs as input and verify them against p+1's extraData and parentBeaconBlockRoot."

**Why it matters.** The condition selects the input set and the verification path for the first block of the range, yet 6.2 never says what makes a block inherit rather than carry a new anchor. A guest that treats every block as carrying its own anchor will demand inputs the other guest does not provide; a guest that treats inheritance too broadly will skip verification that one of them performs. The difference is a proof that one guest accepts and the other rejects.

**Would close it.** define inheritance (or cite the 3.3/3.4 rule that does) and state the required input set for both cases.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q6.2-14

**"Cuts not authenticated by execution" has no evidence test**

**Text.** "cuts not authenticated by execution must not be accepted."

**Why it matters.** The preceding sentences say later cuts come from real headers and execution/consumption results, but the rejection rule does not say what evidence makes a cut authenticated: its header on the reference chain, a recomputation from the execution result, a committed root, or all three. Nor does it say whether non-grid cuts are inputs at all — the same item says cuts cover `p` through `h` continuously while `cutHash` covers only grid points, so the status of the in-between cuts is open. A guest can accept a cut that another rejects without either violating a stated condition.

**Would close it.** enumerate the evidence that authenticates a cut, and state whether non-grid cuts are proof inputs or internal witnesses.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)
