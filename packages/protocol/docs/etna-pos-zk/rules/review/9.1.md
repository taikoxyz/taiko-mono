## Q9.1-1

**"Verifiable same-round conflicting votes" is not defined, and the submission path is unnamed**

**Text.** "Anyone may collect verifiable same-round conflicting votes from different peers and submit directly to L1; Comet evidence inclusion is not required."

**Why it matters.** 4.1 already defines the registry's evidence checks: same vote type, height, round and generation, different block ids, and both signatures recovering to the stored key. If "verifiable" means exactly that, this sentence adds nothing normative and the "submit directly to L1" path is simply `slashDoubleSign` (which 4.1 makes permissionless). If it means something else — for example a new batch-submission interface with different checks — the interface is unspecified, and a submitter cannot know what to send. "From different peers" also adds nothing, since the votes are self-authenticating.

**Would close it.** name the submission entry point and define "verifiable" by explicit reference to 4.1's checks (or by restating them).

## Q9.1-2

**The archive obligation has no subject and no duration**

**Text.** "Archives must retain these dependencies; public blobs or an EL snapshot alone are insufficient."

**Why it matters.** No role in the spec is an archive operator. The sentence does not say whether every node must archive, whether a public archive is assumed, or whether the DAO funds one; nor for how long. Without a subject the requirement cannot be satisfied or breached, and there is no failure mode: a node that cannot reach the archive is simply unable to recover, which section 8 does not classify. LIMIT-12-15 repeats the dependence but also assigns no owner.

**Would close it.** name who must retain the material and for how long, and what a recovering node does when it is missing.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q9.1-3

**The text says what does not authenticate an archive, not what does**

**Text.** "File hashes detect corruption but do not authenticate committees."

**Why it matters.** 5.3 requires archive contents to be "authenticated against L1 commitments" and SYNC-9.1-4 authenticates the specific inputs of a recovery. But the archive itself — the committee snapshots, entries and witnesses that 9.1 says must be retained — has no stated authentication path. A reader cannot tell whether an archive whose file hashes match may be used at all, or must be re-authenticated against L1 on every use. This is the difference between an archive being a convenience and being trusted input.

**Would close it.** state the authentication requirement for archive contents (for example, every committee snapshot is re-checked against a registry checkpoint under an L1-authenticated root) and whether unauthenticated snapshots may be used as inputs at all.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q9.1-4

**"Extra committee metadata" is named as unauthenticated but not identified or replaced**

**Text.** "An EL snapshot's `app_hash` does not authenticate extra committee metadata."

**Why it matters.** Which committee fields are "extra" — pending committees, witnesses, the activation schedule? And what authenticates them if `app_hash` does not? SYNC-9.1-4 says recovery reselects from R's registry snapshot, which suggests the registry checkpoint is the authenticator, but that is inference. Two implementers can disagree on whether committee fields carried in a snapshot are usable, and the guest's committee proof (6.2 item 2) depends on the answer.

**Would close it.** name the extra metadata and its authenticating material, and state that a snapshot's committee fields are used only after that authentication.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q9.1-5

**No minimum retention requirement and no detection assumption**

**Text.** "Evidence retention/submission is independent of the local pool's 100,000-block/48-hour limits and should track confirmExit and withdrawal boundaries." and "Retention does not guarantee observation of every conflicting vote".

**Why it matters.** The only retention guidance is "should", and the only statement about detection is negative. A node that retains evidence for a single block conforms to every numbered rule while the slashing guarantee in 4.1 assumes evidence is actually produced. The gap between "evidence is admissible" (4.1) and "evidence is observed" (9.1) is the entire economic margin of same-round slashing — the limitation is item 3 of section 12, but section 9.1 could state the assumption that makes it acceptable.

**Would close it.** state a minimum retention requirement, or say explicitly that retention policy is outside the protocol, and record the detection assumption the design relies on.
