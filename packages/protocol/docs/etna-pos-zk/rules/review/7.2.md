# 7.2 — review notes

**Non-normative.** Nothing here can be conformed to or failed against. This is what decomposing [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) section 7.2 into citable rules turned up: questions the text does not settle, and sentences whose normative status was ambiguous. Each item says what would close it.

## Normativity boundary

Under the convention in [../README.md](../README.md), a statement with an ID is normative and everything else is not. These sentences in 7.2 carry no ID, deliberately — confirming each is a judgement call worth an explicit decision:

| Sentence | Why it has no ID                                                                                               | Verdict proposed            |

## Q7.2-1

**Skip legality is delegated to an external implementation**

**Text.** FI-7.2-8 makes a skip legal only if "a complete build of `[tx]` on the committed parent with the same attributes, **in alethia's given-list mode**, yields an empty block".

**Why it matters.** The normative content of the rule is defined by another system's behaviour rather than by this document. If `alethia` changes its given-list mode, the set of legal skips changes without a change here — and two guests that implement the wording differently can both believe they conform. This is the "rule depends on something the spec does not fix" shape.

**Would close it.** state that the given-list build result is pinned by version/image alongside the EL rule version, or replace the reference with a self-contained definition of the two possible outcomes and when each applies.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-2

**Proposer can force skips by moving the base fee**

**Text.** In the eligibility detail: "A proposer can push the base fee to the 1 gwei cap at no cost (3.2), so an FI transaction should set `maxFeePerGas` to at least 1 gwei to be sure it is not skipped for its fee."

**Why it matters.** Taken with FI-7.2-8, this means a proposer **chooses** whether a due entry is skippable, by choosing the base fee — for any entry priced below the cap. The queue-head obligation is then not "include or skip by rule" but "include or skip by the proposer's discretion within the base-fee cap". 7.3 says a due head "must be included or legally skipped"; legally, yes, but the proposer decides which. This is worth an explicit decision, not a footnote, because it is the difference between forced inclusion as a guarantee and forced inclusion as a best effort against a fee floor.

**Would close it.** either (a) accept it and state the guarantee as "entries priced at or above the base-fee cap are never fee-skippable", or (b) make skip-for-fee ineligible (require the build to be empty for a non-fee reason), or (c) bound base-fee movement within a batch.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-3

**After due, unfetchable raw bytes: what happens?**

**Text.** "After due, it cannot propose without them."

**Why it matters.** This forbids proposing but does not say what the proposer or the chain does. If the bytes are permanently unfetchable (L1 log pruning, provider failure, reorg), the queue head cannot be included and cannot be skipped as ineligible — because eligibility is judged on the bytes. Does the chain halt? Does this become a recovery trigger? The current wording leaves a liveness hole whose resolution is unstated.

**Would close it.** add the outcome — for example "the proposer waits; if the entry remains unfetchable, this is a halt condition under section 8", or state why it cannot persist.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-4

**The due clock is the block's anchor, the guarantee clock is block time**

**Text.** FI-7.2-3 measures due against `A.timestamp`; 7.3's guarantee is stated against `block.timestamp` with `maxAnchorAge`. `maxAnchorAge` comes from 3.4.

**Why it matters.** The two clocks are bridged only by reference. A reader cannot tell from 7.2 whether a proposer that picks an old-but-legal anchor can make a due entry not-due. It probably cannot beyond the `maxAnchorAge` bound, but the argument lives in another section and is never stated where the rule is.

**Would close it.** one sentence in FI-7.2-3 stating the bound and citing 3.4, or a cross-reference in 7.3 back to the per-block anchor.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-5

**`c > tail(A)` reachability**

**Text.** FI-7.2-2 branches on `c ≥ tail(A)` and requires no witness in that case.

**Why it matters.** `c ≥ tail(A)` treats "exactly caught up" and "ahead of the anchor's own view" identically. If `c > tail(A)` is reachable — the landing advanced past what this block's anchor knew — the no-witness branch is doing two different jobs and an invariant is missing.

**Would close it.** state that `c ≤ tail(A)` always holds, or specify the `c > tail(A)` case separately.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-6

**Overflow outcome of the due comparison**

**Text.** "evaluated without overflow".

**Why it matters.** The rule says how not to evaluate it, not what the answer is. Saturating, reverting, and treating as not-due are all consistent with the words, and they differ observably at the boundary.

**Would close it.** name the width and the outcome (e.g. "compared as uint256; a sum exceeding the width is treated as not due").

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-7

**A zk-aborted included transaction leaves `c` unstated**

**Text.** FI-7.2-5 says a zk-aborted transaction absent from the final body "does not" count as inclusion. FI-7.2-1 only describes inclusion and skip.

**Why it matters.** The outcome split is not whether the attempt was made but what the given-list build returned. If the complete build of `[tx]` yielded an empty block, FI-7.2-8 makes this a legal skip with `c`+1; if it contained the transaction, omitting it leaves a due block that is neither included nor legally skipped, and FI-7.2-10 makes it invalid. The text does not say which branch a zk-aborted entry falls into, so an implementer must guess - and a blanket "block valid, `c` unchanged" reading would contradict both FI-7.2-8 and FI-7.2-10.

**Would close it.** one clause in FI-7.2-5: state that a zk-aborted entry is a legal skip with `c`+1 when the given-list build was empty, and an invalid due block otherwise.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-8

**Can a block legally skip and still contain the transaction later?**

**Text.** FI-7.2-10: only position 0 counts as inclusion. FI-7.2-8: a skip needs an empty singleton build.

**Why it matters.** If the singleton build of `[tx]` is empty (say, a transaction-level failure), the entry may be legally skipped — yet the same transaction could still appear later in the block as an ordinary transaction. Whether that is allowed is not stated, and it changes whether the queue and the block body can diverge.

**Would close it.** state that a legally skipped entry must not appear anywhere in the execution block, or permit it explicitly.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-9

**"processes no new consensus messages" is not a block property**

**Text.** In FI-7.2-9's detail: "while waiting, that node processes no new consensus messages."

**Why it matters.** This is an operational rule about a node's event loop, not something a vector, a guest, or an external observer can check. It is part of FI-7.2-9's text, so it binds, but no block shows whether it was obeyed.

**Would close it.** restate it as an observable (for example "the node must not commit or vote during the wait"), or move it to implementation guidance.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.2-10

**Does blanking shrink the queue?**

**Text.** FI-7.2-13: a blanked entry "can never be included, and once due it is skipped as ineligible".

**Why it matters.** Blanking consumes a cursor step without removing the entry. Over a long recovery this means the queue is never compacted, and `tail − head` (which 7.1 uses for the fee) still counts blanked entries until they are consumed. That may be intended; it is not stated.

**Would close it.** one sentence confirming blanked entries remain in the queue until consumed, and that the fee formula counts them.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)
