# 7.3 — review notes

**Non-normative.** Nothing here can be conformed to or failed against. This is what decomposing [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) section 7.3 into citable rules turned up: questions the text does not settle, and sentences whose normative status was ambiguous. Each item says what would close it. Only the spec owner decides.

## Normativity boundary

| Sentence                                                      | Why it has no ID                                             | Proposed treatment               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| "let F be the fees actually paid for an attacker's enqueues." | defines a term used by FI-7.3-5; asserts nothing falsifiable | keep un-numbered as a definition |

## Q7.3-1

**The block-time implication is derived in another section**

**Text.** "For the current queue-head entry, any valid block with `block.timestamp ≥ entry.timestamp + forcedInclusionDelay + maxAnchorAge` necessarily sees it as due, using overflow-safe arithmetic."

**Why it matters.** Due is defined against the anchor timestamp (FI-7.2-3), not the block timestamp. The implication holds only because 3.4 rule 7 bounds `timestamp − anchor.timestamp` by `maxAnchorAge`. The argument is never written where the guarantee is, so a reader of 7.3 cannot check the claim, and a client that implements the two rules independently could diverge. It also leaves open whether the bound is applied to the candidate's timestamp or to some other clock.

**Would close it.** Add one sentence to FI-7.3-2 citing 3.4 rule 7 as the premise, or restate the guarantee in terms of the anchor.

## Q7.3-2

**"Senders must hold a balance" is stated without a binding or a consequence**

**Text.** "Senders must hold a balance of gasLimit × `maxFeePerGas`".

**Why it matters.** FI-7.2-4 makes eligibility a predicate over raw bytes only, explicitly ignoring state, and FI-7.2-8 makes an empty singleton build a legal skip. A zero-balance sender's transaction is therefore eligible and may be skipped; nothing in 7.2 treats the balance as a validity condition. The sentence reads as advice to senders, but it is written with "must". If it is normative, an implementer needs to know who is bound and what a breach changes.

**Would close it.** State that this is advice to senders (and leave it un-numbered), or make it a validity condition and say which check applies and what a failure produces.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.3-3

**The economic claim has unstated assumptions**

**Text.** "With fees belonging to the protocol, no share of successful landings lets the attacker recover F by consuming those entries."

**Why it matters.** Appendix C.2 has the DAO paying landers for landing work. The claim is scoped to "successful landings", but if DAO operating funds compensate a landing that includes the attacker's entries, the attacker can recover value through a different channel. The sentence is also stated as an unconditional fact, so it is either a protocol guarantee (and needs its assumptions) or an argument (and should not carry an ID).

**Would close it.** Name the assumptions — no per-entry or per-landing payment to the submitter, no rebate, no other protocol reward tied to consumption — and state whether DAO funding is in or out of scope.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.3-4

**"No age-only deletion" versus governance blanking**

**Text.** "A due queue-head entry must be included or legally skipped under 7.2; there is no age-only deletion." Section 8 step 3 lets the DAO blank listed entries by replacing `txHash` with `keccak256("")`.

**Why it matters.** Blanking is the only mechanism that removes an entry's content, and FI-7.3-1 says no deletion is by age alone. The text does not say whether blanking is considered a deletion, whether the "unprovable content only" scope in section 8 is the guard that keeps age out, or how a reviewer is meant to test "age-only". An entry that is old and unprovable can be blanked; an entry that is old and provable cannot, but nothing on-chain records the reason.

**Would close it.** State that the only content-removal path is section 8 step 3, that it is limited to unprovable content, and that age is never by itself a ground.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.3-5

**"Queue length" here, `pending` in 7.1**

**Text.** "the fee rises linearly with the queue length"; 7.1 defines the fee over `pending = tail − head`, which "while settlement lags ... includes entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed".

**Why it matters.** The two phrases are not the same quantity: the physical queue length is `tail − head`, but the settled-but-unlanded entries are in that number even though L2 has consumed them. The linearity claim is true of `pending`, and a reader can take "queue length" to mean unconsumed entries, which would make the claim false during settlement lag.

**Would close it.** Use `pending` (or `tail − head`) in FI-7.3-4, and say explicitly that consumed-but-unlanded entries count.
