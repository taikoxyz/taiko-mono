## Q7.1-1

**Fee arithmetic: rounding, width and units are unspecified**

**Text.** "a fee of `fee × (threshold + pending) / threshold` gwei" and "actual fee charged in wei".

**Why it matters.** The formula is stated over integers but the division is not specified: floor, ceiling or exact rational, and at which step the gwei result becomes wei. At `threshold = 32` and `pending = 8` the exact charge is 1.25 gwei; implementations that floor, round or truncate after converting to wei charge different amounts, and the difference is protocol revenue and appears in the enqueue event. A user cannot compute the overpayment they are owed, and two nodes can disagree about whether a submission paid enough.

**Would close it.** State the field widths, the division rounding, the gwei-to-wei conversion order, and give one worked example, or state that the charge is computed in wei throughout and name the rounding.

## Q7.1-2

**The 8,192-byte cap: where is it enforced, and what happens above it?**

**Text.** "`saveForcedInclusion(bytes tx)`: at most 8,192 bytes" and "encoding is not checked at submission".

**Why it matters.** The cap sits in the same sentence as the fee formula, but the text does not say whether it is checked at submission, at consumption, or both, nor what a violation produces. "Encoding is not checked at submission" makes the submission-time check question sharper. If an oversized entry can be enqueued, it is not clear which consumption branch applies: FI-7.2-4's eligibility predicate does not mention size, while FI-7.2-5 describes the included entry as "at most 8,192 bytes". A due oversized entry could be neither includable nor skippable as ineligible.

**Would close it.** State that `saveForcedInclusion` rejects `tx` longer than 8,192 bytes (and that this is the only submission-time predicate), or keep submission permissive and state which consumption branch an oversized entry takes.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.1-3

**Refund recipient and failed refund**

**Text.** "overpayment is refunded" and "Only `saveForcedInclusion` accepts ETH (overpayment is refunded in the same transaction)".

**Why it matters.** The recipient is unstated: `msg.sender`, the transaction's `from`, or an explicit parameter. If the sender is a contract that rejects ETH, the text does not say whether the submission reverts (as a failed sweep transfer does) or the overpayment is left in the Inbox. The two choices differ observably for the same call.

**Would close it.** Name the refund recipient and state that a failed refund reverts the enqueue, or say where the unrefunded amount goes.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.1-4

**Does a settlement rollback raise the fee for later submissions?**

**Text.** "pending is the number of entries on L1 not yet consumed by a landing (`tail − head`; while settlement lags this includes entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed)" together with section 8's rollback, which restores the FI head to the value written by the last trusted landing.

**Why it matters.** The rollback can move `head` back, so `tail − head` grows and every subsequent submission is charged more, for entries that were already paid for and consumed before the rollback. The section says historical per-entry charges "are recorded only in enqueue events, not used as consumption or recovery inputs", which is consistent, but it does not say that a rollback raises future prices. That consequence is observable and worth an explicit decision.

**Would close it.** State that `pending` is computed from the current (possibly restored) head, and confirm that a rollback raises the charge for later submissions.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.1-5

**Treasury address and sweep: enforcement and interaction with recovery**

**Text.** "deployment fixes a nonzero DAO treasury address, `protocolFeeRecipient` (an Inbox immutable), and publishes it in the deployment manifest"; "Anyone may call `sweepProtocolFees()` ..."; "Sweeping does not depend on the queue head or landing and does not modify FI entries or queue progress."

**Why it matters.** Two gaps. First, the nonzero requirement is stated for deployment but the text gives no consequence for a deployment that violates it, and no component is named as the checker (10.5 also says "Fix a nonzero DAO treasury"). Second, 8.3's reentrancy rule lists `land`, `recover`, nonce invalidation and registry mutations, but not `sweepProtocolFees`, so it is unstated whether a sweep may run during a recover call or interrupt one. The anchor witness does not prove the Inbox balance, so the risk may be only to the sweep, but the omission is a decision.

**Would close it.** State where the nonzero check is enforced, and whether the sweep shares the recover/land reentrancy lock.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q7.1-6

**Who may call `saveForcedInclusion`?**

**Text.** "`saveForcedInclusion(bytes tx)`: at most 8,192 bytes, with a fee of ..."

**Why it matters.** `sweepProtocolFees` is explicitly "Anyone may call"; `saveForcedInclusion` has no stated caller. Forced inclusion is the censorship-resistance mechanism, so a permissioned enqueue (allowlist, deposit, minimum stake) would change the section 1 guarantee while still satisfying every sentence in 7.1.

**Would close it.** State that `saveForcedInclusion` is permissionless and nonpayable apart from the fee, or name the caller restriction.
