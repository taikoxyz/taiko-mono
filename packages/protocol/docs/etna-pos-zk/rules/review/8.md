# 8 — review notes

**Non-normative.** Nothing here can be conformed to or failed against. This is what decomposing [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) section 8 into citable rules turned up: questions the text does not settle, and sentences whose normative status was ambiguous. Each item says what would close it. Only the spec owner decides.

## Q8-1

**No objective trigger separates a long liveness pause from a recovery**

**Text.** "**Scope**: an automatically retrying liveness pause loses no blocks; executing governance recovery discards the suffix above c." The recovery bullet is "for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear", and 8.1 adds "recover has no separate time threshold; the shortest stall before recovery depends on the delay of the DAO's authorization path."

**Why it matters.** A liveness pause may last indefinitely (section 12 item 17 names indefinite L1 finality failure). Recovery destroys the unsettled suffix, yet nothing in section 8 says when a pause stops being a pause. 6.4's U is the only quantitative signal, and 8.1 explicitly says slow progress, low TPS, a wall-clock FI delay or a missed 20 min target are never reasons to recover. The result is that the most consequential governance power in the document has no stated test, only a description ("long", "cannot clear") that the DAO applies. That may be intended, but it should be intentional rather than inferred.

**Would close it.** Either name a test the DAO is expected to apply (for example, back-pressure reached U and the settlement path is unavailable for a stated period), or state explicitly that the trigger is DAO judgement with no on-chain or objective condition.

## Q8-2

**The liveness/safety line for malformed data**

**Text.** Liveness: "So does an anchor header whose hash matches the canonical hash but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 (L1 changed the header format)". Safety: "malformed or unverifiable data from the node's own L1 node or EL (section 9; an L1 header whose hash matches but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 is excluded)".

**Why it matters.** The exclusion makes the two lists consistent, but the discriminator is only implicit: an authenticated-by-hash header that this client cannot parse is a liveness problem, while anything else "malformed or unverifiable" is a safety halt. A truncated header, a proof that fails to verify, or a header whose hash does not match can all be described as "malformed", and an implementer must decide which bucket each falls into. The buckets differ observably: one keeps the process alive and retrying, the other exits it.

**Would close it.** State the discriminator for malformed or unverifiable data explicitly (for example: a payload whose hash matches the node's own authenticated L1 view is a liveness pause if unparseable; other malformed or unverifiable data from the own L1 node or EL is a safety halt) and apply it to the proof cases too. Transient infrastructure failures stay liveness pauses, as section 8 and FI-7.2-9 already say: a lagging, unreachable or timed-out node, or a SYNCING `newPayload`.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-3

**Committee-derivation failure: which kind of halt?**

**Text.** "an empty candidate set or failed authentication/numeric constraint under 4.3 rejects S(t) proposals and may persistently halt production. Do not locally scale, add members or skip rotation."

**Why it matters.** Section 8 defines two halt classes with different process behaviour: a liveness pause keeps the process running and resumes automatically; a safety halt exits and needs manual investigation. This bullet says "persistently halt production" without saying which. That determines whether an operator keeps the node up (and whether it can resume when candidates appear) or treats it as a safety incident. The prohibition on local scaling is also unenforced and unclassified: it is a rule on operators, not on the protocol.

**Would close it.** Classify the derivation failure as a liveness pause (most consistent with "may persistently halt" and automatic resume when stake returns) or a safety halt, and state what the operator is expected to do.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-4

**Consequence of violating the requirements that name none**

**Text.** Section 8 mixes four kinds of rule. It has block-validity statements (the Scope sentence; superseded generations), node-process rules (liveness and safety halts, ABCI constraints), on-chain call preconditions and effects (8.3, 8.4), and governance obligations (8.1). Only a few name their consequence: step 2 says "otherwise reject the whole operation"; 8.3 says "any failure reverts everything"; 8.4 says "A missing record is rejected". The rest say "must", "never", "cannot" or "only" without an outcome.

**Why it matters.** When a requirement is violated, an implementer needs to know whether the result is a block-validity failure (consensus-time rejection), a proof-side rejection (the batch cannot be proven), a revert of the recover call, a node halt, or merely a governance breach with no on-chain effect. The vectors therefore say "violates <ID>" rather than naming one. Until this is decided, two implementers can both conform to the wording and enforce in different places.

**Would close it.** State it once per mechanism — for example, "any violation of an 8.3 precondition makes the recover call revert", "any violation of an 8.4 genesis rule makes the block invalid or the proof fail", and "8.1 rules are governance obligations with no on-chain check" — or add the consequence to each rule.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-5

**The verifier-failure rollback has no on-chain definition of "the first bad landing"**

**Text.** "The last trusted landing is the last landing before the first bad one; if there is none, restore the initialization values." And: "Node rules for handling the rollback ship with it."

**Why it matters.** The rollback is an Inbox/registry upgrade, so its parameters are chosen when the upgrade is written. Nothing on chain records which landing is bad; the identification depends on the DAO's investigation. The text also defers the node rules to the upgrade, so the document that constitutes the protocol does not contain the rules for the change with the largest state effect in the document. A reviewer cannot validate the rollback against section 8 alone.

**Would close it.** State that the target landing is a DAO-supplied parameter of the upgrade and how it is authenticated, and either include the node rules or name the document that will carry them and its acceptance gate.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-6

**"Every exit confirmation" may not fit in one transaction**

**Text.** "The registry re-checks every exit confirmation against the restored snapshot, keeping it only if that snapshot ≥ the entry's exitEffectiveL1 and clearing it otherwise."

**Why it matters.** The registry has 4096 slots, so "every exit confirmation" can be thousands of storage updates in the same upgrade transaction. No bound, pagination or multi-transaction procedure is given, and a partial re-check would leave some confirmations validated against a snapshot that no longer exists. This is a liveness and correctness constraint on the recovery path, which is exactly when gas headroom is least likely.

**Would close it.** State the bound and the procedure when the set exceeds it (pagination, a Merkle-based check, or an invariant that makes the re-check unnecessary).

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-7

**The orphan generation above the restored c**

**Text.** "The rollback returns c to an earlier value ... the rollback itself does not change the generation." And 8.3: "generation never decreases."

**Why it matters.** If a governance recovery executed at a c above the restored one, that generation's slot-290 origin lies above the restored c, and the section says "no landing can succeed in it; the DAO must follow the rollback with a new governance recovery from the restored c". That leaves a written, never-deletable origin record for a generation that cannot produce. The text does not say whether nodes must refuse to start that generation, whether its `chain_id` is permanently unused, or whether the next recovery's origin (also written once) coexists with it. This is derivable from the rules but not stated, and it affects signers and joining nodes.

**Would close it.** State that such a generation is abandoned (nodes never authenticate or sign it), that its origin record is retained only for history, and that the next recovery starts from the restored c.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-8

**"Excluded entries" means two different lists**

**Text.** Step 2: "Each excluded entry must still match the entryKey given in the call". Step 3: "Each fiToClear index ...". Step 5: "Excluded entries are ineligible; other already active candidates may enter."

**Why it matters.** The exclusions list names registry entries by `(bondId, entryKey)`; the `fiToClear` list names FI entries by index. Step 5's "excluded entries" are registry candidates, but a reader moving from step 3 can read it as FI entries. The two interpretations predict different post-recovery committee membership, which is the outcome the exclusions exist to control.

**Would close it.** Say "excluded registry entries" in step 5, and use "FI entries" consistently for `fiToClear`.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-9

**"Execution details and complete lists" has no schema**

**Text.** Step 4: "Increment generation ..., write the fixed origin in 8.4, and emit execution details and complete lists."

**Why it matters.** Indexers, nodes and the DAO need the exact event names and fields to reconstruct what happened. Appendix A lists `RecoveryNonceInvalidated` and the forfeiture events but no recovery event. Without a schema, two implementations can emit different data and both claim conformance, and public reconstruction (5.3) cannot rely on the event.

**Would close it.** Name the event(s) and their fields in appendix A, or state that the emission is informative and not part of the interface.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8-10

**"Different" Inbox generation at a trust check versus 3.4's smaller-generation rejection**

**Text.** Section 8: "a startup or signer-session trust check that finds an Inbox generation different from this chain's, is a safety halt." 3.4 rule 4: "If the Inbox's generation is larger, this chain has been superseded (section 8); if it is smaller, reject the block."

**Why it matters.** "Different" at a trust check includes a smaller Inbox generation. But a smaller Inbox generation is the case 3.4 describes as a block rejection, which is a proposal-level action, not a halt. If a node starts with generation G and the Inbox reports G−1, is that a safety halt (the local chain is ahead of L1) or a rejection? The answer decides whether the operator is paged.

**Would close it.** Say whether "different" means larger only, or both, and reconcile the smaller case with 3.4's rejection.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)
