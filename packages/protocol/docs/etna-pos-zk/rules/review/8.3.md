## Q8.3-1

**What binds the expected values and `validUntil` to a prior DAO authorization?**

**Text.** "At execution expectedGeneration, expectedC and expectedRecoveryNonce must equal current state, and `block.timestamp <= validUntil`; validUntil is an absolute L1 Unix timestamp in seconds fixed at authorization." And: "For recovery authorization, Inbox stores only a monotonically increasing uint256 recoveryNonce, initially zero, with no recovery queue or scheduling records."

**Why it matters.** The Inbox keeps no record of the authorization, so it cannot check that any value was "fixed at authorization". The owner at execution can submit any `validUntil` in the future and any parameters that match current state; the binding to a DAO decision is governance-process, not on-chain. That is workable, but the section reads as if the contract enforces it, and a reviewer cannot tell which values are trusted.

**Would close it.** State that authorization is the DAO's on-chain proposal and where its parameters are recorded, or say explicitly that the contract checks only the values at execution and the binding is a governance-process obligation.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-2

**Can the owner burn a nonce with no pending authorization?**

**Text.** "Only the Inbox owner may call `invalidateRecoveryNonce(expectedRecoveryNonce)`; require a match and increment to invalidate all unexecuted authorizations with that nonce."

**Why it matters.** The check is a match against the current nonce, not against a set of outstanding authorizations (the Inbox keeps none). So a matching call always increments and burns a nonce even when nothing is pending. That may be intended as a simple kill switch, but it means the owner can advance the nonce at any time, and observers cannot tell whether an authorization was actually invalidated. Appendix A lists `RecoveryNonceInvalidated(oldNonce,newNonce)`; this section does not require the event.

**Would close it.** State that a match alone is sufficient (and that a no-op invalidation is allowed), and require the event.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-3

**How does an upgrade increment the nonce atomically?**

**Text.** "Verifier-failure rollback also preserves and increments the current nonce"; "The rollback returns c to an earlier value, so the same upgrade must preserve and increment the current recoveryNonce (8.3)".

**Why it matters.** The rollback is an Inbox upgrade, not a call. The text requires the upgrade to read slot 292 and write the incremented value, but does not say how (an initializer, a migration step, a storage write in the upgrade transaction) or what happens if the increment fails while the rest of the rollback succeeds. If the upgrade ships without it, every prior authorization survives — the exact failure the rule exists to prevent. This is a concrete, checkable requirement on a future upgrade that is currently only prose.

**Would close it.** State the mechanism and that the rollback aborts if the increment cannot be performed, and add it to the upgrade's acceptance checks.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-4

**`generation + 1` exceeding uint48 names no outcome**

**Text.** "Increment generation (generation+1 must fit uint48)".

**Why it matters.** The parenthetical states a precondition without a consequence. At the maximum generation, does `recover` revert (and the chain is permanently unrecoverable), or is there another path? This is the last-resort mechanism, so "recovery is impossible at the maximum generation" is a statement worth making explicitly rather than leaving to be inferred from a bounds check.

**Would close it.** State that a generation at the uint48 maximum cannot recover, or define the behaviour at the boundary.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-5

**`sweepProtocolFees` is absent from the reentrancy list**

**Text.** "reject cross-function reentrancy that could change the checkpoint, generation, recovery lists or withdrawal eligibility, including land, recover, recovery-nonce invalidation and registry mutations or withdrawals."

**Why it matters.** `sweepProtocolFees` (7.1) is an Inbox entry point that makes an external call to the treasury. It cannot change the checkpoint, generation, lists or withdrawal eligibility, so the stated criterion excludes it — but it is an external call during which a contract treasury can reenter. The list reads as exhaustive ("including"), so a reader can conclude the sweep is deliberately out of scope, or overlook it.

**Would close it.** State whether the sweep shares the recover/land lock and confirm that it cannot change recovery state.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-6

**"Does not restrict owner exits" versus rejecting reentrant registry mutations**

**Text.** "This does not restrict owner exits, double-sign slashing (4.1) or the independent responsibility forfeiture and forced exit in 4.4." And, in the same bullet: "During recovery, reject cross-function reentrancy that could change ... withdrawal eligibility ... including ... registry mutations or withdrawals."

**Why it matters.** The two sentences read as opposites if "during recovery" is taken broadly: one says exits are not restricted, the other says reentrant withdrawals are rejected. The consistent reading is that independent transactions may still exit and be slashed, while reentrant calls inside `recover` are rejected. That reading should be stated, because the difference decides whether an owner's exit can be processed in the block that performs recovery.

**Would close it.** Say explicitly that "does not restrict" applies to separate transactions, and that calls reentering `recover` are rejected.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.3-7

**A failed precondition names no revert**

**Text.** "only the Inbox owner (the DAO) may call `recover`"; "Both recover and invalidateRecoveryNonce require Inbox initialization"; "At execution expectedGeneration, expectedC and expectedRecoveryNonce must equal current state".

**Why it matters.** The bullet states conditions with "only", "require" and "must" but no outcome. The atomicity bullet says "any failure reverts everything", which covers failures inside the transition, but a caller or precondition failure is arguably outside it. Implementers will revert (the natural Solidity reading), and the vectors say "does not satisfy" rather than "reverts" to avoid inventing it; a one-line statement would remove the ambiguity.

**Would close it.** State that every failed condition in this bullet reverts the call.
