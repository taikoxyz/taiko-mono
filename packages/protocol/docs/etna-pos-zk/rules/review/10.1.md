## Q10.1-1

**No artifact encodes the upgrade plan, and no enforcement mechanism exists**

**Text.** "An upgrade plan must bind the deployment, generation, boundary height H ..., old/new client and EL rules, guest images and verifier acceptance ranges. Authenticate it through the actual DAO/timelock authority and enforce it in both nodes and L1 verification; an off-chain manifest or operator agreement alone is insufficient."

**Why it matters.** The rule states what is insufficient (an off-chain manifest) but not what is sufficient: there is no schema, no on-chain object, no acceptance-range format, and no activation transaction. Nodes cannot enforce a plan they cannot parse, and L1 cannot enforce a plan it does not store. The section itself says the mechanism "will be added later", so today every requirement here is unenforceable, and START-10.1-6 implies the rules are frozen — but no rule says so.

**Would close it.** define the plan artifact, the height-scoped on-chain acceptance ranges and the activation transaction (or state explicitly that the rules are frozen until that mechanism lands and that 10.1 is currently a design constraint only).

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-2

**Who checks the boundary height's grid condition, and what happens when it fails**

**Text.** "boundary height H (a grid point, since landings end only at grid points, 6.1; an upgrade that changes K needs H to be a multiple of both the old and the new K, or no origin on the new grid remains)".

**Why it matters.** This is arithmetic that someone must verify: a node, a guest, or an L1 acceptance range. The text names no checker and no consequence. If a plan picks an H that is not a multiple of both K values, the new grid has no origin, and the failure would surface much later — as an unprovable or unlandable batch — rather than at activation. Two implementers can disagree about whether H must be a grid point under the old K, the new K, or both.

**Would close it.** name the checker and the failure (reject the plan at activation, refuse the landing, or fail the proof) and state which K the grid-point condition uses on each side of H.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-3

**"Settle" is the precondition of the switch and is not a checkable predicate**

**Text.** "Protocol upgrades stop production and settle before switching rules" and "Settle through H under the old rules and authenticate its EL/CometBFT checkpoint before activating the new rules at H + 1."

**Why it matters.** Is "settled through H" a landed checkpoint whose height is exactly H, a checkpoint at any grid point ≥ H, or all L2 blocks ≤ H decided? The difference matters when settlement has stalled near H and when §6.4's U bound applies. Without a predicate, two operators can disagree about whether the precondition holds, and the switch becomes a judgement call at the most dangerous moment.

**Would close it.** define settle-through-H (for example "a landing whose checkpoint height is exactly H exists on L1 and is adopted by an anchor on the new chain's first block") and say who checks it.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-4

**The line between an operational restart and a protocol upgrade is not drawn**

**Text.** "Operational restarts that preserve validity rules retain all same-domain signer state."

**Why it matters.** A restart that swaps a client binary or a guest image may or may not change the validity rules, and this section treats image changes as upgrades elsewhere. An operator can therefore label a rule change an "operational restart" and skip H, the plan and the acceptance ranges — the exact bypass START-10.1-3 forbids for immutables. The test for "preserves validity rules" is never given.

**Would close it.** define rule-preserving by reference to the bound artifacts (same statement/config/image versions and acceptance ranges), and state what changes still count as a restart.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-5

**"Explicitly invalidated" names no mechanism**

**Text.** "Old jobs must validly settle before the switch or be explicitly invalidated, never rewritten into a new version."

**Why it matters.** A lander holds a signed transaction; unless something on L1 refuses it, the job remains submittable and may land after the switch, proving old rules above H. "Explicitly invalidated" could mean an on-chain acceptance-range change, a published cancellation list, or an operator announcement. Only the first is enforceable, and the text does not choose. The same silence surrounds "never rewritten": rewriting is prohibited, but nothing detects it.

**Would close it.** name the invalidation artifact and its effect (for example "after H, L1 rejects any proof whose image checks only the old rules"), and say who may invalidate.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-6

**Acceptance ranges may overlap at the boundary, and the text does not say whether that is allowed**

**Text.** "Retain old rule/image material for historical replay, but L1 settlement above H must no longer accept an image that only checks the old rules. New images must not implicitly change history through H."

**Why it matters.** "An image that only checks the old rules" leaves open an image that checks both. Such an overlapping image is the natural way to let a pre-boundary job settle after H — but it also allows a proof about old-rule blocks to be produced after the switch, which is exactly what the first sentence seems to forbid. An implementer cannot tell which side of the line an overlapping image is on.

**Would close it.** state whether acceptance ranges may overlap at or after H and, if so, what an overlapping image is permitted to prove.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q10.1-7

**A signer that does not stop is not addressed**

**Text.** "Before the boundary, coordinate signers to stop above H while retaining all HRS state." and "The plan must take effect before signing crosses the boundary."

**Why it matters.** "Coordinate" is not a rule on an individual signer: nothing says what happens if one signs at H+1 before the plan is effective. Is the block invalid under 3.4? Is it a safety halt? Is the vote a slashable double sign? 8.2's per-generation domains and 9's checkpoint fence do not cover same-generation votes above H, so the one behaviour the whole upgrade depends on has no stated consequence.

**Would close it.** state the consequence for a signature above H before plan activation, and whether it is slashable or only unlandable.
