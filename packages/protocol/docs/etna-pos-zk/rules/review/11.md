## Q11-1

**U = 3,600 is stated as a target, a reference and an imperative**

**Text.** §10: "Use U=3,600"; §11 row U: "the target deployment selects U=3,600 (about 4 h), subject to validation"; PARAM-11-2: "Values in the table are references only".

**Why it matters.** A deployment with U = 10,000 satisfies PARAM-11-8's bounds and every other numbered rule, yet arguably violates "Use U=3,600". Conversely a target deployment at U = 3,600 needs no exception. The three sentences give the same number three different statuses, and the answer is observable at acceptance: either U is a launch requirement or it is a suggestion.

**Would close it.** state one status for U = 3,600 — a launch requirement for the target deployment, or a reference value with no obligation — and align §10's imperative with it.

## Q11-2

**K is called a protocol constant and simultaneously "not fixed yet"**

**Text.** row K: "Protocol constant built into the Inbox and both guests ... Not fixed yet; set from proving capacity measured during acceptance".

**Why it matters.** K defines the grid (every landing height is a multiple of K, 6.1) and therefore the meaning of every historical grid point. If K can change after launch, 10.1 requires an H that is a multiple of both the old and the new K, and historical heights are reinterpreted; if K cannot change, the row should say it is deployment-immutable. The row says neither, so a deployment that fixes K at launch and one that treats K as upgradeable both conform.

**Would close it.** classify K (deployment-immutable, upgradeable only with a 10.1 boundary, or config-hash parameter) and state whether a change reinterprets past grid points.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q11-3

**The leaf-calldata bound names no gas limit and no conversion**

**Text.** "maxBatchBlocks ≥ K, and small enough that its leaf calldata ((maxBatchBlocks/K − 1) × 32 bytes) fits within the per-transaction gas limit".

**Why it matters.** The formula produces bytes, while the constraint is a gas limit, and no calldata gas price or margin is given. "The per-transaction gas limit" could be the L1 block gas limit, an L1 transaction cap the spec does not otherwise define, or the L2 block gas limit. The bound is not checkable until the limit and the conversion are named, and a deployment could satisfy it under one reading and fail under another.

**Would close it.** name the limit and the worst-case gas price/margin (for example "fits within 30,000,000 L1 gas at 16 gas per non-zero byte"), or express the bound directly in bytes.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q11-4

**"The double-sign evidence window" is not the 48-hour or 100,000-block window the spec elsewhere defines**

**Text.** "`withdrawalDelay` runs from exitConfirmedAtL1 and is no shorter than the double-sign evidence window (suggested ≥ 7 days, about 50,400 L1 blocks)".

**Why it matters.** The only other evidence windows in the spec are the CometBFT evidence limits (100,000 blocks / 48 h, 3.1 and 10) and the local pool's 100,000-block/48-hour limits (9.1). Seven days is 50,400 L1 blocks at 12 s, so the comparison target appears to be a new quantity. If "the double-sign evidence window" meant the Comet 100,000-block limit, the suggested seven days would violate the row; if it means seven days, the row is circular. A deployment cannot check the inequality.

**Would close it.** define the evidence window in L1 blocks (or state that it is exactly the suggested 7 days / 50,400 blocks) and make the inequality refer to that definition.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q11-5

**"This response budget" has no antecedent, owner or acceptance**

**Text.** "Publish and exercise this response budget; no guaranteed reaction time is implied."

**Why it matters.** "This" points at the preceding analysis of detection, DAO decision and L1 inclusion, but there is no budget format, no publisher, no threshold and no place to publish. "Exercise" is untestable. Yet this is the only rule that addresses the emergency-execution risk the paragraph itself raises ("forfeiture needs timely emergency execution or may be lost"). A deployment that writes "no guaranteed reaction time" and nothing else conforms.

**Would close it.** make the budget a deployment-manifest field with its components (detection, DAO authorization, L1 inclusion) and a tested drill, or state that it is guidance.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q11-6

**How a change to the EL or zk-gas parameters is activated is unspecified**

**Text.** row: "A change needs a coordinated client upgrade"; 10.1: "registering a new image alone is not a protocol upgrade".

**Why it matters.** These two rules together imply that changing `L2_BLOCK_ZK_GAS_LIMIT` or the multiplier table needs a client upgrade plus a 10.1 plan, and 10.1's mechanism "will be added later". So today the parameters arguably cannot change at all — but no rule says they are frozen, and START-10.1-6 only forbids treating image registration alone as an upgrade. Operators facing a metering bug have no stated path.

**Would close it.** state the activation path for these parameters (a special case of 10.1, with which artifact carries the new values) or state that they are frozen until 10.1 lands.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q11-7

**"No wall-clock takeover warning" is a limitation living inside a rule row**

**Text.** row L: "Choose L for rotation and ordinary L+2 history, independently of U; no wall-clock takeover warning".

**Why it matters.** The clause is a disclosed limitation (4.3 repeats it), but it sits inside a row whose ID, PARAM-11-4, marks a rule. A reader citing the limitation cites a rule that binds nothing about warnings, and section 12's register — the place for limitations — does not contain it. Either the monitoring expectation that replaces a warning is stated, or the clause belongs in section 12.

**Would close it.** move the clause to section 12 as a limitation, or state the monitoring/alerting expectation that substitutes for a wall-clock warning.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)
