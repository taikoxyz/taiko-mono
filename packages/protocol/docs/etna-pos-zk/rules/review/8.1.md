# 8.1 — review notes

**Non-normative.** Nothing here can be conformed to or failed against. This is what decomposing [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) section 8.1 into citable rules turned up: questions the text does not settle, and sentences whose normative status was ambiguous. Each item says what would close it. Only the spec owner decides.

## Normativity boundary

| Sentence | Why it has no ID | Proposed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| "The emergency path that skips the veto period should be used only for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear, and on that path the lists may become public only at execution, with no advance notice." | "should" is guidance, and the public-visibility claim is not something L1 can enforce (calldata is public when broadcast) | keep un-numbered; see [Q8.1-1](#q81-1) and [Q8.1-7](#q81-7) |

Every other requirement-bearing sentence in 8.1 carries an ID.

## Q8.1-1

**What is "a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear"?**

**Text.** "recover has no separate time threshold; the shortest stall before recovery depends on the delay of the DAO's authorization path." The un-numbered sentence adds: "The emergency path that skips the veto period should be used only for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear".

**Why it matters.** Recovery discards the unsettled suffix, and section 8 gives the DAO no test to apply before using it. 6.4's U is the only quantitative signal in the document, but reaching U is a normal back-pressure pause, and 8.1 says slow progress, low TPS, a wall-clock FI delay or a missed 20 min target are never reasons to recover. The same words ("long", "cannot clear") are used for the emergency path, which skips the veto period entirely. Two different DAOs can read this differently, and the difference is measured in discarded blocks.

**Would close it.** State the evidence the DAO is expected to require (for example: back-pressure at U, no landing possible because the settlement path is unavailable, and the failure has persisted for a stated period), or say explicitly that the judgement is the DAO's and no objective test exists.

## Q8.1-2

**A landing during the authorization period destroys the recovery, and landings are not frozen**

**Text.** "expectedC is set to the current c and fixed at authorization, so any landing after that makes the call unexecutable"; 8.3 adds "authorization or queueing does not freeze landing."

**Why it matters.** On the standard path the DAO waits for a veto period plus a timelock; during that window any honest lander can advance c and invalidate the authorization. The text states the implication but not the procedure: does the DAO re-authorize with the new c (8.3's "changed parameters require fresh DAO authorization"), and are landers expected to stop landing during an authorized recovery? The first is mechanical, the second is coordination that nothing records. If landers keep working normally, a standard-path recovery can be starved indefinitely by successful settlement, which is arguably the right outcome (settlement is working), but the text does not say so.

**Would close it.** State the expected behaviour when a landing invalidates an authorization, and whether landers are asked to pause during the authorization window.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.1-3

**The pre-authorization checklist is not measurable as written**

**Text.** "Before scheduling and executing, it checks a nonempty intended set, more than 2/3 online power, no party at or above 1/3, and available history and signers".

**Why it matters.** "Party" is undefined (operator, entity, address cluster?), "online power" has no measurement method or time window, and "available history and signers" has no threshold. The checks also have no stated failure action: does a failed check forbid recovery, require a retry, or require disclosure? These are the checks that are supposed to prevent a recovery that cannot finish, so leaving them qualitative is a real gap even for a governance-process rule.

**Would close it.** Define each quantity and its measurement window, and state the action when a check fails.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.1-4

**Which "honest nodes" define the match, and is any mismatch enough?**

**Text.** "confirms that c's landing matches honest nodes' local chain: the EL hash, Comet hash and FI head in its slot 285 record, the SignalService checkpoint at c, and slot 284 (the selection snapshot of the committee that signed c). A mismatch means the verifier has failed".

**Why it matters.** The four items are compared against "honest nodes' local chain", but the text does not say whose view is authoritative when nodes disagree, nor whether a single mismatch is sufficient to conclude verifier failure and trigger the rollback. A node on a different fork, a stale local node, or a wrong slot-284 read can all produce a mismatch without a verifier failure — and the prescribed response is the largest state change in the document.

**Would close it.** Name the comparison source (the DAO's own L1 view, plus at least one independent honest node?) and state whether corroboration is required before the rollback.

## Q8.1-5

**Economic review: owner, deadline and effect of a severe shortfall**

**Text.** "estimate the intended committee against the launch checklist's capital target, then reassess the actual snapshot after R is finalized, including newly activated registrations and top-ups." And: "A capital shortfall or expired valuation triggers an alert, public disclosure and replenishment, not a new recovery or signer veto; existing quorum, concentration and authentication checks remain."

**Why it matters.** The reassessment happens "after R is finalized", but signing for the new generation can start once authentication passes; the text does not order the two. It also does not say who performs the review, who discloses, or what happens if the shortfall is large enough to break the section 1 security assumption. The text says the shortfall is not a signer veto, which implies signing continues with a disclosed shortfall. That may be intended, but it deserves an explicit statement because "continue with a publicly known weak committee" is a consequential choice.

**Would close it.** State the order of the reassessment relative to the first signature, the responsible party, and whether any magnitude of shortfall stops the recovery or only triggers disclosure.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.1-6

**Exclusion is not forfeiture — is that deliberate?**

**Text.** Step 2 of recovery: "Inbox calls the registry to set exitEffectiveL1=min(existing,R), with no forfeiture by recover itself". 8.1: "Recover neither executes nor cancels responsibility forfeiture and does not wait for it".

**Why it matters.** A recovery exclusion forces an entry out of the committee but takes no principal. Section 4.4 forfeiture is the only penalty path and needs its own authorization and `validUntil`. A reader can ask whether excluding an operator for misbehaviour without forfeiture is the intended separation (committee hygiene versus punishment), and whether the DAO is expected to run both procedures for the same incident. The text says they are independent, but not what the intended combination is.

**Would close it.** Confirm that exclusion is not a penalty and that 4.4 is the only path that takes principal, and say whether the two are expected to be used together.

Raised by: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (dantaik)

## Q8.1-7

**"No advance notice" cannot be enforced on L1**

**Text.** "on that path the lists may become public only at execution, with no advance notice."

**Why it matters.** An L1 transaction's calldata is public when it is broadcast to the mempool, before execution. The protocol has no confidential-execution feature, so "public only at execution" can only mean "not announced by the DAO", not "not observable". Read as a protocol rule it is false; read as an operational intent it is fine. The distinction matters because reviewers and auditors will otherwise test for a property the system cannot have.

**Would close it.** Reword as an operational intent (no pre-announcement; the lists are not queued on chain) or state how the transaction is kept private until inclusion.
