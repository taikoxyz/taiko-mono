# Etna PoS + ZK Design Spec

> **In one sentence**: In Etna, a committee that stakes TAIKO on L2 produces blocks with stock CometBFT and finalizes them instantly, and a key set the DAO sets on L1 produces them while that committee's stake is below a threshold the DAO sets; anyone can land a proven batch on L1, and funds on L1 recognize only landed checkpoints.
>
> **Scope**: Etna begins after height S, the Etna start: the last Unzen block on a network that switches from Unzen ([SWITCH.md](SWITCH.md)), or block 0 on a new network.
>
> **Target**: Stage 0; Stage 1 is not a goal for now.

## 1. Positioning and security model

- **Positioning**: a PoS-sequenced validity rollup, not a based rollup. L2 PoS decides transaction order: the committee is the bonds seated in the L2 staking contract (4.1), and while their total stake is below the threshold the DAO sets in the Inbox (`minTotalStake`), the fallback key set the DAO sets there signs instead (4.3). Ethereum does not sequence, it only provides DA (blobs), proof verification, settlement and the bridge's anchor.
- **Funds**: L1 recognizes only landed checkpoints, and every landing needs two proofs, at least one of them ZK (6.2). Even the whole committee acting maliciously cannot take funds on L1. The same holds while the fallback key set signs: the chain then runs on keys the DAO chose, which no stake backs, and funds on L1 are still protected by the two proofs.
- **Stake token**: the committee's stake is the bridged L2 TAIKO token (STAKE-4.1-37a), so its security also rests on the integrity of that token. Anything that mints L2 TAIKO that no TAIKO on L1 backs, such as a forgery against the L1 → L2 bridge or its token vault, or an upgrade of the token, lets its holder register bonds that take seats at once and sign from three blocks later (4.1, 4.3): enough to hold more than 2/3 of the voting power, or to fill every seat so that honest registrations revert. The DAO's response is to set `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1, which makes the fallback key set sign whatever stake L2 state shows (COMM-4.3-3a, COMM-4.3-19), and to forfeit those bonds under 4.4. A committee taken over this way still cannot take funds on L1 through a false landing, which the two proofs prevent. The proofs do not protect the TAIKO that L1 holds for the bridge from the unbacked units themselves: a valid landing lets their holder bridge them to L1 like any other TAIKO, and limiting that loss is a matter for the bridge and its token vault, which this specification does not cover (section 12, item 28).
- **Finality**: while Byzantine voting power stays below 1/3, a CometBFT commit is final and consensus never rolls back a committed block; the only exception is governance recovery, which discards blocks above the latest checkpoint (section 8). Nothing at or below a checkpoint can be rolled back except by a DAO upgrade after verifier failure (section 8), so exchanges and bridges should rely on checkpoints. While the fallback key set signs, the bound applies to its keys, each with power 1, and no stake backs them: a double sign by one of them can be slashed only through a bond registered to the same key (COMM-4.3-8c), so finality then rests on the key holders the DAO chose (section 12, item 23).
- **Liveness**: needs more than 2/3 of the voting power of the set that signs online, whether the committee or the fallback key set (COMM-4.3-20), L1 finalizing, and landings continuing (6.4).
- **Censorship resistance**: forced inclusion is enforced by the proof: a due queue-head entry can only be included or legally skipped, otherwise the block is invalid. Entries cannot be deleted merely because they are old; the only exception is that governance recovery (section 8) can explicitly blank specified entries. Progress depends on consensus, L1 finality, queue service and settlement; this does not provide a wall-clock inclusion deadline (sections 7 and 3.4).
- **Upgrades and trust**: `EtnaInbox`, the L1 `SignalService` and the L1 Bridge are upgradeable proxies whose owner should be the DAO (behind a timelock), and the L2 `EtnaStaking` is an upgradeable proxy owned by the DAO's execution address on L2 (STAKE-4.1-40); an upgrade can bypass any rule in this spec, and an upgrade of `EtnaStaking` can change the committee rules (section 12, item 27). Verifier soundness, including the management of SGX instances and RISC0/SP1 images, is a separate assumption. **[MODEL-1-1]** Proof completeness is another: every block that is valid under this spec and within the EL limits (gas, zk gas) and the 3.4 block size bound must be provable by at least one valid proof combination, and **[MODEL-1-2]** each such block must be provable on its own within one landing's two proofs (6.1), counting in the worst case its 7.2 single-transaction build trial (which can use up to another full block's zk budget) and the block-start staking call that the block and that trial each make (CONS-3.2-13, FI-7.2-8), each at its full 30,000,000-gas limit, which neither the block's gasUsed nor its zk gas meters. A mismatch between the EL and the guests, or a guest resource limit below the EL limits, violates it; step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) can blank such a transaction while it is in the forced inclusion queue, but blanking does not stop the same bytes from being enqueued again (section 12 item 20). **[MODEL-1-3]** `land` and `saveForcedInclusion` are not affected by pause; if both proof systems fail at once, losses can only be contained by pausing `SignalService` and the Bridge and by QuotaManager limits, and settlement is rolled back with the Inbox upgrade that replaces the verifier (section 8).
- **Out of scope**: PoS issuance rewards and bootstrapping CometBFT from L1 alone.
- **Slashing scope**: same-round double signing forfeits all remaining principal (4.1). While blocks are produced, a seated bond whose key misses `missLimit` consecutive blocks automatically loses `missSlashBps` basis points of its stake, rounded up (STAKE-4.2-6). A halt produces no blocks and so no automatic penalty (RECOV-8-21); for it, the DAO may attribute responsibility and apply a one-time fixed-rate forfeiture with forced exit under 4.4. The fallback keys have no stake to lose unless a bond is registered to the same key (COMM-4.3-8c).
- **DAO forfeiture authority**: the DAO forfeits on the L2 staking contract, through an L1 → L2 governance message (FORF-4.4-1), whenever L2 produces blocks: during normal operation or in the generation a governance recovery starts, including immediate execution through its emergency path. The staking contract adds no waiting, freeze or appeal period and does not distinguish governance paths. It checks authority, bond identity, amount and authorization expiry, not the correctness of attribution. Section 1's upgrade trust remains.

## 3. Consensus and blocks

### 3.1 Consensus engine

- **[CONS-3.1-1]** Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 ([source commit `0880b4d378f347ab16e54ec677ff50d803f37d62`](https://github.com/cometbft/cometbft/tree/0880b4d378f347ab16e54ec677ff50d803f37d62)) runs in its own process and drives the application over an ABCI++ socket. Use the official upstream executable. The offline store tool for 5.4 import and CONS-3.1-9 certified first-block repair may link the pinned CometBFT module to write its native stores. Run it with CometBFT stopped and the signer fenced, verify and durably stage an aligned Comet/app/EL boundary, and start the official executable only after publication. The tool never runs consensus or signs. The deployment manifest identifies its upstream release artifact for each supported platform and pins the executable digest, source commit, build flags and configuration. Check the digest against the installed executable; a reported version string alone does not identify the build. **[CONS-3.1-2]** Proposer selection, timeouts, locking and BlockID all use stock behavior; the engine is not forked.

<details>
<summary>Details: secp256k1eth signing format and chain_id construction</summary>

- **[CONS-3.1-3]** Consensus keys use CometBFT v0.40.0's `secp256k1eth`: sign bytes are hashed with keccak256 (no prefix) and signed; the signature is 65 bytes `R‖S‖V` (`V ∈ {0, 1}`, low-s required); the public key is a 33-byte compressed SEC1 key; and the validator address is the Ethereum address `keccak256(uncompressed key without its first byte)[12:]`. A contract can therefore verify any vote directly with `ecrecover` (double-sign slashing in 4.1). CometBFT does not batch-verify these keys. Under 4.3, a candidate member's voting power is the power in its snapshot seat record (COMM-4.3-7), which the staking contract sets to ⌊effStake / `vp_unit`⌋, and each fallback key's is 1, and the total is at most CometBFT's cap `(2^63 − 1) / 8`.
- **[CONS-3.1-4]** `chain_id = "etna-" + base64url_no_padding(chainDomain)` is exactly 48 ASCII bytes. `chainDomain` is the full 256-bit keccak256 digest binding the version, complete L1/L2 chain ids, Inbox proxy address and generation (appendix A), without truncation. Nodes, signers, guests and the staking contract recompute the same value. **[CONS-3.1-5]** Old-generation votes cannot authorize a new generation; another deployment's votes cannot authorize a commit or slashing here. Signing the same height in different domains is not a double sign. Stock CometBFT VoteSignBytes and vote encoding remain unchanged.

</details>

- **[CONS-3.1-6]** The mempool is `nop`: user transactions travel over EL devp2p, and the proposer's EL picks them from its own txpool.
- **[CONS-3.1-7]** CometBFT height equals the EL block number; consensus starts at S + 1, the block after the Etna start S (section 10). Each CometBFT block's `Data.Txs` holds exactly one item: the envelope (5.1).

<summary>Details: ABCI response constraints</summary>

- **[CONS-3.1-8]** ABCI responses: `app_hash` is appendix A's appHash, which commits the hash of the executed EL block and the validator state CometBFT holds after committing it, as the app computes that state under SYNC-9-5c (InitChain returns the appHash of the Etna start's hash (the genesis hash when S = 0) for the initial deployment and of ELHash(c) for governance recovery under 8.4, each with the validator state CometBFT builds from that generation's genesis); every transaction result (ExecTxResult) is fixed at code 0, empty data, and gas_wanted and gas_used of 0, all four of which enter LastResultsHash; **[CONS-3.1-9]** Info's `app_version` is always the app version in this generation's genesis consensus params (CometBFT reads it only while its own state holds no committed block); the header's app version is constant within a generation, and the ruleset that applies to each block comes from the 10.1 schedule, not from Info; consensus params are never updated. Info's `last_block_height` never exceeds the last height the app holds as committed, which is the height of its last completed Commit or, right after a 5.4 import at a checkpoint above the generation's start height, the boundary s; `last_block_app_hash` is the `app_hash` of the height reported, and the app answers only once its own state and the EL agree at that height. Before starting the stock handshake, reconcile any EL lag with signing fenced. From the last common verified state, re-execute the missing committed blocks using retained inputs and check their canonical EL hashes and execution state against the app’s committed boundary. Alternatively, start at an earlier fully aligned app/EL boundary only if the app can serve that state and the stock handshake can replay the retained suffix. Bind or fence any rebuilt stores under RECOV-8.2-2. Establish the boundary to report independently of Info and further CometBFT callbacks. If material is unavailable, report the missing height and retry acquisition outside the handshake. A contradictory result stops startup for investigation. Until the app holds a block of this generation as committed the height is 0, so that CometBFT sends InitChain; it does so on every start that finds the height 0, so InitChain returns the same `app_hash` each time, even if the EL already holds the generation's first block. The bound exists because CometBFT v0.40.0 stores a block's FinalizeBlock response before it calls Commit: for a height the app has only finalized, its startup handshake either fails, having no stored response, or replays the block from the stored response without calling the app's Commit. Reporting the committed height instead makes CometBFT, after a crash between the two, call FinalizeBlock for the next height again, and that call must return the same result even if the EL already holds the block. A generation's first block is not covered when initial_height is above 1: CometBFT's own state stays at height 0 until that block's state is saved, so after a crash between saving that block and saving its state, stock CometBFT v0.40.0 stops at its handshake whatever the app reports. Fence signing, preserve the original block store (including the seen commit), state store, consensus WAL, app/EL stores and external signer state. Recover the exact decided block and its >2/3 commit for the full BlockID from these records or a peer, verifying them against the fixed genesis, chain_id and genesis validator set (COMM-4.3-9). A single affected node may catch up with signing disabled from that genesis or an authenticated 5.4 checkpoint on the decided chain. If the committee is affected together, retain the same certified block and make it available for every node to apply or verify on its recovered chain. A deployment must rehearse the offline installation under CONS-3.1-1 or an equivalent procedure. Install an aligned, verified Comet/app/EL boundary for that block or a later authenticated boundary proven to descend from it, then resume stock operation. While the block, certificate or aligned state is unavailable keep affected signers fenced; never elect a replacement block for the same height. Rebuilt stores follow RECOV-8.2-2 before any signing. This specification uses `APP_VERSION` 1 (encoding versions in appendix A); checkpoint-trust metadata is node-local and does not enter `app_hash` or block bytes.

</details>

- The block interval is set by `timeout_commit`, an operating parameter rather than a validity rule (target about 4 s, assuming n ≲ 128). On a validator the same setting is how long the node waits for late precommits before the next height starts, so START-10-6 sets a lower bound for it.
- **[CONS-3.1-10]** Every Comet block has an empty evidence list. The stock proposer selects zero evidence bytes and stock block validation rejects a nonempty list before checking individual items, regardless of the local pending pool (START-10-21). The stock evidence reactor, pool and vote gossip stay, because CometBFT v0.40.0 cannot run without them, but nothing in this protocol reads the pool. No block commits a pending item, so only expiry removes one, and the stock pool limits neither how many valid items it holds nor how long they live while no block commits (section 12, item 22). The supervisor therefore deletes the node's pending-evidence store, with CometBFT stopped, every time it starts CometBFT, and START-10-21 keeps the age limits short. Anyone may collect conflicting signed votes and submit them directly to the staking contract on L2; double-sign slashing happens only through its `slashDoubleSign` (4.1).

**[CONS-3.2-1]** Every field except `beneficiary` is determined by the committed parent (and its parent's timestamp), the block's anchor, its LastCommit (for `extraData`'s `missBitmap`, 3.4 rule 6), BFT time and chain constants. The proposer builds with these values; other nodes recompute them and compare field by field.

stateRoot, receipts, gasUsed (the sum of included transactions’ EVM gasUsed) and the zk gas in `difficulty` are checked by the EL's `newPayload`. **[CONS-3.2-11]** `difficulty` accumulates only included transactions’ zk gas, including ordinary EVM revert/OOG transactions, not discarded over-limit trials or unselected transactions. **[CONS-3.2-12]** Block-start system processing, exactly the EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 system writes and the staking call that CONS-3.2-13 defines, is not rolled back with an aborted transaction.

**[CONS-3.2-13]** **Block-start staking call**: in every Etna block, after the EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 system writes and before the first transaction, the EL makes one message call to `onBlockStart` on the staking contract (STAKE-4.2-4), at the `EtnaStaking` proxy address built into it (PARAM-11-10b), from the system caller `0xfffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffffe`, with exactly 30,000,000 gas available to the call's frame. No intrinsic gas is charged for it, no transaction gas cap (EIP-7825) applies to it, and the system caller's nonce and balance do not change. The call starts with empty transient storage (EIP-1153) and with the accessed addresses and storage keys that EIP-2929 and EIP-3651 give a transaction from the system caller to the staking contract at its start, whatever the EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 writes before it accessed. The calldata is appendix A's (ENC-A-10d), with `generation` and `missBitmap` taken from this block's own `extraData`. Like the two writes, the call is block-start system processing: it is not counted in the block's gasUsed or in its zk gas (`difficulty`), and it pays no fee. Like them, it produces no receipt, and any log it emits enters neither a receipt nor the block's logsBloom, so its missed-signature penalties and the seats they release are not observable as events, only in the state the call leaves, for example through `snapshot()` and the seat lookups (STAKE-4.1-14d, STAKE-4.2-7). The call fails exactly when it reverts or halts exceptionally, running out of gas included. If it fails, the state stays as it was before the call and the failure does not make the block invalid; 3.4 applies its rules as to any other block. A call to an address that holds no code succeeds and changes nothing, so its outcome is the same. In either case the stored `snapshotHeight` differs from this block's height, so 4.3 hands the block two heights above this one to the fallback key set (COMM-4.3-3a). A fault of the node's own storage or environment, such as a failed read of its database, is not a failure of the call, and the node must not treat the block as valid on that basis.

### 3.3 Anchor

- **[CONS-3.3-1]** Every block has an anchor: the L1 block whose state it reads. A block either carries an anchor witness (the raw L1 header plus the Inbox account and storage proofs) or inherits its parent's anchor. **[CONS-3.3-2]** The first block of each generation and any block that moves the anchor must carry one; a block that keeps the anchor must not. **[CONS-3.3-3a]** The witness proves the Inbox's `recoveryGeneration`, `lastCheckpoint` (height, hash), `fallbackKeysHash` and `minTotalStake` (4.3), the forced inclusion queue's head and tail, and the rules schedule (10.1).
- **[CONS-3.3-4]** The proposer picks max(parent's anchor, local L1 `finalized`); a generation's first block carries a new anchor witness (CONS-3.3-2) taken from the local L1 `finalized`, because its parent is an Unzen block or a block of an older generation, or, on a new network, the genesis block, whose anchor `L1_0` is the generation's L1 origin anyway. A retained proposal keeps its original anchor. The proposer may keep the parent's anchor for a short time after its own L1 node first shows a newer block as finalized, so that validators whose view is slightly behind do not reject the proposal (RECOV-8-5). How long is the proposer's choice: no validity rule depends on it, and a block that keeps its parent's anchor is valid for as long as the anchor's age allows (3.4). **[CONS-3.3-5]** In ProcessProposal, a validator checks with its own L1 node that the anchor header is canonical and finalized, rejecting without waiting an anchor that node has not finalized yet (RECOV-8-5), and checks its age against the candidate block's timestamp (3.4); advancing the local finalized head alone cannot make that proposal expire.

<details>
<summary>Details: decoding L1 headers</summary>

- **[CONS-3.3-6]** **Decoding L1 headers**: `rawL1Header` must be an RLP list of at most 4,096 bytes with at least 12 items. Nodes and guests read only four fields, by position: 0 `parentHash` and 3 `stateRoot` (32 bytes each), and 8 `number` and 11 `timestamp` (integers of at most 8 bytes without leading zeros). Every other item need only be a well-formed RLP item and is not interpreted, so fields an L1 fork appends at the end (for example Glamsterdam's EIP-7928 `block_access_list_hash`) are accepted without an upgrade. **[CONS-3.3-7]** A header's identity is always keccak256(rawL1Header) over the raw bytes; it is never decoded and re-encoded. A node fetches the canonical hash for the anchor block number in `extraData` from its own L1 node and compares it with keccak256(rawL1Header): if they differ, the witness is invalid; if they match but the header cannot be parsed under this rule, L1 has changed the header format and the node needs an upgrade, which is a liveness pause (section 8), not a safety halt. When a node reads any other header from its own L1 node (trust checks, cold sync), it likewise relies only on these four fields and ignores unknown ones.

</details>

### 3.4 Block validity

**[CONS-3.4-1]** ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock apply the same deterministic rules, in order:

1. **[CONS-3.4-2]** height = parent + 1, and `parentHash` links to the parent.
2. **[CONS-3.4-3]** The presence or absence of an anchor witness follows 3.3, and the witness verifies.
3. **[CONS-3.4-4]** The anchor does not regress: it is at least the parent's anchor (for a generation's first block, only the L1 origin applies) and the current generation's L1 origin, the l1BlockNumber of its fixed origin record (8.4): `L1_0` for generation 0 and R after a governance recovery.
4. **[CONS-3.4-5]** Generation: recompute `chain_id` per appendix A from `extraData`'s uint48 generation and this deployment's parameters; it must exactly match the current chain and CometBFT header. Do not parse a generation from the digest string. If the Inbox's generation is larger, this chain has been superseded (section 8); if it is smaller, reject the block.
5. **[CONS-3.4-6]** Back-pressure: height − the `lastCheckpoint.height` proven by the anchor ≤ U, so at most U blocks are unsettled. U is the unsettled-block limit committed directly in the config hash; see 6.4 for its constraints.
6. **[CONS-3.4-7a]** Miss bitmap: `extraData`'s `missBitmap` (ENC-A-10c) equals the value this rule gives. The validators considered are exactly the set that signs the parent, stock CometBFT's last validator set for this block, whether its members are candidate members or fallback keys. A validator is missing when the block's LastCommit, its commit for the parent, holds no precommit by it for the parent block: its vote is absent or nil, that is, its `block_id_flag` is anything other than `BLOCK_ID_FLAG_COMMIT`. For each missing validator, read the value of the live key-to-seat mapping (STOR-B-21, STAKE-4.1-14d) for its validator address (CONS-3.1-3) in the parent's post-state (STAKE-4.2-5). Its seat is s when that value has holdsSeat = 1, seat = s below 128 and every bit from bit 16 up zero; any other value gives it no seat, including one that is not well formed: a holdsSeat other than 0 or 1, a seat of 128 or more, or a nonzero bit from bit 16 up. A fallback key counts like any other key: with no seated bond it has no seat, and one that is also the key of a seated bond gives that bond's seat. Bit s of `missBitmap` is 1 exactly when some missing validator's seat is s, so a seat that two addresses map to has its bit set when either is missing; every other bit is 0. The first block of a generation has no LastCommit, and its `missBitmap` is 0. ProcessProposal takes the commit from `proposed_last_commit` and FinalizeBlock from `decided_last_commit`, and the proposer builds with `local_last_commit` in PrepareProposal; each lists every validator of that set with its `block_id_flag`.
7. **[CONS-3.4-8]** The header matches 3.2 field by field; anchor freshness: timestamp − anchor.timestamp ≤ `maxAnchorAge`.
8. **[CONS-3.4-9]** The forced inclusion rules (section 7).
9. **[CONS-3.4-10]** The EL's `newPayload` returns VALID. Discarding transactions for zk-limit exceedance/overflow applies to candidate-list construction only (3.1): the final EL body, envelope and blob payload contain exactly the retained transactions, with matching transaction root, receipts, stateRoot, gasUsed and difficulty. **[CONS-3.4-11]** Validation must execute every transaction listed in a committed block; any listed transaction triggering zk-limit exceedance/overflow invalidates the whole block. Never silently drop or truncate transactions of a Comet-signed block and return VALID.

**[CONS-3.4-11a]** These are the rules of the block's ruleset, which 10.1 selects from the anchor that rules 2 and 3 verify. 10.1's planned stop does not depend on the ruleset and is checked with them: a block that violates it is invalid. If the node does not implement the block's ruleset, 10.1's refusal applies instead, a liveness pause (section 8).

**[CONS-3.4-12]** Freshness is determined only by the candidate and the committed parent: the application does not reject a proposal merely because the node's finalized L1 head advances, since otherwise a locked block re-proposed unchanged in a later round might never gather more than 2/3 of the voting power. PBTS is not enabled. BFT time's relationship to real time requires Byzantine voting power below 1/3, reasonably synchronized honest clocks and continued consensus progress; a coalition above that threshold can manipulate block time. Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 also judges a block invalid when its time is not earlier than the node's own clock plus its `block_time_tolerance` setting (START-10-6): the node prevotes nil for such a proposal, and if more than 2/3 of the voting power prevotes for the block or decides it all the same, the node's consensus stops until it is restarted after its clock has passed that point, while block sync stalls until then. A block dated that far ahead of honest clocks has this effect on honest nodes, and so has an ordinary block on a node whose own clock runs that far behind, which resumes after its clock is corrected and it is restarted; neither is evidence of a block that is invalid under the rules above.

The following rules describe proposal admission and liveness:

- **[CONS-3.4-13]** **Block size bound**: the envelope's block item (the payload's executionBlock, including its RLP list header, with every transaction, legacy included, counted as an RLP byte string of its EIP-2718 encoding) is at most 124,000 bytes long. The rule looks only at the uncompressed length, so every implementation gets the same answer; ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock both check it, and the proposer must respect it while building: alethia's build interface takes a byte limit, measured the same way, that it enforces together with gas when selecting transactions, and always keeps the FI transaction at position 0. The bound guarantees that each block fits in one blob on its own, so a landing of a single block (6.1) always fits in one transaction, and a six-blob landing holds six maximum-size blocks. **[CONS-3.4-14]** If more than 2/3 of malicious voting power decides a block that violates this rule, honest nodes halt for safety in FinalizeBlock, and only governance recovery can fix that.

<details>
<summary>Details: where the 124,000-byte bound comes from, and single-blob packing</summary>

The bound leaves headroom within the 130,044 bytes one blob carries: compressing one maximum-size block's payload list with standard zlib at default settings yields at most 124,055 bytes including the 0x06 prefix, even for incompressible data (zlib's deflateBound), so every decided block fits in one blob on its own. Six maximum-size blocks compress to at most 744,244 bytes, within six blobs (780,264 bytes), so up to six blocks always fit in one six-blob landing; no special compression mode is needed.

</details>

- **[CONS-3.4-15]** **No usable anchor, no block**: if no available finalized anchor satisfies the deterministic age bound against the candidate block timestamp, L2 pauses until one is usable. This is not an independent wall-clock timer; its timing inherits the BFT-time assumptions above.
- **[CONS-3.4-16]** **Reject a latest checkpoint conflicting with local history**: when the anchor changes, nodes check the anchor's proven `lastCheckpoint`: its height is at most the parent height, and its hash equals the EL block hash at that height on the authenticated local chain. A proposal that fails either condition is rejected, and the node raises an alert. What follows for the node itself differs, because the checkpoint an anchor proves is in the node's own finalized L1 view (SYNC-9-6a). A checkpoint above the proposal's parent is one the node's chain has not reached: the node signs nothing until it has reached that height and compared, and a match restores signing without an operator. A hash that differs at a height the node holds contradicts finalized L1: the node revokes its trust and does not keep producing on an older anchor. Guests verify only the witness's storage proofs; nodes do the comparison with the local chain. This check constrains block ancestry only and does not authenticate other landing metadata (FI head, stateRoot and so on); off-chain monitors check that metadata without being claimed to prevent bridge payments.

## 4. Staking and committees

### 4.1 Staking contract

- **[STAKE-4.1-1]** `EtnaStaking` is an L2 contract at a fixed address, the proxy address that the EL, the nodes and both guests build in (PARAM-11-10b), and the stake it holds is TAIKO on L2, the staking token of STAKE-4.1-37a: a staker first bridges TAIKO from L1. `register(bytes publicKey, uint256 amount, bytes popSig)`: amount ≥ minStake. **[STAKE-4.1-2]** The staking contract records msg.sender as the bond's owner. There is no activation delay: the new bond takes a seat in the same call (STAKE-4.1-14a). **[STAKE-4.1-3]** A successful registration creates an unwithdrawn bond with effStake = amount and `exitHeight` = 2\^48 − 1, meaning no exit is set, inheriting no exit or withdrawal state from an earlier call. requestExit reverts unless the current L2 block number is below that value.
  - **[STAKE-4.1-4]** Each key can be registered only once, ever, keyed by `ckAddr` and never released after withdrawal; the staking contract maps each used `ckAddr` to its bond id. The key lookup returns `(registered, bondId)`: an unused key returns `(false, 0)`, whereas `(true, 0)` identifies bond 0. The registration flag is never cleared, including on withdrawal. **[STAKE-4.1-5]** The staking contract stores only `ckPrefix = publicKey[0]` and the following 32-byte `ckX`, not the publicKey itself; `ckY` is derived internally.

<details>
<summary>Details: public-key format, decompression and PoP checks</summary>

- **[STAKE-4.1-6]** `publicKey` is exactly 33 bytes of canonical SEC1 compressed secp256k1 encoding: `ckPrefix ‖ ckX`, with prefix 0x02 or 0x03 and a 32-byte big-endian X retaining leading zeros. Registration tools may accept the user’s existing hex/Base64 public key and convert it to those 33 raw bytes; users do not supply or split X and Y. **[STAKE-4.1-7]** The contract rejects textual encodings, 64/65-byte uncompressed keys, hybrid encodings and trailing bytes.
- **[STAKE-4.1-8]** The staking contract decompresses and validates the public key internally under appendix A: require X \< p without reducing X modulo p, a finite valid secp256k1 point, Y parity matching the prefix, and byte-for-byte equality when recompressed. **[STAKE-4.1-9]** Any failure rejects the entire registration without creating a bond or consuming the key identity.
- **[STAKE-4.1-10]** The staking contract computes `ckAddr = keccak256(ckX ‖ ckY)[12:]` from the two decoded 32-byte coordinates, not by hashing the compressed encoding. **[STAKE-4.1-11]** Require `popSig` to be exactly 65 bytes with `V ∈ {0,1}`, `0 < r < n` and `0 < s ≤ n/2`; recovery over appendix A’s registration digest must return a nonzero address equal to ckAddr. **[STAKE-4.1-12]** The digest binds the L2 chain id, the staking contract and `msg.sender`. The signature must match this supplied public key, not merely recover some valid address. Copying the transaction cannot change the owner.

</details>

- **[STAKE-4.1-13a]** **Bond ids**: `register` returns a new bond id, counting up from 0 and never reused. An id not issued by a successful registration denotes no bond. Every state-changing operation of the staking contract that takes an unissued id reverts without writing any state, even if default storage would otherwise satisfy its conditions; read-only lookups return their absence results instead (STAKE-4.1-14d). Issuance is permanent: a withdrawn bond remains issued, and a reverted registration issues no id. The key lookup of STAKE-4.1-4 is `bondForKey(ckAddr)`.
- **[STAKE-4.1-13b]** **Seats**: there are `n_max` seats, numbered 0 to n_max − 1, and deployment requires 1 ≤ n_max ≤ 128. A bond holds a seat exactly while it is issued and not withdrawn, has no exit set and has effStake ≥ minStake. It takes a seat in the call that makes it meet these conditions (STAKE-4.1-14a) and releases its seat in the call that ends one of them. Taking a seat resets that seat's miss count (4.2) to zero.
- **[STAKE-4.1-14a]** **Admission**: a bond that meets the conditions of STAKE-4.1-13b and holds no seat takes the empty seat with the lowest number and never moves or removes another bond. Deployment requires minStake × n_max ≥ 10\^27 base units, TAIKO's fixed total supply: while L2 holds no more TAIKO than that, at most n_max bonds can have effStake ≥ minStake at once, so a bond that comes to meet the conditions always finds an empty seat. As a backstop, if no seat is empty, the call that would give a bond a seat reverts.
- **[STAKE-4.1-14c]** **Below minStake**: a missed-signature penalty (4.2) that leaves a bond's effStake below minStake releases its seat but sets no exit. The owner can top the bond up to minStake or more, which gives it a seat again (STAKE-4.1-14a), or request an exit.
- **[STAKE-4.1-14d]** **Seat lookups**: `seatOfBond(bondId)` returns `(true, seat)` for a bond that holds a seat and `(false, 0)` for any other id, issued or not; `bondInSeat(seat)` returns `(true, bondId)` for an occupied seat and `(false, 0)` for an empty seat or a number at or above n_max. `(true, 0)` identifies seat 0 or bond 0; numeric zero alone never means absence in these lookups or in `bondForKey`. These lookups and the key-to-seat mapping that nodes and guests read (appendix B) are live: the call that takes or releases a seat updates them in that call, so a block's post-state shows the seats as the block's transactions left them.
- **[STAKE-4.1-14e]** **Voting power**: a seated bond's power is floor(effStake / vp_unit). Deployment requires vp_unit ≥ 867,361,738 and minStake ≥ vp_unit, so every seated bond has power at least 1 and all seated bonds together stay within CometBFT's limit (4.3).
- **[STAKE-4.1-15]** **Top-ups**: `topUp(bondId, amount)` is owner-only. Require an issued, unwithdrawn bond with no exit set, and amount > 0. **[STAKE-4.1-16]** Transfer the TAIKO into the staking contract and add it to effStake at once, rejecting overflow. A top-up changes neither owner nor key; a bond that it brings to minStake or more takes a seat in the same call (STAKE-4.1-14a).
- **[STAKE-4.1-20]** **Top-up liability**: once an exit is set, no new top-up is allowed. A top-up cannot be withdrawn separately; it leaves only with the rest of the bond's principal, through withdrawal. **[STAKE-4.1-21]** All principal in a bond backs its key, including still-admissible double-sign evidence from before the top-up, missed-signature penalties (4.2) and responsibility forfeiture under 4.4. `balance = effStake` below and in 4.4.
- **[STAKE-4.1-23]** `requestExit(bondId)`: callable only by the bond's owner. It sets `exitHeight` to the current L2 block number, and the bond releases its seat in the same call (STAKE-4.1-13b). **[STAKE-4.1-24]** It may be called only for a bond with no exit set; once set, `exitHeight` only moves earlier (double-sign slashing, forfeiture), never later.
- **[STAKE-4.1-27]** `withdraw(bondId, to)`: requires an issued, unwithdrawn bond with an exit set and `block.number ≥ exitHeight + withdrawalDelay`. When `balance > 0`, only the owner may call it, and all remaining effStake is paid to `to`; when `balance = 0`, anyone may call it, with no payment. **[STAKE-4.1-28]** Withdrawal zeros the bond's balance, clears its owner and records the bond as withdrawn. It keeps the bond's id and issued status, key, lifetime `ckAddr` registration, `exitHeight` and lifetime forfeiture flag keyed by bond id (4.4).
- **[STAKE-4.1-28a]** **Withdrawal delay**: `withdrawalDelay` counts L2 blocks, and deployment requires withdrawalDelay ≥ U + 2 + the evidence window (section 11). A bond whose exit is set in block X last signs as a candidate member at X + 2, because the set that signs X + 2 is decided at X (4.3). Back-pressure keeps every block within U blocks of a checkpoint its anchor proves (CONS-3.4-6), so by X + 2 + U an anchor proves a checkpoint at or above X + 2, the last block the bond's seat let that key sign, and the evidence window then remains for double-sign reports. Except after a verifier-failure rollback (section 8), which can lower the latest checkpoint after a withdrawal, no bond whose key signed a block above the latest checkpoint as a candidate member can therefore have withdrawn; a signature the same key gives as a fallback key (COMM-4.3-8c) is not covered. Because the delay counts L2 blocks, it does not run while the chain is halted. With U = 151,200 and a 7-day evidence window, it is about 14 days at 4 s blocks.
- **[STAKE-4.1-30]** The owner cannot be changed after registration; an operator that may need to change custody should register from a contract (such as a multisig), which then becomes the owner. Consensus keys cannot be rotated in place: to change keys, request an exit, withdraw, and register again with the new key. If a key leaks, the owner should request an exit at once; until the bond is withdrawn, the leaked key can still be used to double-sign and get the bond slashed.
- **[STAKE-4.1-31]** `slashDoubleSign(bondId, voteA, voteB)`: callable by anyone. Each vote gives its type, height, round, block id (hash and part set header; both hashes empty and total zero for nil; each hash is empty or exactly 32 bytes, including all-zero bytes), timestamp (the vote's own int64 seconds and int32 nanoseconds, so that a vote timestamped before 1970 is covered; appendix A), uint48 generation and 65-byte signature. The caller cannot choose an Inbox, chain id or deployment tag.

- **[STAKE-4.1-32]** Both votes have the same type, prevote (1) or precommit (2), and the same height, round and generation, with different block ids. The generation must not exceed the generation that `onBlockStart` last stored (STAKE-4.2-4). Valid evidence from historical generations remains admissible after recovery; future generations and other deployments are rejected.
- **[STAKE-4.1-33]** Recompute chainDomain, chain_id and both sign-byte strings per appendix A from this deployment’s full L1/L2 chain ids, the Inbox proxy fixed in the staking contract (STAKE-4.1-37) and the votes’ shared generation. Both `ecrecover(keccak256(signBytes))` results must be the same nonzero address, and the lifetime key lookup of that address (STAKE-4.1-4) must return exactly `(true, bondId)`. Callers supply neither coordinates nor a public key, and vote input cannot override these deployment values.

On success the whole principal is slashed: effStake becomes zero, `exitHeight` becomes min(its old value, the current L2 block number) and the bond releases its seat; the caller receives `bounty = floor(slashed * slashBountyBps / 10000)`, where `slashed` is the whole slashed principal, and the remaining `slashed - bounty` goes to `slashSink`. - **[STAKE-4.1-37]** **Deployment values**: the staking contract fixes the L1 chain id and the Inbox proxy address as immutables, which `slashDoubleSign` uses to recompute chain_id (STAKE-4.1-33); only an upgrade (STAKE-4.1-40) can change them.
- **[STAKE-4.1-37a]** **Staking token**: the staking contract also fixes as the immutable `token` the address of the bridged L2 TAIKO token, the TAIKO on L2 of STAKE-4.1-1. It takes stake in (`register`, `topUp`) and pays it out (withdrawals, bounties and `slashSink` shares) only in that token, and only an upgrade (STAKE-4.1-40) can change it. It counts every unit at face value and cannot tell L2 TAIKO that TAIKO on L1 backs from L2 TAIKO minted without that backing, so the committee is only as sound as that token and whatever can mint it (section 1; section 12, item 28).
- **[STAKE-4.1-40]** **Upgrades and owner**: the staking contract is an upgradeable proxy. Its owner is the DAO's execution address on L2, which carries out the DAO's L1 → L2 governance messages; only the owner can upgrade the contract or call `forfeit` (4.4).

### 4.2 Missed signatures

- **[STAKE-4.2-4]** **Block-start call**: at the start of every Etna block, the EL calls `onBlockStart(uint48 generation, uint128 missBitmap)` on the staking contract from the system caller, with both arguments taken from the block's `extraData` (3.2). The contract reverts a call from any other address. It stores `generation`, the upper bound for double-sign evidence (STAKE-4.1-32), and then takes the following three steps, in order.
  1. **[STAKE-4.2-5]** **Count misses**: the call runs on the parent's post-state, in which the live key-to-seat mapping (STAKE-4.1-14d) shows the seats as the parent's transactions left them, and 3.4 sets the bits of missBitmap from that mapping, so a seat that a bond vacated in the parent block carries none of that bond's misses. For each occupied seat s, if bit s, `(missBitmap >> s) & 1`, is 1, the seat's miss count increases by 1; if it is 0, the count resets to 0. Bits of empty seats and bits at or above n_max change nothing.
  2. **[STAKE-4.2-6]** **Penalize**: for each seat whose miss count has reached `missLimit`, deduct `ceil(effStake × missSlashBps / 10000)` from the seated bond's effStake, send all of it to `slashSink` with no bounty, and reset the count to 0; a bond that this leaves below minStake releases its seat (STAKE-4.1-14c). Deployment requires `1 ≤ missLimit ≤ 65,535` and `0 < missSlashBps ≤ 10000`.
  3. **[STAKE-4.2-7]** **Candidate snapshot**: store the seat table as the candidate snapshot: 128 snapshot seat records, one for each seat number from 0 to 127 whatever n_max is, where record s holds the key (`ckPrefix`, `ckX`) and power (STAKE-4.1-14e) of the bond in seat s and is all zero when seat s is empty, so records at or above n_max stay zero; `snapshotTotalStake`, the sum of the effStake of the bonds in those records; and `snapshotHeight`, the current block number (appendix B gives the layout). Only this call writes these values, so the block's transactions cannot change them, and a stake change in block X enters the snapshot at X + 1; the live key-to-seat mapping (STAKE-4.1-14d) is not part of the snapshot. `snapshot()` returns the height, as the whole uint256 word at T (STOR-B-21), the total and the occupied records as `SeatMember` entries in ascending seat order; it is a convenience view, and nodes and guests read the records themselves (COMM-4.3-3).
- **[STAKE-4.2-8]** **Bounded work**: the call's work is bounded by n_max seats, whatever earlier blocks' transactions did. Its worst case, with every seat occupied, every bit set and every seated bond reaching missLimit in the same call, must fit the 30,000,000 gas available to the call's frame (CONS-3.2-13).

**Why consecutive misses.** The next block's proposer assembles LastCommit from the precommits it holds when it proposes, and stock CometBFT requires only more than 2/3 of the voting power in it and ignores the absent signatures. A penalty for a single miss would let any one proposer penalize members holding less than 1/3 of the power, and one late precommit would penalize an honest member. With a penalty only after missLimit consecutive misses, a proposer acting alone adds at most one miss for each block it proposes, and a single precommit that reaches a LastCommit resets the count. This does not limit the penalty to members that stop signing: a member whose precommits are left out by colluding proposers, withheld under this specification's own rules or delivered too late, missLimit times in a row, is penalized as well (section 12, items 24, 31 and 33). `missLimit` (section 11) sets how long such a run must last, and START-10-6 sets how long every validator waits for late precommits.

### 4.3 Committee derivation

- **[COMM-4.3-1]** **One rule for every generation**: let o be the current generation's start height, S for generation 0 and c after a governance recovery (section 8). The fallback keys whose hash the generation's origin record holds sign o + 1 and o + 2 (COMM-4.3-9). For every block H from o + 1 on, the set that signs H + 2 is decided at H from H's L2 state and H's anchor, as follows; generation 0 and every generation that a recovery starts apply the same rules.
  1. **[COMM-4.3-3]** **Candidate**: the candidate is the snapshot that H's `onBlockStart` stored (STAKE-4.2-7). Nodes and guests read `snapshotHeight`, `snapshotTotalStake` and all 128 snapshot seat records from H's post-state (appendix B), whatever n_max is; the convenience view `snapshot()` is not a source for them. A record whose two words are both zero is an empty seat; every other record is a member with the key and power it holds, subject to COMM-4.3-4 and COMM-4.3-8. H's transactions cannot change the snapshot, so a stake change in block X reaches the candidate at X + 1 and the signers at X + 3.
  2. **[COMM-4.3-3a]** **Usable candidate**: let M be the `minTotalStake` that H's anchor, newly carried or inherited, proves (3.3), and the fallback key set the `fallbackKeys` list whose hash under appendix A equals the `fallbackKeysHash` that anchor proves. The candidate is usable exactly when all four hold: M is not 2\^256 − 1, the largest uint256, the value with which the DAO makes the fallback key set sign whatever H's L2 state holds (COMM-4.3-19); its `snapshotHeight`, the whole word at T compared in all 256 bits (appendix B), equals H, so H's own call wrote it; it is a valid validator set (COMM-4.3-4, COMM-4.3-8); and `snapshotTotalStake` ≥ M. With M = 2\^256 − 1 no candidate is usable, including one whose `snapshotTotalStake` is 2\^256 − 1, so that decision depends on nothing in L2 state.
  3. **[COMM-4.3-4]** **Valid keys**: every member's key is a valid compressed public key under STAKE-4.1-6 and STAKE-4.1-8, and no two members share a key.
  4. **[COMM-4.3-7]** Each member's power is the power in its snapshot seat record, which the staking contract sets to floor(effStake / vp_unit) (STAKE-4.1-14e); nodes and guests use it as stored.
  5. **[COMM-4.3-8]** **Valid set**: the candidate has 1 to 128 members, each power is at least 1, the powers' exact sum, computed without 64-bit wraparound, is at most MAX = floor((2\^63 − 1) / 8), CometBFT's limit, and no record has a nonzero bit in its second word outside `ckPrefix` and `power` (appendix B). A candidate that fails COMM-4.3-4 or this rule is not usable; do not truncate integers, drop members or apply node-selected scaling to make it valid.

<details>
<summary>Details: why these conditions, and why power cannot overflow</summary>

- **Height**: if `onBlockStart` fails, the block stays valid and its state is unchanged (3.2), so the stored snapshot is an older one, which can still list a bond that has since exited or withdrawn. Requiring `snapshotHeight` = H hands H + 2 of such a block to the fallback key set instead; the chain keeps producing while the DAO repairs the contract through a governance message.
- **Validity**: normally the staking contract guarantees it. The check is the backstop that nodes and guests share, because the contract is upgradeable: a bug or a faulty upgrade must not hand CometBFT an invalid set, which would stop the nodes.
- **Power cannot overflow**: TAIKO has a fixed total supply of 10\^27 base units, and deployment requires vp_unit ≥ ⌈10\^27 / MAX⌉ = 867,361,738, so the power of all seated bonds together never exceeds MAX; the check in COMM-4.3-8 is only a backstop. **[COMM-4.3-8a]** If TAIKO supply ever increases, choose vp_unit for the new supply and upgrade the staking contract to it first.

</details>

- **[COMM-4.3-8b]** **Signers of H + 2**: a usable candidate signs H + 2, each member with its power. Otherwise the fallback key set signs H + 2, each key with power 1. Every set that signs a block, the candidate or the fallback key set, therefore has 1 to 128 members whatever n_max is: a usable candidate has 1 to 128 (COMM-4.3-8), and every fallback key list, including the origin record's, which signs o + 1 and o + 2 (COMM-4.3-9), has 1 to 128 keys (COMM-4.3-18).
- **[COMM-4.3-8c]** **Fallback keys are not staked**: no stake backs the fallback keys' power. A double sign by a fallback key can be slashed only through a bond registered to the same key (STAKE-4.1-33), and while the fallback key set signs, the chain runs on keys the DAO chose.
- **[COMM-4.3-9]** **First two blocks**: whatever the candidate, o + 1 and o + 2 are signed by the keys of the list whose hash equals the `fallbackKeysHash` in the generation's origin record (8.4), each with power 1. This is the validator set of the generation's CometBFT genesis (section 10).
- **[COMM-4.3-13]** **Validator updates**: FinalizeBlock(H), for every H from o + 1 on, returns the difference between the set that signs H + 2 and the set that signs H + 1, because stock CometBFT applies the updates to the set that signs H + 1 and they take effect at H + 2: a key that leaves is returned with power 0, a new key or one whose power changes is returned with its new power, and a key whose power stays the same is not returned. An empty list keeps the set.
- **[COMM-4.3-13a]** **Mode switch**: when the signers of H + 1 and H + 2 come from different sources, one the candidate and the other the fallback key set, the whole set is replaced at H + 2 through that one update list; there is no gradual handover.
- **[COMM-4.3-14]** **New generation**: a governance recovery discards every validator update that the old generation returned and that had not taken effect; the new generation's first sets follow only from its origin record (COMM-4.3-9).
- **[COMM-4.3-16]** **Determinism**: H's L2 state and H's anchor uniquely determine the set that signs H + 2, and the origin record those that sign o + 1 and o + 2. The choice between the candidate and the fallback key set is made again at every block, without hysteresis, and there is no limit on how much power changes from one block to the next.
- **[COMM-4.3-18]** **Fallback key set and threshold on L1**: the Inbox keeps the `fallbackKeys` list with its `fallbackKeysHash` (slot 278) and `minTotalStake` (slot 279), and only the Inbox owner changes them. `setFallbackKeys(keys)` requires 1 to 128 `FallbackKey` entries, each a valid compressed public key under STAKE-4.1-6 and STAKE-4.1-8, in strictly ascending order of the 33-byte big-endian value `ckPrefix ‖ ckX`, so that no key repeats and the list and its hash are unique; it stores the list and its hash (appendix A) and emits `FallbackKeysUpdated(fallbackKeysHash, keys)` (ENC-A-36d). `setMinTotalStake(value)` stores any uint256, of which 2\^256 − 1 makes no candidate usable (COMM-4.3-3a), and emits `MinTotalStakeUpdated(value)` (ENC-A-36e). Either call reverts when the caller is not the Inbox owner or a check fails. `getFallbackKeys()` returns the stored list. Inbox initialization must set both values, and it emits both events as the two calls do (section 10). Neither call takes a generation, and neither has a lead time.
- **[COMM-4.3-19]** **When a DAO change applies**: a change to the fallback key set or to `minTotalStake` enters the decision at block H once H's anchor, newly carried or inherited, is at or after the L1 block that made it, and so first affects the set that signs H + 2. A proposer can postpone it only by keeping an older anchor, which `maxAnchorAge` bounds (CONS-3.4-8). Setting `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1 makes every candidate unusable (COMM-4.3-3a), so the fallback key set signs until the DAO sets a lower value. Any lower value leaves the decision to the candidate's `snapshotTotalStake`, a word of L2 state: a value above TAIKO's total supply of 10\^27 base units keeps the candidate from signing only while L2 TAIKO and the staking contract are sound (section 12, items 27 and 28).
- **[COMM-4.3-20]** **Fallback key holders stay online**: whoever holds the fallback keys must run, at all times and for each key, a validator instance of its own that follows the chain, with that key's signer (COMP-2-1a), also while the candidate signs: an exit in block X can drop the candidate's total stake below M and hand X + 3 to the fallback key set. A key can sign then only if its own instance has caught up with the chain: a running process and signer do not show that, and one instance cannot vote for a second key. If a third or more of the fallback keys cannot sign then, consensus halts until enough of them return or the DAO replaces them and executes governance recovery (section 8).

### 4.4 DAO responsibility forfeiture

- **[FORF-4.4-1]** **Authority and identity**: `forfeit(bondId, incidentId, validUntil)` is callable only by the staking contract's owner, the DAO's execution address on L2 (STAKE-4.1-40), which the DAO reaches through an L1 → L2 governance message. **[FORF-4.4-2]** The target is its permanently issued bondId in the staking contract (STAKE-4.1-13a). Before authorizing, the DAO verifies the intended bond's issuance and registered key against the L2 state at a landed checkpoint. **[FORF-4.4-3]** The DAO authorization binds the deployment, full call parameters and published attribution. incidentId identifies that attribution; the same incident may name multiple bonds. The staking contract keeps no incident case or global incident-id deduplication state.
- **[FORF-4.4-4]** **Execution checks**: require an issued, unwithdrawn bond, `forfeited[bondId] = false`, `block.timestamp <= validUntil`, and `balance = effStake > 0`. **[FORF-4.4-5]** A bond with an exit set remains punishable until it is withdrawn; neither a seat nor current committee membership is required. **[FORF-4.4-6]** validUntil is an absolute Unix timestamp in seconds, fixed in the authorization and compared with the L2 `block.timestamp`.
- **[FORF-4.4-7]** **Immediate, fixed-rate deduction**: compute `penalty = ceil(balance * forfeitBps / 10000)` from the remaining principal at execution and deduct it from effStake. The deployment fixes `0 < forfeitBps <= 10000`. **[FORF-4.4-9]** Send the full penalty to slashSink, with no bounty. **[FORF-4.4-10]** Top-ups made after authorization but before execution are included: all principal in the bond backs its key, without historical-balance accounting.
- **[FORF-4.4-11]** **Once per bond, with exit**: in the same transaction set `forfeited[bondId] = true` and `exitHeight = min(existing, block.number)`, which releases the bond's seat (STAKE-4.1-13b). **[FORF-4.4-12]** The flag starts false and is never reset by withdrawal or by the verifier-failure rollback; a governance recovery removes it only by discarding the block that set it (FORF-4.4-21). **[FORF-4.4-13]** A forfeiture in block X enters the candidate snapshot at X + 1 (STAKE-4.2-7) and first affects the set that signs X + 3 (4.3); it never rewrites a snapshot already written or a set already decided. **[FORF-4.4-14]** Remaining principal follows the normal withdrawal rules (STAKE-4.1-27); an exit set earlier keeps its `exitHeight`, so the withdrawal delay is not restarted. **[FORF-4.4-15]** Valid double-sign evidence may still take all remaining principal; if double signing emptied the bond first, forfeit fails on zero balance without blocking normal withdrawal.
- **[FORF-4.4-16]** **Authorization expiry and replay**: there is no forfeiture nonce, revocation entry point, queue, hold or extra execution delay. Successful execution immediately completes the forfeiture; the lifetime flag prevents repetition. The authorized id always denotes the same bond and registered key. **[FORF-4.4-17]** An unexecuted authorization remains executable until validUntil if all checks pass, even if the DAO changes its decision; this interface cannot cancel it. Expired calls revert without cleanup. **[FORF-4.4-18]** Landing, a generation change and recover do not invalidate the authorization.
- **[FORF-4.4-19]** **Atomicity and operations**: update the balance, exit, seat and lifetime flag before transferring funds; reject cross-function reentrancy, and revert all effects on any failure. Emit bondId, incidentId, pre-deduction balance and penalty. **[FORF-4.4-20]** Execute before lawful withdrawal; this mechanism cannot recover withdrawn funds. Before execution the DAO assesses the online quorum, concentration and total stake of the bonds that keep their seats: the forfeited bond stops signing from the third block after the forfeiture (FORF-4.4-13), and a total below `minTotalStake` hands signing to the fallback key set (4.3). This version has a single-bond API. Execute forfeitures of different bonds independently when failures must not affect each other. A forfeiture executed above the c of a later governance recovery is discarded with that suffix (FORF-4.4-21), so when a recovery is planned, execute forfeitures in the new generation.
- **[FORF-4.4-21]** **Forfeiture ends the seat, and recovery can discard it**: forfeiture also sets an exit, which nothing reverses, so a forfeited bond never holds a seat again; an operator that wants to return must withdraw and register a new bond with a new key, and that bond can be forfeited in its turn. A governance recovery discards every forfeiture executed in the suffix above c together with that suffix, flag and exit included (section 8); on the new chain that authorization is unexecuted again, and FORF-4.4-17 and FORF-4.4-18 apply to it.

## 5. Data formats and DA

### 5.1 Envelope (private)

**[DATA-5.1-1]** The single tx in each CometBFT block: `0x06 || rlp([block, anchor, forced])` (the last two are each an empty list or exactly one item), **[DATA-5.1-2]** committed to by the CometBFT header's `data_hash` (encoding in appendix A). **[DATA-5.1-3]** It propagates only within the consensus network and never goes to L1.

### 5.2 Blob payload (public)

- **[DATA-5.2-1]** Each batch uses 1–6 blobs (EIP-7594's per-transaction limit) **[DATA-5.2-2]** with Unzen's blob encoding, which appendix A writes out: each blob decodes to at most 130,044 bytes, every non-final blob is full, and all padding bits are 0.
- **[DATA-5.2-3]** Content: `0x06 || zlib(rlp([executionBlock, …]))`, where each `executionBlock` is the list `[EL header, [ordered EIP-2718 transactions]]`, the same item as the envelope's block (appendix A). It is encoded once, as a list inside the payload list, not as a byte string that holds its encoding. **[DATA-5.2-4]** Each transaction, legacy included, is an RLP byte string holding its EIP-2718 encoding.

<details>
<summary>Details: blob payload acceptance rules</summary>

- The acceptance rules use fixed limits that operator settings cannot change:
  - **[DATA-5.2-5]** Exactly one complete zlib stream, with no preset dictionary and no trailing bytes; at most 64 MiB decompressed, and each `executionBlock` in the list at most 124,000 bytes, measured as in CONS-3.4-13.
  - **[DATA-5.2-6]** The RLP encodings are canonical and re-encode byte for byte. The zlib stream has no canonical form and is not re-encoded: any stream that meets DATA-5.2-5 and decompresses to that RLP is accepted, whatever compressor or settings produced it, and the blob bytes themselves stay bound through `blobHashes` (PROOF-6.2-35).
  - **[DATA-5.2-7]** Exactly `lastHeight − parentHeight` blocks at heights `parentHeight + 1 … lastHeight`; EL `parentHash` links block by block, the first block's parent equals `parentHash`, and the last block's hash and stateRoot equal `lastBlockHash` and `lastStateRoot`.

</details>

- **[DATA-5.2-8]** Not published: CometBFT headers, original envelopes, anchor/forced witnesses, the raw forced transaction of a skip, evidence, commits and signatures, including each block's LastCommit, which the guest needs to check the block's `missBitmap` (CONS-3.4-7a). They are private proof inputs: the guest authenticates each original envelope against `data_hash`, then proves that its execution part equals the blob byte for byte.
- **[DATA-5.2-9]** **Recovery from DA**: with a sound proof and a finalized L1 checkpoint, archived blobs plus the starting state suffice to rebuild the landed EL chain and check the last block's hash and stateRoot, without witnesses or signatures. This is not the same as bootstrapping CometBFT from L1 alone; unlanded blocks still need the full consensus history.

- **[DATA-5.4-6]** **P2P continuation and authorization**: after successful import, start stock CometBFT P2P block sync at s + 1, execute every subsequent block through normal FinalizeBlock and all 3.4/7.2/9 checks, and enter normal consensus when caught up. Preserve the state at s and the parent state of each subsequently executed block for canonical FI skip replay; a non-retryable missing-parent-state failure still safety-halts under RECOV-8-13. Both an import at the latest checkpoint and one at an older retained current-generation checkpoint must satisfy the session's own finalized-L1 latest-target EL/Comet hash checks (SYNC-9-5) before section 9 trust, public service or signing. A generation change during import invalidates admission to the old generation.

- **[DATA-5.4-7]** **Signer state is never imported**: the consensus archive contains no private key or signer HRS state. Preserve all existing per-chain_id signer state outside the imported stores and apply 8.2/9 on every session, including RECOV-8.2-2’s automatic height floor and committed-chain check on rebuilt stores. An import cannot reset, lower or replace HRS, revive old-generation signing, or make an uncertain/lost signer state usable; a new key/session follows the same authorization rules. Import completion alone grants no signing authority.

- **[DATA-5.4-8]** **Availability and independent evidence**: retain and make available to joining app nodes at least one verifiable current-generation app-import package, holding the validator-state preimage for s - 1 (DATA-5.4-3) and the material DATA-5.4-2 lists, and the consensus/state data needed to reach the latest finalized checkpoint, replacing a supported path only after its replacement is available. The complete consensus history from o stays archived under 9.1, so fixed-genesis replay remains possible; it need not be published on L1 or be part of the public EL-only path in 5.3, and it remains an operational availability dependency. Replay does not pass a decided block that the replaying node's own stock CometBFT keeps rejecting, whatever history the node holds, and such a block can lie at or below a landed checkpoint (RECOV-8.1-7). A rejection that depends on the node's own clock is not of that kind: a block dated ahead of that clock passes once the clock has reached it or has been corrected, with the retry or restart CONS-3.4-12 describes, and needs neither an import nor a recovery. Where a rejection does not clear, because it follows from the block and the state the chain has committed, such as a header whose app_hash commits a different validator state (SYNC-9-5c), the way past it is governance recovery from a checkpoint at or above it (RECOV-8.1-7), and the new generation is replayed from its own genesis (8.4). An imported node retains consensus history only from its window and cannot serve older history to peers. Normal CONS-3.4-16 ancestry comparisons may use the authenticated EL header chain installed by the import, including at or below s. The validator state is not settled on L1: every node that executes a block checks the state its parent committed, because stock CometBFT compares the header's app_hash with the app's own (CONS-3.1-8, SYNC-9-5c). That comparison shows only that the apps agree with one another: a running node's CometBFT keeps its own priorities and never reads the app's, while an imported node's CometBFT starts from the app's. Whoever produces an import package therefore checks its validator-state preimage against the sets, priorities and stored proposers that a stock CometBFT which itself committed those heights holds for them, read from a height-labelled, consistent local copy of the pinned stock state store's `State` immediately after that height (`Store.Load()`, fields `Validators` and `NextValidators`, including `Proposer`; at o use the state after InitChain). Retain this snapshot when producing the height or obtain it by independent stock replay stopped there. A later state, `LoadValidators(height)`, `/validators` and an app's copy cannot establish the stock priorities (SYNC-9-5c). The producer publishes no package until that comparison passes; a mismatch raises an alert. Import is not independent EL execution through s and cannot supply that evidence for bad-landing monitoring or the recovery checks in RECOV-8.1-7 and RECOV-8.1-8; those require nodes that independently executed and retained the relevant history.

## 6. Landing and settlement

### 6.1 land

**[LAND-6.1-1]** land is permissionless and nonpayable. **[LAND-6.1-2]** Proofs and blobs cover all of (p,h\], from proof origin p to terminal height h; **[LAND-6.1-3]** c, the latest checkpoint height, is read atomically at execution and only the new suffix (c,h\] is settled, **[LAND-6.1-4]** so another lander advancing c first does not invalidate the same transaction. **[LAND-6.1-5]** A landing may end at any height h above c, so any height can become a checkpoint, and land carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h) (6.3 explains why). **[LAND-6.1-6]** The checks are, in order: range and origin record; **[LAND-6.1-7]** the leaves are exactly those of the heights strictly inside (p,h) and **[LAND-6.1-8]** the leaf at c matches the current checkpoint record; FI head advance; reference block and blobs; computing cutHash and verifying both proofs. **[LAND-6.1-9]** Once all pass, land atomically updates the checkpoint, FI head, slot 285 record and SignalService.

**[LAND-6.1-10]** The reference Solidity in the toggle below lists every check land performs; the checks are normative. **[LAND-6.1-11]** All checks precede any state change and **[LAND-6.1-12]** the leaf checks precede proof verification; the remaining order is illustrative. **[LAND-6.1-13]** The land signature, LandInput's seven fields and the cutLeaf and cutHash encodings (appendix A) are the interface. Local names and helper bodies are illustrative: helper results are defined in appendix A and the comments, and storage variables map to the normative layout in appendix B.

```solidity
// Storage (appendix B): recoveryGeneration 268, configHash 269, lastCheckpoint 270/271,
// fiHead/fiTail 281, landings 285; generationStartHeight() reads 290.
// Immutables: verifier, maxBatchBlocks, signalService

struct LandInput {
    uint256 parentHeight;            // proof origin p
    uint256 lastHeight;              // terminal height h
    bytes32 lastBlockHash;           // h's EL block hash
    bytes32 lastCometHash;           // h's CometBFT block hash
    bytes32 lastStateRoot;           // h's EL stateRoot
    uint256 l1ReferenceBlockNumber;  // reference block
    uint256 forcedInclusionHead;     // FI head after settlement
}

// Cuts: the EL block hash, CometBFT block hash and FI cursor at each height, with leaf
// cutLeaf(x) = keccak256(abi.encode(x, blockHash, cometHash, forcedHead)) (appendix A).
// A landing may end at any height above the checkpoint. Both proofs authenticate cutHash, a hash
// over the leaves of every height strictly inside (p, h) (appendix A), and land carries all of
// them (6.3 explains why).

function land(
    LandInput calldata input,
    bytes32[] calldata leaves,    // leaves of heights p+1, p+2, ..., h-1 inside (p, h), in height order
    bytes calldata proof          // the two proofs (6.2)
) external nonReentrant {          // permissionless, nonpayable; shares recover's reentrancy lock (8.3)
    uint256 p = input.parentHeight;
    uint256 h = input.lastHeight;
    uint256 c = lastCheckpoint.height;          // read atomically at execution

    // 1. Range. c >= h means the target is already settled: reject without mutation.
    require(generationStartHeight() <= p && p <= c && c < h && h < 2**48);
    require(h - p <= maxBatchBlocks);

    // 2. Origin p needs a retained slot 285 record in the current generation; a missing p is rejected.
    //    The only exception is the current generation's origin, which may reuse an older
    //    generation's record. Proofs cannot cross generations. isValidLanding rejects absent
    //    and voided records, including when old storage remains after a rollback.
    Landing memory origin = landings[p];
    require(isValidLanding(p) && origin.elHash != 0);   // a written record never has EL hash 0 (the start write stores the start's hash; its Comet hash is 0)
    require(origin.generation == recoveryGeneration || p == generationStartHeight());

    // 3. leaves must be exactly the leaves of the heights strictly inside (p, h). If c > p, the leaf
    //    at c must equal the leaf the contract computes from c's slot 285 record: height, EL hash,
    //    Comet hash and FI cursor all match (comparing only height or stateRoot is insufficient); if
    //    c = p, step 2 and the statement already bind the origin record. The FI cursor in c's record
    //    must also equal the current FI head. All of this is checked before proof verification. The
    //    other leaves only feed cutHash; a wrong one fails proof verification in step 6.
    require(leaves.length == h - p - 1);
    Landing memory cur = landings[c];
    require(cur.head == fiHead);
    if (c > p) {
        require(leaves[c - p - 1] == keccak256(abi.encode(c, cur.elHash, cur.cometHash, uint256(cur.head))));
    }

    // 4. FI head only advances from its pre-execution value to forcedInclusionHead, never beyond
    //    tail and by at most h - c. The proof authenticates all consumption from p; L1 applies
    //    only the suffix and never consumes the prefix again.
    uint256 headAfter = input.forcedInclusionHead;
    require(fiHead <= headAfter && headAfter <= fiTail && headAfter - fiHead <= h - c);

    // 5. The reference block is in the past with a readable hash (blockhash's 256-block or
    //    EIP-2935's 8191-block window). The transaction carries 1-6 blobs binding all of (p, h];
    //    they cannot be replaced by suffix-only blobs.
    require(input.l1ReferenceBlockNumber < block.number);
    bytes32 refHash = l1BlockHash(input.l1ReferenceBlockNumber);
    require(refHash != 0);
    bytes32[] memory blobs = txBlobHashes();   // in index order
    require(blobs.length >= 1 && blobs.length <= 6);

    // 6. Compute cutHash from leaves, build the statement and verify both proofs (encodings in
    //    appendix A); wrong leaves fail proof verification. parentHash, parentCometHash and
    //    headBefore come from origin p; the statement's other words are deployment constants and
    //    configHash (slot 269).
    bytes32 cutHash = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32("ETNA_LAND_CUTS_V1"), recoveryGeneration, p, h,
                                           keccak256(abi.encodePacked(leaves))));
    bytes32 st = statement(recoveryGeneration, input, origin, refHash, keccak256(abi.encodePacked(blobs)), cutHash);
    verifier.verifyProof(0, st, proof);   // age 0 = not applicable; reverts unless both proofs verify

    // 7. Update atomically; any failure reverts everything. FI fees are not paid out. The landing
    //    record's parent is always actual c, never proof origin p. Once the frontier advances,
    //    recovery authorizations bound to the old c can no longer execute (8.3).
    lastCheckpoint = Checkpoint(h, input.lastBlockHash);
    fiHead = headAfter;
    landings[h] = Landing({parentHeight: c, generation: recoveryGeneration, head: headAfter,
                           elHash: input.lastBlockHash, cometHash: input.lastCometHash}); // slot 285
    signalService.saveCheckpoint(ICheckpointStore.Checkpoint({
        blockNumber: uint48(h), blockHash: input.lastBlockHash, stateRoot: input.lastStateRoot
    }));
    emit Landed(p, c, h, cutHash, VERSION);
}
```

### 6.3 Batches

- **[BATCH-6.3-1]** A batch may end at any height (6.1). **[BATCH-6.3-2]** A change of signing set, including a switch between the candidate and the fallback key set, may fall inside a batch or at its end; a recovery start is a checkpoint. **[BATCH-6.3-3]** All of these use the sets that 4.3 actually gives rather than avoiding a problematic cut. **[BATCH-6.3-4]** `l1ReferenceBlockNumber` can be any recent L1 block, avoiding expiry of the reference hash alone. A lander should pick one that its own L1 node reports as finalized where the hash window allows, because the statement binds the reference block's hash and a reorg of that block invalidates both proofs. When the old unsettled suffix above c is proven after a long outage without governance recovery, **[BATCH-6.3-5]** the old anchors those blocks used must still be authenticated within the supported ancestry/proving budget, and choosing a recent reference does not remove that requirement. **[BATCH-6.3-6]** Landers and provers need a resource bound on the reference-header chain (reference value 131,072 headers, about 18 days of L1 blocks at 12 s); **[BATCH-6.3-7]** a suffix beyond it cannot be landed the ordinary way and can only be discarded by governance recovery under section 8 (at most U blocks), restarting from R; the outage before R does not count toward this bound (8.4). At the target U = 151,200 (6.4), a full unsettled suffix produced at a sustained 4 s block interval spans about 7 days, about 50,400 L1 blocks at 12 s, which is within the reference value; if L2 then stays paused at U for roughly the remaining 80,000 L1 blocks (about 11 days), the ancestry back to the suffix's oldest anchor exceeds the bound, and that suffix can only be discarded by governance recovery.
- **[BATCH-6.3-8]** Prove from an authenticated, retained historical checkpoint p, normally the latest when work begins. **[BATCH-6.3-9]** It cannot precede this generation's starting point, and the proof cannot span generations. **[BATCH-6.3-10]** If another lander advances to c, the original transaction (two proofs, blobs and leaves) stays valid unchanged whenever p ≤ c \< h: execution settles (c, h\] with no reproving, reaggregation or parameter change. **[BATCH-6.3-11]** If c ≥ h, the target is already settled and work can be cancelled. **[BATCH-6.3-12a]** Reference expiry, a block whose ruleset the job's images do not implement (10.1) or a generation transition can still invalidate a job; **[BATCH-6.3-13]** signed jobs must not be rewritten to bypass those boundaries. **[BATCH-6.3-14]** Copying a transaction cannot change any proven content; landing carries no reward, so who submits it does not affect settlement.

<details>
<summary>Details: why land carries a leaf for every height</summary>

- **Why check c at all**: a proof may start at a p older than c, while c advances through other landings. If more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signs, one height can have two valid blocks; without checking that the proof's chain at c is the block L1 recorded, L1 could settle one fork and then attach the other, and the bridge could be double-spent. Requiring p = c avoids the check, but then every competing landing forces reproving.
- **Why every leaf**: a proof is generated without knowing c at execution, so it must commit to every possible c, and any height in (p,h) can be c. A Merkle tree over the cuts would keep the calldata constant, but it takes a window and sibling nodes, with the lander guessing at signing time where c will be and reverting on a wrong guess. land instead carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h): the same transaction works wherever c is, the lander guesses nothing, and a competitor cannot make an honest submission fail by landing first (section 12, item 16). The contract does one hash and one indexed comparison, and the guest builds no Merkle tree.
- **Costs**: leaves grow linearly with the batch: a batch of B blocks carries B − 1 leaves of 32 bytes each (299 leaves and 9,568 bytes for 300 blocks), which bounds maxBatchBlocks (section 11). In return a lander never waits for a particular height, a single block can always be landed on its own (3.4, section 1), and governance recovery never discards a decided block that could still be proven and landed (8.1).

</details>

- Landers may coordinate active jobs off chain, monitor checkpoints and cancel stale work, reusing only inputs or sub-proofs that can be reauthenticated and are compatible with the target version. Coordination grants no exclusive rights and cannot bypass 6.4's U bound. Wasted proving and retry costs are covered by DAO operating funds (appendix C).
- Follow 6.4's proactive batching and submission policy. One remaining producible height is an alarm requiring immediate submission of an available batch, not the point to start proving.

### 6.4 Back-pressure

**[BACKP-6.4-1]** L2 height cannot exceed the anchor-proven lastCheckpoint.height + U, **[BACKP-6.4-2]** where U is an integer parameter committed directly in the config hash, with `1 ≤ U ≤ 262,144`. **[BACKP-6.4-3]** U is a circuit breaker for settlement failure, not the pace of normal settlement: it should pause L2 only when proving or landing stalls for a long time or a decided block cannot be proven, capping the blocks governance recovery may discard. **[BACKP-6.4-4]** The target deployment uses **U = 151,200** (about 7 days at 4 s blocks); **[BACKP-6.4-5]** approximately 4 s blocks and no more than 20 min between L1 inclusions of successive successful landings are operating targets, not block-validity rules. **[BACKP-6.4-7]** A landing stall permits at most U further blocks. **[BACKP-6.4-8]** L1 inclusion alone does not release the pause: the landing state must finalize and be adopted by a new anchor.

<details>
<summary>Details: latency budget and unsettled-block arithmetic</summary>

An approximately 4 s block interval is an operating target, not a minimum interval or uninterrupted-production guarantee. End-to-end settlement includes batching, queues/proving, retries, L1 inclusion/finality and anchor adoption. Assess back-pressure from actual `height − anchor.lastCheckpoint.height`, not average-interval conversion alone. U=151,200 corresponds to 7 days at a sustained 4 s block interval, not a wall-clock outage guarantee. In normal operation the unsettled count is about “the interval between landing inclusions + the previous batch's terminal-block-to-inclusion latency + the next landing's finality and anchor-adoption latency”, counting the actual critical path of the two proofs; at 20 + 20 + 20 min that is about 900 blocks, leaving almost the whole 7 days for a proving or landing outage. This latency budget is an operating target: missing it shrinks the margin but does not pause L2. Sustained 4 s production is about 15 blocks/min; settlement throughput must keep pace without a growing unsettled backlog. At one batch every 20 min, each must cover about 300 blocks on average. If maxBatchBlocks, six-blob capacity or proving capacity cannot support that batch, settle more frequently or provide the missing capacity rather than merely delaying the pause with a larger U; after an outage, proving capacity must exceed the block rate to clear the backlog. The 20 min finality/adoption component neither bounds L1 finality nor relaxes maxAnchorAge. Exceeding the allowance causes a normal pause, never a bypass of U; this arithmetic establishes neither a measured pause fraction nor a no-pause SLA.

</details>

For uninterrupted operation, U should exceed the number of blocks produced during one landing round trip. Landers should continuously prepare and submit batches from the latest checkpoint, submitting early based on actual unsettled blocks and remaining proving/finality time. The 20 min target is not a mandatory batching wait and grants no exclusive landing right. “One producible height left” is an emergency signal, not the point to start proving. The target remains 4 s; a result obtained by temporarily slowing production does not establish that target.

**[BACKP-6.4-9]** U is fixed at deployment and cannot be overridden by local node configuration. **[BACKP-6.4-10]** An anchor is a finalized L1 block, and checkpoints only advance, so anchor-based accounting never undercounts unsettled blocks; **[BACKP-6.4-11]** the sole exception is the section 8 verifier-failure rollback, whose accounting rules ship with that upgrade. **[BACKP-6.4-12]** L1 finality lag only brings the pause forward and cannot authorize exceeding U. **[BACKP-6.4-13]** Raising U raises the maximum number of blocks governance recovery under section 8 may discard; before recovery, blocks are settled up to the last provable block (8.1), so only blocks that truly cannot be settled are discarded. At the target U = 151,200, governance recovery may discard at most 151,200 blocks; a history forged with old keys can extend at most 151,200 blocks beyond its fork point, which can be old (SYNC-9-3); and once the backlog is full, proving capacity must exceed the block rate to clear it.

**[BACKP-6.4-15]** **Acceptance**: before using the target configuration, pass the resource and latency acceptance in [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md); until then, do not claim deployment readiness or target attainment. **[BACKP-6.4-16]** Do not omit latest-checkpoint checks, use non-finalized anchors, remove a proof, locally loosen U or silently expand resource limits.

## 7. Forced inclusion

### 7.1 Submission

**[FI-7.1-1]** `saveForcedInclusion(bytes tx)`: at most 8,192 bytes (a longer `tx` reverts the submission), with a fee of `fee × (threshold + pending) / threshold` gwei, where pending is the number of entries on L1 not yet consumed by a landing (`tail − head`; while settlement lags this includes entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed); overpayment is refunded. **[FI-7.1-2]** Each entry stores only `keccak256(tx)`, the L1 timestamp and the L1 block number. **[FI-7.1-3]** The enqueue event records the entry index, raw transaction bytes and actual fee charged in wei, excluding refunded overpayment; encoding is not checked at submission; of the `tx` bytes, only the length is (FI-7.1-1). **[FI-7.1-4]** On successful enqueue, the fee calculated and collected using the formula becomes protocol revenue and stays in the Inbox. **[FI-7.1-5]** The fee buys queue admission, not successful execution: inclusion, valid skipping, governance blanking and replay after a settlement rollback neither refund it, charge it again nor pay it to a lander. **[FI-7.1-6]** Historical per-entry charges are recorded only in enqueue events, not used as consumption or recovery inputs. **[FI-7.1-7]** If the submission transaction reverts, neither enqueueing nor charging takes effect.

<details>
<summary>Details: sweeping protocol fees</summary>

**[FI-7.1-8]** **Sweeping protocol fees**: deployment fixes a nonzero DAO treasury address, `protocolFeeRecipient` (an Inbox immutable), and publishes it in the deployment manifest. **[FI-7.1-9]** Only `saveForcedInclusion` accepts ETH (overpayment is refunded in the same transaction) and every other Inbox entry point rejects ETH, so, apart from force-sent ETH, the Inbox balance is the FI fees not yet swept. **[FI-7.1-10]** Anyone may call `sweepProtocolFees()` to transfer the Inbox's entire ETH balance to that address; a failed transfer reverts. **[FI-7.1-11]** Force-sent ETH is swept with it. **[FI-7.1-12]** Neither `saveForcedInclusion` nor `land` transfers funds to the treasury, so a treasury receipt failure affects only the sweep. **[FI-7.1-13]** Sweeping does not depend on the queue head or landing and does not modify FI entries or queue progress.

</details>

### 7.2 Consumption rules

- **[FI-7.2-1]** L2 state keeps a cursor c (the number of entries consumed, initially 0). Each block consumes at most one entry, and only entry c. After a landing, the Inbox's head equals c at the checkpoint.
- **[FI-7.2-2]** Each block uses its own anchor A: if `c ≥ tail(A)`, it carries no forced witness; otherwise it must carry entry c's witness, a storage proof under A's stateRoot.
- **[FI-7.2-3]** **Due**: `A.timestamp ≥ entry.timestamp + forcedInclusionDelay`, evaluated without overflow. Entries never expire.

<details>
<summary>Eligible transaction: exactly one canonical EIP-2718 transaction (detailed rules)</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-4]** **Eligible transaction**: the raw bytes are exactly one canonical EIP-2718 transaction. Canonicity depends only on the raw bytes, never on state, and nodes and guests implement it against the same shared accept/reject vectors rather than any library's decoder: decoding consumes all input and re-encoding reproduces the bytes; the type is a non-blob type accepted by the pinned EL version (type 3 and unknown types are ineligible); a typed transaction's chain_id equals the L2 chain id and its y_parity is 0 or 1, and a legacy transaction's v is 27, 28, 2·l2ChainId + 35 or 2·l2ChainId + 36; nonce and gas are below 2\^64, each fee field is below 2\^128, and value is below 2\^256; the signature's r and s lie in \[1, n − 1\] (n is the secp256k1 order) with s ≤ n/2 and recover a sender; and gas is at most `L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT`. These checks cover only the transaction's own fields and signature: type-4 authorization tuples need only the field widths EIP-7702 requires, and an invalid tuple signature or chain id makes the EL skip that tuple without affecting eligibility. Eligibility ignores fees: a transaction whose `maxFeePerGas` (`gasPrice` for type 0/1) is below the block's base fee has an empty singleton build, so it may be legitimately skipped. A proposer can push the base fee to the 1 gwei cap at no cost (3.2), so an FI transaction should set `maxFeePerGas` to at least 1 gwei to be sure it is not skipped for its fee.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Details: given-list build (deciding whether a skip is legal)</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-9]** **Given-list build**: the build returns only after completing the deterministic build decision for `[tx]`, and has exactly two possible results: a block containing the transaction, or an empty block. It must not return a placeholder from before the build completes. If an eligible transaction cannot execute for any transaction-level reason (nonce, balance, intrinsic gas and so on), the result is an empty block, never an error. Transient infrastructure failures such as timeouts, a dropped connection or a SYNCING response from newPayload are not results: ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock both wait and retry until a result exists, and never reject a proposal, judge a block invalid or skip because of them; while waiting, that node processes no new consensus messages. If the EL definitively cannot build on that parent (for example because the historical state was pruned), the node halts for safety under section 8 and restarts after the state is restored. An eligible transaction's gas is at most one block's limit, so each build does bounded work. This singleton build starts from that parent with the same attributes and candidate list `[tx]` only, with full EVM-gas and zk-gas block budgets; ordinary transactions may not consume them first. A completed empty result after fully discarding `tx` for zk-limit exceedance/metering overflow under the shared rules is deterministic and reproducible. An included ordinary EVM revert/OOG is not empty. Nodes and guests make the same complete determination; a host timeout, exhausted prover resources or a smaller local budget cannot justify skipping.

</details>

- **[FI-7.2-10]** A block at or after due that neither includes nor legally skips the entry is invalid. Only position 0 counts as inclusion: a replacement transaction with the same sender and nonce, or the transaction placed later in the block, does not count.

<details>
<summary>Details: raw bytes in the witness and fetching them</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-11]** The raw bytes in the witness may be empty; if nonempty, their hash must match the entry. A skip must carry the raw bytes, and empty bytes can only prove that the submission itself was empty. For an inclusion the guest authenticates transaction 0 of the execution block directly, so proposers leave the bytes empty to save data.
- **[FI-7.2-12]** The proposer fetches the raw bytes with `eth_getLogs` at the entry's L1 block and checks the hash. Before due, if they cannot be fetched it may propose an ordinary block; after due, it cannot propose without them.

</details>

- **[FI-7.2-13]** **Blanked entries**: after submission, an entry's `txHash` changes only when step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) overwrites it with the hash of the empty byte string, `keccak256("")`. Under an anchor that shows this value, the raw bytes are the empty string and need no `eth_getLogs`; such an entry can never be included, and once due it is skipped as ineligible, without the EL.

### 7.3 Guarantees and bounds

- **[FI-7.3-1]** A due queue-head entry must be included or legally skipped under 7.2; there is no age-only deletion. **[FI-7.3-2]** For the current queue-head entry, any valid block with `block.timestamp ≥ entry.timestamp + forcedInclusionDelay + maxAnchorAge` necessarily sees it as due, using overflow-safe arithmetic. This is a block-time implication, not a wall-clock inclusion deadline. Wall-clock progress also depends on the BFT-time assumptions in 3.4, L1 finality, queue backlog and settlement.
- **[FI-7.3-3]** Senders must hold a balance of gasLimit × `maxFeePerGas`; **[FI-7.3-4]** the queue consumes at most one entry per block, and the fee rises linearly with the queue length.
- **Backlog cost accounting**: let F be the fees actually paid for an attacker's enqueues. **[FI-7.3-5]** With fees belonging to the protocol, no share of successful landings lets the attacker recover F by consuming those entries. Add L1 submission gas, proving/landing/blob costs the attacker chooses to incur, and the L2 gas actually executed separately; account for other rewards or proceeds separately as well. Production fees, threshold, service rate under sustained attack and honest-user costs require evaluation; [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md) gives an illustrative calculation.

## 8. Halts and recovery

**[RECOV-8-1]** **Scope**: an automatically retrying liveness pause loses no blocks; **[RECOV-8-2]** executing governance recovery discards the suffix above c.

- **[RECOV-8-3]** **Liveness pauses**: the process keeps running and CometBFT keeps rounding; production resumes automatically once the cause clears, and no blocks are lost. **[RECOV-8-4]** Causes: the anchor not yet finalized or older than `maxAnchorAge`, back-pressure, unavailable raw bytes for a due forced transaction, an unavailable fallback key list (SYNC-9-16), or a block whose ruleset the node's software does not implement (10.1). **[RECOV-8-5]** A local L1 node that lags, is unreachable or times out falls in this class too (section 9). When the node's own L1 node has not finalized a proposal's anchor, for one of these reasons or because its view is behind the proposer's, ProcessProposal rejects at once and does not wait for the view to advance: the node prevotes nil and judges a later round's proposal against its view at that time. FinalizeBlock of a block that was decided all the same waits and retries until the node's own L1 node shows the anchor finalized, and halts only on a contradiction from that node (SYNC-9-2). **[RECOV-8-6]** So does an anchor header whose hash matches the canonical hash but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 (L1 changed the header format): ProcessProposal rejects, FinalizeBlock waits and retries, and production resumes once the node is upgraded.

<details>
<summary>Details: ABCI constraints that keep a pause lossless under stock CometBFT</summary>

**[RECOV-8-7]** For a liveness pause to lose no blocks with stock CometBFT v0.40.0, the app must never return an error or exception from any ABCI method on any connection (CometBFT exits or panics); Query and the snapshot methods return only error codes or empty results. **[RECOV-8-8]** ProcessProposal returns only ACCEPT or REJECT. **[RECOV-8-9]** PrepareProposal stays within the request's `max_tx_bytes`; **[RECOV-8-10]** during a pause, or when the required envelope does not fit, it returns a placeholder proposal with no transactions, which ProcessProposal rejects, and CometBFT moves to the next round as usual. **[RECOV-8-11]** InitChain returns only `app_hash`, with no validators or consensus params, and Commit always returns a `retain_height` of 0. Automatic recovery after refused signatures is in section 9.

</details>

- **[RECOV-8-12]** **Safety halts**: the process exits and needs manual investigation. **[RECOV-8-13]** Causes: a decided block that violates the validity rules or contradicts local state or the EL (including an EL `-38002` rejection); fork evidence found during sync or trust checks, or malformed or unverifiable data from the node's own L1 node or EL (section 9; an L1 header whose hash matches but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 is excluded); or, while replaying a canonical skip during cold sync, an EL that refuses to build on the parent with a non-retryable error, usually because it lacks the parent's historical state, which must be restored before restarting.
- **[RECOV-8-14]** **Superseded generation**: once an anchor shows a `recoveryGeneration` larger than this chain's generation, this chain cannot advance and the node can only restart under the new generation. **[RECOV-8-15]** ProcessProposal rejects a proposal carrying such an anchor and revokes checkpoint trust while the process keeps running, and a running node revokes it as soon as its own finalized L1 view shows the larger generation, without waiting for a proposal (SYNC-9-6a); **[RECOV-8-16]** a decided block carrying such an anchor, or a startup or signer-session trust check that finds an Inbox generation different from this chain's, is a safety halt.
- **[RECOV-8-20]** **Less than 1/3 of the voting power offline**: consensus continues. While the candidate signs, a seated member that misses `missLimit` consecutive blocks is penalized automatically (STAKE-4.2-6), and one that the penalty leaves below minStake loses its seat (STAKE-4.1-14c). A penalty in block H's call is already in the snapshot that the same call stores (STAKE-4.2-7), so it first changes the set that signs H + 2.
- **[RECOV-8-21]** **At least 1/3 of the candidate's voting power offline**: consensus halts, and without blocks no missed-signature penalty applies (4.2) and the withdrawal delay does not run (STAKE-4.1-28a). Returning members may restore it. Otherwise the DAO takes three steps. First, in one proposal, it calls `setMinTotalStake` with 2\^256 − 1 and then executes governance recovery (below): every anchor of the new generation is at or after R (RECOV-8.4-12, RECOV-8.4-13), so no candidate is usable whatever c's L2 state holds (COMM-4.3-3a, COMM-4.3-19) and the fallback key set signs from c + 1 on (COMM-4.3-9, COMM-4.3-8b), whereas with any lower value the candidate that c's L2 state gives, offline members included, signs c + 3 whenever it is usable. Second, while the fallback key set signs, it calls `forfeit` (4.4) on the offline bonds through L1 → L2 governance messages (FORF-4.4-1), which releases their seats (FORF-4.4-11); outside the signing set, they accrue no misses (CONS-3.4-7a). Third, it lowers `minTotalStake` again with `setMinTotalStake`, and once a usable candidate's `snapshotTotalStake` reaches it, the candidate signs again, the whole set changing at once (COMM-4.3-13a).
- **[RECOV-8-22]** **At least 1/3 of the fallback key set offline while it signs**: consensus halts (COMM-4.3-20). Returning key holders may restore it. Otherwise the DAO first replaces the keys with `setFallbackKeys` and then executes governance recovery, whose origin record holds the new keys' hash (RECOV-8-47), so the new keys sign c + 1 and c + 2 (COMM-4.3-9) and every later block that the fallback key set signs.
- **[RECOV-8-23]** **Settlement stall**: L2 produces at most U more blocks and then stops on back-pressure.
- **[RECOV-8-23a]** **Planned stop**: past the point where a 10.1 stop ends production, PrepareProposal returns the placeholder proposal and ProcessProposal rejects, so CometBFT keeps rounding without producing blocks; only `recover` ends it, and a decided block past that point is a safety halt.
- **Verifier failure**: both proofs accepting a wrong result is outside the threat model (section 1). **[RECOV-8-24]** A bad landing may corrupt the checkpoint or the FI head, and ordinary governance recovery cannot repair it. **[RECOV-8-25]** Contain losses first by pausing `SignalService` and the Bridge and by QuotaManager limits. **[RECOV-8-26]** Replacing the verifier already requires an Inbox upgrade, and the settlement rollback is executed in the same DAO upgrade of the Inbox, together with the `SignalService` and Bridge changes that RECOV-8-27 and RECOV-8-27a need; node rules for handling the rollback ship with it. **[RECOV-8-27]** The Inbox restores lastCheckpoint (slots 270/271) and the FI head (281) to the values written by the last trusted landing and voids later landing records and the SignalService checkpoints those landings wrote; checkpoints at or below S that the Unzen Inbox wrote are kept. A voided landing is ineligible as a proof origin and for snapshot import, including at a generation origin. A later valid landing at the same height is a new record. After the rollback no voided checkpoint can be read through the `SignalService` or proven against. Every other checkpoint stays usable as before, and a checkpoint that a landing writes after the rollback is usable as usual, at whatever height: moving the whole checkpoint mapping to a new storage version, by itself, would hide the ones that stay valid too. How the voided ones are made unusable is not bound here, and neither is where the received-signal cache is kept (RECOV-8-27a). **[RECOV-8-27a]** **Derived authorizations**: voiding checkpoints does not revoke what they already authorized, because two paths use a signal without checking a checkpoint: an empty proof for a signal the L1 `SignalService` cached as received, and a retry of an L1 Bridge message left `RETRIABLE`. The same upgrade therefore makes every signal cached, and every message left `RETRIABLE`, at or after the L1 block of the first bad landing unusable until it is proven again against a checkpoint that `SignalService` holds after the rollback; revoking earlier ones as well is allowed, but the revocation must not remove the checkpoints RECOV-8-27 keeps. It must not take a legitimate message's value either: a message whose signal is on the chain that later landings settle becomes provable again once a landing covers it, and then completes as usual, except that a fee already paid is not paid again. `SignalService` and the Bridge, paused under RECOV-8-25, stay paused until the upgrade has executed. **[RECOV-8-28]** The last trusted landing is the last landing before the first bad one; if there is none, restore the values written at initialization (START-10-5) and by the one-time start write (START-10-5b). Landings of every generation count, and a governance recovery adds none, so when no landing is trusted the restore returns to that start even inside a later generation, whose number the rollback keeps (RECOV-8-32). **[RECOV-8-31]** The rollback returns c to an earlier value, so the same upgrade must preserve and increment the current recoveryNonce (8.3), invalidating every prior recovery authorization without restoring a historical nonce; **[RECOV-8-32]** the rollback itself does not change the generation. **[RECOV-8-33]** If a governance recovery executed at a c above the restored one, that generation starts above the restored c and no landing can succeed in it; **[RECOV-8-34]** the DAO must follow the rollback with a new governance recovery from the restored c. **[RECOV-8-35]** Bridge payments already made using bad checkpoints cannot be recovered, and neither can state that other contracts derived from them, through signals or by reading the checkpoints directly. The rollback can also lower the latest checkpoint below the last block that an exited bond's key signed as a candidate member, X + 2 for an exit set in block X, which voids the checkpoint at or above X + 2 that STAKE-4.1-28a relies on; this is the exception STAKE-4.1-28a names. A bond that has withdrawn by then is no longer slashable (STAKE-4.1-34), although its key signed blocks above the restored c: until a landing settles those blocks, they carry that much less slashable stake. A `withdrawalDelay` whose evidence window, the part beyond U + 2 (STAKE-4.1-28a), is longer than the time to detect a bad landing and execute the rollback prevents such a withdrawal (PARAM-11-15). Where one has happened all the same, this is accepted: the rollback takes no further action for that bond and does not change the generation on its account (RECOV-8-32); **[RECOV-8-36]** L2 fees were paid to proposers at execution and are unaffected.
- **Governance recovery**: for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear, a structural upgrade, or replacing a ruleset that has taken effect (10.1). **[RECOV-8-37a]** The call is recover(expectedGeneration, expectedC, expectedRecoveryNonce, validUntil, fiToClear, rules). **[RECOV-8-38]** The fiToClear list contains strictly increasing FI entry indices. Call conditions, replay protection and atomicity are in 8.3; timing is in 8.1.
  1. **[RECOV-8-39]** Validate the list and all state before applying the following transition in one transaction. Check the complete authorized transaction against L1 gas limits when adding optional FI entries.
  2. **[RECOV-8-42]** `recover` itself slashes, forfeits and exits no bond; responsibility forfeiture (4.4) and double-sign evidence (STAKE-4.1-31) remain available as calls to the L2 staking contract.
  3. **[RECOV-8-43]** Each fiToClear index must still be in \[head,tail). **[RECOV-8-44]** Replace txHash with keccak256(""), retaining timestamp and enqueue block. **[RECOV-8-45]** This is for handling unprovable content only; unlisted entries survive and are consumed by the new chain under the normal rules. **[RECOV-8-46]** Admission fees are neither refunded nor recharged, and no lander is paid.
  4. **[RECOV-8-47]** Increment generation (generation+1 must fit uint48), write the fixed origin in 8.4, whose fourth word is the `fallbackKeysHash` that slot 278 holds at that moment, and emit execution details and the complete fiToClear list. **[RECOV-8-47a]** Set `stopTime` in slot 293 to 0. A nonzero `rules` must equal `nextRules + 1`, otherwise reject the whole operation; it sets `activeRules` and `nextRules` to `rules` and `rulesTime` to 0, so the new generation runs that ruleset from its first block (10.1). Emit the new slot 293 value. The new generation's genesis and initial committee (8.4) use the ruleset that slot 293 at R selects for R's timestamp. With `rules` 0, a pending `nextRules` carries into the new generation and takes effect at its `rulesTime` as usual. **[RECOV-8-48]** Preserve checkpoint c's landing record and FI head.
  5. **[RECOV-8-51]** Nodes rebuild the EL from authenticated c. The keys of the fallback key set whose hash the new origin record holds sign c + 1 and c + 2, each with power 1 (COMM-4.3-9), and no validator set or power carries over from the old generation. **[RECOV-8-53]** Discard every pending update from the old generation (COMM-4.3-14); from c + 1 on, 4.3 decides at every block H the set that signs H + 2, on global heights. **[RECOV-8-54]** No signing for the new generation before the 8.2 authentication completes. **[RECOV-8-55]** Nodes and guests accept the fallback key list for c + 1 and c + 2 only when it matches the `fallbackKeysHash` of the origin record they authenticate (8.4), and take no keys or weights from governance-supplied input.
  6. **[RECOV-8-56]** Discard only the unsettled suffix above c; **[RECOV-8-57]** users resubmit those transactions, and fees in those blocks are void with them. **[RECOV-8-58]** Do not rewind the settled prefix. **[RECOV-8-59]** Staking operations in the discarded suffix are void with it: registrations, top-ups, exits, withdrawals, double-sign slashes, missed-signature penalties and forfeitures (FORF-4.4-21) executed above c do not exist on the new chain, which continues from c's L2 state. Double-sign evidence whose slash was discarded can be submitted again in the new generation, where the old generation's votes remain admissible (STAKE-4.1-32).

### 8.1 Recovery authority and user-visible boundaries

- **[RECOV-8.1-1]** Only the DAO authorizes recovery and FI blanking; ordinary on-call or pause authority has no such powers. **[RECOV-8.1-2]** Bridge/SignalService pauses limit future exposure but cannot recover paid bridge funds or withdrawn stake.
- **[RECOV-8.1-3]** **Timing**: recover has no separate time threshold; the shortest stall before recovery depends on the delay of the DAO's authorization path. **[RECOV-8.1-4]** expectedC is set to the current c and fixed at authorization, so any landing after that makes the call unexecutable; a recovery executed through the standard path (veto period plus timelock) therefore implies no landing during that whole period. The emergency path that skips the veto period should be used only for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear, and on that path the lists may become public only at execution, with no advance notice. **[RECOV-8.1-5]** Slow progress, low TPS, a wall-clock FI delay or missing the 20 min landing target is never a reason to recover. Landers continue to submit every provable suffix during authorization. If c advances, discard the stale authorization, audit through the new c and reassess whether a stall still requires recovery before seeking fresh authorization. Repeated invalidation alone does not qualify for the emergency path.
- **[RECOV-8.1-6]** **Before authorizing**: the DAO has landers settle up to the last provable block and sets expectedC to the resulting c, so only blocks that truly cannot be settled are discarded. **[RECOV-8.1-7]** Before scheduling and executing, it confirms that the fallback key set the new origin record will hold, the one slot 278 commits when `recover` executes, is online: more than 2/3 of its keys each have a validator instance of their own in sync with the chain and their signer running (COMM-4.3-20), no party holds 1/3 or more of them, and history is available. It checks whether the L2 committee is healthy, that is whether the bonds seated in c's L2 state have more than 2/3 of their power online and no party at or above 1/3, and if it is not, sets `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1 before `recover` in the same proposal (RECOV-8-21). It confirms that c's landing matches honest nodes' local chain: the EL hash, Comet hash and FI head in its slot 285 record, and the stateRoot in the SignalService checkpoint at c. Matching these items does not show how each landing was made, because executing a block does not look at the transaction that settled it. So on every path, whether honest nodes hold the settled block at c or not, each landing above the audit boundary is also checked as a whole, with the p, h and reference block in the LandInput of its own L1 transaction (6.1), against everything 6.2 has the guests prove of a landing beyond the validity of its blocks (items 1 to 10): among it, all anchors and roots satisfy PROOF-6.2-33 against that transaction's reference block, including its narrowly scoped settled-header alternative, and its blobs decode to exactly those blocks (PROOF-6.2-21, PROOF-6.2-37). An anchor one block later than that reference can be canonical and finalized, and its block can pass every check on single blocks, yet no sound proof covers that landing. The DAO runs this check continuously on finalized landings. Its audit boundary is a contiguous, independently verified prefix of the currently settled history. For each landing the audit record binds the deployment, generation, finalized L1 block hash, transaction hash and position, previous audit record, p/c/h, reference hash, ordered versioned blob hashes, checked results and audit software revision. Independent archive operators retain original blob bytes, transaction inputs and the 9.1 witness dependencies until the DAO has verified the prefix and a second operator has verified and durably backed up the evidence. A hash list, monitor status or operator signature alone cannot establish a boundary: retain the inputs in an independently restorable archive whenever the saved evidence cannot independently establish the checks. Snapshot rotation never retires the last such copy. Before authorizing recovery the DAO verifies continuity from its recorded boundary and checks every subsequent landing; absent evidence starts the check at the start write. If inputs are missing, first restore the independently verifiable audit evidence for that prefix or fetch the missing original inputs and finish the checks. Public execution replay and comparison of L1 writes may narrow the gap but cannot establish missing blob bindings, Comet ancestry or commit checks. If a required fact remains unverifiable, ordinary recovery stays pending; missing data alone does not trigger verifier rollback. Section 12 records the resulting indefinite halt and [Q8.1-8](rules/review/8.1.md#q81-8) tracks an alternative with equivalent assurance. After rollback discard audit records depending on a voided landing. It uses the blocks honest nodes executed themselves, or those the independent verification below executes, and executes none again. Honest nodes can lack the settled block at c, or hold a different one, without a verifier failure. A block at or below c that more than 2/3 of the voting power decided can pass every check the proofs make and still be invalid under stock CometBFT, for example a header whose app_hash commits a different validator state (PROOF-6.2-42, SYNC-9-5c), and honest nodes do not get past such a block (SYNC-9-5c). And when more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signs, one height can have two valid blocks (6.3): L1 settles one of them, and honest nodes that committed the other hold different hashes from that height on. In both cases the settled chain is verified independently, starting from the last settled block that honest nodes executed themselves, with the EL state and FI cursor they hold there: execute the settled blocks above it up to c from the landings' blobs (DATA-5.3-5, DATA-5.3-6), which gives each block's EL hash and stateRoot; recompute the FI cursor block by block under 7.2; check the Comet headers up to c as 6.2 requires of a guest (hash links, DataHash against the executed EL blocks, the validator sets of item 2 and the commits of item 3); and apply to each of those blocks every other rule of 3.4 under its ruleset, with 10.1's planned stop (CONS-3.4-11a), as FinalizeBlock applies them in cold sync (SYNC-9-1). 6.2 has the guests prove the rules of sections 3 and 7 for every block they cover (PROOF-6.2-20, PROOF-6.2-45, PROOF-6.2-45a), and matching hashes, stateRoots, cursors and commits do not show that those rules hold: a block whose height is more than U above the lastCheckpoint.height its anchor proves (CONS-3.4-6) can match all of them. The cursor is not an output of that execution, because a legal skip leaves nothing in the EL block (DATA-5.2-8), and it is never taken from the slot 285 records being checked: for each block, read the queue tail and the entry under the block's anchor in the node's own L1 view, take the entry's raw bytes from its enqueue event or the block's envelope (the empty string for a blanked entry, FI-7.2-13) and check them against the entry's hash, and where a due entry is not the block's transaction 0, require a legal skip, by ineligible raw bytes (FI-7.2-7) or by an empty singleton build on the parent state that this execution produced (FI-7.2-8, FI-7.2-9). When the results match the records, those blocks pass these checks and the landings pass the landing check above, the verifier has not failed and recovery proceeds from c: nodes take the EL state at c from that execution, in place of any blocks of their own that differ from the settled ones, and the new generation starts from its own genesis validator state (8.4). **[RECOV-8.1-8]** A mismatch in that comparison means the verifier has failed: perform the section 8 verifier-failure rollback first, then authorize recovery. A mismatch is an item that differs from the own state of honest nodes that hold the settled block at c, a landing that fails the landing check, on either path, or a record that the independent verification contradicts, including a settled block that fails a check it applies, even when every recorded item matches. A block that only stock CometBFT rejects is not a mismatch, and neither is a different valid block that honest nodes hold at a settled height.
- **[RECOV-8.1-9]** **Economic review and independent forfeiture**: review `minTotalStake` against the launch checklist's capital target and compare it with the stake of the bonds seated in c's L2 state, from which the new generation's first candidate is taken; from then on 4.3 compares every candidate's `snapshotTotalStake` with the `minTotalStake` that its block's anchor proves (COMM-4.3-3a), so a candidate below it never signs. **[RECOV-8.1-10]** A capital shortfall or an expired valuation behind `minTotalStake` triggers an alert, public disclosure and replenishment or a new `minTotalStake`, not a new recovery or signer veto; existing quorum, concentration and authentication checks remain. **[RECOV-8.1-11]** Recover neither executes nor cancels a forfeiture authorization and does not wait for it; a forfeiture executed above c is discarded with the suffix and can be executed again in the new generation (FORF-4.4-21); **[RECOV-8.1-12]** resumed production does not erase responsibility under 4.4.

### 8.2 Recovery signer

- **[RECOV-8.2-1]** The signer keeps separate stock HRS state for each full `chain_id` (which includes the generation), persists each signature before returning it, and never resets or rolls that state back; **[RECOV-8.2-2]** no copy of the key may sign outside that state. Fence the old signer instance before failover. Let H be the height of the last signature in this key’s durable HRS for the generation. Bind a never-used key/domain to the consensus store instance before its first signature. The supervisor assigns a fresh instance ID whenever it creates, imports or rebuilds the stores. On every connection, including after manual deletion or partial restore, the signer authenticates the Comet endpoint under START-10-6, verifies its process and store instance over the authenticated supervisor channel, and compares that instance with its own durable binding. PubKey and Ping remain available. An ID copied with an older database cannot establish continuity. To permit same-height restart, also bind the last signing decision to its committed predecessor and the flushed WAL prefix needed to replay it, recording the predecessor block ID and the length and digest of the flushed WAL bytes for H alongside HRS before returning the signature. For a live signature, take the flushed bytes after the predecessor end-height marker. They must cover the complete input record that triggered signing and may also contain round-state records already written while handling that input. If the marker is absent, record that same-height replay is unavailable on restart. For a used key, an absent or changed binding, a rebuild, or uncertain continuity automatically fences signing through H. The signer persists a minimum signable height of at least H + 1 with its anti-rollback state before admitting the replacement session. Rebinding cannot lower the floor. It covers every instance, restart and retry, including proposals and nil votes. A lost or uncertain HRS follows RECOV-8.2-3. On an intact instance with H still undecided, the supervisor must verify the predecessor state (H − 1, or genesis at the generation’s first height) and establish that stock v0.40.0 can replay H: this startup neither block-synced a block nor state-synced, stock’s WAL search finds `#ENDHEIGHT H−1` (or `#ENDHEIGHT 0` at the generation’s initial height), and the bytes following that marker match the signer’s recorded prefix length and digest. Ordinary block sync does not write the predecessor marker. If it is missing, `catchupReplay` returns an error and consensus logs it and starts anyway. Stock v0.40.0 also keeps at most 1 GiB of consensus WAL files and deletes the oldest, and it writes a peer's message to the WAL before it discards a duplicate, so a height that stays undecided for long enough, or under enough traffic from the node's direct peers (START-10-6 limits who they are), loses the marker and the start of the bound prefix; a restart in that height then has no eligible replay. Before replay, verify the previously signed prefix, then pin the length and digest of the contiguous, decodable WAL bytes from that marker through the last complete record at startup, including round-state records. Use a stable view of the bound WAL with no other writer. If the preflight detects a torn tail, take the repair path below before opening replay signing. This startup prefix must cover the input for any first signature issued during replay. In a trusted, authenticated replay from the verified predecessor, the request that repeats the HRS and canonical sign bytes saved at session entry confirms the last signing decision (apply the stock timestamp-only retry in SYNC-9-13). Return the saved signature. There is no comparison between the reader position and the end of either prefix. Higher-HRS requests in that uninterrupted replay can now receive fresh signatures. Atomically persist each new HRS, sign bytes and signature with the pinned startup prefix before replying. On the next restart validate that binding even if the new signature’s reply was lost. Replay-created round-state records carry no signing inputs. If the last signed step was a proposal, stock may rebuild a different BlockID when it has no ValidBlock. A request at that proposal HRS marks progress to the propose step only. Apply SYNC-9-13: refuse conflicting sign bytes, including a changed BlockID or POLRound. In that same authenticated attempt, before the first fresh vote, the supervisor may independently confirm that replay has restored the round and lock (locked round and full locked BlockID, or no lock) from the verified predecessor and the bound WAL inputs. For the original-proposal path, verify that stock has accepted the WAL proposal matching the signer's saved canonical proposal bytes and signature, including BlockID, part-set header and POLRound; verify the vote follows the restored consensus state. For example, in round zero with no prior lock, verify the original proposal and parts, the app’s successful ProcessProposal for that block hash in the same replay, and the requested prevote for its full BlockID. In a later round independently reconstruct any prior lock changes before admitting its vote. The check may complete at the vote request during replay. Merely seeing the propose HRS, or a log announcing a proposal before stock handles it, cannot release the vote. For a planned ruleset upgrade during a pause at an undecided H, arm the signer to stop on a precommit. Serialize admission so the signer persists and returns that precommit (nil or block) as its last signature and admits no later request before the supervisor stops CometBFT. If a precommit is already the last signature, close the gate under the same serialization. With vote extensions disabled, stock chooses its precommit from the lock, the proposal and the recorded prevotes. It does not call ProcessProposal at that step. On eligible replay the upgraded app may accept a proposal it previously rejected. Refuse any conflicting prevote and keep fresh signing closed until the saved precommit is confirmed under these replay checks. If a precommit boundary cannot be reached or replay cannot confirm it, the usual independent confirmation or certified-chain path applies. Without the matching sign-byte retry or independent confirmation of restored state, keep fresh signing at H closed. The supervisor must distinguish the bound process’s current replay attempt from live consensus at the time of the request (see below). Clear replay admission on repair, error, process exit or disconnect; a new attempt must confirm again against the latest durable HRS. For a torn tail after all signer-bound input, keep signing closed during stock repair, verify the repaired prefix against the signer's durable binding and start replay from the verified predecessor. This recoverable tail alone does not raise the height floor. A missing marker, damaged bound prefix, skipped replay, uncorrected failure or unverifiable continuity triggers the durable floor. A fenced signer can sign above H once the node verifies a committed chain covering H in this domain: verify the block and its >2/3 commit under the actual committee and their connection to the trusted chain, or authenticate a finalized landed descendant and sync under 5.4. The first new signature extends that chain. An intact node already past a verified commit for H can continue above it without replaying H. Both paths require section 9 trust. If H cannot be certified and the signer cannot safely resume at H, it remains a non-signing node. **[RECOV-8.2-3]** If the state is lost or its latest value is uncertain, stop signing; deleting state cannot restore service.
- **[RECOV-8.2-4]** Sign for a new generation only after the node's own finalized L1 view authenticates the recovery execution (the new generation, origin c and the `fallbackKeysHash` that the new origin record holds) and both the 8.4 origin-record and genesis checks and section 9 checkpoint trust pass. **[RECOV-8.2-5]** The new generation starts its own HRS state at c + 1, and once the recovery is authenticated, signing requests for the old generation are refused; a process that was running when the recovery executed reaches that point when its own finalized L1 view shows the new generation (SYNC-9-6a). **[RECOV-8.2-6]** Votes in different generations use different signing domains and are not double signs. The signer enforces the same-domain fencing and height floor in RECOV-8.2-2 independently of node storage.

**Observing stock v0.40.0 replay.** The private-validator RPC carries no replay flag. One supervisor implementation gives each Comet process a dedicated, lossless local log pipe with synchronous, unbuffered writes and parses the structured consensus log records. For each signer RPC, while that process is waiting for its reply, consume all records already written to the pipe before classifying the request. The height-bearing `Catchup by replaying consensus messages` opens an attempt. `Replay: Done` ends it; the corruption/repair diagnostics and `error on catchup replay; proceeding to start state anyway` cancel it. A background log tail can lag behind a live request. Bind the pipe and signer connection to the same process lifetime; on loss of ordering or attribution keep the gate closed. Stock’s repair loop reopens the WAL on the same consensus state. To ensure a clean retry, let it repair with signing closed, then restart from the verified predecessor and repeat the prefix, trust and confirmation checks (or verify a commit if the node has advanced).

### 8.3 Execution-time checks in recover

- **[RECOV-8.3-1]** **Caller and conditions**: only the Inbox owner (the DAO) may call recover. **[RECOV-8.3-2]** Both recover and invalidateRecoveryNonce require Inbox initialization, and recover also requires the start to be written (START-10-5b). **[RECOV-8.3-3]** At execution expectedGeneration, expectedC and expectedRecoveryNonce must equal current state, and `block.timestamp <= validUntil`; **[RECOV-8.3-4]** validUntil is an absolute L1 Unix timestamp in seconds fixed at authorization. **[RECOV-8.3-5]** For recovery authorization, Inbox stores only a monotonically increasing uint256 recoveryNonce, initially zero, with no recovery queue or scheduling records. **[RECOV-8.3-6]** It does not distinguish DAO authorization paths; **[RECOV-8.3-7]** authorization or queueing does not freeze landing.
- **[RECOV-8.3-8]** **Replay and invalidation**: increment recoveryNonce on successful recover. **[RECOV-8.3-9]** Only the Inbox owner may call `invalidateRecoveryNonce(expectedRecoveryNonce)`; require a match and increment to invalidate all unexecuted authorizations with that nonce. **[RECOV-8.3-10]** Verifier-failure rollback also preserves and increments the current nonce; **[RECOV-8.3-11]** no path may restore an older value or wrap it. **[RECOV-8.3-12]** Landing changes c, recovery changes generation, and generation never decreases. **[RECOV-8.3-13]** A rollback returning c to an old value cannot revive old authorizations. **[RECOV-8.3-14]** Every retry must pass all checks; changed parameters require fresh DAO authorization. **[RECOV-8.3-15]** This recovery nonce is independent of the bondId lifetime forfeiture flag in 4.4.
- **[RECOV-8.3-16]** **Atomicity**: list validation, FI blanking, counter updates and origin recording all happen inside one recover call, and any failure reverts everything, leaving no partial FI edit, origin record or generation change. **[RECOV-8.3-17]** FI blanking exists only inside recover and cannot be applied in an earlier transaction. **[RECOV-8.3-18]** This does not restrict owner exits, double-sign slashing (4.1) or the independent responsibility forfeiture and forced exit in 4.4, which are calls to the L2 staking contract. **[RECOV-8.3-19]** During recovery, reject cross-function reentrancy that could change the checkpoint, the generation or the FI queue that fiToClear is checked against, including land, recover and recovery-nonce invalidation.

### 8.4 Fixed origin and genesis of a new generation

**[RECOV-8.4-1]** Recovery uses stock CometBFT v0.40.0 without adding an authorizer or trusted snapshot service. **[RECOV-8.4-2]** c is the fixed recovery origin and R the canonical L1 block that executed recover; nodes authenticate both from their own finalized L1 view.

- **[RECOV-8.4-3]** **Fixed origin record**: slot 290 stores four words per generation, (startHeight, l1BlockNumber, l1Timestamp, fallbackKeysHash) = (c, R.number, R.timestamp, the `fallbackKeysHash` in slot 278 when `recover` writes the record); generation 0's is (S, L1_0, L1_0's timestamp, the `fallbackKeysHash` in slot 278 at the start write), written by the start write (START-10-5b), and describes the Etna start, not a recovery. The record's `fallbackKeysHash` fixes the keys that sign the generation's first two blocks (COMM-4.3-9), whatever the Inbox's fallback key set is later. **[RECOV-8.4-4]** Each record is written once and never overwritten or deleted. **[RECOV-8.4-5]** A missing record is rejected; mapping defaults or the latest checkpoint are not substitutes. **[RECOV-8.4-6]** generationStartHeight and every other reader of the record read it directly, without caches.
- **[RECOV-8.4-7]** **Unique genesis**: chain_id uses the new generation, initial_height=c+1, genesis_time=R.timestamp, and InitChain returns the appHash of ELHash(c) and the genesis validator state (CONS-3.1-8). Generate and check the full START-10-21 consensus parameters, including zero block evidence bytes, for this ruleset; a structural upgrade changing them must define the complete successor values in the authenticated 10.1 ruleset. They stay fixed within a generation; **[RECOV-8.4-8]** EL state must match c's authenticated EL hash and stateRoot. **[RECOV-8.4-9]** Genesis validators are the keys of the fallback key set whose hash the origin record holds, each with voting power 1 (COMM-4.3-9, ENC-A-36b), even if the Inbox's fallback key set has changed since R.
- **[RECOV-8.4-10]** **First block**: uses stock genesis's empty LastBlockID/LastCommit; c's Comet hash serves only as the proving boundary (6.2 item 2). Stock CometBFT requires the block time at a chain's initial height to equal genesis_time, so the BFT time that 3.2 uses for this block is R.timestamp, as it is the timestamp of `L1_0` for S + 1 in generation 0 (START-10-20). **[RECOV-8.4-11]** The FI cursor starts at the authenticated origin head. **[RECOV-8.4-12]** The first block must carry a finalized anchor witness no earlier than R and does not use c's anchor. **[RECOV-8.4-13]** Every anchor and L1 root used by the new generation's proofs is no earlier than R (3.4 rule 3).
- **[RECOV-8.4-14a]** **Joining nodes**: either replay from the fixed genesis or import an authenticated checkpoint under 5.4 and replay its suffix; match the latest checkpoint on their own finalized L1 as SYNC-9-5 requires before becoming trusted, which is the EL hash alone while that checkpoint is the generation's origin; **[RECOV-8.4-15]** the latest checkpoint is never a new genesis for the same domain.

## 9. Sync and weak subjectivity

- **[SYNC-9-1]** **Replay uses the live rules**: during cold sync (replay or blocksync), for a block it did not judge live that carries an anchor witness, FinalizeBlock likewise first checks with its own L1 node that the anchor header is canonical and finalized, and checks the witness's lastCheckpoint per 3.4. **[SYNC-9-2]** An own L1 node that lags, is unreachable or times out only makes it wait and retry. The node halts for safety only on contradictory evidence from its own L1 node.
- **[SYNC-9-3]** **Checkpoint fence**: FinalizeBlock passes the `lastCheckpoint.hash` proven by the anchor to the EL's forkchoice as `finalized`; a history that does not descend from that checkpoint is rejected by the EL (`-38002`), and the node halts for safety. A history forged with old keys can therefore extend at most U blocks beyond its fork point, but that point can be old; forging blocks above the checkpoint needs more than 2/3 of the voting power of the sets that sign those heights (4.3). The withdrawal delay keeps a candidate member's stake locked until an anchor proves a checkpoint at or above the last block its seat let it sign (STAKE-4.1-28a, which states the exceptions); no stake backs the fallback keys (COMM-4.3-8c).
- **[SYNC-9-4]** **Weak subjectivity is solved by L1**: every app startup and signer session begins untrusted and authenticates the Inbox's latest checkpoint in its own finalized L1 view. A signer session lasts for one authenticated signer-to-Comet connection (START-10-6). Disconnect ends its signing and replay admission. A reconnect, including one after a transport timeout to the same live process, repeats the binding and trust checks with durable HRS and height floor intact. The node **[SYNC-9-5]** becomes trusted only after reaching that height with an exact match of the EL hash and the CometBFT hash in the slot 285 record. While the latest checkpoint is the current generation's origin, the Etna start S or a recovery origin c, the node compares the EL hash only. ## 10. Startup and deployment

Etna begins after height S, the Etna start (START-10-4). On a network that switches from Unzen, the one-time procedure is in [SWITCH.md](SWITCH.md); on a new network S is the L2 genesis. Steps that name one kind of network apply only to it.

1. **[START-10-1]** Deploy the Inbox proxy (not yet initialized; only its owner can initialize it), then the `EtnaStaking` proxy (4.1) with the L1 chain id and that Inbox proxy address fixed (STAKE-4.1-37): on a new network it is part of the L2 genesis, and on a switching network it is deployed on L2 before `ETNA_FORK_TIME`, so that it exists at S (SYNC-9-5a, PROOF-6.2-46a). On a switching network one L2 transaction deploys the `EtnaStaking` implementation and the proxy and initializes the proxy with its owner (STAKE-4.1-40), at addresses fixed in advance with CREATE2, so that the proxy and implementation addresses that the EL, the nodes and both guests build in (PARAM-11-10b, PARAM-11-12a) are known before their release and no one else can initialize the proxy; the readiness check of START-10-5d verifies that deployment. Stakers bridge TAIKO to L2 and register under 4.1, signing the registration digest (appendix A) with their consensus keys; on a switching network they can register before the fork. No stake is needed at S: the fallback key set signs S + 1 and S + 2 (COMM-4.3-9) and every later block whose candidate is not usable (COMM-4.3-8b).
2. **[START-10-3]** On a new network, install EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 in the L2 genesis; a switching network verifies canonical runtime bytecode at both addresses and matching pre-execution system writes on build, import and proving paths on L2 before the L2 `SignalService` upgrade of step 4, which reads EIP-4788 whenever it saves a checkpoint, and so before `ETNA_FORK_TIME` ([ANCHORLESS.md section 7](ANCHORLESS.md#7-prerequisite-eip-4788-and-eip-2935-on-l2)). Set the base-fee sharing percentage to 100.
3. **[START-10-4]** **The Etna start S** is the last Unzen block on a network that switches from Unzen and block 0 on a new network. On a new network, compute block 0's hash and stateRoot with the EL; on a switching network, S and its block hash and stateRoot come from the Unzen chain (START-10-5a).
4. **[START-10-5]** Deploy the shared SignalService on L1 with immutable `_authorizedSyncer` set to the Inbox and `usesBeaconRoots` false; on a switching network, upgrade the existing L1 SignalService to that configuration instead, in the same DAO proposal as the Unzen Inbox upgrade (START-10-5d), keeping its checkpoint storage version, `VERSION`. Fix a nonzero DAO treasury as protocolFeeRecipient. Initialize recoveryGeneration and recoveryNonce to 0, with recover callable only by the owner. On a new network the same transaction performs the start write (START-10-5b) with S = 0 and the genesis hash and stateRoot, and saves the block-0 checkpoint in SignalService; on a switching network the start write follows under START-10-5a.
   - **[START-10-5a]** On a switching network only the designated multisig may call `setOrigin(S)`. ### 10.1 Protocol upgrades

- **[START-10.1-2]** Operational restarts that preserve validity rules retain all same-domain signer state. **[START-10.1-2a]** **Deployment core**: the Inbox proxy address and the address of the L2 staking contract, which the config hash commits (ENC-A-36), the L1 and L2 chain ids, generation 0's genesis, how a block's anchor is carried or inherited, checked and decoded (3.3, the envelope's anchor item and appendix A's witness slot order), slot 293's location and meaning, the header's timestamp and `parentBeaconBlockRoot` and the first 13 bytes of `extraData`, its first byte and its generation and anchor fields (3.2), 3.4 rules 2 and 3, and this section's ruleset selection, refusal and stop rules change only through a new-Inbox/new-genesis migration (section 10), which this section cannot bypass. The `missBitmap` field of `extraData` (CONS-3.4-7a) and the semantics of the block-start staking call (CONS-3.2-13) are not part of the deployment core; they belong to the ruleset. Decoding changes keyed only by an anchor's own L1 header, including the format of the account and storage proofs checked under that anchor's root, follow L1's schedule instead (section 12, item 10). **[START-10.1-2b]** Nodes and guests select a block's ruleset from slot 293 at the block's anchor (3f), which the EL does not read. A ruleset that changes what the EL executes or checks for a block, such as block-start processing (CONS-3.2-12) or the layout of `extraData` after its first 13 bytes (ENC-A-10), can therefore be scheduled, or named in `recover`'s `rules` (14e), only once its definition states how the EL selects it for exactly the blocks that this section assigns to it. Ruleset 0 is unaffected.
- **[START-10.1-3a]** **Rulesets**: a ruleset is a complete, published definition of what nodes and guests check and execute for each block; sections 3 to 7 and appendix A define ruleset 0. **[START-10.1-3b]** Each later ruleset is published as a change to an earlier one before it is scheduled, and a ruleset number names only one definition. **[START-10.1-3c]** A **rule upgrade** switches blocks to a new ruleset from an L2 block timestamp onward, without stopping production. **[START-10.1-3d]** A ruleset that `scheduleRules` activates keeps the deployment core and cannot change what L1 checks or stores (the statement, `land`, cuts, U, the config hash, the L2 staking contract's address and appendix B), the envelope and payload framing (5.1, 5.2 and appendix A), the block size bound (3.4), the consensus parameters, anything the CometBFT version fixes that other nodes or the guests must match (block and vote validity, sign bytes, header and validator-set hashing, the P2P wire protocol and the validator-state arithmetic that appendix A binds to CONS-3.1-1's source revision), `chain_id`, rule 7's anchor freshness bound or `maxAnchorAge`. Changing any of these outside the deployment core is a **structural upgrade**: a new ruleset that only `recover` installs, at the start of a generation (14e). A CometBFT build that changes none of them, such as a security patch that interoperates with the pinned build, is neither a ruleset nor a structural upgrade: once CONS-3.1-1 and the deployment manifest name its source commit and digests, operators install it node by node without a stop, and its release repeats the stock comparisons of the acceptance checklist. Where it changes a stock behavior that sections 8 and 9 cite, those rules are revised with it. Owner changes that only L1 applies, such as registering an image or replacing the verifier (6.2, section 8), are neither, but must keep 6c true.
- **[START-10.1-3e]** **Schedule**: Inbox slot 293 holds `activeRules`, `nextRules`, `rulesTime` and `stopTime` (appendix B); its initial value 0 means ruleset 0, nothing scheduled and no stop. **[START-10.1-3f]** A block's ruleset is `nextRules` if the block's timestamp is at least `rulesTime`, and `activeRules` otherwise, both read from slot 293 at the block's anchor (carried or inherited).
- **[START-10.1-3g]** Only the Inbox owner (the DAO, behind its timelock) writes the schedule, through the two calls below and `recover` (section 8); nodes and both guests enforce it for every block, and L1 enforces it by accepting only their proofs. An off-chain manifest or operator agreement alone is insufficient. **[START-10.1-5a]** `scheduleRules(expectedGeneration, rules, time)`: if `block.timestamp ≥ rulesTime`, first set `activeRules = nextRules`; otherwise require `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < rulesTime`. Then require `rules` to be `nextRules` or `nextRules + 1` and `time ≥ block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge`, and set `nextRules = rules` and `rulesTime = time`. An equal `rules` reschedules the pending ruleset, and `time` = 2\^48 − 1 cancels it. **[START-10.1-5b]** `scheduleStop(expectedGeneration, time)`: if `stopTime ≠ 0`, require `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < stopTime`; then require `time = 0` or `time ≥ block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge`, and set `stopTime = time`; 0 cancels a stop. Before governance authorizes a nonzero stop, its proposal preview shows the proposed stopTime, the current maxAnchorAge and the cancellation/replacement condition `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < stopTime`. The last eligible L1 timestamp is `stopTime − maxAnchorAge − 1` seconds, subject to a block actually being included. At the simulated execution timestamp, distinguish a rejected schedule from an accepted minimum-lead schedule. For equality, display “Once this proposal executes, the stop cannot be cancelled or rescheduled.” Label the assumed execution time and refresh the preview if it changes. **[START-10.1-5c]** Before either call can change or cancel a schedule, require expectedGeneration to equal the current recoveryGeneration in Inbox slot 268. Both calls are owner-only, require Inbox initialization, read `maxAnchorAge` from slot 282, compute the sums without overflow, revert on a value that does not fit its 48-bit field (appendix B), emit the new slot 293 value and revert on any failed check.

<details>
<summary>Details: why maxAnchorAge is the deadline</summary>

A block's timestamp is never earlier than its anchor's (3.2) and at most `maxAnchorAge` later (3.4 rule 7). Every block whose timestamp is at or after some time X therefore uses an anchor from no earlier than X − `maxAnchorAge`, and an anchor's state includes its own L1 block's transactions: a value written at least that long before X is seen by all of them, and a value that can no longer change within that window is the same for all of them. Every change either takes effect at least `maxAnchorAge` after the write or, when `scheduleRules` promotes `nextRules`, only restates the ruleset of blocks that already used it. So within a generation a block's ruleset depends only on its timestamp and its stop only on its parent, not on which valid anchor the proposer picked, and the ruleset number never decreases along the chain. A number never names two definitions (3b), so a number in an image's set keeps meaning the same rules. The strict bound for changing a pending value is conservative: equality would also be safe, because the anchor's state includes its own block's transactions.

</details>

- **[START-10.1-6]** Registering a new image alone is not a protocol upgrade. **[START-10.1-6a]** **Images**: each guest image implements a declared set of rulesets, checks every block it proves under the ruleset and stop selected above, and produces no proof that covers a block whose ruleset is outside its set. **[START-10.1-6b]** The verifier's accepted image set stays a flat set (6.2): an image is not removed because a ruleset it lacks takes effect, and its proofs of blocks under rulesets it implements stay valid. **[START-10.1-6c]** At all times the accepted set must contain a valid proof combination (6.2) whose images implement every ruleset that applies (3f) to a timestamp later than the latest checkpoint block's, which includes `activeRules` and, unless `rulesTime` is 2\^48 − 1, `nextRules`. Blocks above the checkpoint may not exist yet: after a production pause, the next block's BFT time comes from its parent's commit, so with an older finalized anchor it can carry a timestamp just after its parent's and an earlier ruleset (3.2). Every block that can still be landed, and so every batch of such blocks, then has one combination for all its rulesets; image registration and revocation and verifier replacement must keep this true. **[START-10.1-14]** A new image proves every block under that block's own ruleset, so it cannot change history.
- **[START-10.1-14a]** **Nodes** compute each block's ruleset and stop as above in PrepareProposal, ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock. **[START-10.1-14b]** If the node's software does not implement the ruleset, PrepareProposal returns the placeholder proposal and ProcessProposal rejects (section 8), while FinalizeBlock waits and retries; validators upgrading during a pause follow RECOV-8.2-2’s precommit procedure at an undecided height. Production resumes when sufficient upgraded voting power can sign. **[START-10.1-14c]** Software that replays history, including 5.3's public reconstruction program, implements every ruleset that history used.
- **[START-10.1-14d]** **Planned stop**: when `stopTime ≠ 0` at a block's anchor, the block is invalid if its parent's timestamp is at least `stopTime`, so production ends at the first height whose timestamp reaches `stopTime`. **[START-10.1-14e]** **Structural upgrade**: schedule a stop; once landers settle the height where production ended, one DAO transaction on the standard path (8.1) makes the contract, verifier and image changes and calls `recover` (section 8) with `expectedC` at that point and a nonzero `rules` naming a ruleset whose definition includes the structural change, so images that lack it refuse the new generation's blocks (6a) and none needs removal. It is a full governance recovery: 8.1's checks apply, and the fallback key set whose hash the new origin record holds signs the new generation's first two blocks (COMM-4.3-9). No decided block is discarded once that height settles; if it cannot, recovery from the last settled checkpoint discards the blocks above it, as any recovery does (section 8); downtime covers settlement, the DAO's authorization delay, R's finality, the 8.2 and 8.4 authentication, the signer restart and the first block.
- **[START-10.1-14f]** Nodes and both guests implement this section's ruleset selection, refusal and stop from launch, because an image that lacks them cannot be made to refuse later rulesets. **[START-10.1-14g]** Before a ruleset is scheduled, the client, its EL and both guests pass shared vectors for its definition, including one landing that covers blocks on both sides of `rulesTime` ([Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md)).
- **Failures** follow from the rules above: a DAO call executed too late reverts and activates nothing; a schedule can be changed or cancelled until `maxAnchorAge` before its `rulesTime` or `stopTime`; if validators holding at least 1/3 of the voting power lack the ruleset, production pauses at `rulesTime` and resumes as they upgrade; a defective ruleset that has taken effect is replaced by a later rule upgrade while production continues, or otherwise by `recover`'s `rules`, which discards unsettled blocks above its checkpoint and uses the DAO path 8.1 allows; and only `recover` clears a stop that has taken effect. L1 hard-fork deadlines are separate (section 12, item 10).

## 12. Known limitations and open items

1. **[LIMIT-12-1]** **No wall-clock FI deadline.** `maxAnchorAge` bounds only anchor age relative to block time; wall-clock progress also depends on 3.4's BFT-time assumptions, queue backlog, L1 finality and settlement (7.3).
2. **[LIMIT-12-2]** **Offline quorum relies on DAO recovery.** A halted chain does not replace members automatically, and without blocks no missed-signature penalty applies (item 12). Returning members may restore it. Otherwise, when a third or more of the candidate's power is offline, the DAO sets `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1 and executes governance recovery in one proposal, so that the fallback key set signs the new generation, then forfeits the offline bonds through L1 → L2 governance messages and lowers `minTotalStake` again (RECOV-8-21); when a third or more of the fallback key set is offline while it signs, the DAO replaces the keys with `setFallbackKeys` and then executes governance recovery (RECOV-8-22). The DAO still judges online availability and operator concentration of both sets (RECOV-8.1-7); success is not guaranteed, and L1 finality failure, incomplete proofs or missing history can still prevent recovery. Ordinary recovery discards the suffix above c, and its duration includes the DAO authorization delay, finality, reconstruction, signer migration and first settlement. If the voting power fenced at the same undecided H, together with power that is offline or otherwise not voting, reaches one third of the current total, the remaining power cannot form a fresh quorum. Unless votes already cast can certify H, the generation stalls. A height that stays undecided beyond the stock WAL's size limit loses same-height resumption for every validator that restarts in it, whether or not it holds a lock (RECOV-8.2-2). Peers connected directly to a validator can fill that limit in seconds, which is why a validator exchanges P2P messages only with listed nodes (START-10-6); a listed node that is itself faulty still can. With durable H + 1 floors the old generation cannot clear those fences by restarting; governance recovery needs its own authenticated origin. Each landing that advances c past an authorization’s fixed expectedC makes that authorization stale; repeated advances can indefinitely postpone standard-path recovery (8.1). Loss of both the original blobs after L1 pruning and sufficient prior audit evidence, or withholding of the settled fork’s required Comet headers or commits during equivocation, can also leave ordinary recovery unauthorized indefinitely. There is no non-governance escape hatch.
3. **[LIMIT-12-3]** **Double-sign slashing is same-round only.** Cross-round amnesia is not covered, and gossip can miss evidence; offline responsibility is penalized automatically only while blocks are produced (4.2), and for a halt it is discretionary DAO forfeiture (4.4), not evidence-based slashing. Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 has no query or event that returns pending evidence or conflicting votes, and a node without a validator key forms no evidence from the votes it sees, so a reporter needs its own collection of votes (SYNC-9.1-7).
4. **[LIMIT-12-4]** **Operations depend on DAO funding.** Landing has no protocol revenue; settlement liveness relies on DAO-funded landers and on validators landing to avoid a back-pressure pause. Budgets and real proving/landing costs still need measurement (appendix C).
5. **[LIMIT-12-5]** **Dependence on L1 finality.** Production pauses when no finalized anchor satisfies `maxAnchorAge` (3.4).
6. **[LIMIT-12-5a]** **The fallback key set must stay online.** The fallback key set is never empty (COMM-4.3-18), but its holders must run, for each key, a validator instance of its own that follows the chain and that key's signer at all times, also while the candidate signs (COMM-4.3-20): an exit or a penalty that drops the candidate's total stake below `minTotalStake` hands signing to the fallback key set a few blocks later (X + 3 for an exit in block X), so if a third or more of its keys cannot sign then, that one drop halts consensus until enough of them return or the DAO replaces them and executes governance recovery (RECOV-8-22).
7. **[LIMIT-12-7]** **No power-change limit.** The set that signs H + 2 is every seated bond in H's candidate snapshot, with its power (4.3), so stake that registers or tops up in block X signs from X + 3, and there is no limit on how much power changes from one block to the next (COMM-4.3-16), no takeover bound and no guaranteed DAO reaction window; continuously assess the actual signing set's less-than-1/3 Byzantine-power assumption (4.3).
8. **[LIMIT-12-8]** **Refunds wait for the withdrawal delay, which a halt stops.** A withdrawal waits `withdrawalDelay` L2 blocks after the exit is set (STAKE-4.1-27), and those blocks do not pass while the chain is halted (STAKE-4.1-28a), so a halt postpones every refund until production resumes, possibly in a new generation after governance recovery, which voids exits and withdrawals executed above c (RECOV-8-59). If no recovery restarts production, the stake stays locked in L2 state. Bridging withdrawn TAIKO back to L1 also depends on landing (item 28).
9. **[LIMIT-12-9]** **The switching network waits for the final Unzen proof.** The outage from the Unzen propose cutoff to the first Etna block includes proving every outstanding Unzen proposal, recording S and finalizing the start-write block on L1 (START-10-5a, LAND-6.1-8a, START-10-6). A slow or unavailable final proof delays both block production and Etna forced inclusion. The Unzen Inbox accepts a proof only from its prover whitelist while that whitelist has members, so the final proof also depends on a whitelisted prover or on the DAO changing the whitelist. If the Unzen image is defective, governance must authorize a repaired Unzen image and the proof must still succeed. No Etna recovery generation substitutes for that proof.
10. **[LIMIT-12-10a]** L1 hard forks are a separate, external deadline: forks that only append header fields are absorbed by the decoding rule in 3.3, while forks that change existing fields or the state commitment (for example EIP-7864's binary trie, which invalidates every MPT witness) need the client and guest upgrades and the new image registrations done before the fork activates on L1, on L1's schedule rather than at an L2 height. A fork that changes L1's slot time, gas costs or per-transaction gas cap need not change rules or images, but what this spec counts in L1 blocks (the reference-header bound of 6.3 and the hash windows of 6.1; the withdrawal delay counts L2 blocks, PARAM-11-13) and the gas measurements of `land`, of the worst-case `recover` transaction and of the verifier-failure rollback ([Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md)) are checked again before it activates.
11. **[LIMIT-12-11]** **Seat count and the L2 supply assumption.** At most n_max bonds, and never more than 128, hold seats at once, so there are no standby candidates (4.1). Deployment requires minStake × n_max ≥ 10\^27 base units, TAIKO's fixed total supply, so that a bond that comes to meet the seat conditions always finds an empty seat (STAKE-4.1-14a). That assumes L2 holds no more TAIKO than the fixed supply: if it ever held more, for example after a supply increase or after L2 TAIKO is minted without TAIKO on L1 backing it (item 28), more than n_max bonds could meet the conditions at once, and while no seat is empty the call that would give a bond a seat reverts (STAKE-4.1-14a), so a registration or a top-up to minStake fails until a seat is released. A bond releases its seat in the call that sets its exit or leaves it below minStake (STAKE-4.1-13b), so no seat is held by a bond that has exited or fallen below minStake. The bound depends on the fixed supply, and it sets the admission threshold: no bond can register with less than 10\^27 / n_max base units (7,812,500 TAIKO with n_max = 128).
12. **[LIMIT-12-11a]** **No automatic penalty during a halt.** A missed-signature penalty needs blocks whose block-start calls count the misses (STAKE-4.2-5), so a halt penalizes nobody, including the members whose withheld signatures cause it (RECOV-8-21). Responsibility for a halt is left to DAO forfeiture (4.4), which can execute only once blocks are produced again, in practice in the generation a governance recovery starts (FORF-4.4-20).
13. **[LIMIT-12-13]** **secp256k1eth is new.** The key type was added only in CometBFT v0.40.0, with one later fix to its signature format, so it is less battle-tested than Ed25519; support in external signers and HSM/KMS must be confirmed separately. CometBFT does not batch-verify it. [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md) requires pinned versions, cross-implementation vectors, signer/HSM/KMS compatibility and n = 128 measurements; a negligible cost impact must not be assumed before testing.
14. **[LIMIT-12-14]** **Registration admission bounds.** The vp_unit lower bound only prevents power overflow (4.3). minStake is at least 10\^27 / n_max base units (item 11), so validators are few and large. While the supply assumption of item 11 holds, a registration with minStake or more always takes a seat, but a seat signs only while the candidate is usable, so seated bonds whose total stake is below `minTotalStake` sign nothing (COMM-4.3-3a); registration does not guarantee signing.
15. **[LIMIT-12-15]** **Private consensus-history availability.** Public blobs only reconstruct the settled EL; ordinary proving, Comet replay, signing evidence and L1 ancestry depend on the 9.1 archives, and unauthenticated snapshots are not substitutes.
16. **[LIMIT-12-16]** **Overlapping proofs provide short-landing protection, pending acceptance.** The leaf rules are in 6.1 and 6.3. A competitor landing first at any height in (p,h) cannot make an honest submission fail. Only a competitor settling h or beyond makes it revert as already settled, and then the competitor has advanced settlement on its behalf, which cannot trigger back-pressure; at worst the honest lander pays blob gas and calldata for that transaction. No throughput or recovery guarantee is claimed under more-than-1/3 collusion, unavailable proofs/data or failed L1 finality.
17. **[LIMIT-12-17]** **Resource and latency figures await validation.** U=151,200, approximately 4 s blocks, 20 min landings, about 900 unsettled blocks in normal operation and about 7 days of outage margin are illustrative, not measured, and must pass [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md). Checking only the latest checkpoint does not remove history retention or fix indefinite L1 finality failure; the unsettled suffix remains governance rollback exposure.
18. **[LIMIT-12-18]** **Economic security and discretionary forfeiture.** At least 1/3 of voting power can halt consensus by withholding signatures, and a halt carries no automatic penalty (item 12). Checklist section 4 sets an independent capital target and conservative reference price, from which the DAO chooses `minTotalStake`; concrete deployment values and launch checks remain required. Operation and recovery may continue with publicly disclosed economic-security shortfalls. Forfeiture depends on correct DAO attribution and execution before lawful withdrawal; it does not guarantee loss for every halt, and has no extra waiting or appeal period. Its unexecuted authorizations cannot be revoked through the staking contract before expiry. If the same principal B is subject to both punishments and the owner can self-report valid double-sign evidence first, its loss is B − floor(B × slashBountyBps / 10000). A forfeiture first takes ceil(B × forfeitBps / 10000) and leaves any remainder slashable. When forfeitBps exceeds 10000 − slashBountyBps, the self-report can cost less, subject to integer rounding. Normal-operation forfeiture grants the DAO forced-exit authority, and the seats it releases can drop the candidate's total stake below `minTotalStake` and hand signing to the fallback key set (FORF-4.4-20).
19. **[LIMIT-12-19]** **Slashed members keep their voting power for two blocks.** Double-sign slashing zeroes the bond's whole principal, sets its exit and releases its seat (STAKE-4.1-34), so a slash in block X enters the candidate snapshot at X + 1 and first changes the set that signs X + 3 (4.3): the member keeps its power for X + 1 and X + 2, including through any halt before they are decided. Further misconduct by that member in this period has no principal left to slash. In this period the member's assigned voting power counts as Byzantine power under section 1's less-than-1/3 bound (4.3). A forfeiture in block X behaves the same way (FORF-4.4-13), and a missed-signature penalty, applied in block H's own call, first changes the set that signs H + 2 (RECOV-8-20).
20. **[LIMIT-12-20]** **Blanking does not stop resubmission.** Step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) replaces each listed entry's `txHash` with `keccak256("")`, but `saveForcedInclusion` (7.1) does not check whether the same bytes were blanked before. The enqueue event publishes the raw bytes (7.1), so anyone can enqueue the same unprovable transaction again for the normal fee. This situation presupposes a violation of section 1's proof completeness, such as an EL/guest mismatch or a guest resource limit below the EL limits. Each recovery blanks only the indices listed in `fiToClear`; unlisted copies enqueued while DAO authorization is pending survive it, and adding them to the list requires fresh DAO authorization (8.3). Further recoveries may be needed while the content remains unprovable. Resolving the underlying proof-completeness failure, or changing admission/skip rules through a DAO upgrade, can end the recurring stall; blanking itself does not prevent resubmission.
21. **[LIMIT-12-21]** **Upgrades depend on the DAO and on validators upgrading in time.** A rule upgrade pauses production at `rulesTime` while validators holding at least 1/3 of the voting power run software without the new ruleset, and the DAO can change a schedule only until `maxAnchorAge` before its `rulesTime` or `stopTime` (10.1). After that, a defective ruleset can be replaced only by a later rule upgrade while production continues, or by governance recovery, which discards unsettled blocks above its checkpoint; a stop that has taken effect is cleared only by recovery. Ruleset numbers only grow, so returning to earlier rules takes a new number. A structural upgrade stops production for settlement and the DAO's authorization delay. Block time can run ahead of L1 time by up to `maxAnchorAge` less the finality lag when it is manipulated (3.4), so validators should run a new ruleset's software by `rulesTime − maxAnchorAge`.
22. **[LIMIT-12-22]** **The stock evidence pool is unbounded.** Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 always runs its evidence reactor and pool. Pending evidence can accumulate in memory and on disk, and consensus also buffers conflicting votes in memory until a block commits. With zero evidence bytes (CONS-3.1-10) no item is ever committed, and during a halt neither the 16-block nor the 3-minute age limit advances. A committee member that double-signs can therefore keep loading the memory, storage and gossip of the nodes it reaches for as long as no block commits, at the cost of its bond once the votes reach the staking contract on L2 (4.1), which needs blocks. This is accepted, because the engine stays the official stock release (CONS-3.1-1, CONS-3.1-2). Three rules limit the exposure without bounding it: the short age limits expire items while blocks commit (START-10-21), a validator exchanges P2P messages only with listed nodes (START-10-6), and the supervisor deletes the pending-evidence store at every start (CONS-3.1-10), so restarting a node under pressure empties it. None of them bounds a running node's usage during a halt. Operators provision memory and storage for it and alert on growth. During a halt, preserve the two signed votes outside the evidence store. Verify the common consensus key and resolve its bond under 4.1, then submit eligible evidence to `slashDoubleSign` for the whole remaining principal in an L2 transaction once blocks commit (SYNC-9.1-7). The votes identify the consensus key. Use operator records to identify any P2P IDs controlled by that signer before removing peers, since an honest sentry can forward the pair. Remove confirmed offender IDs from the affected operators’ dial and inbound lists and disconnect those peers. Coordinate affected validators and sentries to stop, clear only the pending-evidence store under CONS-3.1-10, and restart with the WAL, block, state, app/EL and signer stores intact. Verify replay admission, sufficient voting power and resumed commits. Slashing leaves the member's voting power in place until the third block after the slash (item 19). Relayed evidence may return. If a quorum remains unavailable, follow section 8. Rehearse this response under [acceptance sections 4 and 7](ACCEPTANCE.md). An official CometBFT release that bounds these paths or can turn them off would remove this limitation; adopting one is an engine change under START-10.1-3d.
23. **[LIMIT-12-23]** **The fallback key set has no stake.** While it signs, the chain runs on keys the DAO chose, each with power 1: no stake backs them, a double sign by one of them can be slashed only through a bond registered to the same key (COMM-4.3-8c), and the withdrawal delay locks no stake behind their signatures (STAKE-4.1-28a). Safety and finality then rest on fewer than 1/3 of those keys being Byzantine. Funds on L1 remain protected by the two proofs of every landing (6.2), as section 1 states.
24. **[LIMIT-12-24]** **Proposers choose whose precommits count.** The next block's proposer assembles LastCommit and may leave out precommits it received, and an omitted precommit counts as a miss (CONS-3.4-7a), so colluders who propose `missLimit` consecutive blocks can get a member penalized, and colluders holding more than 2/3 of the voting power can get any member penalized (4.2).
25. **[LIMIT-12-25]** **Staking in the unsettled suffix can be discarded.** Stake lives in L2 state, so registrations, top-ups, exits, withdrawals, double-sign slashes, missed-signature penalties and forfeitures executed above c are void when governance recovery discards that suffix (RECOV-8-59), up to U blocks, about 7 days at the target U (BACKP-6.4-13). Discarded double-sign evidence can be submitted again (RECOV-8-59), and a discarded forfeiture can be executed again (FORF-4.4-21).
26. **[LIMIT-12-26]** **Mode switches have no hysteresis.** The choice between the candidate and the fallback key set is made again at every block (COMM-4.3-16), so while the candidate's total stake is near `minTotalStake`, a top-up, an exit or a missed-signature penalty can switch the signers back and forth, and each switch replaces the whole validator set at once (COMM-4.3-13a).
27. **[LIMIT-12-27]** **The DAO controls the committee rules.** Setting `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1 hands every block to the fallback key set (COMM-4.3-19), and the DAO owns the L2 staking contract and can upgrade it (STAKE-4.1-40), which can change seats, penalties, power and the candidate snapshot, and with them the committee that 4.3 derives. 4.3's validity check keeps an invalid candidate from signing (COMM-4.3-8), but not a valid one that a faulty upgrade produces, whose stored `snapshotTotalStake` can be any value; against it the DAO's only switch that depends on nothing in L2 state is `minTotalStake` = 2\^256 − 1 (COMM-4.3-3a).
28. **[LIMIT-12-28]** **Stake is TAIKO bridged to L2.** A staker first bridges TAIKO from L1 (STAKE-4.1-1), and after withdrawal, bridging it back to L1 depends on landing, because L1 recognizes only landed checkpoints (section 1), so a settlement stall delays it. The committee's security also depends on the integrity of the bridged L2 TAIKO token (STAKE-4.1-37a): the staking contract counts every unit at face value, and `minStake`, `minTotalStake` and the capital target `C_CAP_MIN` behind it assume that TAIKO on L1 backs each one. Anything that mints L2 TAIKO without that backing, such as a forgery against the L1 → L2 bridge or its token vault, or an upgrade of the token, lets its holder register bonds that take seats in the same call (STAKE-4.1-14a) and sign from three blocks later (COMM-4.3-3), with power in proportion to what they stake. Holding more than 2/3 of the voting power takes over consensus, and holding every seat makes honest registrations and top-ups to minStake revert (item 11). The DAO's response is to set `minTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1, so that the fallback key set signs whatever stake the snapshot shows (COMM-4.3-3a, COMM-4.3-19), with governance recovery if the chain halts first (section 8), and then to forfeit those bonds under 4.4 while the fallback key set signs. A value above TAIKO's total supply is not enough, because the unbacked stake can exceed it. The two proofs of every landing (6.2) still keep a committee taken over this way from taking funds on L1 through a false landing, as section 1 states. They do not protect the TAIKO that L1 holds for the bridge from the unbacked units themselves: a valid landing lets their holder bridge them to L1 like any other TAIKO, and switching the signers does not undo that. Limiting that loss is a matter for the bridge and its token vault, which this specification does not cover.
29. **[LIMIT-12-29]** **A failing block-start call is not a block error.** If `onBlockStart` reverts or halts exceptionally, running out of gas included, or the staking contract's address holds no code, the block stays valid (CONS-3.2-13), the fallback key set signs two heights later (COMM-4.3-3a), and while the call keeps failing no miss is counted and no penalty applies. The generation the call last stored also stays as it was, so after a governance recovery, until the call first succeeds in the new generation, `slashDoubleSign` rejects evidence from that generation (STAKE-4.1-32). Finding the cause depends on monitoring; the DAO repairs the contract through a governance message (4.3). No metering bounds what the call costs to prove: it can use its whole 30,000,000 gas in every block and again in a 7.2 singleton trial (FI-7.2-8), and neither the block's gasUsed nor its zk gas meters it (CONS-3.2-13). An implementation, faulty or upgraded, that spends that gas on work that is expensive to prove therefore keeps every block valid for nodes but can make the blocks unprovable within one landing's two proofs, contrary to MODEL-1-2: settlement stops, production pauses once U blocks are unsettled (CONS-3.4-6), a governance recovery from a c whose L2 state already holds that code restarts with it, and every block that could carry the DAO's repair makes the call before its transactions (CONS-3.2-13). [Acceptance section 1](ACCEPTANCE.md#1-resource-and-latency-acceptance-spec-64) measures the call's worst proving cost.
30. **[LIMIT-12-30]** **Double-sign reports can be delayed.** A report is an L2 transaction to `slashDoubleSign` (SYNC-9.1-7) that colluding proposers can leave out; the only guaranteed path is forced inclusion (section 7), which has no wall-clock deadline (item 1). A report included only after the bond is withdrawn slashes nothing (STAKE-4.1-34), which is why the evidence window must allow for it (PARAM-11-15).
31. **[LIMIT-12-31]** **Honest refusals to sign count as misses.** A validator that votes nil or does not vote because its own L1 view lags (RECOV-8-5), its checkpoint trust has not passed (section 9) or its signer is cut off is missing like any other (CONS-3.4-7a), and its misses count toward `missLimit`. Section 11's `missLimit` guidance leaves room for this.
32. **[LIMIT-12-32]** **The signing set can change at every block.** A top-up or a missed-signature penalty changes a member's power, so any staker can make the signing set differ at every block (COMM-4.3-16), and in the worst case the guest verifies a commit of up to 128 signatures for every block of a landing (PROOF-6.2-16). This bound is accepted: there is no top-up minimum and no limit on how fast power changes.
33. **[LIMIT-12-33]** **A precommit that arrives late counts as a miss.** A validator that signs every block can still be counted as missing. After finalizing a block, a node accepts late precommits for that block's committing round only while it is in `RoundStepNewHeight` at the next height. Once it leaves that step, it ignores any precommit it processes for the previous height, even one that arrived before the timeout was due. The next proposer assembles LastCommit from the precommits it holds (CONS-3.4-7a). `timeout_commit` is each node's own setting and no validity rule checks it: START-10-6's lower bound binds operators, not blocks, so nothing in the protocol bounds how late an honest precommit may arrive and still count. A member whose precommits miss `missLimit` consecutive LastCommits this way is penalized like one that is offline (STAKE-4.2-6) and loses its seat if the penalty leaves it below minStake (STAKE-4.1-14c). `missLimit` sets how long such a run must last (PARAM-11-7a); no value excludes it.

## Appendix A: Encodings

**[ENC-A-1]** **Versions**: APP_VERSION=1, and every versioned domain tag is V1; the outer envelope/payload prefix is 0x06. **[ENC-A-2]** Config has four words/128 bytes. **[ENC-A-3]** Slot 285 has three words (appendix B). **[ENC-A-4a]** Changing clients or registering an image does not activate new rules; only the 10.1 schedule selects a block's ruleset, and guest-image acceptance is the verifier's accepted set (6.2).

**Shared vectors**: every encoding, interface, storage layout and boundary condition needs accept/reject vectors shared by nodes, contracts and both guests; the list is in [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md).

**[ENC-A-5]** Protocol hashes are keccak256 over `abi.encode`, with domain tags as right-zero-padded `bytes32` ASCII; the exceptions are the blob hash list and the cut leaf list (`abi.encodePacked`). Every formula below writes its tag as `bytes32("...")`: a tag is one static word and is never encoded as a dynamic string.

```txt
// [ENC-A-6]
chainDomain = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32("ETNA_COMET_DOMAIN_V1"),
                uint256(l1ChainId), uint256(l2ChainId), address(inbox), uint48(generation)))
// [ENC-A-7]
chain_id    = "etna-" + base64url_no_padding(chainDomain)  // raw 32-byte digest -> 43 chars; total 48 ASCII bytes
  // [ENC-A-8]
  // RFC 4648 section 5 alphabet, canonical zero pad bits, no "=" or whitespace; case-sensitive
  // [ENC-A-9]
  // inbox is the EtnaInbox proxy address; generation starts at 0 and never wraps or decreases
// [ENC-A-10]
extraData  = 0x64 || uint48_be(generation) || uint48_be(anchorNumber) || uint128_be(missBitmap)   // 29 bytes
// [ENC-A-10a]
baseFee    = min(max(raw, minBaseFee), 1 gwei)          // EIP-4396 for block b; p is b's parent, g is p's parent; every division floors
  target   = p.gasLimit / 2                                                        // ELASTICITY_MULTIPLIER = 2
  adjusted = min(target * (p.timestamp - g.timestamp) / 4, p.gasLimit * 95 / 100)  // BLOCK_TIME_TARGET = 4 s, MAX_GAS_TARGET_PERCENT = 95
  delta    = p.baseFeePerGas * |p.gasUsed - adjusted| / target / 8                 // BASE_FEE_MAX_CHANGE_DENOMINATOR = 8
  raw      = p.baseFeePerGas                    if p.gasUsed == adjusted
           = p.baseFeePerGas + max(delta, 1)    if p.gasUsed >  adjusted
           = max(p.baseFeePerGas - delta, 0)    if p.gasUsed <  adjusted
  // p.gasLimit and p.gasUsed are the values in p's header, also when p is an Unzen block; minBaseFee is 0.01 gwei for
  //   chain id 167000 and 0.005 gwei otherwise; block 1 with S = 0 is fixed at 0.025 gwei instead (3.2). The 4 s target is
  //   the operating block interval (3.1) and applies to every Etna block, S + 1 included; Unzen blocks used 2 s
// [ENC-A-10b]
mixHash    = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32(p.difficulty), uint256(b.number)))       // 2 words, 64 bytes; p.difficulty as a big-endian word
// [ENC-A-10c]
missBitmap bit of seat s = (missBitmap >> s) & 1        // s in [0, 128); seat 0 is the low bit of extraData[28], the last byte, and seat 127 the high bit of extraData[13]
  // 3.4 states which bits a block sets, and 4.2 what onBlockStart does with them
// [ENC-A-10d]
onBlockStart calldata = 0xea987592 || abi.encode(uint48(generation), uint128(missBitmap))   // 4 + 2 * 32 = 68 bytes
  // 0xea987592 = bytes4(keccak256("onBlockStart(uint48,uint128)")); generation and missBitmap are the block's own extraData fields,
  //   each one 32-byte big-endian word with zero high bits (standard ABI encoding); no other bytes follow

// [ENC-A-11]
envelope   = 0x06 || rlp([block, anchor, forced])
  // [ENC-A-12]
  block     = [elHeader, [tx, ...]]                    // tx: RLP byte string of its EIP-2718 encoding, legacy included
  // [ENC-A-13]
  anchor    = [] or [[rawL1Header, inboxWitness]]       // rawL1Header: byte string holding the L1 header's raw RLP, decoded per 3.3
  // [ENC-A-15]
  forced    = [] or [[index, entryWitness, tx]]         // tx may be empty; if nonempty its hash must match; required for skips
  // [ENC-A-16]
  // Strict decoding (identical for nodes and guests; any violation makes the proposal invalid): the envelope is exactly 0x06 plus one RLP list, with no trailing bytes;
  // [ENC-A-17]
  // every list has exactly the items above, and anchor/forced are empty or hold exactly one item; integers use minimal big-endian encoding, 0 is the empty string, no leading zeros;
  // [ENC-A-18]
  // fixed-length byte strings (hashes, stateRoots, slots) have exactly their length (slots 32 bytes, addresses 20 bytes); no extra items or unknown fields
  // [ENC-A-19]
  // Field types: hashes, stateRoots and slots are 32-byte strings and addresses 20-byte strings; all other numbers are integers;
  // [ENC-A-20]
  // storage values always use minimal integer encoding, including slots that hold hashes; RLP length prefixes must also be minimal;
  // [ENC-A-21]
  // AccountWitness.address must be the expected contract: the Inbox for anchor and forced witnesses

// [ENC-A-23]
```

## Appendix C: Operating funding and cost assumptions

This appendix states operating requirements, not validity rules.

- **[COST-C-1]** **The DAO funds operations**: validators, proving, landing, historical data and on-call service, including low/zero transaction load, FI consumption, repeated DA, discarded competitive work and retries. **[COST-C-2]** Landing has no protocol revenue (base fees and tips go to proposers, FI fees to the protocol); this operating budget covers all landing and proving costs.
- **[COST-C-3]** **How funding is paid**: funding grants no exclusive landing permission. **[COST-C-4]** Landers do not receive FI fees, and funding is not paid per FI entry consumed or by FI fee value, so an attacker cannot recover its FI fees through funding (7.3). **[COST-C-5]** land binds no recipient and a public blob transaction can be copied, so DAO compensation must not be a per-landing bounty paid to the submitting address; fund named operators or attribute work off chain.
- **[COST-C-6]** **Fund the sustained worst case**: measure and fund the budget for the operating targets of approximately 4 s blocks and at most 20 min between L1 inclusions of successive successful landings (6.4); cost estimates alone are not evidence that funding is in place. **[COST-C-7]** A proposer pays no net fee for any load in its own blocks (3.2), whether zk-heavy or data-heavy; such load is bounded only by the per-block gas, zk-gas and 124,000-byte (3.4) limits. Any other sender pays only the L2 fee for the gas its transactions use, at a base fee that can sit at the chain's minimum (3.2), and nothing for the proving, blob and landing cost that load causes, so the worst cases below can be caused by any sender for that fee alone. **[COST-C-8]** Without zk-to-charged-gas conversion, a zk-heavy workload with low EVM gas may use almost the whole per-block zk-gas limit. **[COST-C-9]** The data worst case is every block filled with incompressible data. **[COST-C-10]** A landing carries at most six blobs, hence six such blocks: about 150 landings an hour. **[COST-C-11]** That is 3 blobs per L1 slot. **[COST-C-12]** Fund that sustained worst case explicitly in the proving, blob and landing budgets.
