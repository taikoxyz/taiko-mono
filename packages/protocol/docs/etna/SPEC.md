# Etna PoS + ZK Design Spec

> **In one sentence**: In Etna, a committee of TAIKO stakers produces blocks with stock CometBFT and finalizes them instantly; anyone can land a proven batch on L1, and funds on L1 recognize only landed checkpoints.
>
> **Scope**: Etna begins after height S, the Etna start: the last Unzen block on a network that switches from Unzen ([SWITCH.md](SWITCH.md)), or block 0 on a new network.
>
> **Target**: Stage 0; Stage 1 is not a goal for now.

## 1. Positioning and security model

- **Positioning**: a PoS-sequenced validity rollup, not a based rollup. L2 PoS decides transaction order; Ethereum does not sequence, it only provides DA (blobs), proof verification, settlement and the bridge's anchor.
- **Funds**: L1 recognizes only landed checkpoints, and every landing needs two proofs, at least one of them ZK (6.2). Even the whole committee acting maliciously cannot take funds on L1.
- **Finality**: while Byzantine voting power stays below 1/3, a CometBFT commit is final and consensus never rolls back a committed block; the only exception is governance recovery, which discards blocks above the latest checkpoint (section 8). Nothing at or below a checkpoint can be rolled back except by a DAO upgrade after verifier failure (section 8), so exchanges and bridges should rely on checkpoints.
- **Liveness**: needs more than 2/3 of the voting power online, L1 finalizing, and landings continuing (6.4).
- **Censorship resistance**: forced inclusion is enforced by the proof: a due queue-head entry can only be included or legally skipped, otherwise the block is invalid. Entries cannot be deleted merely because they are old; the only exception is that governance recovery (section 8) can explicitly blank specified entries. Progress depends on consensus, L1 finality, queue service and settlement; this does not provide a wall-clock inclusion deadline (sections 7 and 3.4).
- **Upgrades and trust**: `EtnaInbox`, the registry, the L1 `SignalService` and the L1 Bridge are upgradeable proxies whose owner should be the DAO (behind a timelock); an upgrade can bypass any rule in this spec. Verifier soundness, including the management of SGX instances and RISC0/SP1 images, is a separate assumption. **[MODEL-1-1]** Proof completeness is another: every block that is valid under this spec and within the EL limits (gas, zk gas) and the 3.4 block size bound must be provable by at least one valid proof combination, and **[MODEL-1-2]** each such block must be provable on its own within one landing's two proofs (6.1), counting in the worst case its 7.2 single-transaction build trial (which can use up to another full block's zk budget). A mismatch between the EL and the guests, or a guest resource limit below the EL limits, violates it; step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) can blank such a transaction while it is in the forced inclusion queue, but blanking does not stop the same bytes from being enqueued again (section 12 item 20). **[MODEL-1-3]** `land`, `saveForcedInclusion` and the registry are not affected by pause; if both proof systems fail at once, losses can only be contained by pausing `SignalService` and the Bridge and by QuotaManager limits, and settlement is rolled back with the Inbox upgrade that replaces the verifier (section 8).
- **Out of scope**: PoS issuance rewards and bootstrapping CometBFT from L1 alone.
- **Slashing scope**: same-round double signing forfeits all remaining principal (4.1). Going offline or withholding signatures is not automatically slashed; the DAO may attribute responsibility and apply a one-time fixed-rate forfeiture with forced exit under 4.4. A recovery exclusion itself only forces exit (section 8 step 2).
- **DAO forfeiture authority**: the DAO may forfeit during normal operation or recovery, including immediate execution through its emergency path. The registry adds no waiting, freeze or appeal period and does not distinguish governance paths. It checks authority, entry identity, amount and authorization expiry, not the correctness of attribution. Section 1's upgrade trust remains.

## 3. Consensus and blocks

### 3.1 Consensus engine

- **[CONS-3.1-1]** Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 ([source commit `0880b4d378f347ab16e54ec677ff50d803f37d62`](https://github.com/cometbft/cometbft/tree/0880b4d378f347ab16e54ec677ff50d803f37d62)) runs in its own process and drives the application over an ABCI++ socket. The deployment manifest pins the binary digest for each supported platform, source commit, build flags and configuration. Check the digest against the installed executable; a reported version string alone does not identify the build. **[CONS-3.1-2]** Proposer selection, timeouts, locking and BlockID all use stock behavior; the engine is not forked.

<details>
<summary>Details: secp256k1eth signing format and chain_id construction</summary>

- **[CONS-3.1-3]** Consensus keys use CometBFT v0.40.0's `secp256k1eth`: sign bytes are hashed with keccak256 (no prefix) and signed; the signature is 65 bytes `R‖S‖V` (`V ∈ {0, 1}`, low-s required); the public key is a 33-byte compressed SEC1 key; and the validator address is the Ethereum address `keccak256(uncompressed key without its first byte)[12:]`. L1 can therefore verify any vote directly with `ecrecover` (double-sign slashing in 4.1). CometBFT does not batch-verify these keys. Every ordinary and recovery-initial committee uses voting power = ⌊effStake / `vp_unit`⌋ under 4.3, and the total is at most CometBFT's cap `(2^63 − 1) / 8`.
- **[CONS-3.1-4]** `chain_id = "etna-" + base64url_no_padding(chainDomain)` is exactly 48 ASCII bytes. `chainDomain` is the full 256-bit keccak256 digest binding the version, complete L1/L2 chain ids, Inbox proxy address and generation (appendix A), without truncation. Nodes, signers, guests and the registry recompute the same value. **[CONS-3.1-5]** Old-generation votes cannot authorize a new generation; another deployment's votes cannot authorize a commit or slashing here. Signing the same height in different domains is not a double sign. Stock CometBFT VoteSignBytes and vote encoding remain unchanged.

</details>

- **[CONS-3.1-6]** The mempool is `nop`: user transactions travel over EL devp2p, and the proposer's EL picks them from its own txpool.
- **[CONS-3.1-7]** CometBFT height equals the EL block number; consensus starts at S + 1, the block after the Etna start S (section 10). Each CometBFT block's `Data.Txs` holds exactly one item: the envelope (5.1).

<details>
<summary>Details: ABCI response constraints</summary>

- **[CONS-3.1-8]** ABCI responses: `app_hash` is appendix A's appHash, which commits the hash of the executed EL block and the validator state CometBFT holds after committing it, as the app computes that state under SYNC-9-5c (InitChain returns the appHash of the Etna start's hash (the genesis hash when S = 0) for the initial deployment and of ELHash(c) for governance recovery under 8.4, each with the validator state CometBFT builds from that generation's genesis); every transaction result (ExecTxResult) is fixed at code 0, empty data, and gas_wanted and gas_used of 0, all four of which enter LastResultsHash; **[CONS-3.1-9]** Info's `app_version` is always the app version in this generation's genesis consensus params (CometBFT reads it only while its own state holds no committed block); the header's app version is constant within a generation, and the ruleset that applies to each block comes from the 10.1 schedule, not from Info; consensus params are never updated. Info's `last_block_height` never exceeds the last height the app holds as committed, which is the height of its last completed Commit or, right after a 5.4 import at a checkpoint above the generation's start height, the boundary s; `last_block_app_hash` is the `app_hash` of the height reported, and the app answers only once its own state and the EL agree at that height. Until the app holds a block of this generation as committed the height is 0, so that CometBFT sends InitChain; it does so on every start that finds the height 0, so InitChain returns the same `app_hash` each time, even if the EL already holds the generation's first block. The bound exists because CometBFT v0.40.0 stores a block's FinalizeBlock response before it calls Commit: for a height the app has only finalized, its startup handshake either fails, having no stored response, or replays the block from the stored response without calling the app's Commit. Reporting the committed height instead makes CometBFT, after a crash between the two, call FinalizeBlock for the next height again, and that call must return the same result even if the EL already holds the block. A generation's first block is not covered when initial_height is above 1: CometBFT's own state stays at height 0 until that block's state is saved, so after a crash between saving that block and saving its state, stock CometBFT v0.40.0 stops at its handshake whatever the app reports. Fence signing, preserve the original block store (including the seen commit), state store, consensus WAL, app/EL stores and external signer state. Recover the exact decided block and its >2/3 commit for the full BlockID from these records or a peer, verifying them against the fixed genesis, chain_id and first committee. A single affected node may catch up with signing disabled from that genesis or an authenticated 5.4 checkpoint on the decided chain. If the committee is affected together, retain the same certified block and make it available for every node to apply or verify on its recovered chain. A deployment must rehearse a recovery tool or procedure that installs an aligned, verified Comet/app/EL boundary for that block, or a later authenticated boundary proven to descend from it, and resumes stock operation. While the block, certificate or aligned state is unavailable keep affected signers fenced; never elect a replacement block for the same height. Rebuilt stores follow RECOV-8.2-2 before any signing. This specification uses `APP_VERSION` 1 (encoding versions in appendix A); checkpoint-trust metadata is node-local and does not enter `app_hash` or block bytes.

</details>

- The block interval is set by `timeout_commit`, an operating parameter rather than a validity rule (target about 4 s, assuming n ≲ 128).
- **[CONS-3.1-10]** Every Comet block has an empty evidence list. The stock proposer selects zero evidence bytes and stock block validation rejects a nonempty list before checking individual items, regardless of the local pending pool (START-10-21). The stock evidence reactor, pool and vote gossip stay, because CometBFT v0.40.0 cannot run without them, but nothing in this protocol reads the pool. No block commits a pending item, so only expiry removes one, and the stock pool limits neither how many valid items it holds nor how long they live while no block commits (section 12, item 22). The supervisor therefore deletes the node's pending-evidence store, with CometBFT stopped, every time it starts CometBFT, and START-10-21 keeps the age limits short. Anyone may collect conflicting signed votes and submit them directly to the registry; double-sign slashing happens only on L1 (`slashDoubleSign` in 4.1).

<details>
<summary>Details: driving the EL, zk-gas metering and execution fees</summary>

- **[CONS-3.1-11]** The EL is a pinned alethia-reth version with Etna support: no anchor transaction, building from a given transaction list, the base-fee sharing percentage read from `extraData[0]`, and zk gas recorded in `difficulty`. EIP-4396 parameters are published with that pinned EL version. The application drives it with `engine_forkchoiceUpdatedV3`, `engine_getPayloadV5` and `engine_newPayloadV4`. What those calls carry beyond the stock Engine API types (the header's `difficulty`, the given transaction list and the build's byte limit of 3.4) uses the pinned EL's extensions, which are published with that version too.
- **[CONS-3.1-12]** **zk gas**: adopt the [ZK Gas spec](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/main/packages/protocol/docs/zk_gas_spec.md) ([pinned revision](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/ce31a49063b83845691dd22f536f5433b7824b35/packages/protocol/docs/zk_gas_spec.md)) for metering, transaction intrinsic cost, opcode/precompile multipliers, CALL/CREATE spawn estimates, uint64 overflow handling and whole-transaction discard on overflow/limit exceedance. Its rule that the remaining transactions are skipped after a limit exceedance or overflow is not a consensus rule here: how building continues is under “Execution and fees” below, and validation of committed blocks is 3.4 rule 9. Its `BLOCK_ZK_GAS_LIMIT` corresponds to this spec’s `L2_BLOCK_ZK_GAS_LIMIT`, a block budget independent of EVM gas. Publish the enabled revision, parameters and shared vectors with the pinned EL/guest versions; later main-branch changes do not automatically change consensus. The document’s 100M is a placeholder: pin the deployment value under section 11 and pass the acceptance in [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md).
- **[CONS-3.1-13]** **Execution and fees**: while building a candidate transaction list, a transaction exceeding the zk budget or overflowing metering arithmetic is fully discarded under the shared rules, restoring the state at that transaction’s start. Rollback includes nonce, authorizations, value transfers, logs, receipts and fees; discarded and unselected transactions are absent from the final block and pay nothing. Whether building then tries further candidates is proposer build policy, not a consensus rule; the recommended policy skips that transaction and its sender’s later nonces, keeps selecting other transactions, and bounds the total work spent on overflowing trials, first-time and repeated. **[CONS-3.1-14]** This policy applies only to pool candidates; an FI transaction the proposer places at position 0 is executed against the actual pre-block state and is never skipped because of remembered overflows. The given-list singleton build (7.2) has only `[tx]` as a candidate, so its result does not depend on this. **[CONS-3.1-15]** Validation or import never discards a transaction listed in a committed block: if any listed transaction exceeds the zk limit or overflows, the whole block is invalid (3.4 rule 9). The 7.2 skip check still runs the singleton build above, which may discard `tx`. **[CONS-3.1-16]** An ordinary EVM revert/OOG remains an included transaction when it fits the zk budget and is charged normally. Use ordinary EVM gas, refunds, base fees and tips without converting zk gas into charged gas. Block gasUsed is the sum of included transactions’ EVM gasUsed, and EIP-4396 uses that value. **[CONS-3.1-17]** `eth_call`/`eth_estimateGas` use the same ruleset with the full block zk budget for standalone simulation; report explicit zk-limit errors rather than a successful empty result after discarding the call.

</details>

**[CONS-3.2-1]** Every field except `beneficiary` is determined by the committed parent (and its parent's timestamp), the block's anchor, BFT time and chain constants. The proposer builds with these values; other nodes recompute them and compare field by field.

| Field                                    | Value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[CONS-3.2-2]** `number` / `parentHash` | height / hash of the committed parent                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **[CONS-3.2-3]** `timestamp`             | max(parent.timestamp + 1, ⌊BFT time⌋, anchor.timestamp)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **[CONS-3.2-4]** `beneficiary`           | any fee recipient the proposer chooses, as in Ethereum; not checked. All of the block's base fees and tips are paid to it at L2 execution, independent of L1 settlement, so a proposer's own transactions in its blocks pay no net fee                                                                                                                                |
| **[CONS-3.2-5]** `extraData`             | 13 bytes `[100 \| generation (uint48) \| anchor block number (uint48)]`; the first byte is the base-fee sharing percentage and is always 100, so all base fees go to the beneficiary                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **[CONS-3.2-6]** `parentBeaconBlockRoot` | the anchor's L1 stateRoot. stateRoot, receipts, gasUsed (the sum of included transactions’ EVM gasUsed) and the zk gas in `difficulty` are checked by the EL's `newPayload`. **[CONS-3.2-11]** `difficulty` accumulates only included transactions’ zk gas, including ordinary EVM revert/OOG transactions, not discarded over-limit trials or unselected transactions. **[CONS-3.2-12]** Block-start system processing still follows 3.2 and is not rolled back with an aborted transaction.

### 3.3 Anchor

- **[CONS-3.3-1]** Every block has an anchor: the L1 block whose state it reads. A block either carries an anchor witness (the raw L1 header plus the Inbox account and storage proofs) or inherits its parent's anchor. **[CONS-3.3-2]** The first block of each generation and any block that moves the anchor must carry one; a block that keeps the anchor must not. **[CONS-3.3-3a]** The witness proves the Inbox's `recoveryGeneration`, `lastCheckpoint` (height, hash), the forced inclusion queue's head and tail, and the rules schedule (10.1).
- **[CONS-3.3-4]** The proposer picks max(parent's anchor, local L1 `finalized`); a generation's first block carries a new anchor witness (CONS-3.3-2) taken from the local L1 `finalized`, because its parent is an Unzen block or a block of an older generation, or, on a new network, the genesis block, whose anchor `L1_0` is the generation's L1 origin anyway. A retained proposal keeps its original anchor. **[CONS-3.3-5]** In ProcessProposal, a validator checks with its own L1 node that the anchor header is canonical and finalized, rejecting without waiting an anchor that node has not finalized yet (RECOV-8-5), and checks its age against the candidate block's timestamp (3.4); advancing the local finalized head alone cannot make that proposal expire.

<details>
<summary>Details: decoding L1 headers</summary>

- **[CONS-3.3-6]** **Decoding L1 headers**: `rawL1Header` must be an RLP list of at most 4,096 bytes with at least 12 items. Nodes and guests read only four fields, by position: 0 `parentHash` and 3 `stateRoot` (32 bytes each), and 8 `number` and 11 `timestamp` (integers of at most 8 bytes without leading zeros). Every other item need only be a well-formed RLP item and is not interpreted, so fields an L1 fork appends at the end (for example Glamsterdam's EIP-7928 `block_access_list_hash`) are accepted without an upgrade. **[CONS-3.3-7]** A header's identity is always keccak256(rawL1Header) over the raw bytes; it is never decoded and re-encoded. A node fetches the canonical hash for the anchor block number in `extraData` from its own L1 node and compares it with keccak256(rawL1Header): if they differ, the witness is invalid; if they match but the header cannot be parsed under this rule, L1 has changed the header format and the node needs an upgrade, which is a liveness pause (section 8), not a safety halt. When a node reads any other header from its own L1 node (trust checks, cold sync), it likewise relies only on these four fields and ignores unknown ones.

</details>

### 3.4 Block validity

**[CONS-3.4-1]** ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock apply the same deterministic rules, in order:

1. **[CONS-3.4-2]** height = parent + 1, and `parentHash` links to the parent.
2. **[CONS-3.4-3]** The presence or absence of an anchor witness follows 3.3, and the witness verifies.
3. **[CONS-3.4-4]** The anchor does not regress: it is at least the parent's anchor (for a generation's first block, only the L1 origin applies) and the current generation's L1 origin, the l1BlockNumber of its fixed origin record (8.4): `L1_0` for generation 0 and R after a governance recovery.
4. **[CONS-3.4-5]** Generation: recompute `chain_id` per appendix A from `extraData`'s uint48 generation and this deployment's parameters; it must exactly match the current chain and CometBFT header. Do not parse a generation from the digest string. If the Inbox's generation is larger, this chain has been superseded (section 8); if it is smaller, reject the block.
5. **[CONS-3.4-6]** Back-pressure: height − the `lastCheckpoint.height` proven by the anchor ≤ U, so at most U blocks are unsettled. U is the unsettled-block limit committed directly in the config hash; see 6.4 for its constraints.
6. **[CONS-3.4-7]** Committee witness: required at switch block S(t), forbidden elsewhere; its registry proofs are verified under the stateRoot of S(t)'s own anchor (4.3). Recovery establishes its authenticated starting committee under section 8 without re-emitting an old pending update. If the first recovered block is S(t), normal switch derivation still applies.
7. **[CONS-3.4-8]** The header matches 3.2 field by field; anchor freshness: timestamp − anchor.timestamp ≤ `maxAnchorAge`.
8. **[CONS-3.4-9]** The forced inclusion rules (section 7).
9. **[CONS-3.4-10]** The EL's `newPayload` returns VALID. Discarding transactions for zk-limit exceedance/overflow applies to candidate-list construction only (3.1): the final EL body, envelope and blob payload contain exactly the retained transactions, with matching transaction root, receipts, stateRoot, gasUsed and difficulty. **[CONS-3.4-11]** Validation must execute every transaction listed in a committed block; any listed transaction triggering zk-limit exceedance/overflow invalidates the whole block. Never silently drop or truncate transactions of a Comet-signed block and return VALID.

**[CONS-3.4-11a]** These are the rules of the block's ruleset, which 10.1 selects from the anchor that rules 2 and 3 verify. 10.1's planned stop does not depend on the ruleset and is checked with them: a block that violates it is invalid. If the node does not implement the block's ruleset, 10.1's refusal applies instead, a liveness pause (section 8).

**[CONS-3.4-12]** Freshness is determined only by the candidate and the committed parent: the application does not reject a proposal merely because the node's finalized L1 head advances, since otherwise a locked block re-proposed unchanged in a later round might never gather more than 2/3 of the voting power. PBTS is not enabled. BFT time's relationship to real time requires Byzantine voting power below 1/3, reasonably synchronized honest clocks and continued consensus progress; a coalition above that threshold can manipulate block time. Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 also judges a block invalid when its time is not earlier than the node's own clock plus its `block_time_tolerance` setting (START-10-6): the node prevotes nil for such a proposal, and if more than 2/3 of the voting power prevotes for the block or decides it all the same, the node's consensus stops until it is restarted after its clock has passed that point, while block sync stalls until then. A block dated that far ahead of honest clocks has this effect on honest nodes, and so has an ordinary block on a node whose own clock runs that far behind, which resumes after its clock is corrected and it is restarted; neither is evidence of a block that is invalid under the rules above.

The following rules describe proposal admission and liveness:

- **[CONS-3.4-13]** **Block size bound**: the envelope's block item (the payload's executionBlock, including its RLP list header, with every transaction, legacy included, counted as an RLP byte string of its EIP-2718 encoding) is at most 124,000 bytes long. The rule looks only at the uncompressed length, so every implementation gets the same answer; ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock both check it, and the proposer must respect it while building: alethia's build interface takes a byte limit, measured the same way, that it enforces together with gas when selecting transactions, and always keeps the FI transaction at position 0. The bound guarantees that each block fits in one blob on its own, so a landing of a single block (6.1) always fits in one transaction, and a six-blob landing holds six maximum-size blocks. **[CONS-3.4-14]** If more than 2/3 of malicious voting power decides a block that violates this rule, honest nodes halt for safety in FinalizeBlock, and only governance recovery can fix that.

<details>
<summary>Details: where the 124,000-byte bound comes from, and single-blob packing</summary>

The bound leaves headroom within the 130,044 bytes one blob carries: compressing one maximum-size block's payload list with standard zlib at default settings yields at most 124,055 bytes including the 0x06 prefix, even for incompressible data (zlib's deflateBound), so every decided block fits in one blob on its own. Six maximum-size blocks compress to at most 744,244 bytes, within six blobs (780,264 bytes), so up to six blocks always fit in one six-blob landing; no special compression mode is needed.

</details>

- **[CONS-3.4-15]** **No usable anchor, no block**: if no available finalized anchor satisfies the deterministic age bound against the candidate block timestamp, L2 pauses until one is usable. This is not an independent wall-clock timer; its timing inherits the BFT-time assumptions above.
- **[CONS-3.4-16]** **Reject a latest checkpoint conflicting with local history**: when the anchor changes, nodes check the anchor's proven `lastCheckpoint`: its height is at most the parent height, and its hash equals the EL block hash at that height on the authenticated local chain. A proposal that fails either condition is rejected, and the node raises an alert. What follows for the node itself differs, because the checkpoint an anchor proves is in the node's own finalized L1 view (SYNC-9-6a). A checkpoint above the proposal's parent is one the node's chain has not reached: the node signs nothing until it has reached that height and compared, and a match restores signing without an operator. A hash that differs at a height the node holds contradicts finalized L1: the node revokes its trust and does not keep producing on an older anchor. Guests verify only the witness's storage proofs; nodes do the comparison with the local chain. This check constrains block ancestry only and does not authenticate other landing metadata (FI head, snapshot, stateRoot and so on); off-chain monitors check that metadata without being claimed to prevent bridge or registry payments.

## 4. Staking and committees

### 4.1 Registry

- **[STAKE-4.1-1]** register(bytes publicKey,amount,popSig): amount ≥ minStake. **[STAKE-4.1-2]** Activation occurs after activationDelay L1 blocks; registry records msg.sender as owner. **[STAKE-4.1-3]** A successful registration creates an unwithdrawn bond with effStake = amount, `exitConfirmedAtL1 = 0`, `pendingTopUp = 0` and `topUpReadyAtL1 = 0`, inheriting no confirmation, withdrawal or top-up state from an earlier call or slot occupant. exitEffectiveL1 starts at 2\^48 − 1, meaning no exit is set; requestExit reverts unless its computed effective block is below that value.
  - **[STAKE-4.1-4]** Each key can be registered only once, ever, keyed by `ckAddr` and never released after withdrawal; the registry maps each used `ckAddr` to its bond id. The key lookup returns `(registered, bondId)`: an unused key returns `(false, 0)`, whereas `(true, 0)` identifies bond 0. The registration flag is never cleared, including on withdrawal. **[STAKE-4.1-5]** The registry stores only `ckPrefix = publicKey[0]` and the following 32-byte `ckX`, not the publicKey itself; `ckY` is derived internally.

<details>
<summary>Details: public-key format, decompression and PoP checks</summary>

- **[STAKE-4.1-6]** `publicKey` is exactly 33 bytes of canonical SEC1 compressed secp256k1 encoding: `ckPrefix ‖ ckX`, with prefix 0x02 or 0x03 and a 32-byte big-endian X retaining leading zeros. Registration tools may accept the user’s existing hex/Base64 public key and convert it to those 33 raw bytes; users do not supply or split X and Y. **[STAKE-4.1-7]** The contract rejects textual encodings, 64/65-byte uncompressed keys, hybrid encodings and trailing bytes.
- **[STAKE-4.1-8]** The registry decompresses and validates the public key internally under appendix A: require X \< p without reducing X modulo p, a finite valid secp256k1 point, Y parity matching the prefix, and byte-for-byte equality when recompressed. **[STAKE-4.1-9]** Any failure rejects the entire registration without creating an entry or consuming the key identity.
- **[STAKE-4.1-10]** The registry computes `ckAddr = keccak256(ckX ‖ ckY)[12:]` from the two decoded 32-byte coordinates, not by hashing the compressed encoding. **[STAKE-4.1-11]** Require `popSig` to be exactly 65 bytes with `V ∈ {0,1}`, `0 < r < n` and `0 < s ≤ n/2`; recovery over appendix A’s registration digest must return a nonzero address equal to ckAddr. **[STAKE-4.1-12]** The digest still binds the L1 chain id, registry and `msg.sender`. The signature must match this supplied public key, not merely recover some valid address. Copying the transaction cannot change the owner.

</details>

- **[STAKE-4.1-13a]** There are 4096 slots, numbered 0 to 4095. `register` returns a new bond id, counting up from 0 and never reused. An id not issued by a successful registration denotes no bond. Every state-changing registry operation taking an unissued id reverts without writing any state, even if default storage would otherwise satisfy its conditions; a recovery exclusion naming an unissued id rejects the whole operation. Read-only lookups retain the absence results in appendix A. Issuance is permanent: a withdrawn bond remains issued, and a reverted registration issues no id. A bond holds a slot from registration until it is withdrawn; only bonds that hold a slot appear in `entries` and entriesRoot, with regLeaf indexed by the slot number, and only they can be selected under 4.3. The witness also represents empty slots below the checkpoint count as specified in 4.3. Every rule in 4.1, 4.4 and section 8 that names a bond or its entry applies whether or not the bond holds a slot, and an unreleased bond is an issued bond that has not been withdrawn. The registry exposes each slot holder as `(occupied, bondId)` and each bond's slot as `(holdsSlot, slot)`. Absence is `(false, 0)`; `(true, 0)` identifies bond 0 or slot 0, respectively. Withdrawal clears the bond's `holdsSlot` flag and leaves its former slot unoccupied; it does not clear the bond's lifetime key registration. Numeric zero alone must never mean absence in these lookups. **[STAKE-4.1-14a]** **Admission**: `register` takes the empty slot with the lowest number and never moves or removes another bond. Require amount ≥ minStake and an empty slot; otherwise the registration is rejected without creating a bond or consuming the key identity. Deployment requires 4096 × minStake > 10\^27 base units, TAIKO's fixed total supply (4.3), so bonds that still hold the stake they registered with can never hold every slot; section 12, item 11 states what holding every slot would take.
- **[STAKE-4.1-15]** **Top-ups**: `topUp(bondId, amount)` is owner-only. Require an unreleased entry, current L1 block ≥ activeFromL1, effStake > 0, no exit set, no pending top-up, and amount > 0. **[STAKE-4.1-16]** Transfer the TAIKO into the registry and record `pendingTopUp = amount` and `topUpReadyAtL1 = block.number + activationDelay`, rejecting overflow. Only one top-up may be pending per entry. It uses no new slot and changes neither owner, key nor activeFromL1; **[STAKE-4.1-17]** pending funds are not effStake, do not enter regLeaf/entriesRoot, and provide no voting power or contribution to S_MIN_TOTAL.
- **[STAKE-4.1-18]** **Top-up activation**: anyone may call `activateTopUp(bondId)` once the ready block is reached, provided a top-up is pending and no exit is set. Move all pending funds into effStake, clear the pending amount and ready block, and write the registry checkpoint. **[STAKE-4.1-19]** Maturity alone does not activate funds; activation does not rewrite historical snapshots or already-derived committees.
- **[STAKE-4.1-20]** **Top-up liability**: once exit is set, no new top-up or activation is allowed. Pending funds cannot be withdrawn separately; they leave only through the entry's normal confirmed-exit and withdrawal process. **[STAKE-4.1-21]** All principal in an entry backs its key, including still-admissible double-sign evidence from before the top-up and responsibility forfeiture under 4.4. `balance = effStake + pendingTopUp` below. **[STAKE-4.1-22]** Top-up and activation are allowed before Inbox initialization subject to these same checks.
- **[STAKE-4.1-23]** `requestExit(bondId)`: callable only by the entry's owner. The exit takes effect at max(current block + `exitDelay`, activeFromL1 + `exitDelay`). **[STAKE-4.1-24]** It may be called only for an entry with no exit set; once set, `exitEffectiveL1` only moves earlier (slashing, forfeiture, exclusion list), never later.
- **[STAKE-4.1-25]** **Exit confirmation and withdrawal**: `exitEffectiveL1` determines selection eligibility, not the start of the evidence clock. **[STAKE-4.1-26]** Anyone may call `confirmExit(bondId)`: the bond must have been issued, the entry must have an exit set (requestExit, slashing, forfeiture or exclusion list) and must not be withdrawn, and the Inbox must have `activeCommitteeSnapshot ≥ exitEffectiveL1`. The first successful call records the current L1 block as `exitConfirmedAtL1`. Repeated calls are idempotent and must not postpone the clock.
- **[STAKE-4.1-27]** `withdraw(bondId, to)`: requires an issued, unwithdrawn bond with a confirmed exit and the current block number ≥ `exitConfirmedAtL1 + withdrawalDelay`. When `balance > 0`, only the owner may call it, and all remaining effStake and pendingTopUp are paid to `to`; when `balance = 0`, anyone may call it, with no payment. **[STAKE-4.1-28]** Withdrawal zeros the bond's balance and its slot's entry, leaving the slot empty; it clears the bond's owner, confirmation, pending amount and top-up ready block, and records the bond as withdrawn. It keeps the bond's id and issued status, key, lifetime `ckAddr` registration, `exitEffectiveL1` and lifetime forfeiture flag keyed by bond id (4.4), so later recovery exclusions can still authenticate the bond and preserve its earlier exit. Confirmation requires that a landed committee no longer includes the stake, so a full evidence window starts only after that; a prolonged halt cannot consume it in advance. **[STAKE-4.1-29]** `activeCommitteeSnapshot` never decreases (the only exception, the section 8 verifier-failure rollback, also re-checks every exit confirmation against the restored snapshot), so withdrawal does not re-check it.
- **[STAKE-4.1-30]** The owner cannot be changed after registration; an operator that may need to change custody should register from a contract (such as a multisig), which then becomes the owner. Consensus keys cannot be rotated in place: to change keys, request an exit, withdraw, and register again with the new key. If a key leaks, the owner should request an exit at once; until the exit completes, the leaked key can still be used to double-sign and get the entry slashed.
- **[STAKE-4.1-31]** `slashDoubleSign(bondId, voteA, voteB)`: callable by anyone. Each vote gives its type, height, round, block id (hash and part set header; all zero for nil), timestamp (the vote's own int64 seconds and int32 nanoseconds, so that a vote timestamped before 1970 is covered; appendix A), uint48 generation and 65-byte signature. The caller cannot choose an Inbox, chain id or deployment tag.

<details>
<summary>Details: double-sign evidence checks and slashing</summary>

- **[STAKE-4.1-32]** Both votes have the same type, prevote (1) or precommit (2), and the same height, round and generation, with different block ids. The generation must not exceed the Inbox's current recoveryGeneration. Valid evidence from historical generations remains admissible after recovery; future generations and other deployments are rejected.
- **[STAKE-4.1-33]** Recompute chainDomain, chain_id and both sign-byte strings per appendix A from this deployment’s full L1/L2 chain ids, the Inbox proxy fixed in the registry and the votes’ shared generation. Both `ecrecover(keccak256(signBytes))` results must be the same nonzero address, and the lifetime key lookup of that address (STAKE-4.1-4) must return exactly `(true, bondId)`. Registration wrote that mapping from the decompressed key and nothing releases it, so this compares both signers with the bond’s own key without decompressing it again. Callers supply neither coordinates nor a public key, and vote input cannot override these deployment values.
- **[STAKE-4.1-34]** The entry must not be withdrawn and must have `balance > 0`. On success the whole principal is slashed: effStake, pendingTopUp and the top-up ready block become zero and `exitEffectiveL1` becomes min(its old value, the current block); the caller receives `bounty = floor(slashed * slashBountyBps / 10000)`, where `slashed` is the whole slashed principal, and the remaining `slashed - bounty` goes to `slashSink`. Self-reporting is allowed: the owner or signer may control the caller and receive the same bounty.
- **[STAKE-4.1-35]** Membership in the committee of the time need not be proven: only the key holder can produce the signatures, and since `chain_id` carries the deployment tag and the generation, re-signing a lower height after a redeployment or governance recovery is not a double sign.

</details>

- **[STAKE-4.1-36]** Every L1 block that changes the entry in a slot ends with one checkpoint (l1Block, count, entriesRoot) reflecting the final state of all slots in that block. Registration, withdrawal, top-up activation, setting an exit, slashing and forfeiture update that checkpoint whenever they change a slot's entry fields. A pending deposit, exit confirmation or exclusion that leaves every slot entry unchanged writes no checkpoint. At each checkpoint, count is zero if every slot is empty and otherwise one plus the highest occupied slot number, including occupied slots with zero balance. Count can shrink after withdrawal. Earlier checkpoints retain their own count and root across withdrawal and slot reuse.
- **[STAKE-4.1-37]** **Inbox address**: the registry fixes the Inbox proxy address at deployment, and it cannot be changed. Inbox initialization checks that the registry's fixed address equals the Inbox's own address and that the registry's `l2ChainId` matches its own. **[STAKE-4.1-38]** Until the Inbox is initialized (the registry checks the Inbox's initialization state), confirmExit, slashDoubleSign and forfeit fail, so no withdrawal is possible; registration, topUp and activateTopUp are unaffected. **[STAKE-4.1-39]** The Inbox can also set the `exitEffectiveL1` of listed entries earlier, to `recoverySnapshot`, during an exclusion-list recovery (section 8 step 2).

### 4.2 Epochs

- **[STAKE-4.2-1]** L = `epochLenL2`, the number of L2 blocks per epoch, and `L1_0` is the L1 block of the start write (START-10-5b): the initialization block on a new network and the `setOrigin` block on a switching network. L is fixed at initialization and `L1_0` at the start write.
- **[STAKE-4.2-2]** Epoch t starts at block `H(t) = 1 + t·L`; block h belongs to epoch ⌊(h − 1) / L⌋.
- **[STAKE-4.2-3]** Epochs are defined by L2 height alone and are not tied to L1 progress; only `maxAnchorAge` (3.4) bounds how old an anchor may be.

### 4.3 Committee derivation

- **[COMM-4.3-1]** **One selection rule**: committee 0, ordinary rotation and governance recovery all apply the following rule to their authenticated registry snapshot, independently of previous voting powers or participation history.
  1. **[COMM-4.3-2]** Verify the count and root of the latest registry checkpoint at that snapshot. Require count ≤ 4096 and exactly count entries in ascending slot order, starting at slot 0. An interior empty slot has the all-zero RegistryEntry encoding. A nonempty list ends in an occupied slot. Recompute entriesRoot under appendix A and require equality with the checkpoint.
  2. **[COMM-4.3-3]** An eligible entry satisfies activeFromL1 ≤ snapshot \< exitEffectiveL1 and effStake ≥ minStake. The same registry minimum governs registration and selection. START-10-12 requires minStake ≥ vp_unit, so an eligible entry has at least one unit of power. **[COMM-4.3-4]** Consensus keys are lifetime-unique; a state in which two eligible entries share a key is invalid, not extra voting power. **[COMM-4.3-5]** Empty slots are all zero and ineligible, so they take no part in this check.
  3. **[COMM-4.3-6]** Sort by descending effStake, breaking ties by ascending set key, and take the first n_max entries, or all entries if fewer qualify. A key's set key is a fixed sort value: the keccak256 of the L2 chain id and the key's compressed public key (`ckPrefix ‖ ckX`), with the formula in appendix A. It does not change with slot reuse or registration order, and step 2 makes eligible keys distinct, so the order has no ties. **[COMM-4.3-7]** Each assignedPower = floor(effStake / vp_unit).
  4. **[COMM-4.3-8]** Encode selected members in ascending set-key order; totalPower is the sum of assignedPower. The result must be nonempty, each power positive and representable as u64, and the sum at most MAX = floor((2\^63 − 1) / 8). Otherwise reject the derivation; do not truncate integers or apply node-selected scaling.

<details>
<summary>Details: why power cannot overflow</summary>

- **Power cannot overflow**: TAIKO has a fixed total supply of 10\^27 base units, and deployment requires vp_unit ≥ ⌈10\^27 / MAX⌉ = 867,361,738, so the power of all entries together never exceeds MAX and registration cannot trigger an overflow; the check in step 4 is only a backstop. **[COMM-4.3-8a]** If TAIKO supply ever increases, choose vp_unit for the new supply and deploy a new configuration first.

</details>

- **[COMM-4.3-9]** **Committee 0**: snapshot = genesisCutoff and targetEpoch=0. Inbox stores its record hash, using the same selection and power rule. Committee 0 governs from S + 1 and its targetEpoch is 0 for any S; the start establishes it directly, without a committee witness or pending update, so S + 1 carries a committee witness only if it is itself an S(t) (CONS-3.4-7); when S > 0, the next S(t) that actually occurs above S performs ordinary rotation, as after governance recovery (COMM-4.3-15).

<details>
<summary>Ordinary rotation: snapshot, proof root and H+2 activation (details)</summary>

- **[COMM-4.3-10]** **Ordinary rotation**: S(t)=H(t)−2 and D(t)=H(t)−3. **[COMM-4.3-11]** The snapshot is max(the committed anchor number of D(t), l1BlockNumber in the current generation's fixed origin record), or that l1BlockNumber alone when D(t) ≤ S, since D(t) is then not an Etna block; the candidate block's new anchor does not select the committee. **[COMM-4.3-12]** Always verify the registry account/storage proofs under the stateRoot of S(t)'s own anchor, newly carried or inherited, i.e. the parentBeaconBlockRoot in S(t)'s header; that root is never earlier than the snapshot. The proof must show the latest registry checkpoint at the selected snapshot. Do not use D(t)'s or any other anchor's root, or the proof bytes would not be unique. The proposer of a fresh D(t) follows CONS-3.3-4. Validators check finality, non-regression and freshness but cannot verify the proposer's local finalized head. If two admissible D(t) anchors straddle an activation or exit, the choice at D(t) can affect membership and power at H(t). Once D(t) commits, S(t) cannot change that choice. Normally the snapshot is D(t)'s anchor; after recovery D(t) may belong to the old generation, so max prevents regression behind the recovery snapshot. **[COMM-4.3-13]** FinalizeBlock at S(t) returns updates effective at H(t), following CometBFT's H+2 rule; S(t) and S(t)+1 remain signed by the original active committee. The updates are the difference from the set that signs S(t)+1, because stock CometBFT applies them to that set; with L ≥ 3 (START-10-7) it is also the set in force at S(t): a member that leaves is returned with power 0, a new member or one whose power changes is returned with its new power, and a member whose key and power stay the same is not returned. Record.targetEpoch=t.

</details>

- **[COMM-4.3-14]** **Governance recovery**: apply the same rule at the authenticated execution block R in section 8, including all candidates eligible there. **[COMM-4.3-15]** Set targetEpoch=floor(c/L), effective immediately at c+1. Discard every pending update from the old generation; the initial set depends only on R's snapshot, not on any earlier committee's members or power. The next S(t) actually occurring in the new generation performs ordinary rotation; this also applies if the recovery's first block is S(t).
- **[COMM-4.3-16]** **Guarantees and limits**: authenticated L1 state uniquely determines membership and power. There is no per-rotation power-change limit, automatic offline eviction, or wall-clock takeover warning. **[COMM-4.3-17]** `activationDelay` delays new registrations and top-up increments; already active stake and standby candidates need not wait again. Only activated increments included in the selection snapshot affect power. Each actual committee remains subject to section 1's voting-power assumptions; the DAO monitors availability and concentration.

### 4.4 DAO responsibility forfeiture

- **[FORF-4.4-1]** **Authority and identity**: `forfeit(bondId, incidentId, validUntil)` is callable only by the registry owner, the DAO's actual execution address (the Controller where used), and only after Inbox initialization. **[FORF-4.4-2]** The target is its permanently issued bondId in this registry (STAKE-4.1-13a). Before authorizing, the DAO verifies the intended bond's issuance and registered key against finalized L1 state. **[FORF-4.4-3]** The DAO authorization binds the deployment, full call parameters and published attribution. incidentId identifies that attribution; the same incident may name multiple entries. The registry keeps no incident case or global incident-id deduplication state.
- **[FORF-4.4-4]** **Execution checks**: require an issued, unwithdrawn bond, `forfeited[bondId] = false`, `block.timestamp <= validUntil`, and `balance = effStake + pendingTopUp > 0`. **[FORF-4.4-5]** Requested or confirmed exits remain punishable until withdrawal; current committee membership is not required. **[FORF-4.4-6]** validUntil is an absolute L1 Unix timestamp in seconds fixed in the authorization.
- **[FORF-4.4-7]** **Immediate, fixed-rate deduction**: compute `penalty = ceil(balance * forfeitBps / 10000)` from the remaining principal at execution. The deployment fixes `0 < forfeitBps <= 10000`. **[FORF-4.4-8]** Deduct from effStake first and then pendingTopUp; clear the ready block if pending reaches zero. **[FORF-4.4-9]** Send the full penalty to slashSink, with no bounty. **[FORF-4.4-10]** Pending funds and deposits made after authorization but before execution are included: all funds in the entry back its key, without historical-balance accounting.
- **[FORF-4.4-11]** **Once per entry, with exit**: in the same transaction set `forfeited[bondId] = true` and `exitEffectiveL1 = min(existing, block.number)`, writing the registry checkpoint for changed entry fields when the bond holds a slot (STAKE-4.1-36). **[FORF-4.4-12]** The flag starts false and is never reset by withdrawal, slot reuse, recovery or verifier-failure rollback. **[FORF-4.4-13]** Changes affect subsequent selection snapshots that include them, not historical snapshots or already-derived committees. **[FORF-4.4-14]** Remaining principal follows normal withdrawal rules; an existing exit confirmation is not reset or delayed. **[FORF-4.4-15]** Valid double-sign evidence may still take all remaining principal; if double signing emptied the entry first, forfeit fails on zero balance without blocking normal withdrawal.
- **[FORF-4.4-16]** **Authorization expiry and replay**: there is no forfeiture nonce, revocation entry point, queue, hold or extra execution delay. Successful execution immediately completes the forfeiture; the lifetime flag prevents repetition. The authorized id always denotes the same bond and registered key. **[FORF-4.4-17]** An unexecuted authorization remains executable until validUntil if all checks pass, even if the DAO changes its decision; this interface cannot cancel it. Expired calls revert without cleanup. **[FORF-4.4-18]** Landing, a generation change and recover do not invalidate the authorization.
- **[FORF-4.4-19]** **Atomicity and operations**: update balances, exit and the lifetime flag before transferring funds; reject cross-function reentrancy, and revert all effects on any failure. Emit bondId, incidentId, pre-deduction balance and penalty. **[FORF-4.4-20]** Execute before lawful withdrawal; this mechanism cannot recover withdrawn funds. Before execution the DAO assesses replacement availability, online quorum and concentration, since normal-operation forced exits may stall a later rotation. This version has a single-entry API. Execute entries independently when failures must not affect each other; do not couple forfeiture to a potentially stale recover in an all-or-nothing governance batch.

## 5. Data formats and DA

### 5.1 Envelope (private)

**[DATA-5.1-1]** The single tx in each CometBFT block: `0x06 || rlp([block, anchor, committee, forced])` (the last three are each an empty list or exactly one item), **[DATA-5.1-2]** committed to by the CometBFT header's `data_hash` (encoding in appendix A). **[DATA-5.1-3]** It propagates only within the consensus network and never goes to L1.

### 5.2 Blob payload (public)

- **[DATA-5.2-1]** Each batch uses 1–6 blobs (EIP-7594's per-transaction limit) **[DATA-5.2-2]** with Unzen's blob encoding: each blob decodes to at most 130,044 bytes, every non-final blob is full, and all padding bits are 0.
- **[DATA-5.2-3]** Content: `0x06 || zlib(rlp([executionBlock, …]))`, where each `executionBlock` is the list `[EL header, [ordered EIP-2718 transactions]]`, the same item as the envelope's block (appendix A). It is encoded once, as a list inside the payload list, not as a byte string that holds its encoding. **[DATA-5.2-4]** Each transaction, legacy included, is an RLP byte string holding its EIP-2718 encoding.

<details>
<summary>Details: blob payload acceptance rules</summary>

- The acceptance rules use fixed limits that operator settings cannot change:
  - **[DATA-5.2-5]** Exactly one complete zlib stream, with no preset dictionary and no trailing bytes; at most 64 MiB decompressed, and each `executionBlock` in the list at most 124,000 bytes, measured as in CONS-3.4-13.
  - **[DATA-5.2-6]** The RLP encodings are canonical and re-encode byte for byte. The zlib stream has no canonical form and is not re-encoded: any stream that meets DATA-5.2-5 and decompresses to that RLP is accepted, whatever compressor or settings produced it, and the blob bytes themselves stay bound through `blobHashes` (PROOF-6.2-35).
  - **[DATA-5.2-7]** Exactly `lastHeight − parentHeight` blocks at heights `parentHeight + 1 … lastHeight`; EL `parentHash` links block by block, the first block's parent equals `parentHash`, and the last block's hash and stateRoot equal `lastBlockHash` and `lastStateRoot`.

</details>

- **[DATA-5.2-8]** Not published: CometBFT headers, original envelopes, anchor/committee/forced witnesses, registry entries, the raw forced transaction of a skip, evidence, commits and signatures. They are private proof inputs: the guest authenticates each original envelope against `data_hash`, then proves that its execution part equals the blob byte for byte.
- **[DATA-5.2-9]** **Recovery from DA**: with a sound proof and a finalized L1 checkpoint, archived blobs plus the starting state suffice to rebuild the landed EL chain and check the last block's hash and stateRoot, without witnesses or signatures. This is not the same as bootstrapping CometBFT from L1 alone; unlanded blocks still need the full consensus history.

## 6. Landing and settlement

### 6.1 land

**[LAND-6.1-1]** land is permissionless and nonpayable. **[LAND-6.1-2]** Proofs and blobs cover all of (p,h\], from proof origin p to terminal height h; **[LAND-6.1-3]** c, the latest checkpoint height, is read atomically at execution and only the new suffix (c,h\] is settled, **[LAND-6.1-4]** so another lander advancing c first does not invalidate the same transaction. **[LAND-6.1-5]** A landing may end at any height h above c, so any height can become a checkpoint, and land carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h) (6.3 explains why). **[LAND-6.1-6]** The checks are, in order: range and origin record; **[LAND-6.1-7]** the leaves are exactly those of the heights strictly inside (p,h) and **[LAND-6.1-8]** the leaf at c matches the current checkpoint record; FI head advance; reference block and blobs; computing cutHash and verifying both proofs. **[LAND-6.1-9]** Once all pass, land atomically updates the checkpoint, active committee snapshot, FI head, slot 285 record and SignalService.

**[LAND-6.1-10]** The reference Solidity in the toggle below lists every check land performs; the checks are normative. **[LAND-6.1-11]** All checks precede any state change and **[LAND-6.1-12]** the leaf checks precede proof verification; the remaining order is illustrative. **[LAND-6.1-13]** The land signature, LandInput's eight fields and the cutLeaf and cutHash encodings (appendix A) are the interface. Local names and helper bodies are illustrative: helper results are defined in appendix A and the comments, and storage variables map to the normative layout in appendix B.

```solidity
// Storage (appendix B): recoveryGeneration 268, committeeConfigHash 269, lastCheckpoint 270/271,
// fiHead/fiTail 281, activeCommitteeSnapshot 284, landings 285; generationStartHeight() reads 290.
// Immutables: verifier, maxBatchBlocks, signalService

struct LandInput {
    uint256 parentHeight;            // proof origin p
    uint256 lastHeight;              // terminal height h
    bytes32 lastBlockHash;           // h's EL block hash
    bytes32 lastCometHash;           // h's CometBFT block hash
    bytes32 lastStateRoot;           // h's EL stateRoot
    uint256 l1ReferenceBlockNumber;  // reference block
    uint256 forcedInclusionHead;     // FI head after settlement
    uint256 activeCommitteeSnapshot; // selection snapshot of the committee actually signing h
}

// Cuts: the EL block hash, CometBFT block hash and FI cursor at each height, with leaf
// cutLeaf(x) = keccak256(abi.encode(x, blockHash, cometHash, forcedHead)) (appendix A).
// A landing may end at any height above the checkpoint. Both proofs authenticate cutHash, a hash
// over the leaves of every height strictly inside (p, h) (appendix A), and land carries all of
// them (6.3 explains why).

function land(
    LandInput calldata input,
    bytes32[] calldata leaves,    // leaves of heights p+1, p+2, ..., h-1 inside (p, h), in height order
    bytes calldata proof          // the two proofs (6.2)
) external nonReentrant {          // permissionless, nonpayable; shares recover's reentrancy lock (8.3)
    uint256 p = input.parentHeight;
    uint256 h = input.lastHeight;
    uint256 c = lastCheckpoint.height;          // read atomically at execution

    // 1. Range. c >= h means the target is already settled: reject without mutation.
    require(generationStartHeight() <= p && p <= c && c < h && h < 2**48);
    require(h - p <= maxBatchBlocks);

    // 2. Origin p needs a retained slot 285 record in the current generation; a missing p is rejected.
    //    The only exception is the current generation's origin, which may reuse an older
    //    generation's record. Proofs cannot cross generations. isValidLanding rejects absent
    //    and voided records, including when old storage remains after a rollback.
    Landing memory origin = landings[p];
    require(isValidLanding(p) && origin.elHash != 0);   // a written record never has EL hash 0 (the start write stores the start's hash; its Comet hash is 0)
    require(origin.generation == recoveryGeneration || p == generationStartHeight());

    // 3. leaves must be exactly the leaves of the heights strictly inside (p, h). If c > p, the leaf
    //    at c must equal the leaf the contract computes from c's slot 285 record: height, EL hash,
    //    Comet hash and FI cursor all match (comparing only height or stateRoot is insufficient); if
    //    c = p, step 2 and the statement already bind the origin record. The FI cursor in c's record
    //    must also equal the current FI head. All of this is checked before proof verification. The
    //    other leaves only feed cutHash; a wrong one fails proof verification in step 6.
    require(leaves.length == h - p - 1);
    Landing memory cur = landings[c];
    require(cur.head == fiHead);
    require(input.activeCommitteeSnapshot >= activeCommitteeSnapshot);
    if (c > p) {
        require(leaves[c - p - 1] == keccak256(abi.encode(c, cur.elHash, cur.cometHash, uint256(cur.head))));
    }

    // 4. FI head only advances from its pre-execution value to forcedInclusionHead, never beyond
    //    tail and by at most h - c. The proof authenticates all consumption from p; L1 applies
    //    only the suffix and never consumes the prefix again.
    uint256 headAfter = input.forcedInclusionHead;
    require(fiHead <= headAfter && headAfter <= fiTail && headAfter - fiHead <= h - c);

    // 5. The reference block is in the past with a readable hash (blockhash's 256-block or
    //    EIP-2935's 8191-block window). The transaction carries 1-6 blobs binding all of (p, h];
    //    they cannot be replaced by suffix-only blobs.
    require(input.l1ReferenceBlockNumber < block.number);
    bytes32 refHash = l1BlockHash(input.l1ReferenceBlockNumber);
    require(refHash != 0);
    bytes32[] memory blobs = txBlobHashes();   // in index order
    require(blobs.length >= 1 && blobs.length <= 6);

    // 6. Compute cutHash from leaves, build the statement and verify both proofs (encodings in
    //    appendix A); wrong leaves fail proof verification. parentHash, parentCometHash and
    //    headBefore come from origin p; the statement's other words are deployment constants and
    //    committeeConfigHash (slot 269). The proof authenticates activeCommitteeSnapshot as the
    //    selection snapshot of the committee actually signing h, no later than the reference block.
    bytes32 cutHash = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32("ETNA_LAND_CUTS_V1"), recoveryGeneration, p, h,
                                           keccak256(abi.encodePacked(leaves))));
    bytes32 st = statement(recoveryGeneration, input, origin, refHash, keccak256(abi.encodePacked(blobs)), cutHash);
    verifier.verifyProof(0, st, proof);   // age 0 = not applicable; reverts unless both proofs verify

    // 7. Update atomically; any failure reverts everything. FI fees are not paid out. The landing
    //    record's parent is always actual c, never proof origin p. Once the frontier advances,
    //    recovery authorizations bound to the old c can no longer execute (8.3).
    lastCheckpoint = Checkpoint(h, input.lastBlockHash);
    activeCommitteeSnapshot = input.activeCommitteeSnapshot;
    fiHead = headAfter;
    landings[h] = Landing({parentHeight: c, generation: recoveryGeneration, head: headAfter,
                           elHash: input.lastBlockHash, cometHash: input.lastCometHash}); // slot 285
    signalService.saveCheckpoint(ICheckpointStore.Checkpoint({
        blockNumber: uint48(h), blockHash: input.lastBlockHash, stateRoot: input.lastStateRoot
    }));
    emit Landed(p, c, h, cutHash, input.activeCommitteeSnapshot, VERSION);
}
```

### 6.2 Proofs

### 6.3 Batches

- **[BATCH-6.3-1]** A batch may end at any height (6.1) and may cross epochs. **[BATCH-6.3-2]** Handover heights, cancelled old activation heights and the next normal handover may fall inside a batch or at its end; a recovery start is a checkpoint. **[BATCH-6.3-3]** All of these use the actual schedule rather than avoiding a problematic cut. **[BATCH-6.3-4]** `l1ReferenceBlockNumber` can be any recent L1 block, avoiding expiry of the reference hash alone. A lander should pick one that its own L1 node reports as finalized where the hash window allows, because the statement binds the reference block's hash and a reorg of that block invalidates both proofs. When the old unsettled suffix above c is proven after a long outage without governance recovery, **[BATCH-6.3-5]** the old anchors those blocks used must still be authenticated within the supported ancestry/proving budget, and choosing a recent reference does not remove that requirement. **[BATCH-6.3-6]** Landers and provers need a resource bound on the reference-header chain (reference value 131,072 headers, about 18 days of L1 blocks at 12 s); **[BATCH-6.3-7]** a suffix beyond it cannot be landed the ordinary way and can only be discarded by governance recovery under section 8 (at most U blocks), restarting from R; the outage before R does not count toward this bound (8.4). An already settled ordinary committee selection does not by itself expire after a long production pause: PROOF-6.2-33 can authenticate its registry proof root from the settled EL chain. A newly covered switch or an inherited anchor in the new suffix still needs the reference ancestry, as does any other root outside that exception. A recovery generation's initial committee is outside it too: until the last block that committee signs is settled, a proof of its blocks needs the ancestry back to the R registry root, so a pause longer than the bound within that span leaves the unsettled suffix to another recovery.
- **[BATCH-6.3-8]** Prove from an authenticated, retained historical checkpoint p, normally the latest when work begins. **[BATCH-6.3-9]** It cannot precede this generation's starting point, and the proof cannot span generations. **[BATCH-6.3-10]** If another lander advances to c, the original transaction (two proofs, blobs and leaves) stays valid unchanged whenever p ≤ c \< h: execution settles (c, h\] with no reproving, reaggregation or parameter change. **[BATCH-6.3-11]** If c ≥ h, the target is already settled and work can be cancelled. **[BATCH-6.3-12a]** Reference expiry, a block whose ruleset the job's images do not implement (10.1) or a generation transition can still invalidate a job; **[BATCH-6.3-13]** signed jobs must not be rewritten to bypass those boundaries. **[BATCH-6.3-14]** Copying a transaction cannot change any proven content; landing carries no reward, so who submits it does not affect settlement.

<details>
<summary>Details: why land carries a leaf for every height</summary>

- **Why check c at all**: a proof may start at a p older than c, while c advances through other landings. If more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signs, one height can have two valid blocks; without checking that the proof's chain at c is the block L1 recorded, L1 could settle one fork and then attach the other, and the bridge could be double-spent. Requiring p = c avoids the check, but then every competing landing forces reproving.
- **Why every leaf**: a proof is generated without knowing c at execution, so it must commit to every possible c, and any height in (p,h) can be c. A Merkle tree over the cuts would keep the calldata constant, but it takes a window and sibling nodes, with the lander guessing at signing time where c will be and reverting on a wrong guess. land instead carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h): the same transaction works wherever c is, the lander guesses nothing, and a competitor cannot make an honest submission fail by landing first (section 12, item 16). The contract does one hash and one indexed comparison, and the guest builds no Merkle tree.
- **Costs**: leaves grow linearly with the batch: a batch of B blocks carries B − 1 leaves of 32 bytes each (299 leaves and 9,568 bytes for 300 blocks), which bounds maxBatchBlocks (section 11). In return a lander never waits for a particular height, a single block can always be landed on its own (3.4, section 1), and governance recovery never discards a decided block that could still be proven and landed (8.1).

</details>

- Landers may coordinate active jobs off chain, monitor checkpoints and cancel stale work, reusing only inputs or sub-proofs that can be reauthenticated and are compatible with the target version. Coordination grants no exclusive rights and cannot bypass 6.4's U bound. Wasted proving and retry costs are covered by DAO operating funds (appendix C).
- Follow 6.4's proactive batching and submission policy. One remaining producible height is an alarm requiring immediate submission of an available batch, not the point to start proving.

### 6.4 Back-pressure

**[BACKP-6.4-1]** L2 height cannot exceed the anchor-proven lastCheckpoint.height + U, **[BACKP-6.4-2]** where U is an integer parameter committed directly in the config hash, with `1 ≤ U ≤ 65,536`. **[BACKP-6.4-3]** U is a circuit breaker for settlement failure, not the pace of normal settlement: it should pause L2 only when proving or landing stalls for a long time or a decided block cannot be proven, capping the blocks governance recovery may discard. **[BACKP-6.4-4]** The target deployment uses **U = 3,600** (about 4 h at 4 s blocks); **[BACKP-6.4-5]** approximately 4 s blocks and no more than 20 min between L1 inclusions of successive successful landings are operating targets, not block-validity rules. **[BACKP-6.4-6]** U and the epoch length L do not constrain each other: the unsettled suffix may span several epochs, and L is chosen separately for committee rotation (section 11). **[BACKP-6.4-7]** A landing stall permits at most U further blocks. **[BACKP-6.4-8]** L1 inclusion alone does not release the pause: the landing state must finalize and be adopted by a new anchor.

<details>
<summary>Details: latency budget and unsettled-block arithmetic</summary>

An approximately 4 s block interval is an operating target, not a minimum interval or uninterrupted-production guarantee. L counts blocks; nominal epoch duration is L × target block interval, while actual duration can be shorter or longer. End-to-end settlement includes batching, queues/proving, retries, L1 inclusion/finality and anchor adoption. Assess back-pressure from actual `height − anchor.lastCheckpoint.height`, not average-interval conversion alone. U=3,600 corresponds to 4 h at a sustained 4 s block interval, not a wall-clock outage guarantee. In normal operation the unsettled count is about “the interval between landing inclusions + the previous batch's terminal-block-to-inclusion latency + the next landing's finality and anchor-adoption latency”, counting the actual critical path of the two proofs; at 20 + 20 + 20 min that is about 900 blocks, leaving about 3 h for a proving or landing outage. This latency budget is an operating target: missing it shrinks the margin but does not pause L2. Sustained 4 s production is about 15 blocks/min; settlement throughput must keep pace without a growing unsettled backlog. At one batch every 20 min, each must cover about 300 blocks on average. If maxBatchBlocks, six-blob capacity or proving capacity cannot support that batch, settle more frequently or provide the missing capacity rather than merely delaying the pause with a larger U; after an outage, proving capacity must exceed the block rate to clear the backlog. The 20 min finality/adoption component neither bounds L1 finality nor relaxes maxAnchorAge. Exceeding the allowance causes a normal pause, never a bypass of U; this arithmetic establishes neither a measured pause fraction nor a no-pause SLA.

</details>

For uninterrupted operation, U should exceed the number of blocks produced during one landing round trip. Landers should continuously prepare and submit batches from the latest checkpoint, submitting early based on actual unsettled blocks and remaining proving/finality time. The 20 min target is not a mandatory batching wait and grants no exclusive landing right. “One producible height left” is an emergency signal, not the point to start proving. The target remains 4 s; a result obtained by temporarily slowing production does not establish that target.

**[BACKP-6.4-9]** U is fixed at deployment and cannot be overridden by local node configuration. **[BACKP-6.4-10]** An anchor is a finalized L1 block, and checkpoints only advance, so anchor-based accounting never undercounts unsettled blocks; **[BACKP-6.4-11]** the sole exception is the section 8 verifier-failure rollback, whose accounting rules ship with that upgrade. **[BACKP-6.4-12]** L1 finality lag only brings the pause forward and cannot authorize exceeding U. **[BACKP-6.4-13]** Raising U raises the maximum number of blocks governance recovery under section 8 may discard; before recovery, blocks are settled up to the last provable block (8.1), so only blocks that truly cannot be settled are discarded. **[BACKP-6.4-14]** Publish the selected L and its nominal epoch duration L × 4 s (60 min at the suggested L = 900), without treating it as a wall-clock guarantee.

**[BACKP-6.4-15]** **Acceptance**: before using the target configuration, pass the resource and latency acceptance in [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md); until then, do not claim deployment readiness or target attainment. **[BACKP-6.4-16]** Do not omit latest-checkpoint checks, use non-finalized anchors, remove a proof, locally loosen U or silently expand resource limits.

## 7. Forced inclusion

### 7.1 Submission

**[FI-7.1-1]** `saveForcedInclusion(bytes tx)`: at most 8,192 bytes (a longer `tx` reverts the submission), with a fee of `fee × (threshold + pending) / threshold` gwei, where pending is the number of entries on L1 not yet consumed by a landing (`tail − head`; while settlement lags this includes entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed); overpayment is refunded. **[FI-7.1-2]** Each entry stores only `keccak256(tx)`, the L1 timestamp and the L1 block number. **[FI-7.1-3]** The enqueue event records the entry index, raw transaction bytes and actual fee charged in wei, excluding refunded overpayment; encoding is not checked at submission; of the `tx` bytes, only the length is (FI-7.1-1). **[FI-7.1-4]** On successful enqueue, the fee calculated and collected using the formula becomes protocol revenue and stays in the Inbox. **[FI-7.1-5]** The fee buys queue admission, not successful execution: inclusion, valid skipping, governance blanking and replay after a settlement rollback neither refund it, charge it again nor pay it to a lander. **[FI-7.1-6]** Historical per-entry charges are recorded only in enqueue events, not used as consumption or recovery inputs. **[FI-7.1-7]** If the submission transaction reverts, neither enqueueing nor charging takes effect.

<details>
<summary>Details: sweeping protocol fees</summary>

**[FI-7.1-8]** **Sweeping protocol fees**: deployment fixes a nonzero DAO treasury address, `protocolFeeRecipient` (an Inbox immutable), and publishes it in the deployment manifest. **[FI-7.1-9]** Only `saveForcedInclusion` accepts ETH (overpayment is refunded in the same transaction) and every other Inbox entry point rejects ETH, so, apart from force-sent ETH, the Inbox balance is the FI fees not yet swept. **[FI-7.1-10]** Anyone may call `sweepProtocolFees()` to transfer the Inbox's entire ETH balance to that address; a failed transfer reverts. **[FI-7.1-11]** Force-sent ETH is swept with it. **[FI-7.1-12]** Neither `saveForcedInclusion` nor `land` transfers funds to the treasury, so a treasury receipt failure affects only the sweep. **[FI-7.1-13]** Sweeping does not depend on the queue head or landing and does not modify FI entries or queue progress.

</details>

### 7.2 Consumption rules

- **[FI-7.2-1]** L2 state keeps a cursor c (the number of entries consumed, initially 0). Each block consumes at most one entry, and only entry c. After a landing, the Inbox's head equals c at the checkpoint.
- **[FI-7.2-2]** Each block uses its own anchor A: if `c ≥ tail(A)`, it carries no forced witness; otherwise it must carry entry c's witness, a storage proof under A's stateRoot.
- **[FI-7.2-3]** **Due**: `A.timestamp ≥ entry.timestamp + forcedInclusionDelay`, evaluated without overflow. Entries never expire.

<details>
<summary>Eligible transaction: exactly one canonical EIP-2718 transaction (detailed rules)</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-4]** **Eligible transaction**: the raw bytes are exactly one canonical EIP-2718 transaction. Canonicity depends only on the raw bytes, never on state, and nodes and guests implement it against the same shared accept/reject vectors rather than any library's decoder: decoding consumes all input and re-encoding reproduces the bytes; the type is a non-blob type accepted by the pinned EL version (type 3 and unknown types are ineligible); a typed transaction's chain_id equals the L2 chain id and its y_parity is 0 or 1, and a legacy transaction's v is 27, 28, 2·l2ChainId + 35 or 2·l2ChainId + 36; nonce and gas are below 2\^64, each fee field is below 2\^128, and value is below 2\^256; the signature's r and s lie in \[1, n − 1\] (n is the secp256k1 order) with s ≤ n/2 and recover a sender; and gas is at most `L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT`. These checks cover only the transaction's own fields and signature: type-4 authorization tuples need only the field widths EIP-7702 requires, and an invalid tuple signature or chain id makes the EL skip that tuple without affecting eligibility. Eligibility ignores fees: a transaction whose `maxFeePerGas` (`gasPrice` for type 0/1) is below the block's base fee has an empty singleton build, so it may be legitimately skipped. A proposer can push the base fee to the 1 gwei cap at no cost (3.2), so an FI transaction should set `maxFeePerGas` to at least 1 gwei to be sure it is not skipped for its fee.

</details>

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[FI-7.2-5]** Transaction 0 of the execution block is the entry (canonical, at most 8,192 bytes, same hash) | Included, c + 1, before or after due. Ordinary transactions may follow, and ordinary EVM revert/OOG counts as inclusion; a zk-aborted transaction absent from the final body does not                                |

<details>
<summary>Details: given-list build (deciding whether a skip is legal)</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-9]** **Given-list build**: the build returns only after completing the deterministic build decision for `[tx]`, and has exactly two possible results: a block containing the transaction, or an empty block. It must not return a placeholder from before the build completes. If an eligible transaction cannot execute for any transaction-level reason (nonce, balance, intrinsic gas and so on), the result is an empty block, never an error. Transient infrastructure failures such as timeouts, a dropped connection or a SYNCING response from newPayload are not results: ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock both wait and retry until a result exists, and never reject a proposal, judge a block invalid or skip because of them; while waiting, that node processes no new consensus messages. If the EL definitively cannot build on that parent (for example because the historical state was pruned), the node halts for safety under section 8 and restarts after the state is restored. An eligible transaction's gas is at most one block's limit, so each build does bounded work. This singleton build starts from that parent with the same attributes and candidate list `[tx]` only, with full EVM-gas and zk-gas block budgets; ordinary transactions may not consume them first. A completed empty result after fully discarding `tx` for zk-limit exceedance/metering overflow under the shared rules is deterministic and reproducible. An included ordinary EVM revert/OOG is not empty. Nodes and guests make the same complete determination; a host timeout, exhausted prover resources or a smaller local budget cannot justify skipping.

</details>

- **[FI-7.2-10]** A block at or after due that neither includes nor legally skips the entry is invalid. Only position 0 counts as inclusion: a replacement transaction with the same sender and nonce, or the transaction placed later in the block, does not count.

<details>
<summary>Details: raw bytes in the witness and fetching them</summary>

- **[FI-7.2-11]** The raw bytes in the witness may be empty; if nonempty, their hash must match the entry. A skip must carry the raw bytes, and empty bytes can only prove that the submission itself was empty. For an inclusion the guest authenticates transaction 0 of the execution block directly, so proposers leave the bytes empty to save data.
- **[FI-7.2-12]** The proposer fetches the raw bytes with `eth_getLogs` at the entry's L1 block and checks the hash. Before due, if they cannot be fetched it may propose an ordinary block; after due, it cannot propose without them.

</details>

- **[FI-7.2-13]** **Blanked entries**: after submission, an entry's `txHash` changes only when step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) overwrites it with the hash of the empty byte string, `keccak256("")`. Under an anchor that shows this value, the raw bytes are the empty string and need no `eth_getLogs`; such an entry can never be included, and once due it is skipped as ineligible, without the EL.

### 7.3 Guarantees and bounds

- **[FI-7.3-1]** A due queue-head entry must be included or legally skipped under 7.2; there is no age-only deletion. **[FI-7.3-2]** For the current queue-head entry, any valid block with `block.timestamp ≥ entry.timestamp + forcedInclusionDelay + maxAnchorAge` necessarily sees it as due, using overflow-safe arithmetic. This is a block-time implication, not a wall-clock inclusion deadline. Wall-clock progress also depends on the BFT-time assumptions in 3.4, L1 finality, queue backlog and settlement.
- **[FI-7.3-3]** Senders must hold a balance of gasLimit × `maxFeePerGas`; **[FI-7.3-4]** the queue consumes at most one entry per block, and the fee rises linearly with the queue length.
- **Backlog cost accounting**: let F be the fees actually paid for an attacker's enqueues. **[FI-7.3-5]** With fees belonging to the protocol, no share of successful landings lets the attacker recover F by consuming those entries. Add L1 submission gas, proving/landing/blob costs the attacker chooses to incur, and the L2 gas actually executed separately; account for other rewards or proceeds separately as well. Production fees, threshold, service rate under sustained attack and honest-user costs require evaluation; [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md) gives an illustrative calculation.

## 8. Halts and recovery

**[RECOV-8-1]** **Scope**: an automatically retrying liveness pause loses no blocks; **[RECOV-8-2]** executing governance recovery discards the suffix above c.

- **[RECOV-8-3]** **Liveness pauses**: the process keeps running and CometBFT keeps rounding; production resumes automatically once the cause clears, and no blocks are lost. **[RECOV-8-4]** Causes: the anchor not yet finalized or older than `maxAnchorAge`, back-pressure, an unavailable committee witness, unavailable raw bytes for a due forced transaction, or a block whose ruleset the node's software does not implement (10.1). **[RECOV-8-5]** A local L1 node that lags, is unreachable or times out falls in this class too (section 9). When the node's own L1 node has not finalized a proposal's anchor, for one of these reasons or because its view is behind the proposer's, ProcessProposal rejects at once and does not wait for the view to advance: the node prevotes nil and judges a later round's proposal against its view at that time. FinalizeBlock of a block that was decided all the same waits and retries until the node's own L1 node shows the anchor finalized, and halts only on a contradiction from that node (SYNC-9-2). **[RECOV-8-6]** So does an anchor header whose hash matches the canonical hash but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 (L1 changed the header format): ProcessProposal rejects, FinalizeBlock waits and retries, and production resumes once the node is upgraded.

<details>
<summary>Details: ABCI constraints that keep a pause lossless under stock CometBFT</summary>

**[RECOV-8-7]** For a liveness pause to lose no blocks with stock CometBFT v0.40.0, the app must never return an error or exception from any ABCI method on any connection (CometBFT exits or panics); Query and the snapshot methods return only error codes or empty results. **[RECOV-8-8]** ProcessProposal returns only ACCEPT or REJECT. **[RECOV-8-9]** PrepareProposal stays within the request's `max_tx_bytes`; **[RECOV-8-10]** during a pause, or when the required envelope does not fit, it returns a placeholder proposal with no transactions, which ProcessProposal rejects, and CometBFT moves to the next round as usual. **[RECOV-8-11]** InitChain returns only `app_hash`, with no validators or consensus params, and Commit always returns a `retain_height` of 0. Automatic recovery after refused signatures is in section 9.

</details>

- **[RECOV-8-12]** **Safety halts**: the process exits and needs manual investigation. **[RECOV-8-13]** Causes: a decided block that violates the validity rules or contradicts local state or the EL (including an EL `-38002` rejection); fork evidence found during sync or trust checks, or malformed or unverifiable data from the node's own L1 node or EL (section 9; an L1 header whose hash matches but that cannot be parsed under 3.3 is excluded); or, while replaying a canonical skip during cold sync, an EL that refuses to build on the parent with a non-retryable error, usually because it lacks the parent's historical state, which must be restored before restarting.
- **[RECOV-8-14]** **Superseded generation**: once an anchor shows a `recoveryGeneration` larger than this chain's generation, this chain cannot advance and the node can only restart under the new generation. **[RECOV-8-15]** ProcessProposal rejects a proposal carrying such an anchor and revokes checkpoint trust while the process keeps running, and a running node revokes it as soon as its own finalized L1 view shows the larger generation, without waiting for a proposal (SYNC-9-6a); **[RECOV-8-16]** a decided block carrying such an anchor, or a startup or signer-session trust check that finds an Inbox generation different from this chain's, is a safety halt.
- **[RECOV-8-17]** **Committee derivation failure**: an empty candidate set or failed authentication/numeric constraint under 4.3 rejects S(t) proposals and may persistently halt production. **[RECOV-8-18]** Do not locally scale, add members or skip rotation. **[RECOV-8-19]** Governance recovery below may reselect from a new R snapshot, but eligible candidates are still required.
- **[RECOV-8-20]** **At least 1/3 voting power offline**: consensus halts without automatic replacement or power reduction. **[RECOV-8-21]** Returning members may restore it; otherwise governance recovery below reselects. **[RECOV-8-22]** Withdrawals still follow 4.1: confirmed exits can withdraw once the waiting period ends, while unconfirmed exits await settlement coverage.
- **[RECOV-8-23]** **Settlement stall**: L2 produces at most U more blocks and then stops on back-pressure.
- **[RECOV-8-23a]** **Planned stop**: past the point where a 10.1 stop ends production, PrepareProposal returns the placeholder proposal and ProcessProposal rejects, so CometBFT keeps rounding without producing blocks; only `recover` ends it, and a decided block past that point is a safety halt.
- **Verifier failure**: both proofs accepting a wrong result is outside the threat model (section 1). **[RECOV-8-24]** A bad landing may corrupt the checkpoint, FI head or snapshot, and ordinary governance recovery cannot repair it. **[RECOV-8-25]** Contain losses first by pausing `SignalService` and the Bridge and by QuotaManager limits. **[RECOV-8-26]** Replacing the verifier already requires an Inbox upgrade, and the settlement rollback is executed in the same DAO upgrade of the Inbox and the registry, together with the `SignalService` and Bridge changes that RECOV-8-27 and RECOV-8-27a need. **[RECOV-8-27]** The Inbox restores lastCheckpoint (slots 270/271), the FI head (281) and activeCommitteeSnapshot (284) to the values written by the last trusted landing and voids later landing records and the SignalService checkpoints those landings wrote; checkpoints at or below S that the Unzen Inbox wrote are kept. A voided landing is ineligible as a proof origin and for snapshot import, including at a generation origin. A later valid landing at the same height is a new record. After the rollback no voided checkpoint can be read through the `SignalService` or proven against. Every other checkpoint stays usable as before, and a checkpoint that a landing writes after the rollback is usable as usual, at whatever height: moving the whole checkpoint mapping to a new storage version, by itself, would hide the ones that stay valid too. How the voided ones are made unusable is not bound here, and neither is where the received-signal cache is kept (RECOV-8-27a). **[RECOV-8-27a]** **Derived authorizations**: voiding checkpoints does not revoke what they already authorized, because two paths use a signal without checking a checkpoint: an empty proof for a signal the L1 `SignalService` cached as received, and a retry of an L1 Bridge message left `RETRIABLE`. The same upgrade therefore makes every signal cached, and every message left `RETRIABLE`, at or after the L1 block of the first bad landing unusable until it is proven again against a checkpoint that `SignalService` holds after the rollback; revoking earlier ones as well is allowed, but the revocation must not remove the checkpoints RECOV-8-27 keeps. It must not take a legitimate message's value either: a message whose signal is on the chain that later landings settle becomes provable again once a landing covers it, and then completes as usual, except that a fee already paid is not paid again. `SignalService` and the Bridge, paused under RECOV-8-25, stay paused until the upgrade has executed. **[RECOV-8-28]** The last trusted landing is the last landing before the first bad one; if there is none, restore the values written at initialization (START-10-5) and by the one-time start write (START-10-5b). Landings of every generation count, and a governance recovery adds none, so when no landing is trusted the restore returns to that start even inside a later generation, whose number the rollback keeps (RECOV-8-32). **[RECOV-8-29]** The registry re-checks every exit confirmation against the restored snapshot, keeping it only if that snapshot ≥ the entry's exitEffectiveL1 and clearing it otherwise. **[RECOV-8-30]** The check does not compare the L1 block numbers of confirmations, because block numbers cannot order transactions within one block. Node rules for handling the rollback ship with it. **[RECOV-8-31]** The rollback returns c to an earlier value, so the same upgrade must preserve and increment the current recoveryNonce (8.3), invalidating every prior recovery authorization without restoring a historical nonce; **[RECOV-8-32]** the rollback itself does not change the generation. **[RECOV-8-33]** If a governance recovery executed at a c above the restored one, that generation starts above the restored c and no landing can succeed in it; **[RECOV-8-34]** the DAO must follow the rollback with a new governance recovery from the restored c. **[RECOV-8-35]** Bridge payments or stake already withdrawn using bad checkpoints cannot be recovered, and neither can state that other contracts derived from them, through signals or by reading the checkpoints directly. A bond withdrawn on an exit confirmation that RECOV-8-29 would have cleared is also no longer slashable (STAKE-4.1-34), while its key stays in the committees that sign above the restored c: until a landing settles those blocks, they carry that much less slashable stake. A `withdrawalDelay` longer than the time to detect a bad landing and execute the rollback prevents such a withdrawal (PARAM-11-15); **[RECOV-8-36]** L2 fees were paid to proposers at execution and are unaffected.
- **Governance recovery**: for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear, a structural upgrade, or replacing a ruleset that has taken effect (10.1). **[RECOV-8-37a]** The call is recover(expectedGeneration, expectedC, expectedRecoveryNonce, validUntil, exclusions, fiToClear, rules). **[RECOV-8-38]** The exclusions list contains strictly increasing registry bond ids. Before authorizing, the DAO verifies each intended bond's issuance and registered key against finalized L1 state. The fiToClear list contains strictly increasing FI entry indices. Call conditions, replay protection and atomicity are in 8.3; timing is in 8.1.
  1. **[RECOV-8-39]** Validate all lists and state before applying the following transition in one transaction.
  2. **[RECOV-8-40a]** At execution each excluded bond id must have been issued by a successful registration. An unissued id rejects the whole operation. A withdrawn bond, which no longer holds a slot, is accepted, and its earlier exit stands. **[RECOV-8-41]** Inbox calls the registry to set exitEffectiveL1=min(existing,R), with no forfeiture by recover itself; **[RECOV-8-42]** independent responsibility forfeiture (4.4) and double-sign evidence remain available.
  3. **[RECOV-8-43]** Each fiToClear index must still be in \[head,tail). **[RECOV-8-44]** Replace txHash with keccak256(""), retaining timestamp and enqueue block. **[RECOV-8-45]** This is for handling unprovable content only; unlisted entries survive and are consumed by the new chain under the normal rules. **[RECOV-8-46]** Admission fees are neither refunded nor recharged, and no lander is paid.
  4. **[RECOV-8-47]** Increment generation (generation+1 must fit uint48), write the fixed origin in 8.4, and emit execution details and complete lists. **[RECOV-8-47a]** Set `stopTime` in slot 293 to 0. A nonzero `rules` must equal `nextRules + 1`, otherwise reject the whole operation; it sets `activeRules` and `nextRules` to `rules` and `rulesTime` to 0, so the new generation runs that ruleset from its first block (10.1). Emit the new slot 293 value. The new generation's genesis and initial committee (8.4) use the ruleset that slot 293 at R selects for R's timestamp. With `rules` 0, a pending `nextRules` carries into the new generation and takes effect at its `rulesTime` as usual. **[RECOV-8-48]** Preserve checkpoint c's landing record and FI head. **[RECOV-8-49]** Recovery must not advance activeCommitteeSnapshot early; only a real landing signed by the new committee advances it. **[RECOV-8-50]** Confirmed exits are unaffected (4.1).
  5. **[RECOV-8-51]** Nodes rebuild EL from authenticated c and select the committee directly from the registry snapshot at execution block R under 4.3, without carrying over the old generation's powers. **[RECOV-8-52]** The registry bonds named in exclusions are ineligible. Other already active candidates may enter. **[RECOV-8-53]** Discard every pending update from the old generation and resume ordinary switches/H+2 on global heights. **[RECOV-8-54]** No signing for the new generation before the 8.2 authentication completes. **[RECOV-8-55]** Nodes/guests independently authenticate R's final snapshot rather than trust governance-supplied weights.
  6. **[RECOV-8-56]** Discard only the unsettled suffix above c; **[RECOV-8-57]** users resubmit those transactions, and fees in those blocks are void with them. **[RECOV-8-58]** Do not rewind the settled prefix.

### 8.1 Recovery authority and user-visible boundaries

- **[RECOV-8.1-1]** Only the DAO authorizes recovery, exclusion and FI blanking; ordinary on-call or pause authority has no such powers. **[RECOV-8.1-2]** Bridge/SignalService pauses limit future exposure but cannot recover paid bridge funds or withdrawn stake.
- **[RECOV-8.1-3]** **Timing**: recover has no separate time threshold; the shortest stall before recovery depends on the delay of the DAO's authorization path. **[RECOV-8.1-4]** expectedC is set to the current c and fixed at authorization, so any landing after that makes the call unexecutable; a recovery executed through the standard path (veto period plus timelock) therefore implies no landing during that whole period. The emergency path that skips the veto period should be used only for a long landing stall that normal settlement cannot clear, and on that path the lists may become public only at execution, with no advance notice. **[RECOV-8.1-5]** Slow progress, low TPS, a wall-clock FI delay or missing the 20 min landing target is never a reason to recover. Landers continue to submit every provable suffix during authorization. If c advances, discard the stale authorization, audit through the new c and reassess whether a stall still requires recovery before seeking fresh authorization. Repeated invalidation alone does not qualify for the emergency path.
- **[RECOV-8.1-6]** **Before authorizing**: the DAO has landers settle up to the last provable block and sets expectedC to the resulting c, so only blocks that truly cannot be settled are discarded. **[RECOV-8.1-7]** Before scheduling and executing, it checks a nonempty intended set, more than 2/3 online power, no party at or above 1/3, and available history and signers, and confirms that c's landing matches honest nodes' local chain: the EL hash, Comet hash and FI head in its slot 285 record, the SignalService checkpoint at c, and slot 284 (the selection snapshot of the committee that signed c). Matching these items does not show how each landing was made, because executing a block does not look at the transaction that settled it. So on every path, whether honest nodes hold the settled block at c or not, each landing above the audit boundary is also checked as a whole, with the p, h and reference block in the LandInput of its own L1 transaction (6.1), against everything 6.2 has the guests prove of a landing beyond the validity of its blocks (items 1 to 10): among it, all anchors and roots satisfy PROOF-6.2-33 against that transaction's reference block, including its narrowly scoped settled-header alternative, and its blobs decode to exactly those blocks (PROOF-6.2-21, PROOF-6.2-37). An anchor one block later than that reference can be canonical and finalized, and its block can pass every check on single blocks, yet no sound proof covers that landing. The DAO runs this check continuously on finalized landings. Its audit boundary is a contiguous, independently verified prefix of the currently settled history. For each landing the audit record binds the deployment, generation, finalized L1 block hash, transaction hash and position, previous audit record, p/c/h, reference hash, ordered versioned blob hashes, checked results and audit software revision. Independent archive operators retain original blob bytes, transaction inputs and the 9.1 witness dependencies until the DAO has verified the prefix and a second operator has verified and durably backed up the evidence. A hash list, monitor status or operator signature alone cannot establish a boundary: retain the inputs in an independently restorable archive whenever the saved evidence cannot independently establish the checks. Snapshot rotation never retires the last such copy. Before authorizing recovery the DAO verifies continuity from its recorded boundary and checks every subsequent landing; absent evidence starts the check at the start write. If inputs are missing, first restore the independently verifiable audit evidence for that prefix or fetch the missing original inputs and finish the checks. Public execution replay and comparison of L1 writes may narrow the gap but cannot establish missing blob bindings, Comet ancestry or commit checks. If a required fact remains unverifiable, ordinary recovery stays pending; missing data alone does not trigger verifier rollback. Section 12 records the resulting indefinite halt and [Q8.1-8](rules/review/8.1.md#q81-8) tracks an alternative with equivalent assurance. After rollback discard audit records depending on a voided landing. It uses the blocks honest nodes executed themselves, or those the independent verification below executes, and executes none again. Honest nodes can lack the settled block at c, or hold a different one, without a verifier failure. A block at or below c that more than 2/3 of the voting power decided can pass every check the proofs make and still be invalid under stock CometBFT, for example a header whose app_hash commits a different validator state (PROOF-6.2-42, SYNC-9-5c), and honest nodes do not get past such a block (SYNC-9-5c). And when more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signs, one height can have two valid blocks (6.3): L1 settles one of them, and honest nodes that committed the other hold different hashes from that height on. In both cases the settled chain is verified independently, starting from the last settled block that honest nodes executed themselves, with the EL state and FI cursor they hold there: execute the settled blocks above it up to c from the landings' blobs (DATA-5.3-5, DATA-5.3-6), which gives each block's EL hash and stateRoot; recompute the FI cursor block by block under 7.2; check the Comet headers up to c as 6.2 requires of a guest (hash links, DataHash against the executed EL blocks, the committee schedule and the commits of item 3); and apply to each of those blocks every other rule of 3.4 under its ruleset, with 10.1's planned stop (CONS-3.4-11a), as FinalizeBlock applies them in cold sync (SYNC-9-1). 6.2 has the guests prove the rules of sections 3 and 7 for every block they cover (PROOF-6.2-20, PROOF-6.2-45, PROOF-6.2-45a), and matching hashes, stateRoots, cursors and commits do not show that those rules hold: a block whose height is more than U above the lastCheckpoint.height its anchor proves (CONS-3.4-6) can match all of them. The cursor is not an output of that execution, because a legal skip leaves nothing in the EL block (DATA-5.2-8), and it is never taken from the slot 285 records being checked: for each block, read the queue tail and the entry under the block's anchor in the node's own L1 view, take the entry's raw bytes from its enqueue event or the block's envelope (the empty string for a blanked entry, FI-7.2-13) and check them against the entry's hash, and where a due entry is not the block's transaction 0, require a legal skip, by ineligible raw bytes (FI-7.2-7) or by an empty singleton build on the parent state that this execution produced (FI-7.2-8, FI-7.2-9). When the results match the records, those blocks pass these checks and the landings pass the landing check above, the verifier has not failed and recovery proceeds from c: nodes take the EL state at c from that execution, in place of any blocks of their own that differ from the settled ones, and the new generation starts from its own genesis validator state (8.4). Admission remains permissionless while an empty slot remains (section 12, item 11). Recompute the intended selection from the current registry before each check; authorization reserves neither slots nor committee membership. From scheduling through the end of execution block R, registrations, stake or top-up activation, effective exits, slashing, forfeiture and withdrawal can change selection, including without a new registration. Nodes/signers authenticate the actual finalized R snapshot under 8.2/8.4; an earlier pre-check cannot guarantee that the intended members remain selected. **[RECOV-8.1-8]** A mismatch in that comparison means the verifier has failed: perform the section 8 verifier-failure rollback first, then authorize recovery. A mismatch is an item that differs from the own state of honest nodes that hold the settled block at c, a landing that fails the landing check, on either path, or a record that the independent verification contradicts, including a settled block that fails a check it applies, even when every recorded item matches. A block that only stock CometBFT rejects is not a mismatch, and neither is a different valid block that honest nodes hold at a settled height.
- **[RECOV-8.1-9]** **Economic review and independent forfeiture**: estimate the intended committee against the launch checklist's capital target, then reassess the actual snapshot after R is finalized, including activation, top-ups, exits, slashing, forfeiture and withdrawal since authorization. **[RECOV-8.1-10]** A capital shortfall or expired valuation triggers an alert, public disclosure and replenishment, not a new recovery or signer veto; existing quorum, concentration and authentication checks remain. **[RECOV-8.1-11]** Recover neither executes nor cancels responsibility forfeiture and does not wait for it; **[RECOV-8.1-12]** resumed production does not erase responsibility under 4.4.

### 8.2 Recovery signer

- **[RECOV-8.2-1]** The signer keeps separate stock HRS state for each full `chain_id` (which includes the generation), persists each signature before returning it, and never resets or rolls that state back; **[RECOV-8.2-2]** no copy of the key may sign outside that state. Fence the old signer instance before failover. Let H be the height of the last signature in this key’s durable HRS for the generation. Bind a never-used key/domain to the consensus store instance before its first signature. The supervisor assigns a fresh instance ID whenever it creates, imports or rebuilds the stores. On every connection, including after manual deletion or partial restore, the signer checks the running instance against its own durable binding over an authenticated supervisor channel. PubKey and Ping remain available. An ID copied with an older database cannot establish continuity. To permit same-height restart, also bind the last signing decision to its committed predecessor and the flushed WAL prefix needed to replay it, recording the predecessor block ID and the length and digest of the flushed WAL bytes for H alongside HRS before returning the signature. For a live signature, take the flushed bytes after the predecessor end-height marker. They must cover the complete input record that triggered signing and may also contain round-state records already written while handling that input. If the marker is absent, record that same-height replay is unavailable on restart. For a used key, an absent or changed binding, a rebuild, or uncertain continuity automatically fences signing through H. The signer persists a minimum signable height of at least H + 1 with its anti-rollback state before admitting the replacement session. Rebinding cannot lower the floor. It covers every instance, restart and retry, including proposals and nil votes. A lost or uncertain HRS follows RECOV-8.2-3. On an intact instance with H still undecided, the supervisor must verify the predecessor state (H − 1, or genesis at the generation’s first height) and establish that stock v0.40.0 can replay H: this startup neither block-synced a block nor state-synced, stock’s WAL search finds `#ENDHEIGHT H−1` (or `#ENDHEIGHT 0` at the generation’s initial height), and the bytes following that marker match the signer’s recorded prefix length and digest. Ordinary block sync does not write the predecessor marker. If it is missing, `catchupReplay` returns an error and consensus logs it and starts anyway. Stock v0.40.0 also keeps at most 1 GiB of consensus WAL files and deletes the oldest, and it writes a peer's message to the WAL before it discards a duplicate, so a height that stays undecided for long enough, or under enough traffic from the node's direct peers (START-10-6 limits who they are), loses the marker and the start of the bound prefix; a restart in that height then has no eligible replay. Before replay, verify the previously signed prefix, then pin the length and digest of the contiguous, decodable WAL bytes from that marker through the last complete record at startup, including round-state records. Use a stable view of the bound WAL with no other writer. If the preflight detects a torn tail, take the repair path below before opening replay signing. This startup prefix must cover the input for any first signature issued during replay. In a trusted, authenticated replay from the verified predecessor, the request that repeats the HRS and canonical sign bytes saved at session entry confirms the last signing decision (apply the stock timestamp-only retry in SYNC-9-13). Return the saved signature. There is no comparison between the reader position and the end of either prefix. Higher-HRS requests in that uninterrupted replay can now receive fresh signatures. Atomically persist each new HRS, sign bytes and signature with the pinned startup prefix before replying. On the next restart validate that binding even if the new signature’s reply was lost. Replay-created round-state records carry no signing inputs. If the last signed step was a proposal, stock may rebuild a different BlockID when it has no ValidBlock. A request at that proposal HRS marks progress to the propose step only. Apply SYNC-9-13: refuse conflicting sign bytes, including a changed BlockID or POLRound. In that same authenticated attempt, before the first fresh vote, the supervisor may independently confirm that replay has restored the round and lock (locked round and full locked BlockID, or no lock) from the verified predecessor and the bound WAL inputs. For the original-proposal path, verify that stock has accepted the WAL proposal matching the signer's saved canonical proposal bytes and signature, including BlockID, part-set header and POLRound; verify the vote follows the restored consensus state. For example, in round zero with no prior lock, verify the original proposal and parts, the app’s successful ProcessProposal for that block hash in the same replay, and the requested prevote for its full BlockID. In a later round independently reconstruct any prior lock changes before admitting its vote. The check may complete at the vote request during replay. Merely seeing the propose HRS, or a log announcing a proposal before stock handles it, cannot release the vote. Without the matching sign-byte retry or independent confirmation of restored state, keep fresh signing at H closed. The supervisor must distinguish the bound process’s current replay attempt from live consensus at the time of the request (see below). Clear replay admission on repair, error, process exit or disconnect; a new attempt must confirm again against the latest durable HRS. For a torn tail after all signer-bound input, keep signing closed during stock repair, verify the repaired prefix against the signer's durable binding and start replay from the verified predecessor. This recoverable tail alone does not raise the height floor. A missing marker, damaged bound prefix, skipped replay, uncorrected failure or unverifiable continuity triggers the durable floor. A fenced signer can sign above H once the node verifies a committed chain covering H in this domain: verify the block and its >2/3 commit under the actual committee and their connection to the trusted chain, or authenticate a finalized landed descendant and sync under 5.4. The first new signature extends that chain. An intact node already past a verified commit for H can continue above it without replaying H. Both paths require section 9 trust. If H cannot be certified and the signer cannot safely resume at H, it remains a non-signing node. **[RECOV-8.2-3]** If the state is lost or its latest value is uncertain, stop signing; deleting state cannot restore service.
- **[RECOV-8.2-4]** Sign for a new generation only after the node's own finalized L1 view authenticates the recovery execution (new generation, origin c, R's registry snapshot and the committee reselected under 4.3) and both the 8.4 origin-record and genesis checks and section 9 checkpoint trust pass. **[RECOV-8.2-5]** The new generation starts its own HRS state at c + 1, and once the recovery is authenticated, signing requests for the old generation are refused; a process that was running when the recovery executed reaches that point when its own finalized L1 view shows the new generation (SYNC-9-6a). **[RECOV-8.2-6]** Votes in different generations use different signing domains and are not double signs. The signer enforces the same-domain fencing and height floor in RECOV-8.2-2 independently of node storage.

**Observing stock v0.40.0 replay.** The private-validator RPC carries no replay flag. One supervisor implementation gives each Comet process a dedicated, lossless local log pipe with synchronous, unbuffered writes and parses the structured consensus log records. For each signer RPC, while that process is waiting for its reply, consume all records already written to the pipe before classifying the request. The height-bearing `Catchup by replaying consensus messages` opens an attempt. `Replay: Done` ends it; the corruption/repair diagnostics and `error on catchup replay; proceeding to start state anyway` cancel it. A background log tail can lag behind a live request. Bind the pipe and signer connection to the same process lifetime; on loss of ordering or attribution keep the gate closed. Stock’s repair loop reopens the WAL on the same consensus state. To ensure a clean retry, let it repair with signing closed, then restart from the verified predecessor and repeat the prefix, trust and confirmation checks (or verify a commit if the node has advanced).

### 8.3 Execution-time checks in recover

- **[RECOV-8.3-1]** **Caller and conditions**: only the Inbox owner (the DAO) may call recover. **[RECOV-8.3-2]** Both recover and invalidateRecoveryNonce require Inbox initialization, and recover also requires the start to be written (START-10-5b). **[RECOV-8.3-3]** At execution expectedGeneration, expectedC and expectedRecoveryNonce must equal current state, and `block.timestamp <= validUntil`; **[RECOV-8.3-4]** validUntil is an absolute L1 Unix timestamp in seconds fixed at authorization. **[RECOV-8.3-5]** For recovery authorization, Inbox stores only a monotonically increasing uint256 recoveryNonce, initially zero, with no recovery queue or scheduling records. **[RECOV-8.3-6]** It does not distinguish DAO authorization paths; **[RECOV-8.3-7]** authorization or queueing does not freeze landing.
- **[RECOV-8.3-8]** **Replay and invalidation**: increment recoveryNonce on successful recover. **[RECOV-8.3-9]** Only the Inbox owner may call `invalidateRecoveryNonce(expectedRecoveryNonce)`; require a match and increment to invalidate all unexecuted authorizations with that nonce. **[RECOV-8.3-10]** Verifier-failure rollback also preserves and increments the current nonce; **[RECOV-8.3-11]** no path may restore an older value or wrap it. **[RECOV-8.3-12]** Landing changes c, recovery changes generation, and generation never decreases. **[RECOV-8.3-13]** A rollback returning c to an old value cannot revive old authorizations. **[RECOV-8.3-14]** Every retry must pass all checks; changed parameters require fresh DAO authorization. **[RECOV-8.3-15]** This recovery nonce is independent of the bondId lifetime forfeiture flag in 4.4.
- **[RECOV-8.3-16]** **Atomicity**: list validation, registry forceExit, FI blanking, counter updates and origin recording all happen inside one recover call, and any failure reverts everything, leaving no partial exit, FI edit or generation change. **[RECOV-8.3-17]** Recovery-list exclusion and FI blanking exist only inside recover and cannot be applied in an earlier transaction. **[RECOV-8.3-18]** This does not restrict owner exits, double-sign slashing (4.1) or the independent responsibility forfeiture and forced exit in 4.4. **[RECOV-8.3-19]** During recovery, reject cross-function reentrancy that could change the checkpoint, generation, recovery lists or withdrawal eligibility, including land, recover, recovery-nonce invalidation and registry mutations or withdrawals.

### 8.4 Fixed origin and genesis of a new generation

**[RECOV-8.4-1]** Recovery uses stock CometBFT v0.40.0 without adding an authorizer or trusted snapshot service. **[RECOV-8.4-2]** c is the fixed recovery origin and R the canonical L1 block that executed recover; nodes authenticate both from their own finalized L1 view.

- **[RECOV-8.4-3]** **Fixed origin record**: slot 290 stores (startHeight, l1BlockNumber, l1Timestamp) = (c, R.number, R.timestamp) per generation; generation 0 is (S, L1_0, L1_0's timestamp), written by the start write (START-10-5b), and only describes the Etna start. **[RECOV-8.4-4]** Each record is written once and never overwritten or deleted. **[RECOV-8.4-5]** A missing record is rejected; mapping defaults or the latest checkpoint are not substitutes. **[RECOV-8.4-6]** generationStartHeight and recoverySnapshot read it directly, without caches.
- **[RECOV-8.4-7]** **Unique genesis**: chain_id uses the new generation, initial_height=c+1, genesis_time=R.timestamp, and InitChain returns the appHash of ELHash(c) and the genesis validator state (CONS-3.1-8). Generate and check the full START-10-21 consensus parameters, including zero block evidence bytes, for this ruleset; a structural upgrade changing them must define the complete successor values in the authenticated 10.1 ruleset. They stay fixed within a generation; **[RECOV-8.4-8]** EL state must match c's authenticated EL hash and stateRoot. **[RECOV-8.4-9]** Genesis validators are selected by 4.3 on the R snapshot (record.targetEpoch=floor(c/L), cutoffL1Block=R).
- **[RECOV-8.4-10]** **First block**: uses stock genesis's empty LastBlockID/LastCommit; c's Comet hash serves only as the proving boundary (6.2 item 2). Stock CometBFT requires the block time at a chain's initial height to equal genesis_time, so the BFT time that 3.2 uses for this block is R.timestamp, as it is the timestamp of `L1_0` for S + 1 in generation 0 (START-10-20). **[RECOV-8.4-11]** The FI cursor starts at the authenticated origin head. **[RECOV-8.4-12]** The first block must carry a finalized anchor witness no earlier than R and does not use c's anchor. **[RECOV-8.4-13]** Every anchor and L1 root used by the new generation's proofs is no earlier than R (3.4 rule 3).
- **[RECOV-8.4-14a]** **Joining nodes**: either replay from the fixed genesis or import an authenticated checkpoint under 5.4 and replay its suffix; match the latest checkpoint on their own finalized L1 as SYNC-9-5 requires before becoming trusted, which is the EL hash alone while that checkpoint is the generation's origin; **[RECOV-8.4-15]** the latest checkpoint is never a new genesis for the same domain.

## 9. Sync and weak subjectivity

- **[SYNC-9-1]** **Replay uses the live rules**: during cold sync (replay or blocksync), for a block it did not judge live that carries an anchor witness, FinalizeBlock likewise first checks with its own L1 node that the anchor header is canonical and finalized, and checks the witness's lastCheckpoint per 3.4. **[SYNC-9-2]** An own L1 node that lags, is unreachable or times out only makes it wait and retry. The node halts for safety only on contradictory evidence from its own L1 node.
- **[SYNC-9-3]** **Checkpoint fence**: FinalizeBlock passes the `lastCheckpoint.hash` proven by the anchor to the EL's forkchoice as `finalized`; a history that does not descend from that checkpoint is rejected by the EL (`-38002`), and the node halts for safety. A history forged with old keys can therefore extend at most U blocks beyond its fork point, but that point can be old; forging blocks above the checkpoint needs more than 2/3 of the keys of the committee governing those heights, and that stake cannot be withdrawn until a checkpoint covers its exit.
- **[SYNC-9-4]** **Weak subjectivity is solved by L1**: every app startup and signer session starts untrusted, authenticates the Inbox's latest checkpoint in its own L1 finalized view, and **[SYNC-9-5]** becomes trusted only after reaching that height with an exact match of the EL hash and the CometBFT hash in the slot 285 record. While the latest checkpoint is the current generation's origin, the Etna start S or a recovery origin c, the node compares the EL hash only. No CometBFT block of this generation exists at that height: the record at S has Comet hash 0, and c's record keeps the Comet hash of its original generation, which only bounds proofs (PROOF-6.2-15) and is not a parent in the new chain (RECOV-8.4-10), so a node that joins through the 8.4 genesis path need not hold that block. 8.4's origin-record and genesis checks authenticate the start instead, and both hashes are compared from the generation's first landing on; **[SYNC-9-5a]** on a switching network, while the Inbox's latest checkpoint is S, the node also checks that S is the last Unzen block, with the same hash, by deriving the Unzen chain from its own finalized L1 view or by reading the Unzen Inbox's `lastFinalizedBlockHash` in that view; that its header of S has timestamp below `ETNA_FORK_TIME` and stateRoot equal to slot 274; and that the L2 `SignalService` proxy's EIP-1967 implementation slot at S, read from its EL state or from an `eth_getProof` at S checked against S's stateRoot, holds the Etna L2 `SignalService` implementation built into the client for the L2 chain id (PARAM-11-12a). The start write authenticates the hash and stateRoot under LAND-6.1-8a. Once a proof from S lands, PROOF-6.2-46a has also established S’s timestamp and the L2 implementation under that root, so a node that joins later, for example from a snapshot under 5.3, needs neither S's header nor the Unzen chain. The node's own fork time is not settled by any landing, so whatever the latest checkpoint, a node on a switching network also checks before it becomes trusted that the Etna fork time it runs with, including any local override, equals the switching Inbox’s immutable fork time in its finalized L1 view. Any mismatch is an authenticated contradiction under SYNC-9-7. **[SYNC-9-5c]** The validator state is not settled on L1; the app commits it in `app_hash` (CONS-3.1-8). After each block it commits in the current generation, the app durably keeps, with its other state, the validator state that valStateHash covers there (appendix A): at the generation's start height it is the state CometBFT builds from that generation's genesis (START-10-20, 8.4); each later block applies CometBFT v0.40.0's per-height state-update arithmetic to the validator updates that block's FinalizeBlock returns (4.3), so the proposer priorities and each set's stored proposer follow stock behavior exactly; after a 5.4 import, the state at s is the one derived under DATA-5.4-3. Appendix A binds the arithmetic to CONS-3.1-1's source revision. Pin the app binary and run the shared priority and appHash vectors against the stock implementation before launch and on every app release. On mismatch report the height, header AppHash, local EL hash and validator-state hash; compare their authenticated preimages where available and leave the cause open otherwise. Stock CometBFT compares each header's app_hash with the one the node's own app returned for the parent, so a block that commits a different validator state is invalid for it: the node prevotes nil, and if the block is decided all the same, the node's consensus stops, while a node in block sync drops the peers that served the block and stays below it, as CONS-3.4-12 describes for a block time the node rejects. More than 2/3 of prevotes for the block stop the node's consensus before any decision only when the node holds the complete proposal and has those prevotes as it first enters that round's precommit step; a node that entered that step earlier, on its prevote timeout, has precommitted nil and does not enter it again when the prevotes arrive, so its consensus goes on to later rounds. CometBFT does not call FinalizeBlock for such a block, so the process keeps running: this is not the process exit of a safety halt (RECOV-8-12), and the node shows it only as stopped consensus or stalled sync. The proofs do not check app_hash (PROOF-6.2-42), so such a block can still be landed; RECOV-8.1-7 covers recovery from a checkpoint at or above it. Stock CometBFT's stored validator-set lookup, at any height, and RPC answers built on it, are not a source: CometBFT keeps a full set only at some heights, such as where the set changed and at its periodic save heights, and rebuilds the others with one multi-step priority increment, which rescales and centres only once, before its first step, and can then differ from the per-height result. A node that has lost its validator state recomputes it with the same arithmetic from a state it holds exactly, such as the generation's genesis or an import preimage (never one rebuilt by that lookup), and the validator updates its app returned since; a node that serves 5.4 import packages also keeps the preimage each package needs (DATA-5.4-8). **[SYNC-9-6]** Once the node is trusted, every anchor change checks the new lastCheckpoint per 3.4. A forged history either stays below the target and is never trusted, or is rejected at the target, so no weak-subjectivity checkpoint and no age limit on the starting point is needed. **[SYNC-9-6a]** 3.4's check runs only when a block's anchor changes and compares only the EL hash, because the anchor witness does not carry slot 285. A trusted node therefore also follows the Inbox's latest checkpoint in its own finalized L1 view, whether or not any proposal has adopted an anchor that shows it. For each latest checkpoint at a height above the last one it matched, the node reads the slot 285 record once, at the same finalized L1 block at which it read the checkpoint, and applies SYNC-9-5's comparison, the EL hash and the CometBFT hash against its own blocks at that height. If either hash differs, the node revokes its checkpoint trust while the process keeps running, as for a superseded generation (RECOV-8-15): it signs nothing further and reports itself untrusted (SYNC-9-15), and a restart finds the same contradiction and halts for safety (SYNC-9-7). If its own chain has not reached that height, the comparison is missing data, and the node signs nothing until it has reached the height and compared; a match then restores signing without an operator (SYNC-9-12). The node follows the Inbox's `recoveryGeneration` in the same view, because `recover` raises it without moving the checkpoint: once it is larger than this chain's generation, the node revokes its checkpoint trust at once, whether or not a proposal carries an anchor that shows it (RECOV-8-14), and refuses requests for the old generation from then on (RECOV-8.2-5). The node keeps the result with its trust status, which the signing gate and SYNC-9-15's status share, so no signature needs an L1 read of its own. A node that cannot reach its L1 node learns of no newer checkpoint and keeps its last result (RECOV-8-5). It learns of a newer checkpoint only together with its record: if either read fails, it has learned of none, keeps its last result and retries. Once it holds both, the comparison needs only its own blocks, and the app completes it before it confirms trust for another signature; a signing request that arrives in the meantime waits for the result, inside the signer's time limit (SYNC-9-11), and is not refused. A new checkpoint whose block the node holds therefore costs it no vote. Every validator sees a newly finalized checkpoint at about the same time: if each refused a vote then, stock CometBFT would not ask for it again (SYNC-9-11), the round could fail, and each would need the restart of SYNC-9-12. A node that has run without interruption and a node that restarts therefore treat one checkpoint alike. A checkpoint at or below the last matched one needs no comparison, including one that a later anchor shows: the matched checkpoint's CometBFT hash commits to every earlier block of the generation through `last_block_id`, and a node that started from a checkpoint import need not hold the CometBFT block there (DATA-5.4-2). 3.4's check of the EL ancestry still applies to such an anchor. The CometBFT hash matters when more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signs: two blocks at one height can then carry the same execution block under different CometBFT headers (6.3), and no block built on the header that L1 did not record can be proven from that checkpoint (PROOF-6.2-15). Guests do not make this comparison. **[SYNC-9-7]** Missing data keeps the node untrusted and retrying; an authenticated contradiction, or malformed or unverifiable data from its own L1 node or EL, halts it for safety, because the node cannot tell that apart from forgery. **[SYNC-9-8]** An L1 header whose hash matches the canonical hash is not in this category: its source is authenticated, and a parse failure only means the node needs an upgrade (3.3). **[SYNC-9-9]** A recovery generation's fixed genesis origin may differ from this latest trust target; distinguish them and use fixed-genesis replay under 8.4 or authenticated checkpoint import under 5.4, never rebuilding same-domain genesis at a newer checkpoint.

## 10. Startup and deployment

Etna begins after height S, the Etna start (START-10-4). On a network that switches from Unzen, the one-time procedure is in [SWITCH.md](SWITCH.md); on a new network S is the L2 genesis. Steps that name one kind of network apply only to it.

1. **[START-10-1]** Deploy the Inbox proxy (not yet initialized; only its owner can initialize it), then deploy the registry with that proxy address fixed; validators sign the registration digest (appendix A) with their consensus keys, register and wait for activation and finality.
2. **[START-10-2]** Pick a past `genesisCutoff` and compute committee 0's record hash and the config hash.
3. **[START-10-3]** On a new network, install EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 in the L2 genesis; a switching network verifies canonical runtime bytecode at both addresses and matching pre-execution system writes on build, import and proving paths on L2 before the L2 `SignalService` upgrade of step 5, which reads EIP-4788 whenever it saves a checkpoint, and so before `ETNA_FORK_TIME` ([ANCHORLESS.md section 7](ANCHORLESS.md#7-prerequisite-eip-4788-and-eip-2935-on-l2)). Set the base-fee sharing percentage to 100.
4. **[START-10-4]** **The Etna start S** is the last Unzen block on a network that switches from Unzen and block 0 on a new network. The L1 reporting and collateral-withdrawal rules are independent (SYNC-9.1-7, SYNC-9.1-8); secp256k1eth keys only; `APP_VERSION = 1`; vote extensions off; no parameter update authority. **[START-10-22]** Initial state: anchor `L1_0`, FI cursor 0, checkpoint (S, the start's hash). **[START-10-23]** The generator writes an empty parameter-update authority. ### 10.1 Protocol upgrades

- **[START-10.1-2]** Operational restarts that preserve validity rules retain all same-domain signer state. **[START-10.1-2a]** **Deployment core**: the Inbox proxy address and the registry bound to it, the L1 and L2 chain ids, generation 0's genesis, how a block's anchor is carried or inherited, checked and decoded (3.3, the envelope's anchor item and appendix A's witness slot order), slot 293's location and meaning, the header's timestamp and `parentBeaconBlockRoot` and `extraData`'s generation and anchor fields (3.2), 3.4 rules 2 and 3, and this section's ruleset selection, refusal and stop rules change only through a new-Inbox/new-genesis migration (section 10), which this section cannot bypass. Decoding changes keyed only by an anchor's own L1 header, including the format of the account and storage proofs checked under that anchor's root, follow L1's schedule instead (section 12, item 10).
- **[START-10.1-3a]** **Rulesets**: a ruleset is a complete, published definition of what nodes and guests check and execute for each block; sections 3 to 7 and appendix A define ruleset 0. **[START-10.1-3b]** Each later ruleset is published as a change to an earlier one before it is scheduled, and a ruleset number names only one definition. **[START-10.1-3c]** A **rule upgrade** switches blocks to a new ruleset from an L2 block timestamp onward, without stopping production. **[START-10.1-3d]** A ruleset that `scheduleRules` activates keeps the deployment core and cannot change what L1 checks or stores (the statement, `land`, cuts, U, the config hash, the registry and appendix B), the envelope and payload framing (5.1, 5.2 and appendix A), the block size bound (3.4), the CometBFT version or consensus parameters, `chain_id`, rule 7's anchor freshness bound or `maxAnchorAge`. Changing any of these outside the deployment core is a **structural upgrade**: a new ruleset that only `recover` installs, at the start of a generation (14e). Owner changes that only L1 applies, such as registering an image or replacing the verifier (6.2, section 8), are neither, but must keep 6c true.
- **[START-10.1-3e]** **Schedule**: Inbox slot 293 holds `activeRules`, `nextRules`, `rulesTime` and `stopTime` (appendix B); its initial value 0 means ruleset 0, nothing scheduled and no stop. **[START-10.1-3f]** A block's ruleset is `nextRules` if the block's timestamp is at least `rulesTime`, and `activeRules` otherwise, both read from slot 293 at the block's anchor (carried or inherited).
- **[START-10.1-3g]** Only the Inbox owner (the DAO, behind its timelock) writes the schedule, through the two calls below and `recover` (section 8); nodes and both guests enforce it for every block, and L1 enforces it by accepting only their proofs. An off-chain manifest or operator agreement alone is insufficient. **[START-10.1-5a]** `scheduleRules(expectedGeneration, rules, time)`: if `block.timestamp ≥ rulesTime`, first set `activeRules = nextRules`; otherwise require `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < rulesTime`. Then require `rules` to be `nextRules` or `nextRules + 1` and `time ≥ block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge`, and set `nextRules = rules` and `rulesTime = time`. An equal `rules` reschedules the pending ruleset, and `time` = 2\^48 − 1 cancels it. **[START-10.1-5b]** `scheduleStop(expectedGeneration, time)`: if `stopTime ≠ 0`, require `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < stopTime`; then require `time = 0` or `time ≥ block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge`, and set `stopTime = time`; 0 cancels a stop. Before governance authorizes a nonzero stop, its proposal preview shows the proposed stopTime, the current maxAnchorAge and the cancellation/replacement condition `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge < stopTime`. The last eligible L1 timestamp is `stopTime − maxAnchorAge − 1` seconds, subject to a block actually being included. At the simulated execution timestamp, distinguish a rejected schedule from an accepted minimum-lead schedule. For equality, display “Once this proposal executes, the stop cannot be cancelled or rescheduled.” Label the assumed execution time and refresh the preview if it changes. **[START-10.1-5c]** Before either call can change or cancel a schedule, require expectedGeneration to equal the current recoveryGeneration in Inbox slot 268. Both calls are owner-only, require Inbox initialization, read `maxAnchorAge` from slot 282, compute the sums without overflow, revert on a value that does not fit its 48-bit field (appendix B), emit the new slot 293 value and revert on any failed check.

<details>
<summary>Details: why maxAnchorAge is the deadline</summary>

A block's timestamp is never earlier than its anchor's (3.2) and at most `maxAnchorAge` later (3.4 rule 7). Every block whose timestamp is at or after some time X therefore uses an anchor from no earlier than X − `maxAnchorAge`, and an anchor's state includes its own L1 block's transactions: a value written at least that long before X is seen by all of them, and a value that can no longer change within that window is the same for all of them. Every change either takes effect at least `maxAnchorAge` after the write or, when `scheduleRules` promotes `nextRules`, only restates the ruleset of blocks that already used it. So within a generation a block's ruleset depends only on its timestamp and its stop only on its parent, not on which valid anchor the proposer picked, and the ruleset number never decreases along the chain. A number never names two definitions (3b), so a number in an image's set keeps meaning the same rules. The strict bound for changing a pending value is conservative: equality would also be safe, because the anchor's state includes its own block's transactions.

</details>

- **[START-10.1-6]** Registering a new image alone is not a protocol upgrade. **[START-10.1-6a]** **Images**: each guest image implements a declared set of rulesets, checks every block it proves under the ruleset and stop selected above, and produces no proof that covers a block whose ruleset is outside its set. **[START-10.1-6b]** The verifier's accepted image set stays a flat set (6.2): an image is not removed because a ruleset it lacks takes effect, and its proofs of blocks under rulesets it implements stay valid. **[START-10.1-6c]** At all times the accepted set must contain a valid proof combination (6.2) whose images implement every ruleset that applies (3f) to a timestamp later than the latest checkpoint block's, which includes `activeRules` and, unless `rulesTime` is 2\^48 − 1, `nextRules`. Blocks above the checkpoint may not exist yet: after a production pause, the next block's BFT time comes from its parent's commit, so with an older finalized anchor it can carry a timestamp just after its parent's and an earlier ruleset (3.2). Every block that can still be landed, and so every batch of such blocks, then has one combination for all its rulesets; image registration and revocation and verifier replacement must keep this true. **[START-10.1-14]** A new image proves every block under that block's own ruleset, so it cannot change history.
- **[START-10.1-14a]** **Nodes** compute each block's ruleset and stop as above in PrepareProposal, ProcessProposal and FinalizeBlock. **[START-10.1-14b]** If the node's software does not implement the ruleset, PrepareProposal returns the placeholder proposal and ProcessProposal rejects (section 8), while FinalizeBlock waits and retries; production resumes once the node runs software that implements it. **[START-10.1-14c]** Software that replays history, including 5.3's public reconstruction program, implements every ruleset that history used.
- **[START-10.1-14d]** **Planned stop**: when `stopTime ≠ 0` at a block's anchor, the block is invalid if its parent's timestamp is at least `stopTime`, so production ends at the first height whose timestamp reaches `stopTime`. **[START-10.1-14e]** **Structural upgrade**: schedule a stop; once landers settle the height where production ended, one DAO transaction on the standard path (8.1) makes the contract, verifier and image changes and calls `recover` (section 8) with `expectedC` at that point and a nonzero `rules` naming a ruleset whose definition includes the structural change, so images that lack it refuse the new generation's blocks (6a) and none needs removal. It is a full governance recovery: 8.1's checks apply and the committee is reselected at R. No decided block is discarded once that height settles; if it cannot, recovery from the last settled checkpoint discards the blocks above it, as any recovery does (section 8); downtime covers settlement, the DAO's authorization delay, R's finality, the 8.2 and 8.4 authentication, the signer restart and the first block.
- **[START-10.1-14f]** Nodes and both guests implement this section's ruleset selection, refusal and stop from launch, because an image that lacks them cannot be made to refuse later rulesets. **[START-10.1-14g]** Before a ruleset is scheduled, the client, its EL and both guests pass shared vectors for its definition, including one landing that covers blocks on both sides of `rulesTime` ([Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md)).
- **Failures** follow from the rules above: a DAO call executed too late reverts and activates nothing; a schedule can be changed or cancelled until `maxAnchorAge` before its `rulesTime` or `stopTime`; if validators holding at least 1/3 of the voting power lack the ruleset, production pauses at `rulesTime` and resumes as they upgrade; a defective ruleset that has taken effect is replaced by a later rule upgrade while production continues, or otherwise by `recover`'s `rules`, which discards unsettled blocks above its checkpoint and uses the DAO path 8.1 allows; and only `recover` clears a stop that has taken effect. L1 hard-fork deadlines are separate (section 12, item 10).

## 12. Known limitations and open items

1. **[LIMIT-12-1]** **No wall-clock FI deadline.** `maxAnchorAge` bounds only anchor age relative to block time; wall-clock progress also depends on 3.4's BFT-time assumptions, queue backlog, L1 finality and settlement (7.3).
2. **[LIMIT-12-2]** **Offline quorum relies on DAO recovery.** A halted chain does not replace members automatically. Returning members may restore it; otherwise the DAO reselects at R with authorized exclusions (section 8). Newly eligible standby candidates may enter directly, but the DAO still judges online availability and operator concentration; success is not guaranteed, and L1 finality failure, incomplete proofs or missing history can still prevent recovery. Ordinary recovery discards the suffix above c, and its duration includes the DAO authorization delay, finality, reconstruction, signer migration and first settlement. If the voting power fenced at the same undecided H, together with power that is offline or otherwise not voting, reaches one third of the current total, the remaining power cannot form a fresh quorum. Unless votes already cast can certify H, the generation stalls. A height that stays undecided beyond the stock WAL's size limit loses same-height resumption for every validator that restarts in it, whether or not it holds a lock (RECOV-8.2-2). Peers connected directly to a validator can fill that limit in seconds, which is why a validator exchanges P2P messages only with listed nodes (START-10-6); a listed node that is itself faulty still can. With durable H + 1 floors the old generation cannot clear those fences by restarting; governance recovery needs its own authenticated origin. Each landing that advances c past an authorization’s fixed expectedC makes that authorization stale; repeated advances can indefinitely postpone standard-path recovery (8.1). Loss of both the original blobs after L1 pruning and sufficient prior audit evidence, or withholding of the settled fork’s required Comet headers or commits during equivocation, can also leave ordinary recovery unauthorized indefinitely. There is no non-governance escape hatch.
3. **[LIMIT-12-3]** **Double-sign slashing is same-round only.** Cross-round amnesia is not covered, and gossip can miss evidence; offline responsibility is discretionary DAO forfeiture, not automatic evidence-based slashing (4.4). Stock CometBFT v0.40.0 has no query or event that returns pending evidence or conflicting votes, and a node without a validator key forms no evidence from the votes it sees, so a reporter needs its own collection of votes (SYNC-9.1-7).
4. **[LIMIT-12-4]** **Operations depend on DAO funding.** Landing has no protocol revenue; settlement liveness relies on DAO-funded landers and on validators landing to avoid a back-pressure pause. Budgets and real proving/landing costs still need measurement (appendix C).
5. **[LIMIT-12-5]** **Dependence on L1 finality.** Production pauses when no finalized anchor satisfies `maxAnchorAge` (3.4).
6. **[LIMIT-12-6]** **Empty committee.** With no eligible candidate, an ordinary switch stops; recovery cannot create quorum from nothing, so eligible stake must be replenished first (4.3, section 8).
7. **[LIMIT-12-7]** **No rotation power-change limit.** Each term directly selects the top n_max by stake, with no takeover bound or guaranteed DAO reaction window; continuously assess the actual committee's less-than-1/3 Byzantine-power assumption (4.3).
8. **[LIMIT-12-8]** **Refunds depend on settlement and the post-confirmation evidence window.** An exit must first be covered by a landed committee, then confirmed, then wait the full withdrawalDelay. If the Inbox cannot recover or must be replaced, unblocking exits still needs a governance upgrade (4.1).
9. **[LIMIT-12-9]** **The switching network waits for the final Unzen proof.** The outage from the Unzen propose cutoff to the first Etna block includes proving every outstanding Unzen proposal, recording S and finalizing the start-write block on L1 (START-10-5a, LAND-6.1-8a, START-10-6). A slow or unavailable final proof delays both block production and Etna forced inclusion. The Unzen Inbox accepts a proof only from its prover whitelist while that whitelist has members, so the final proof also depends on a whitelisted prover or on the DAO changing the whitelist. If the Unzen image is defective, governance must authorize a repaired Unzen image and the proof must still succeed. No Etna recovery generation substitutes for that proof.
10. **[LIMIT-12-10a]** L1 hard forks are a separate, external deadline: forks that only append header fields are absorbed by the decoding rule in 3.3, while forks that change existing fields or the state commitment (for example EIP-7864's binary trie, which invalidates every MPT witness) need the client and guest upgrades and the new image registrations done before the fork activates on L1, on L1's schedule rather than at an L2 height. A fork that changes L1's slot time, gas costs or per-transaction gas cap need not change rules or images, but what this spec counts in L1 blocks (the registry delays of PARAM-11-13, the reference-header bound of 6.3 and the hash windows of 6.1) and the gas measurements of `land`, of the worst-case registry and `recover` transactions and of the verifier-failure rollback ([Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md)) are checked again before it activates.
11. **[LIMIT-12-11]** **Registry capacity.** At most 4096 bonds hold slots at once, a registration needs an empty slot, and a bond leaves its slot only through its own withdrawal (4.1). Deployment requires 4096 × minStake > 10\^27 base units, TAIKO's fixed supply (4.3), so the stake that 4096 bonds registered with exceeds the supply and they cannot all still hold it. Every slot can therefore be held only if tokens that double-sign slashing or forfeiture took from bonds still holding slots return to circulation and are staked again; those tokens go to `slashSink` and, for slashing, partly to the reporter (STAKE-4.1-34, 4.4). A bond keeps its slot until it is withdrawn, including one with an exit set or with zero balance; anyone may withdraw a zero-balance bond once its exit is confirmed and `withdrawalDelay` has passed (STAKE-4.1-27). If every slot were held, registration would fail until a withdrawal frees one. While no landing succeeds that can last: an exit that took effect after activeCommitteeSnapshot is confirmed only once a landing advances that snapshot (STAKE-4.1-26, RECOV-8-49), so such a bond cannot be withdrawn even with zero balance, and only its owner can withdraw a bond that keeps a balance after forfeiture. A governance recovery could then admit no new member and would select only from bonds already registered (8.1); the DAO could free slots only by upgrading the registry (section 1). Unless `slashSink` returns tokens to circulation, every slot can be held only while more than (10000 − `slashBountyBps`)/10000 of the whole supply is held in those bonds or has been paid from them to `slashSink`. The bound depends on the fixed supply, and it sets the admission threshold: no bond can register with 10\^27 / 4096 base units (244,140.625 TAIKO) or less.
12. **[LIMIT-12-12]** **No automatic gradual-offline handling.** The protocol does not reduce power for low participation, so offline members can keep their stake ranking; removal depends on owner exit, replacement by other eligible stake, DAO responsibility forfeiture with forced exit (4.4), or post-stall governance recovery.
13. **[LIMIT-12-13]** **secp256k1eth is new.** The key type was added only in CometBFT v0.40.0, with one later fix to its signature format, so it is less battle-tested than Ed25519; support in external signers and HSM/KMS must be confirmed separately. CometBFT does not batch-verify it. [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md) requires pinned versions, cross-implementation vectors, signer/HSM/KMS compatibility and n = 128 measurements; a negligible cost impact must not be assumed before testing.
14. **[LIMIT-12-14]** **Registration admission bounds.** The vp_unit lower bound only prevents power overflow (4.3); selection is still limited by the 4096 registry slots, stake ranking and eligibility, so registration does not guarantee selection.
15. **[LIMIT-12-15]** **Private consensus-history availability.** Public blobs only reconstruct the settled EL; ordinary proving, Comet replay, signing evidence and L1 ancestry depend on the 9.1 archives, and unauthenticated snapshots are not substitutes.
16. **[LIMIT-12-16]** **Overlapping proofs provide short-landing protection, pending acceptance.** The leaf rules are in 6.1 and 6.3. A competitor landing first at any height in (p,h) cannot make an honest submission fail. Only a competitor settling h or beyond makes it revert as already settled, and then the competitor has advanced settlement on its behalf, which cannot trigger back-pressure; at worst the honest lander pays blob gas and calldata for that transaction. No throughput or recovery guarantee is claimed under more-than-1/3 collusion, unavailable proofs/data or failed L1 finality.
17. **[LIMIT-12-17]** **Resource and latency figures await validation.** U=3,600, approximately 4 s blocks, 20 min landings, about 900 unsettled blocks in normal operation and about 3 h of outage margin are illustrative, not measured, and must pass [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md). Checking only the latest checkpoint does not remove history retention or fix indefinite L1 finality failure; the unsettled suffix remains governance rollback exposure.
18. **[LIMIT-12-18]** **Economic security and discretionary forfeiture.** At least 1/3 of voting power can halt consensus by withholding signatures. Checklist section 4 defines S_MIN_TOTAL from an independent capital target and conservative reference price; concrete deployment values and launch checks remain required. Operation and recovery may continue with publicly disclosed economic-security shortfalls. Forfeiture depends on correct DAO attribution and execution before lawful withdrawal; it does not guarantee loss for every halt, and has no extra waiting or appeal period. Its unexecuted authorizations cannot be revoked through the registry before expiry. If the same principal B is subject to both punishments and the owner can self-report valid double-sign evidence first, its loss is B − floor(B × slashBountyBps / 10000). A forfeiture first takes ceil(B × forfeitBps / 10000) and leaves any remainder slashable. When forfeitBps exceeds 10000 − slashBountyBps, the self-report can cost less, subject to integer rounding. Normal-operation forfeiture grants the DAO forced-exit authority and may disrupt later rotations. Top-ups replenish existing entries whether or not a slot is free; a new operator needs an empty slot (4.1, item 11).
19. **[LIMIT-12-19]** **Slashed members keep their voting power until a committee that excludes them takes effect.** Double-sign slashing zeroes the entry's whole principal and sets `exitEffectiveL1` to min(its old value, the slashing block) (4.1), so no selection snapshot at or after the slashing block includes the entry. A committee that already includes the member keeps its power until a committee that excludes it takes effect in ordinary rotation (4.3), or until governance recovery reselects (section 8). That committee can be one selected before the slashing, when the member's earlier exit already excluded it from that snapshot. A rotation using a pre-slashing snapshot can still include the member if it was eligible there. Further misconduct by that member in this period has no principal left to slash. In this period the member's assigned voting power counts as Byzantine power under section 1's less-than-1/3 bound (4.3).
20. **[LIMIT-12-20]** **Blanking does not stop resubmission.** Step 3 of governance recovery (section 8) replaces each listed entry's `txHash` with `keccak256("")`, but `saveForcedInclusion` (7.1) does not check whether the same bytes were blanked before. The enqueue event publishes the raw bytes (7.1), so anyone can enqueue the same unprovable transaction again for the normal fee. This situation presupposes a violation of section 1's proof completeness, such as an EL/guest mismatch or a guest resource limit below the EL limits. Each recovery blanks only the indices listed in `fiToClear`; unlisted copies enqueued while DAO authorization is pending survive it, and adding them to the list requires fresh DAO authorization (8.3). Further recoveries may be needed while the content remains unprovable. Resolving the underlying proof-completeness failure, or changing admission/skip rules through a DAO upgrade, can end the recurring stall; blanking itself does not prevent resubmission.
21. **[LIMIT-12-21]** **Upgrades depend on the DAO and on validators upgrading in time.** A rule upgrade pauses production at `rulesTime` while validators holding at least 1/3 of the voting power run software without the new ruleset, and the DAO can change a schedule only until `maxAnchorAge` before its `rulesTime` or `stopTime` (10.1). After that, a defective ruleset can be replaced only by a later rule upgrade while production continues, or by governance recovery, which discards unsettled blocks above its checkpoint; a stop that has taken effect is cleared only by recovery. Ruleset numbers only grow, so returning to earlier rules takes a new number. A structural upgrade stops production for settlement and the DAO's authorization delay. Block time can run ahead of L1 time by up to `maxAnchorAge` less the finality lag when it is manipulated (3.4), so validators should run a new ruleset's software by `rulesTime − maxAnchorAge`.
22. **[LIMIT-12-22]** **The stock evidence pool is unbounded.** CometBFT v0.40.0 always runs its evidence reactor and pool. The pool keeps every valid pending item until it expires, sets no limit on their number and sends them to every peer; with zero evidence bytes (CONS-3.1-10) no item is ever committed, and items expire only as blocks commit. A committee member that double-signs can therefore load the memory, storage and gossip of every node with valid items, at the cost of its bond (4.1), for as long as a height at which it was a member stays within the age limits, which is indefinitely while no block commits. The short age limits (START-10-21) shorten how long an item lives while blocks commit, and the clearing of the store at every start (CONS-3.1-10) lets a node restart. Neither limits how many items a node holds at once, and neither helps while no block commits. This is an open launch item, not an accepted cost. The launch gate ([Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md)) closes it in one of two ways only. Either the deployed implementation enforces a limit on the pool's storage, memory and relay work at every enabled way evidence enters it (peer gossip, submission by RPC and the conflicting votes that consensus reports), and the limit does not depend on blocks committing again; or nodes run without the evidence path this protocol does not consume, and an independent collector delivers conflicting votes to L1 slashing. A test of limited length can check such a limit but does not establish one. Stock v0.40.0 offers neither: it has no such limit and cannot run without the reactor. Either way therefore needs a build other than the stock one, and CONS-3.1-2 and CONS-3.1-10 must first name the permitted build and what it changes.

## Appendix A: Encodings

**[ENC-A-1]** **Versions**: APP_VERSION=1, and every versioned domain tag is V1; the outer envelope/payload prefix is 0x06. **[ENC-A-2]** Config has seven words/224 bytes; CommitteeRecord has five fields. **[ENC-A-3]** Slot 285 has three words (appendix B). **[ENC-A-4a]** Changing clients or registering an image does not activate new rules; only the 10.1 schedule selects a block's ruleset, and guest-image acceptance is the verifier's accepted set (6.2).

**Shared vectors**: every encoding, interface, storage layout and boundary condition needs accept/reject vectors shared by nodes, contracts and both guests; the list is in [Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist](ACCEPTANCE.md).

**[ENC-A-5]** Protocol hashes are keccak256 over `abi.encode`, with domain tags as right-zero-padded `bytes32` ASCII; the exceptions are the inner nodes of `entriesRoot` (plain concatenation) and the blob hash list and the cut leaf list (`abi.encodePacked`). Every formula below writes its tag as `bytes32("...")`: a tag is one static word and is never encoded as a dynamic string.

## Appendix C: Operating funding and cost assumptions

This appendix states operating requirements, not validity rules.

- **[COST-C-1]** **The DAO funds operations**: validators, proving, landing, historical data and on-call service, including low/zero transaction load, FI consumption, repeated DA, discarded competitive work and retries. **[COST-C-2]** Landing has no protocol revenue (base fees and tips go to proposers, FI fees to the protocol); this operating budget covers all landing and proving costs.
- **[COST-C-3]** **How funding is paid**: funding grants no exclusive landing permission. **[COST-C-4]** Landers do not receive FI fees, and funding is not paid per FI entry consumed or by FI fee value, so an attacker cannot recover its FI fees through funding (7.3). **[COST-C-5]** land binds no recipient and a public blob transaction can be copied, so DAO compensation must not be a per-landing bounty paid to the submitting address; fund named operators or attribute work off chain.
- **[COST-C-6]** **Fund the sustained worst case**: measure and fund the budget for the operating targets of approximately 4 s blocks and at most 20 min between L1 inclusions of successive successful landings (6.4); cost estimates alone are not evidence that funding is in place. **[COST-C-7]** A proposer pays no net fee for any load in its own blocks (3.2), whether zk-heavy or data-heavy; such load is bounded only by the per-block gas, zk-gas and 124,000-byte (3.4) limits. Any other sender pays only the L2 fee for the gas its transactions use, at a base fee that can sit at the chain's minimum (3.2), and nothing for the proving, blob and landing cost that load causes, so the worst cases below can be caused by any sender for that fee alone. **[COST-C-8]** Without zk-to-charged-gas conversion, a zk-heavy workload with low EVM gas may use almost the whole per-block zk-gas limit. **[COST-C-9]** The data worst case is every block filled with incompressible data. **[COST-C-10]** A landing carries at most six blobs, hence six such blocks: about 150 landings an hour. **[COST-C-11]** That is 3 blobs per L1 slot. **[COST-C-12]** Fund that sustained worst case explicitly in the proving, blob and landing budgets.
