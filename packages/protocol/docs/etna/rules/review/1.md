## Q1-1

**"Not affected by pause" names no pause mechanism**

**Text.** "`land`, `saveForcedInclusion` and the registry are not affected by pause".

**Why it matters.** The spec nowhere defines a pause state, a pause function, or who may pause. Section 8.1 speaks of "ordinary on-call or pause authority", and section 1 itself mentions "pausing `SignalService` and the Bridge", so at least three readings exist: (a) the three calls survive a SignalService/Bridge pause; (b) there is a separate Inbox pause that does not cover them; (c) "pause" is an operational halt, not an on-chain state. The readings differ observably when a pause is active, and a vector cannot be written until one is fixed.

**Would close it.** name the pause mechanism and its scope, and state the exempt entry points as an observable rule (for example "while `SignalService` is paused, `land`, `saveForcedInclusion` and every registry entry point remain callable").

## Q1-2

**Who enforces proof completeness, and what happens when it fails?**

**Text.** "every block that is valid under this spec and within the EL limits (gas, zk gas) and the 3.4 block size bound must be provable by at least one valid proof combination, and any grid segment of K such consecutive blocks (6.1) must be provable together within one landing's two proofs, counting in the worst case one 7.2 single-transaction build trial per block".

**Why it matters.** The requirement is a liveness/settlement guarantee, not a block-validity condition: a valid but unprovable block can still be decided. The text names no actor, deadline, or consequence. The explicit transaction-specific remedy is for a queued forced transaction ("can blank such a transaction while it is in the forced inclusion queue"), with repeat admission disclosed in LIMIT-12-20. The responsibility and outcome for an ordinary unprovable block remain unclear. Implementers cannot tell whether this is a launch gate, a halt condition, or only a design target.

**Would close it.** state who must establish completeness (deployment? prover? acceptance?) and the outcome when a valid non-forced block cannot be proven — for example a halt under section 8, or an explicit "this is a launch gate, not a consensus rule".

## Q1-3

**DAO/timelock ownership is stated as guidance, and upgrade trust has no boundary**

**Text.** "`EtnaInbox`, the registry and the L1 `SignalService` are upgradeable proxies whose owner should be the DAO (behind a timelock); an upgrade can bypass any rule in this spec."

**Why it matters.** "Should" is not a checkable deployment condition, yet the rest of the security model assumes upgrades are legitimate. A deployment with an EOA owner satisfies every numbered rule in this section while invalidating its trust claims. Conversely, "an upgrade can bypass any rule" means every ID in this directory is conditional on the upgrade authority, which no rule records.

**Would close it.** state whether DAO/timelock ownership is a launch-gate requirement and where it is checked; and say whether "an upgrade can bypass any rule" is an accepted trust assumption or is bounded by section 10.1's upgrade rules.

## Q1-4

**Threat model for "the whole committee cannot take funds"**

**Text.** "L1 recognizes only landed checkpoints, and every landing needs two proofs, at least one of them ZK (6.2). Even the whole committee acting maliciously cannot take funds on L1."

**Why it matters.** The claim depends on verifier soundness, on at least one proof backend being honest, and on the L1 contracts enforcing the two-proof rule — none of which is stated here. Section 8 separately contemplates "both proofs accepting a wrong result" as outside the threat model, and section 1 says an upgrade can bypass any rule. The sentence therefore reads as an unconditional security guarantee while resting on unstated assumptions.

**Would close it.** state the assumptions under which the claim holds (verifier soundness, one honest proof system, upgrade authority), and whether a violation is a protocol bug or an accepted trust failure.
