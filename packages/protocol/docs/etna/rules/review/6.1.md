## Q6.1-3

**Which fields "the current checkpoint record" covers in the leaf-at-c check**

**Text.** "the leaves are exactly those of the grid points in (p,h) and the leaf at c matches the current checkpoint record".

**Why it matters.** "Checkpoint record" could mean `lastCheckpoint` (height and EL block hash) alone, or the slot 285 landing record (EL hash, Comet hash, FI cursor). The appendix A leaf contains all of those words. The prose also does not say that the FI cursor in `c`'s record must equal the current FI head; that requirement is stated only in the fence. Two implementations that both cite LAND-6.1-8 can therefore accept different leaves, and only one of them is consistent with the leaf encoding.

**Would close it.** enumerate the compared fields (height, EL hash, Comet hash, FI cursor) and say explicitly whether the FI-cursor equality is part of this check or of the FI-head check.

## Q6.1-5

**"The checks are, in order" versus "the remaining order is illustrative"**

**Text.** "The checks are, in order: range, grid and origin record; the leaves are exactly those of the grid points in (p,h) and the leaf at c matches the current checkpoint record; FI head advance; reference block and blobs; computing cutHash and verifying both proofs." … "the remaining order is illustrative."

**Why it matters.** The two sentences can be read as (a) the enumerated order is binding and only the internal order within a check group is illustrative, or (b) only two ordering constraints bind — all checks before any state change, and the leaf checks before proof verification — while the enumeration is presentation. The readings differ observably: under (a), verifying proofs before the range check does not conform; under (b) it does. A vector cannot settle it, because the difference is in what a conformance test may assert.

**Would close it.** say which ordering constraints are normative, or delete "in order" and keep only the two explicit ordering rules.
