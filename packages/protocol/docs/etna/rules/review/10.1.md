# 10.1 — review notes

**Non-normative.** The timestamp design is in SPEC.md. These questions concern concrete activation interfaces and implementation evidence still required by START-10.1-15; they do not choose an implementation or claim deployment readiness.

## Normativity boundary

Every section-body bullet is covered by a rule ID. The un-numbered sentence "L1 hard-fork deadlines are separate (section 12, item 10)." is a cross-reference. The heading is a label.

## Q10.1-1

**Which on-chain artifact and proof binding authenticate the schedule?**

**Text.** START-10.1-4a binds deployment, applicable generation, T and the rule/artifact schedule. START-10.1-5 requires accepted images to bind that schedule or authenticate a commitment enforced by L1 verification; the existing committeeConfigHash does not commit to it.

**Why it matters.** Per-block timestamp selection and dual-version guests fix the intended semantics, but the plan schema, authority record, schedule commitment and exact image/admission binding remain unspecified. Neither a private input nor a historical anchor may select an obsolete schedule. Recovery must also authenticate applicability to its new generation without resetting the deployment's schedule.

**Would close it.** Specify the authority artifact, deployment/generation and historical-schedule encoding, the node/guest authentication path and L1-enforced binding. Demonstrate rejection of mismatched T, stale schedules, unauthorized plans and cross-generation substitutions, without claiming the unchanged statement or committeeConfigHash already supplies the binding.

## Q10.1-4

**What evidence establishes that a replacement preserves validity rules?**

**Text.** START-10.1-2 distinguishes a rule-preserving operational restart from a schedule or validity-rule change.

**Why it matters.** The distinction is now explicit, but a deployment still needs evidence for a binary/image replacement claimed to preserve behavior. A matching version label does not establish equivalence for malformed inputs, historical replay or resource boundaries.

**Would close it.** Pin the old/new artifacts and publish the conformance and review evidence used to classify the replacement, including all historical rule revisions it must support. Do not silently change a scheduled activation under an operational-restart label.

## Q10.1-5

**Which transaction prepares schedule-aware-only proof admission?**

**Text.** START-10.1-9 requires finalized L1 preparation before any post-T block can be signed or admitted; START-10.1-12a removes grandfathering for V1-only proofs after preparation.

**Why it matters.** The required effect is specified, but the concrete transaction, atomicity across all accepted SGX/ZK combinations, node finality check and restart persistence are not. A partial image update or stale acceptance path could retain a V1-only bypass. Pending signed jobs also need durable receipt/nonce reconciliation when a proof becomes inadmissible.

**Would close it.** Specify and test the preparation transaction and all verifier routes, finalized-state authentication, interrupted/reorged preparation and pending-job handling. Show that no allowed combination admits a retired image and that preparation cannot be inferred solely from L1 wall-clock time.

## Q10.1-7

**Which supported supervisor procedure handles an incomplete rollout?**

**Text.** START-10.1-7a refuses unsupported post-T proposal/signing without HRS reset or automatic trust-retry restart. START-10.1-10 prohibits retrospective reclassification and a V1-only software rollback.

**Why it matters.** The safety requirements do not provide the durable readiness record, fencing procedure or repair commands. After preparation but before T, or after signatures already cross T, a process restart must not silently forget the plan, postpone T or reconnect an unsupported signer.

**Would close it.** Publish and exercise the readiness/supervisor procedure, including crashes before/after preparation, retained proposals, missed T, signer failover, forward repair and software rollback that still implements the authorized schedule. Recovery remains subject to section 8 rather than becoming a deployment-failure shortcut.
