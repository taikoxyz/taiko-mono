# Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist

This page collects the acceptance, shared vectors and launch gate for [Etna PoS + ZK Design Spec](SPEC.md); the spec body remains authoritative for validity rules.

## 1. Resource and latency acceptance (spec 6.4)

1. Pin K, U, L, n_max, maxBatchBlocks, EL gas/zk-gas limits, maxAnchorAge and software/hardware configuration. Test an anchor spanning U/K single-segment landings, combined with rotation, all 4096 registry slots, maximum-committee commits and an 8,192-byte FI payload. Bound the complete Protobuf block conservatively and demonstrate a legal proposal always fits 22,020,096 bytes and all other limits; optional transactions may be reduced but the anchor witness, committee rotation data and forced-inclusion data may not be omitted. Merely rejecting oversized proposals is insufficient.
2. Measure propagation, decoding, MPT checks, ProcessProposal/FinalizeBlock, memory, signatures and real-guest cycles/latency for legal maximum combinations. Validate ordinary L+2 history and recovery's current origin, R registry and necessary L1 ancestry. Every valid block must be provable by at least one legal two-proof combination, and any grid segment of K consecutive blocks, worst case included, must be provable together in one landing (this sets the value of K); measure any claimed fallback combination too. Publish high percentiles, maxima, hardware and samples. Mock/host results do not replace real guests, and nodes cannot trim validation to local resource budgets.
3. Jointly validate 4 s production, 20 min landing cadence and the capacity budget under sustained target load across multiple complete epochs and settlement cycles, a saturated FI queue, blocks filled with incompressible data, competing/stale landing jobs, retries and L1 finality delays. Record peaks and headroom of height − anchor.lastCheckpoint.height directly. Cover U−1/U/U+1, single/multiple landings, several landings within one L1 block, switch boundaries, cold sync and governance recovery. Normal target scenarios must sustain progress with headroom; over-budget scenarios must pause normally and resume under the rules when the cause clears. Missing the 20 min target does not itself authorize recovery.
4. Budget proving for full B=h−p, not suffix h−c, and check B and the leaf count before allocation. Calldata carries the leaves of every grid point in (p,h), B/K − 1 leaves of 32 bytes each for the chosen K (for 300 blocks, 99 leaves and 3,168 bytes at K = 3, or 49 leaves and 1,568 bytes at K = 6). Add ABI, statement, two proofs and transaction overhead; these are byte counts, not measured gas. Validate both verifiers, grid and leaf checks, landing storage writes within the L1 transaction gas, six-blob and guest limits; the guest authenticates all B+1 cuts and computes cutHash over the grid-point leaves. The worst single grid segment (K maximum-size blocks) must land independently, and target multi-segment batches must pass too. Do not skip grid or leaf checks, remove a proof or raise limits to pass. DAO costs include repeated prefix blobs, submissions that fail because the target was already settled, and retries.
5. Reuse the same immutable two-proof result across p=c, p\<c\<h, c≥h, c at p and at every grid point in (p,h), the same transaction succeeding unchanged after a competitor lands first, repeated frontier advances in one L1 block, epoch boundaries, historical origins, cross-generation rejection, tampering with the leaf at c or other leaves, out-of-range heights/FI cursors. If optional public blob replay is supplied, exercise it over the same cases; the independent replay program is not a launch requirement. The settled prefix is never executed or consumed twice, and slot 285 always records actual c→h. During sustained shortest-landing interference (one grid segment each), the same transaction for an honest large batch must still advance to h or observe h already settled; one successful overlap is insufficient. Under 8.3/8.4, test recovery preconditions, replay protection, atomicity and list identity, separately for empty, exclusion, FI-blanking and combined cases.
6. Unified zk-gas acceptance must cover ordinary EVM revert/OOG versus zk discard, uint64 overflow in intrinsic/addition/multiplication, exact-limit versus over-limit cases, direct and nested precompile calls, and atomic rollback of nonce/authorizations/logs/receipts/fees. Resource budgets must include the actual block, FI singleton counterfactual and the entire first crossing atomic operation/precompile, not merely final difficulty. Even discarded unpaid FI trials require real legal two-proof acceptance for the worst valid grid segment, multi-segment batches, repeated expensive FI entries and sustained queue service. A guest-verifiable cost check may end work certain to be discarded only if proved equivalent to the shared execution result, EVM failure precedence and metering; a host estimate or timeout is insufficient. Do not deploy a configuration unable to prove these valid blocks: adjust and pin EL/guest/limits and repeat acceptance, never skip at runtime based on local budgets.
7. Measure complete L1 registry transactions with all 4096 slots held and cold storage: `register`, `topUp`, `activateTopUp`, `requestExit`, `confirmExit`, `withdraw`, `slashDoubleSign` and `forfeit`, including all required minimum-index maintenance, token transfers, signature checks, checkpoint/root updates and events. Include full-registry displacement and index-update worst cases for distinct balances, ties, zero-balance held slots and pending top-ups; repeat each operation after other operations change the minimum or slot contents. Measure registry exclusion updates inside complete `recover` transactions in section 2 as well. Publish the implementation, compiler/EVM settings, state setup, required gas limit before refunds and receipt gas usage. Demonstrate that the worst-case successful transactions fit the deployment L1's per-transaction gas cap ([EIP-7825](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7825) on Ethereum) and block gas limit; an index that makes slashing, forfeiture or withdrawal unexecutable does not pass acceptance. The 4096-slot bound or an asymptotic-complexity claim alone is insufficient; the admission data structure remains an implementation choice.

Do not claim deployment readiness or target attainment before acceptance; reassess resources/scheduling and review the spec after failures.

## 2. Recovery acceptance (spec section 8)

Sections 8, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4 and 9.1 require joint implementation and acceptance. Editing the spec, passing mock/host checks or producing blocks after recovery does not replace a first real two-proof landing becoming finalized.

1. Exercise empty-list, offline-member exclusion, FI blanking and combined recovery from stall through DAO scheduling to the first real two-proof landing becoming finalized. Check c, generation, FI head and the Inbox balance, and that confirmed exits keep aging across recovery.
2. Cover c=D(t), S(t), H(t)−1 (c can only be a grid point, so test with values of L that put these heights on grid points), first block at S(t), repeated recovery at one c, admission of newly eligible standby candidates, excluded entries staying out, slot reuse, displaced and withdrawn bonds in the exclusion list and entryKey mismatch. Nodes/guests must derive identical R snapshots, genesis, powers, schedules and proposers.
3. Cover non-owner calls, execution via both the standard path and an emergency path that skips the veto period (Inbox does not distinguish them), changed lists and replay of executed calls. Test stale generation/c/nonce, expired authorizations, owner-only nonce invalidation, initialization guards, rollback to an old c without revival, nonce overflow, reverted-operation retries, and failures/reentrancy at every atomic step without partial effects.
4. Cover landing-before-recovery and recovery-before-landing, plus missing checkpoint/origin records. Recovery preserves the settled prefix and never revives old authorizations or old-generation signatures. Test that signers keep HRS state through crashes, restarts and failover without reset or rollback.
5. Rebuild and settle using an independent archive. Publish the L1 ancestry from R to the reference block, R registry witness, necessary ordinary committee history, bundle size, node resources, both guests' cycles/memory/latency and L1 gas under combined measured loads; do not omit authentication. Separately publish the maximum supported outage for proving and landing an old suffix without recovery (spec 6.3).

## 4. Launch gate (spec section 10)

- **Capital target**: before launch the manifest pins an independent `C_CAP_MIN` (the target value of stake controlling enough power to block a commit), a conservative per-TAIKO `P_REF` in the same currency, and its source, haircut, validity, review frequency and responsible owner. In TAIKO base units, set `S_MIN_TOTAL = ceil(3 * C_CAP_MIN * 10^18 / P_REF)`. Require positive inputs and publish the concrete values; unspecified or expired inputs do not pass initial launch acceptance.
- **Initial committee**: on the actual committee-0 snapshot require both `sum(selected effStake) >= S_MIN_TOTAL` and `ceil(totalPower / 3) * vp_unit * P_REF / 10^18 >= C_CAP_MIN`. The second check uses integer voting power because commit needs strictly more than 2/3. Unselected stake and pendingTopUp do not count. Each entry and known common-control aggregate must be strictly below 1/3 power; retain the ZkRequiredVerifier requirement in 6.2. This is a launch-acceptance gate, not an on-chain price oracle or block-validity rule. The capital target is not a guarantee of acquisition cost or irreversible loss; no loss target divided by the forfeiture rate enters the formula.
- **Operation and recovery**: monitor each actual committee and revalue periodically; estimate the recovery committee before authorization and reassess finalized R, including activation, top-ups, exits, slashing, forfeiture, withdrawal and displacement since authorization. Admission stays permissionless through the end of execution block R: third-party registrations and successive DAO-funded registrations can displace intended candidates, so pre-authorization estimates do not reserve the set; re-check the actual finalized R selection, online quorum and concentration before new-generation signing. A shortfall or expired valuation requires alerts, public disclosure of reduced security and a replenishment plan, not blocked recovery, rotation or signing. Existing authentication, online-quorum and concentration checks still apply. The DAO may fund multiple independent online owners to top up, or register, displacing the smallest-balance bond when every slot is held; DAO-controlled entries aggregate as one controller, while funding alone does not establish common control. Owner-only topUp does not let the DAO directly deposit into others' entries. The vp_unit bound only prevents overflow and guarantees no reaction window.
- **Upgrade readiness**: the first release of nodes and both guests reads slot 293 from every anchor, selects each block's ruleset and planned stop, and refuses rulesets it does not implement (spec 10.1), with vectors for an unknown ruleset, a block at `rulesTime − 1` and at `rulesTime`, and a stopped grid point.
- Pass section 1's resource/latency acceptance and section 7's snapshot/app import, genesis/origin availability and EL-only serving acceptance, and jointly validate two-proof settlement, latest-checkpoint checks, grid cuts and recovery under 8.3/8.4. Do not claim readiness until real guests, recover's execution-time checks, signers, independent archives, the switch rehearsal (section 8) and first real settlement pass, or advertise unmeasured recovery duration as a service commitment.

## 5. FI backlog example (spec 7.3)

For illustration only, use a fee of 0.01 ETH, threshold = 50 and initial pending = 0, with no L1 head advancement while submitting 900 entries. The total is `0.01 × (900 + 900 × 899 / (2 × 50)) = 89.91 ETH`. Once the entries are due, consuming 900 entries takes about 60 min at a sustained target interval of 4 s without additional stalls. This excludes waiting for entries to become due, L1 finality and settlement pauses and is not a wall-clock guarantee. Production fees, threshold, service rate under sustained attack and honest-user costs still need evaluation; this example is not production anti-backlog acceptance.

## 6. Upgrade acceptance (spec 10.1)

Editing the spec or publishing a ruleset definition does not replace these checks; they apply before each `scheduleRules`, and before the first structural upgrade.

1. Publish the ruleset's definition as a change to an earlier ruleset. The client, the EL and both guests pass its shared vectors: blocks at `rulesTime − 1` and `rulesTime`, a forced-inclusion singleton trial on both sides, one landing whose segments cover blocks on both sides, one grid segment holding blocks of three rulesets after a lossless production pause between two activations, a first block after such a pause that carries an older anchor and a timestamp just after its parent's, cold-sync replay across `rulesTime`, and an image without the new ruleset proving earlier segments but producing no proof for any segment that contains a block at or after `rulesTime`.
2. Contract vectors for `scheduleRules`, `scheduleStop` and `recover`'s `rules`: the deadline at `block.timestamp + maxAnchorAge` one second before, at and after the pending time, promotion, rescheduling, cancellation with 2\^48 − 1 and with zeros, numbers other than `nextRules` and `nextRules + 1`, a `grid` that is not a positive multiple of K, non-owner and uninitialized calls, the emitted slot value, and `recover` clearing the stop and accepting only `rules` 0 or `nextRules + 1`.
3. Rehearse on a test deployment: a rule upgrade with software rolled out before `rulesTime`; the same with at least 1/3 of the voting power not upgraded, showing the pause at `rulesTime` and automatic resumption once it upgrades; a cancellation before the deadline; and a structural upgrade, including one that changes K, through a planned stop, settlement of its grid point, one DAO transaction with `recover`, and the first real two-proof landing of the new generation becoming finalized.

## 8. Switch rehearsal (SWITCH.md)

The procedure is in [SWITCH.md](SWITCH.md).

1. Run the whole SWITCH.md procedure on a test network that runs Unzen, from a live Unzen chain through the first Etna landing, before mainnet. Record the measured outage from the propose cutoff to the first Etna block, and check that the time from the first Etna block to the first adopted Etna landing fits within U × block time.
2. Exercise the failure paths: a wrong start or stateRoot (nodes refuse it under SYNC-9-5a and every landing reverts under LAND-6.1-8a); a missing `Anchor` upgrade with correct start values (nodes refuse it under SYNC-9-5a and proofs fail under PROOF-6.2-46a); a pruned node restarting after the first landing (it becomes trusted); `setOrigin` before `ETNA_FORK_TIME` or with a zero hash (reverts); the Unzen Inbox upgrade on the live proxy (the cutoffs take effect); a late DAO proposal (its first action reverts); a superseded L2 upgrade message relayed after its replacement executed (it reverts on its implementation precondition); and a late Unzen final proof (Etna pauses on back-pressure and resumes once a landing is adopted by an Etna anchor, BACKP-6.4-8).
3. Run the LAND-6.1-8a vectors against the Inbox implementation, the PROOF-6.2-46a vectors against both guests, and the grid-from-S vectors (LAND-6.1-5, PROOF-6.2-43, ENC-A-33) against the Inbox and both guests, and check the interfaces LAND-6.1-8a reads (`getCoreState()` with its three fields, and `getCheckpoint`) against the deployed Unzen Inbox and the upgraded SignalService, including that, with every proposal finalized, the deployed Unzen Inbox reports `nextProposalId` = `lastFinalizedProposalId` + 1.
4. Check bridge continuity: an L2 → L1 withdrawal from a block at or below S and one from a block above S, and an L1 → L2 message sent before the cutoff and processed after the switch.
5. Confirm that S's timestamp is below `ETNA_FORK_TIME`, that the L2 `Anchor` proxy's EIP-1967 implementation slot at S holds the Etna `Anchor` implementation address that clients and guests build in, that the L1 SignalService `VERSION` is unchanged by the switch proposal, and that the proposal's cross-check of `ETNA_FORK_TIME` passed on L1 and on L2.
6. Confirm on the deployed Unzen Inbox and its derivation the facts the `PROPOSE_MARGIN` bound uses: at most one proposal per L1 block, at most 10 forced inclusions per proposal, and single-block forced-inclusion sources.
