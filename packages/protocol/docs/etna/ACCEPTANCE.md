# Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist

This page collects the acceptance, shared vectors and launch gate for [Etna PoS + ZK Design Spec](SPEC.md); the spec body remains authoritative for validity rules.

## 1. Resource and latency acceptance (spec 6.4)

1. Pin U, n_max, maxBatchBlocks, EL gas/zk-gas limits, maxAnchorAge and software/hardware configuration. Test the worst legal combination: all 128 seats occupied, a LastCommit and a commit from a set of 128 members with as many missing validators as a valid LastCommit allows, each of them precommitting nil rather than being absent, so that all 128 signatures remain (CONS-3.4-7a; absent votes would understate the block size and the signature work, and `missBitmap` itself is a fixed 16 bytes), a mode switch whose validator updates replace 128 members with 128 other keys (COMM-4.3-13a), the anchor witness and an 8,192-byte FI payload. Bound the complete Protobuf block conservatively and demonstrate a legal proposal always fits the envelope's share of `block.max_bytes` (START-10-21) and all other limits; optional transactions may be reduced but the anchor witness and forced-inclusion data may not be omitted. Merely rejecting oversized proposals is insufficient. Exercise the U bound together with the reference-header bound (BATCH-6.3-6, BATCH-6.3-7) in two separate runs. (a) Produce a full unsettled suffix of U blocks at the sustained 4 s interval, whose oldest anchor is then about 50,400 L1 blocks old, and settle it through ordinary landings, recording the reference-header chain each landing authenticates and checking that it stays within the pinned bound (reference value 131,072 headers). (b) Produce a fresh U-block suffix the same way, withhold every landing while L2 stays paused at U for roughly 80,000 more L1 blocks (about 11 days), until the ancestry back to the suffix's oldest anchor exceeds the bound, then check that an ordinary landing of the suffix fails and that governance recovery discards it, restarting from R; run (b) takes about 18 days of wall-clock time.
2. Measure propagation, decoding, MPT checks, ProcessProposal/FinalizeBlock, memory, signatures and real-guest cycles/latency for legal maximum combinations, including the app's validator-state hash, whose preimage grows with the committee size, and the staking words of each post-state that the guests read through the state witness: `snapshotHeight`, `snapshotTotalStake`, all 128 snapshot seat records and the key-to-seat words of missing validators (PROOF-6.2-12, PROOF-6.2-29a). Validate the proof-origin inputs (PROOF-6.2-12a, PROOF-6.2-12b, PROOF-6.2-12c, PROOF-6.2-47), recovery's current origin and necessary L1 ancestry. Every valid block must be provable by at least one legal two-proof combination, and each such block, worst case included, must be provable on its own within one landing's two proofs (MODEL-1-2); measure any claimed fallback combination too. That worst case includes the block-start call (CONS-3.2-13), which neither gasUsed nor zk gas meters: measure the real-guest cycles of an `onBlockStart` call that spends its whole 30,000,000 gas on the EVM work that costs the guests the most cycles per unit of gas, since an upgrade can install any code there (STAKE-4.1-40), once in the block and once in the block's 7.2 singleton trial, which makes the same call (FI-7.2-8), together with the guest cycles of the deployed contract's worst case of item 7. Publish high percentiles, maxima, hardware and samples. Mock/host results do not replace real guests, and nodes cannot trim validation to local resource budgets.
3. Jointly validate 4 s production, 20 min landing cadence and the capacity budget under sustained target load across multiple complete settlement cycles, a saturated FI queue, blocks filled with incompressible data, competing/stale landing jobs, retries and L1 finality delays. Record peaks and headroom of height − anchor.lastCheckpoint.height directly. For blocks filled with incompressible data, state the landing rate they need separately from the 20 min target (at most six such blocks fit one landing, so 4 s blocks need about one landing every 24 s), measure the net rate at which settlement keeps up with production, and show the unsettled count falling again after an induced landing outage. Record the peak age of the newest finalized L1 block that nodes see across L1 epoch boundaries, including boundaries with missed slots, and show that the pinned maxAnchorAge keeps headroom above it (PARAM-11-5). Cover U−1/U/U+1, single/multiple landings, several landings within one L1 block, switch boundaries, cold sync and governance recovery. Normal target scenarios must sustain progress with headroom; over-budget scenarios must pause normally and resume under the rules when the cause clears. Missing the 20 min target does not itself authorize recovery.
4. Budget proving for full B=h−p, not suffix h−c, including a commit of up to 128 signatures for every block when the signing set changes at every block (item 7), and check B and the leaf count before allocation. Calldata carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h), B − 1 leaves of 32 bytes each (for 300 blocks, 299 leaves and 9,568 bytes). Add ABI, statement, two proofs and transaction overhead; these are byte counts, not measured gas. Validate both verifiers, the leaf checks, landing storage writes, including the three-word slot 285 record, within the L1 transaction gas, six-blob and guest limits; the guest authenticates all B+1 cuts and computes cutHash over the leaves of the heights strictly inside (p,h). The worst single block must land on its own, and target multi-block batches must pass too. Exercise a proof origin p whose anchor is beyond the reference-header bound: the guest authenticates that anchor's root, which it uses only for the set that signs p + 2, through p's EL header and p's slot 285 record (PROOF-6.2-12b, PROOF-6.2-33), while every anchor of (p,h] keeps the ordinary L1 ancestry check. Reject a sibling header, a shortcut across generations and omission of reference ancestry for p+1’s inherited anchor. Do not skip leaf checks, remove a proof or raise limits to pass. DAO costs include repeated prefix blobs, submissions that fail because the target was already settled, and retries. Measure the reference block's age at L1 inclusion, retries included, against the window in which its hash stays readable (256 blocks, or 8,191 with EIP-2935).
5. Reuse the same immutable two-proof result across p=c, p\<c\<h, c≥h, c at p and at every height in (p,h), the same transaction succeeding unchanged after a competitor lands first, repeated frontier advances in one L1 block, changes of signing set and mode switches inside the batch (BATCH-6.3-2), historical origins, cross-generation rejection, tampering with the leaf at c or other leaves, out-of-range heights/FI cursors. If optional public blob replay is supplied, exercise it over the same cases; the independent replay program is not a launch requirement. The settled prefix is never executed or consumed twice, and slot 285 always records actual c→h. During sustained shortest-landing interference (one block each), the same transaction for an honest large batch must still advance to h or observe h already settled; one successful overlap is insufficient. Under 8.3/8.4, test recovery preconditions, replay protection, atomicity and list identity, separately with an empty and a nonempty fiToClear list.
6. Unified zk-gas acceptance must cover ordinary EVM revert/OOG versus zk discard, uint64 overflow in intrinsic/addition/multiplication, exact-limit versus over-limit cases, direct and nested precompile calls, and atomic rollback of nonce/authorizations/logs/receipts/fees. Resource budgets must include the actual block, FI singleton counterfactual and the entire first crossing atomic operation/precompile, not merely final difficulty. Even discarded unpaid FI trials require real legal two-proof acceptance for the worst valid block, multi-block batches, repeated expensive FI entries and sustained queue service. A guest-verifiable cost check may end work certain to be discarded only if proved equivalent to the shared execution result, EVM failure precedence and metering; a host estimate or timeout is insufficient. Do not deploy a configuration unable to prove these valid blocks: adjust and pin EL/guest/limits and repeat acceptance, never skip at runtime based on local budgets.
7. Measure complete L2 staking transactions with cold storage and every seat occupied at n_max = 128: `register`, `topUp`, `requestExit`, `withdraw`, `slashDoubleSign` and `forfeit`, the last through its L1 → L2 governance message (FORF-4.4-1), including the lookup of the lowest empty seat, token transfers, signature checks, the seat, key-to-seat and lookup updates (STAKE-4.1-14d) and events. Include a registration that takes the last empty seat, a registration rejected because every seat is held (STAKE-4.1-14a), a top-up that gives a bond below minStake its seat again (STAKE-4.1-14c), and an exit, a double-sign slash and a forfeiture that each release the lowest and the highest seat. Repeat each operation after other operations change the seats. Measure the block-start call (CONS-3.2-13) at the call-level bound of STAKE-4.2-8, calling `onBlockStart` directly as the system caller, since a legal block cannot set every bit: all 128 seats occupied, every bit of `missBitmap` set and every seated bond reaching missLimit in the same call, once with every penalty leaving its bond at or above minStake and once with every penalty releasing its seat, followed by the write of all 128 snapshot seat records; show that the call completes within the 30,000,000 gas available to its frame (STAKE-4.2-8), and that a call that runs out of gas leaves the state as it was and the block valid (CONS-3.2-13); item 2 measures what the call costs the guests. Measure both guests' cost when the signing set differs at every block, for example because a top-up or a missed-signature penalty changes one member's power at every height: a commit of up to 128 signatures verified for every block of a maximum batch (PROOF-6.2-16, spec section 12, item 32); item 4's proving budget includes it. Publish the implementation, compiler/EVM settings, state setup, required gas limit before refunds and receipt gas usage. Demonstrate that the worst-case successful transactions fit L2's block gas limit (CONS-3.2-7) and any per-transaction gas cap the L2 EL applies; a data structure that makes slashing, forfeiture or withdrawal unexecutable does not pass acceptance. The seat bound or an asymptotic-complexity claim alone is insufficient; the seat data structure remains an implementation choice. Repeat the gas measurements of `land` in item 4 and of `recover` and the verifier-failure rollback in section 2 before an L1 fork that changes gas costs or the per-transaction gas cap activates (spec section 12, item 10).

Do not claim deployment readiness or target attainment before acceptance; reassess resources/scheduling and review the spec after failures.

## 2. Recovery acceptance (spec section 8)

Sections 8, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4 and 9.1 require joint implementation and acceptance. Editing the spec, passing mock/host checks or producing blocks after recovery does not replace a first real two-proof landing becoming finalized.

1. Exercise the three offline cases of section 8 from the first missed signature through any DAO scheduling to the first real two-proof landing becoming finalized. Where a recovery executes, check c, generation, FI head and the Inbox balance, and that exits set at or below c keep their `exitHeight` while registrations, top-ups, exits, withdrawals, penalties and forfeitures above c are void (RECOV-8-59).
   - Less than 1/3 of the candidate's power offline: consensus continues, each offline member is penalized after missLimit consecutive misses and loses its seat once a penalty leaves it below minStake, and each penalty first changes the set that signs two heights later (RECOV-8-20). No recovery takes place. Then keep one member signing every block and hold back its precommits. Judge each case by the `block_id_flag` that the next block's LastCommit carries for the member and by the proposer's own record of what it did with the precommit (stock CometBFT logs at Debug level a late precommit that it adds to LastCommit and one that it ignores), not by the time at which the precommit reached the proposer's node. A precommit of the committing round that the proposer processes while it is still in the step that precedes the next height's first round is added, and no miss is counted; the same holds for one that it processes at that height before it has finalized the block. A precommit that it processes after it has left that step is ignored and counts as a miss, and so does a precommit of a round other than the committing one. Repeat the ignored case for missLimit consecutive blocks: the member is penalized like an offline member (LIMIT-12-33). Check on every validator that the effective `timeout_commit` is no lower than the pinned value (START-10-6).
   - At least 1/3 of the candidate's power offline: in one proposal, `setMinTotalStake` to 2\^256 − 1 and then `recover`; the fallback key set signs from c + 1 on, which the check follows through c + 3 and later heights, because c + 1 and c + 2 are its blocks whatever the threshold is (COMM-4.3-9); while it signs, `forfeit` each offline bond through an L1 → L2 governance message; then lower `minTotalStake` again and observe the candidate signing once it is usable, the whole set changing at once (RECOV-8-21). Repeat with c's L2 state written so that the candidate at c + 1 is a valid set whose stored `snapshotTotalStake` is 2\^256 − 1, and show that the fallback key set still signs c + 3 (COMM-4.3-3a). Repeat with the threshold left as it was and show that the candidate from c's L2 state, offline members included, signs c + 3 whenever it is usable.
   - At least 1/3 of the fallback key set offline while it signs: `setFallbackKeys`, then `recover`; the new keys sign c + 1 and c + 2 and every later block the fallback key set signs (RECOV-8-22).
   - Combine each recovery with an empty and with a nonempty fiToClear list (FI blanking).
   - Publish the two L1 gas limits and a conservative upper bound for the complete DAO execution transaction. Include calldata and call forwarding, a `setFallbackKeys` with 128 keys or a `setMinTotalStake` made first in the same proposal, FI clearing, origin writes and events. Cover an empty fiToClear list and the largest one the DAO may authorize, and check the complete authorized transaction against gas limits required by transactions on the pinned L1 fork before refunds (RECOV-8-39). Repeat the gate when the implementation or gas schedule changes.
2. Cover c at the heights just before and just after a mode switch, c at a block whose block-start call failed, and repeated recovery at one c. Check that the origin record's fallback keys sign c + 1 and c + 2 whatever c's L2 state holds (COMM-4.3-9), that 4.3 decides the set that signs c + 3 at c + 1 from that block's candidate snapshot, that double-sign evidence whose slash was discarded is accepted again in the new generation and that a forfeiture executed above c can be executed again (RECOV-8-59, FORF-4.4-21). Cover a recovery from a checkpoint above a block that more than 2/3 of the voting power decided with a header whose app_hash commits a different validator state: honest nodes stop there (consensus stops or block sync stalls, without a process exit), independent execution, the FI cursor recomputed under 7.2, the 6.2 header checks and the other 3.4 rules confirm c's records and blocks, no verifier-failure rollback is performed and the new generation starts from c (RECOV-8.1-7, RECOV-8.1-8). Cover a recovery from a checkpoint above a height at which more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signed, with honest nodes holding the block L1 did not settle: the settled chain is verified from the last block they share, no rollback is performed, and those nodes take the EL state at c in place of their own blocks. In both, include a settled block that legally skips a due, eligible forced transaction, which takes a singleton build on the parent state: the verification reproduces the cursor from the queue under each block's anchor and the enqueue events' raw bytes without reading a slot 285 record, and a skip whose singleton build is not empty is a mismatch that leads to the rollback. Also include a settled chain in which every recorded item matches and the height of one block is U + 1 above the lastCheckpoint.height its anchor proves: the verification applies every 3.4 rule, finds that block invalid, and the rollback precedes recovery (RECOV-8.1-8). Also include a landing in which every block passes those rules and the anchor of one covered block is one L1 block later than the landing's reference block: the verification checks each landing with the p, h and reference block of its own L1 transaction and treats this one as a mismatch (PROOF-6.2-33). Repeat that case with honest nodes that executed and hold the exact settled block at c, where every listed item matches: the landing check is still made, from the audit boundary and without executing those blocks again, and the rollback precedes recovery. Cover a recovery from a c whose L2 state holds every seat, with a test supply above 10\^27 base units on L2 so that more than n_max bonds meet the seat conditions, the case spec section 12, item 11 describes: the new generation keeps c's seats, a registration or a top-up to minStake reverts until a seat is released (STAKE-4.1-14a), and a released seat goes to the next bond that meets the conditions. Include a recovery in which more than 1/3 of the fallback key set's voting power holds no consensus history of the old generation, its keys having been added by `setFallbackKeys` or its nodes re-synced through the 8.4 genesis path: those nodes become trusted at c by the EL hash and the origin checks alone, sign the first block, and compare both hashes from the new generation's first landing on (SYNC-9-5). Nodes and guests must derive identical genesis, validator sets, powers and proposers from the origin record, c's L2 state and the anchors. For a new snapshot that leaves every key and power unchanged, and for a mode switch between a candidate and a fallback key set with the same keys and powers, compare both guests’ statements for batches that end immediately before and at the first block the new set signs: neither adds an intermediate commit, and both sets are still derived and checked under 4.3 (PROOF-6.2-17).
3. Cover non-owner calls, execution via both the standard path and an emergency path that skips the veto period (Inbox does not distinguish them), changed lists and replay of executed calls. Test stale generation/c/nonce, expired authorizations, owner-only nonce invalidation, initialization guards, rollback to an old c without revival, nonce overflow, reverted-operation retries, and failures/reentrancy at every atomic step without partial effects.
4. Cover landing-before-recovery and recovery-before-landing, plus missing checkpoint/origin records. Recovery preserves the settled prefix and never revives old authorizations or old-generation signatures. Test that signers keep HRS state through crashes, restarts and failover without reset or rollback. Check both supported signer transports, rejection of a wrong peer, reconnection after a read timeout, and refusal at the wrong height with HRS unchanged. Include a non-one initial height and an imported boundary. Crash the app and the EL, separately and together, at each step between FinalizeBlock and the completion of Commit, and after Commit before the EL has persisted the block. Also crash CometBFT at the point where the app's Commit has returned and CometBFT has not yet saved its own state for that height: its handshake then brings that state forward from the stored FinalizeBlock response without executing the block on the app again. For EL lag after Commit, check that the supervisor and app restore the missing block before starting the handshake. Withhold the recovery input, observe the reported height and closed signing gate, then supply it and verify the same app hash. Each restart must pass the stock CometBFT handshake and reach the same state without manual repair (CONS-3.1-9). The exception is a generation's first block when initial_height is above 1, where stock CometBFT stops at its handshake after such a crash (CONS-3.1-9): crash there too and record the outcome. For this first block, rehearse the single-validator and committee-wide crash separately with the pinned binaries and documented repair procedure. Retain original stores, WALs, external signer states, decided block and commit. Demonstrate that each recovered node authenticates exactly that BlockID under its genesis validator set, aligns Comet/app/EL at that boundary or a verified descendant, and continues the same certified chain. Repeat with the block or certificate unavailable: affected validators stay fenced. Record inputs, restore commands, time to first subsequent commit and failure behavior; a plain store wipe and re-proposal do not pass. Include a node whose EL head is above c when recovery executes: its EL must end at c's authenticated hash and stateRoot (RECOV-8.4-8) and accept the new generation's first block. Include a recovery that finalizes on L1 while c stays where it is, no new proposal arrives and no signer reconnects: every running node of the old generation revokes its trust once its own finalized L1 view shows the new generation, and refuses old-generation signing requests (SYNC-9-6a).
   - Rebuild a previously signing validator from a checkpoint below its durable signing height H while its WAL/lock is missing. Restart and fail over during catch-up. Even after matching an older finalized-L1 target, all proposals and votes through H, including later rounds and nil votes, stay fenced by a durable floor of at least H + 1. Verify a same-generation committed chain covering H before reopening above H. Try a wrong committee, wrong BlockID, uncertified peer head and lost or uncertain HRS; none releases signing. Delete stores manually without using the importer; test changed and missing instance IDs, and an old WAL copied with the matching ID. In each case the signer itself persists the floor before a vote. Compare a restart from the predecessor with complete matching WAL and no block sync against a restart that block-syncs one block to that same predecessor: only the former can replay the lock and resume at H. Also import a settled tip s above the generation origin, start with a fresh WAL, sign at H = s + 1 and restart at that undecided height without syncing a block or state. Keep the same store binding and WAL bytes. With no `#ENDHEIGHT s`, verify stock starts after logging the replay error and the signer persists a floor of at least H + 1 before any fresh signature. In the successful replay case verify the prefix after the required marker, including the `#ENDHEIGHT 0` case at a non-one initial height. Fence exactly one third at an undecided H without enough outstanding precommits to certify it and verify that no fresh quorum can form; exercise new-generation recovery.
   - With enough otherwise healthy voting power for a commit, withhold session trust after the prevote so the first precommit is refused and its signature is absent. Restore trust, restart from the matching predecessor and observe the saved prevote retry and the first durable higher-HRS precommit during replay, its delivery, and a verified commit for that block at H. Delay the restarted session’s trust check as a separate run: fresh signing stays closed while untrusted, then the automatic restart path must still produce the vote and commit. Restart again after persisting that first precommit to check its binding to the startup WAL, including the input that triggered it. Exercise two input orderings: (a) receive more than two thirds of prevotes before the last block part, sign the precommit live when that part arrives and refuse a vote in a later round at H; (b) receive the complete round-zero proposal before the NewHeight timeout, sign the prevote live and refuse the precommit. In both runs, require the first signature for the refused vote during replay, delivery to peers and a verified commit at H. Include the round-state records written after the triggering input and before signing.
   - Hard-stop a signer-backed node after a durable signature with a partial trailing WAL record beyond its bound prefix. Let stock repair it with signing closed. Verify the recovered prefix against the latest HRS and require a fresh confirmation from the predecessor before resuming at H. Repeat with corruption inside the bound prefix: verify the H + 1 floor. Delay log consumption across a repair or live fallback and verify a matching RPC cannot reuse the previous attempt’s admission.
   - Start with the local proposal durably signed and recorded in the WAL, its prevote refused, and no ValidBlock. Change the builder inputs so replay requests a proposal for a different BlockID at the same HRS. Verify the signer refuses it, then stock accepts the original signed WAL proposal and parts. After independently checking the restored round and lock, require a fresh prevote during replay, delivery and a verified commit with quorum. Repeat with a conflicting POLRound and with the original proposal missing: neither the HRS nor an unverified replay log authorizes a fresh vote.
   - For a validator using the log observer, run with consensus filtered to Error, then with a stock-supported format incompatible with the configured parser. Each startup must diagnose the effective setting and keep observer-dependent signing closed. With matching format, Info and Error, and the dedicated synchronous pipe, verify the trusted replay path succeeds.
   - Run the real signer and stock WAL. Crash after vote inputs are flushed and synced and the signer durably saves HRS, sign bytes and signature but before the reply or local vote is recorded. With recovered lock state, replay identical bytes and a timestamp-only change; verify the original timestamp/signature, refusal of a different BlockID at equal HRS, and uninterrupted fencing of the old instance.
   - Exercise a long pause accumulating high rounds with voting power sufficient to stall. Measure the elapsed pause, highest round, WAL size, replay time, signer refusals and time to the next commit after the cause clears. Compare the WAL size with the stock 1 GiB limit, drive one height past that limit with peer traffic, and show that a validator restarted there has no eligible replay and takes the floor (RECOV-8.2-2). Show that a validator refuses a P2P connection from a node its operator has not listed and dials none, and that a node connected only to the validator's sentry cannot grow the validator's WAL with messages the sentry does not accept (START-10-6). Record retries for unavailable signers and the time to first PubKey separately from signing readiness; withhold L1 trust until stock P2P catches up. Test a missed 3 s initial connection and a restarted, reachable but untrusted signer. Ping/PubKey work and no vote or proposal leaves the signer before admission.
5. Rebuild and settle using an independent archive. Publish the L1 ancestry from R to the reference block, every `fallbackKeys` list that the origin record and the anchors commit (SYNC-9-16), the proof-origin inputs (PROOF-6.2-12a, PROOF-6.2-12b, PROOF-6.2-12c), bundle size, node resources, both guests' cycles/memory/latency and L1 gas under combined measured loads; do not omit authentication. Repeat after blob pruning and EL snapshot rotation with the primary archive offline. Restore audit evidence independently, reject a gap, a receipt from a voided landing and a digest without verifiable support, then check the remaining landings against their exact original blob bytes and finalize recovery. Separately remove a required pruned blob from both archives without equivalent evidence, and withhold the settled fork’s Comet headers or commits during equivocation. In both exercises record the missing predicates and keep ordinary recovery pending even if available public replay matches the L1 writes. Do not classify a missing input as a verifier failure. Separately publish the maximum supported outage for proving and landing an old suffix without recovery (spec 6.3).
6. Exercise the verifier-failure rollback (section 8) as one DAO upgrade: the restored checkpoint and FI head, voided later landing records and checkpoints, kept earlier and Unzen checkpoints and the incremented recovery nonce. Publish the upgrade's gas as a function of the number of voided landings, next to the deployment L1's per-transaction gas cap. Demonstrate that revocation takes effect at every read, proof-origin, import and retry entry point in the same upgrade, even when voided storage remains. Include the largest supported invalid suffix; an upgrade that can exceed the cap while the affected authorizations remain usable fails acceptance. Include a bond whose exit was set in a block X with X + 2 above the restored checkpoint: measure the time from the first bad landing to the executed upgrade against the evidence window, the part of `withdrawalDelay` beyond U + 2, and check that the bond is still unwithdrawn when the upgrade executes (RECOV-8-35, PARAM-11-15). Between the bad landing and the pause, cache a forged and a legitimate signal and leave a forged and a legitimate message, each with a nonzero fee, `RETRIABLE`. After the upgrade, neither an empty proof nor a retry is accepted for any of them until it is proven again against a checkpoint `SignalService` holds; the legitimate ones complete once a checkpoint `SignalService` holds after the rollback covers them, with each fee paid only once, the forged ones cannot be proven against any checkpoint the later landings write, and `SignalService` and the Bridge stay paused until the upgrade executes (RECOV-8-27a).
7. Switch between the candidate and the fallback key set in both directions on stock CometBFT v0.40.0 (COMM-4.3-13, COMM-4.3-13a): FinalizeBlock(H) returns one update list that gives power 0 to every key of the set that signs H + 1 not in the new set and adds or re-weights the others, and the whole set changes at H + 2. Cover 128 members replaced by 128 other keys, a fallback key that is also a seated bond's key (returned with its new power, not removed), the first switch from a generation's genesis keys to a usable candidate, switches at consecutive heights, since there is no hysteresis (spec section 12, item 26), and switches caused by a stake change, a missed-signature penalty, a DAO change of `minTotalStake` or `fallbackKeys` and a failed block-start call. Check that stock CometBFT accepts each update list, the validator-state hash and appHash (SYNC-9-5c), the proposer priorities of the new members, both guests' verification of the commit of the last block the old set signs (PROOF-6.2-17), and a restart of each node at every height around the switch.
8. Drop the candidate's total stake below `minTotalStake` and check that the fallback key set takes over two heights after the block whose candidate snapshot first falls below it, without a pause while its holders keep, for every key, a validator instance of its own and that key's signer running (COMM-4.3-20). Before the switch, confirm that each key's instance has caught up with the chain, and after it observe a vote from each expected validator address: processes and signers that are merely running do not pass, because they can leave too little power able to sign. Include a holder of two keys, which runs two instances, each bound to one key, and show that a single instance votes for only one address whatever signers run beside it (COMP-2-1a). Cover each cause of the switch: an exit or a forfeiture in block X lowers the snapshot at X + 1 and hands X + 3 to the fallback key set; a missed-signature penalty in H's block-start call hands H + 2 to it (RECOV-8-20); a `minTotalStake` raised above the candidate's total stake hands it H + 2 for the first block H whose anchor is at or after the change, and one set to 2\^256 − 1 does so whatever total the snapshot holds (COMM-4.3-3a, COMM-4.3-19); and a failed block-start call in H does the same (COMM-4.3-3a). Repeat with a third or more of the fallback keys offline: consensus halts at the first height they must sign, and item 1's procedure for an offline fallback key set restarts it (RECOV-8-22).

## 3. Shared vectors (spec appendix A)

Compare config, `extraData` with `missBitmap`'s bit order (ENC-A-10, ENC-A-10c), the block-start call's calldata (ENC-A-10d), `fallbackKeysHash` and the fallback key set as validators (ENC-A-36a, ENC-A-36b), `SeatMember` (ENC-A-36c), the `FallbackKeysUpdated` and `MinTotalStakeUpdated` events (ENC-A-36d, ENC-A-36e), the base-fee and mixHash formulas, the blob encoding, statements, the four-word cutLeaf encoding, the validator-state hash, appHash and cutHash, land/recover/sweepProtocolFees interfaces and storage layouts, including the staking words that nodes and guests read (STOR-B-21) and packed-field widths and order, byte for byte. ## 5. FI backlog example (spec 7.3)

For illustration only, use a fee of 0.01 ETH, threshold = 50 and initial pending = 0, with no L1 head advancement while submitting 900 entries. The total is `0.01 × (900 + 900 × 899 / (2 × 50)) = 89.91 ETH`. Once the entries are due, consuming 900 entries takes about 60 min at a sustained target interval of 4 s without additional stalls. This excludes waiting for entries to become due, L1 finality and settlement pauses and is not a wall-clock guarantee. It also leaves out settlement lag: pending counts entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed (FI-7.1-1), so while 900 consumed entries await landing the next submission costs `0.01 × (50 + 900) / 50 = 0.19 ETH`, even though no entry waits on L2. Production fees, threshold, service rate under sustained attack and honest-user costs still need evaluation; this example is not production anti-backlog acceptance.
