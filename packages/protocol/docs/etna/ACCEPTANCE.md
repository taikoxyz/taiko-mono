# Etna Acceptance and Launch Checklist

This page collects the acceptance, shared vectors and launch gate for [Etna PoS + ZK Design Spec](SPEC.md); the spec body remains authoritative for validity rules.

## 1. Resource and latency acceptance (spec 6.4)

1. Pin U, L, n_max, maxBatchBlocks, EL gas/zk-gas limits, maxAnchorAge and software/hardware configuration. Test an anchor spanning U single-block landings, combined with rotation, all 4096 registry slots, maximum-committee commits and an 8,192-byte FI payload. Bound the complete Protobuf block conservatively and demonstrate a legal proposal always fits 22,020,096 bytes and all other limits; optional transactions may be reduced but the anchor witness, committee rotation data and forced-inclusion data may not be omitted. Merely rejecting oversized proposals is insufficient.
2. Measure propagation, decoding, MPT checks, ProcessProposal/FinalizeBlock, memory, signatures and real-guest cycles/latency for legal maximum combinations, including the app's validator-state hash, whose preimage grows with the committee size. Validate ordinary L+2 history and recovery's current origin, R registry and necessary L1 ancestry. Every valid block must be provable by at least one legal two-proof combination, and each such block, worst case included, must be provable on its own within one landing's two proofs (MODEL-1-2); measure any claimed fallback combination too. Publish high percentiles, maxima, hardware and samples. Mock/host results do not replace real guests, and nodes cannot trim validation to local resource budgets.
3. Jointly validate 4 s production, 20 min landing cadence and the capacity budget under sustained target load across multiple complete epochs and settlement cycles, a saturated FI queue, blocks filled with incompressible data, competing/stale landing jobs, retries and L1 finality delays. Record peaks and headroom of height − anchor.lastCheckpoint.height directly. Record the peak age of the newest finalized L1 block that nodes see across epoch boundaries, including boundaries with missed slots, and show that the pinned maxAnchorAge keeps headroom above it (PARAM-11-5). Cover U−1/U/U+1, single/multiple landings, several landings within one L1 block, switch boundaries, cold sync and governance recovery. Normal target scenarios must sustain progress with headroom; over-budget scenarios must pause normally and resume under the rules when the cause clears. Missing the 20 min target does not itself authorize recovery.
4. Budget proving for full B=h−p, not suffix h−c, and check B and the leaf count before allocation. Calldata carries the leaf of every height strictly inside (p,h), B − 1 leaves of 32 bytes each (for 300 blocks, 299 leaves and 9,568 bytes). Add ABI, statement, two proofs and transaction overhead; these are byte counts, not measured gas. Validate both verifiers, the leaf checks, landing storage writes, including the three-word slot 285 record, within the L1 transaction gas, six-blob and guest limits; the guest authenticates all B+1 cuts and computes cutHash over the leaves of the heights strictly inside (p,h). The worst single block must land on its own, and target multi-block batches must pass too. Do not skip leaf checks, remove a proof or raise limits to pass. DAO costs include repeated prefix blobs, submissions that fail because the target was already settled, and retries. Measure the reference block's age at L1 inclusion, retries included, against the window in which its hash stays readable (256 blocks, or 8,191 with EIP-2935).
5. Reuse the same immutable two-proof result across p=c, p\<c\<h, c≥h, c at p and at every height in (p,h), the same transaction succeeding unchanged after a competitor lands first, repeated frontier advances in one L1 block, epoch boundaries, historical origins, cross-generation rejection, tampering with the leaf at c or other leaves, out-of-range heights/FI cursors. If optional public blob replay is supplied, exercise it over the same cases; the independent replay program is not a launch requirement. The settled prefix is never executed or consumed twice, and slot 285 always records actual c→h. During sustained shortest-landing interference (one block each), the same transaction for an honest large batch must still advance to h or observe h already settled; one successful overlap is insufficient. Under 8.3/8.4, test recovery preconditions, replay protection, atomicity and list identity, separately for empty, exclusion, FI-blanking and combined cases.
6. Unified zk-gas acceptance must cover ordinary EVM revert/OOG versus zk discard, uint64 overflow in intrinsic/addition/multiplication, exact-limit versus over-limit cases, direct and nested precompile calls, and atomic rollback of nonce/authorizations/logs/receipts/fees. Resource budgets must include the actual block, FI singleton counterfactual and the entire first crossing atomic operation/precompile, not merely final difficulty. Even discarded unpaid FI trials require real legal two-proof acceptance for the worst valid block, multi-block batches, repeated expensive FI entries and sustained queue service. A guest-verifiable cost check may end work certain to be discarded only if proved equivalent to the shared execution result, EVM failure precedence and metering; a host estimate or timeout is insufficient. Do not deploy a configuration unable to prove these valid blocks: adjust and pin EL/guest/limits and repeat acceptance, never skip at runtime based on local budgets.
7. Measure complete L1 registry transactions with all 4096 slots held and cold storage: `register`, `topUp`, `activateTopUp`, `requestExit`, `confirmExit`, `withdraw`, `slashDoubleSign` and `forfeit`, including the lookup of the lowest empty slot, token transfers, signature checks, checkpoint/root updates and events. Include a registration that takes the last empty slot, a registration rejected because every slot is held, zero-balance held slots and pending top-ups; repeat each operation after other operations change slot contents. Measure registry exclusion updates inside complete `recover` transactions in section 2 as well. Publish the implementation, compiler/EVM settings, state setup, required gas limit before refunds and receipt gas usage. Demonstrate that the worst-case successful transactions fit the deployment L1's per-transaction gas cap ([EIP-7825](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7825) on Ethereum) and block gas limit; a data structure that makes slashing, forfeiture or withdrawal unexecutable does not pass acceptance. The 4096-slot bound or an asymptotic-complexity claim alone is insufficient; the admission data structure remains an implementation choice. Repeat these measurements, and the gas measurements of `land` in item 4 and of `recover` and the verifier-failure rollback in section 2, before an L1 fork that changes gas costs or the per-transaction gas cap activates (spec section 12, item 10).

Do not claim deployment readiness or target attainment before acceptance; reassess resources/scheduling and review the spec after failures.

## 2. Recovery acceptance (spec section 8)

Sections 8, 8.2, 8.3, 8.4 and 9.1 require joint implementation and acceptance. Editing the spec, passing mock/host checks or producing blocks after recovery does not replace a first real two-proof landing becoming finalized.

1. Exercise empty-list, offline-member exclusion, FI blanking and combined recovery from stall through DAO scheduling to the first real two-proof landing becoming finalized. Check c, generation, FI head and the Inbox balance, and that confirmed exits keep aging across recovery.
2. Cover c=D(t), S(t), H(t)−1, first block at S(t), repeated recovery at one c, admission of newly eligible standby candidates, excluded entries staying out, slot reuse, withdrawn bonds in the exclusion list and entryKey mismatch. Cover a recovery from a checkpoint above a block that more than 2/3 of the voting power decided with a header whose app_hash commits a different validator state: honest nodes stop there (consensus stops or block sync stalls, without a process exit), independent execution, the FI cursor recomputed under 7.2, the 6.2 header checks and the other 3.4 rules confirm c's records and blocks, no verifier-failure rollback is performed and the new generation starts from c (RECOV-8.1-7, RECOV-8.1-8). Cover a recovery from a checkpoint above a height at which more than 1/3 of the voting power double-signed, with honest nodes holding the block L1 did not settle: the settled chain is verified from the last block they share, no rollback is performed, and those nodes take the EL state at c in place of their own blocks. In both, include a settled block that legally skips a due, eligible forced transaction, which takes a singleton build on the parent state: the verification reproduces the cursor from the queue under each block's anchor and the enqueue events' raw bytes without reading a slot 285 record, and a skip whose singleton build is not empty is a mismatch that leads to the rollback. Also include a settled chain in which every recorded item matches and the height of one block is U + 1 above the lastCheckpoint.height its anchor proves: the verification applies every 3.4 rule, finds that block invalid, and the rollback precedes recovery (RECOV-8.1-8). Also include a landing in which every block passes those rules and the anchor of one covered block is one L1 block later than the landing's reference block: the verification checks each landing with the p, h and reference block of its own L1 transaction and treats this one as a mismatch (PROOF-6.2-33). Repeat that case with honest nodes that executed and hold the exact settled block at c, where every listed item matches: the landing check is still made, from the audit boundary and without executing those blocks again, and the rollback precedes recovery. Cover a recovery with every slot held and no landing possible: a registration is rejected, an exit that took effect after activeCommitteeSnapshot cannot be confirmed, and selection uses only bonds already registered (spec section 12, item 11). Nodes/guests must derive identical R snapshots, genesis, powers, schedules and proposers.
3. Cover non-owner calls, execution via both the standard path and an emergency path that skips the veto period (Inbox does not distinguish them), changed lists and replay of executed calls. Test stale generation/c/nonce, expired authorizations, owner-only nonce invalidation, initialization guards, rollback to an old c without revival, nonce overflow, reverted-operation retries, and failures/reentrancy at every atomic step without partial effects.
4. Cover landing-before-recovery and recovery-before-landing, plus missing checkpoint/origin records. Recovery preserves the settled prefix and never revives old authorizations or old-generation signatures. Test that signers keep HRS state through crashes, restarts and failover without reset or rollback. Crash the app and the EL, separately and together, at each step between FinalizeBlock and the completion of Commit, and after Commit before the EL has persisted the block. Also crash CometBFT at the point where the app's Commit has returned and CometBFT has not yet saved its own state for that height: its handshake then brings that state forward from the stored FinalizeBlock response without executing the block on the app again. Each restart must pass the stock CometBFT handshake and reach the same state without manual repair (CONS-3.1-9). The exception is a generation's first block when initial_height is above 1, where stock CometBFT stops at its handshake after such a crash (CONS-3.1-9): crash there too and record the outcome. What a node does next is an open review question ([Q3.1-8](rules/review/3.1.md#q31-8)), so a repair used there, a wipe of the CometBFT stores included, is recorded as an observation and not as an accepted procedure. Include a node whose EL head is above c when recovery executes: its EL must end at c's authenticated hash and stateRoot (RECOV-8.4-8) and accept the new generation's first block.
5. Rebuild and settle using an independent archive. Publish the L1 ancestry from R to the reference block, R registry witness, necessary ordinary committee history, bundle size, node resources, both guests' cycles/memory/latency and L1 gas under combined measured loads; do not omit authentication. Separately publish the maximum supported outage for proving and landing an old suffix without recovery (spec 6.3).
6. Exercise the verifier-failure rollback (section 8) as one DAO upgrade: the restored checkpoint, FI head and snapshot, voided later landing records and checkpoints, kept earlier and Unzen checkpoints, re-checked exit confirmations and the incremented recovery nonce. Publish the upgrade's gas as a function of the number of voided landings, next to the deployment L1's per-transaction gas cap. Between the bad landing and the pause, cache a forged and a legitimate signal and leave a forged and a legitimate message, each with a nonzero fee, `RETRIABLE`. After the upgrade, neither an empty proof nor a retry is accepted for any of them until it is proven again against a checkpoint `SignalService` holds; the legitimate ones complete once a checkpoint `SignalService` holds after the rollback covers them, with each fee paid only once, the forged ones cannot be proven against any checkpoint the later landings write, and `SignalService` and the Bridge stay paused until the upgrade executes (RECOV-8-27a).

The recovery rehearsal also covers the [two-stage trust-revocation procedure](RECOVERY.md): a committee that releases an invalidating landing during an authorization, ordinary Inbox pause leaving landing enabled, all accepted proof combinations and complete image/program inventory, proofs prepared before revocation, revocation of only one ZK lane leaving another combination enabled, both lanes revoked and finalized before the tuple is read, exact-tuple recovery, and restoring only intended trust after recovery finalizes. Record the separate governance transactions, finality observations and first real two-proof landing; contract tests with remote cryptography stubbed do not establish this joint acceptance.

## 4. Launch gate (spec section 10)

- **Capital target**: before launch the manifest pins an independent `C_CAP_MIN` (the target value of stake controlling enough power to block a commit), a conservative per-TAIKO `P_REF` in the same currency, and its source, haircut, validity, review frequency and responsible owner. In TAIKO base units, set `S_MIN_TOTAL = ceil(3 * C_CAP_MIN * 10^18 / P_REF)`. Require positive inputs and publish the concrete values; unspecified or expired inputs do not pass initial launch acceptance.
- **Initial committee**: on the actual committee-0 snapshot require both `sum(selected effStake) >= S_MIN_TOTAL` and `ceil(totalPower / 3) * vp_unit * P_REF / 10^18 >= C_CAP_MIN`. The second check uses integer voting power because commit needs strictly more than 2/3. Unselected stake and pendingTopUp do not count. Each entry and known common-control aggregate must be strictly below 1/3 power; retain the ZkRequiredVerifier requirement in 6.2. This is a launch-acceptance gate, not an on-chain price oracle or block-validity rule. The capital target is not a guarantee of acquisition cost or irreversible loss; no loss target divided by the forfeiture rate enters the formula.
- **Operation and recovery**: monitor each actual committee and revalue periodically; estimate the recovery committee before authorization and reassess finalized R, including activation, top-ups, exits, slashing, forfeiture and withdrawal since authorization. Admission stays permissionless through the end of execution block R: third-party registrations and successive DAO-funded registrations can outrank intended candidates in selection, so pre-authorization estimates do not reserve the set; re-check the actual finalized R selection, online quorum and concentration before new-generation signing. A shortfall or expired valuation requires alerts, public disclosure of reduced security and a replenishment plan, not blocked recovery, rotation or signing. Existing authentication, online-quorum and concentration checks still apply. The DAO may fund multiple independent online owners to top up, or to register while an empty slot remains; DAO-controlled entries aggregate as one controller, while funding alone does not establish common control. Owner-only topUp does not let the DAO directly deposit into others' entries. The vp_unit bound only prevents overflow and guarantees no reaction window.
- **Upgrade readiness**: the first release of nodes and both guests reads slot 293 from every anchor, selects each block's ruleset and planned stop, and refuses rulesets it does not implement (spec 10.1), with vectors for an unknown ruleset, a block at `rulesTime − 1` and at `rulesTime`, and a block whose parent's timestamp has reached `stopTime`.
- Pass section 1's resource/latency acceptance and section 7's snapshot/app import, genesis/origin availability and EL-only serving acceptance, and jointly validate two-proof settlement, latest-checkpoint checks, per-height cuts and recovery under 8.3/8.4. Do not claim readiness until real guests, recover's execution-time checks, signers, independent archives, the switch rehearsal (section 8) and first real settlement pass, or advertise unmeasured recovery duration as a service commitment.

## 5. FI backlog example (spec 7.3)

For illustration only, use a fee of 0.01 ETH, threshold = 50 and initial pending = 0, with no L1 head advancement while submitting 900 entries. The total is `0.01 × (900 + 900 × 899 / (2 × 50)) = 89.91 ETH`. Once the entries are due, consuming 900 entries takes about 60 min at a sustained target interval of 4 s without additional stalls. This excludes waiting for entries to become due, L1 finality and settlement pauses and is not a wall-clock guarantee. It also leaves out settlement lag: pending counts entries already consumed on L2 but not yet landed (FI-7.1-1), so while 900 consumed entries await landing the next submission costs `0.01 × (50 + 900) / 50 = 0.19 ETH`, even though no entry waits on L2. Production fees, threshold, service rate under sustained attack and honest-user costs still need evaluation; this example is not production anti-backlog acceptance.

2.
