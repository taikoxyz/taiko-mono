Taiko · Preconfer Auction · Design Walkthrough

The Preconfer Auction

How Taiko becomes permissionless: a perpetual auction of preconfer / proposer rights on L1, with TAIKO bonds that are slashed on liveness faults, and a fallback ladder so the chain never stalls.
Companion to packages/protocol/docs/auction_based_permissionless_preconf.md — use ← → arrow keys, buttons, or the dots below.
Step 0 · Why we need this

The problem

Today: permissioned preconf

The PreconfWhitelist picks one operator per epoch (beacon-root randomness). If that operator goes offline, nobody else can propose for the rest of the epoch — a 6.4-minute stall, with no slashing.

Permissionless path is blocked

The URC/lookahead design (PR #22012) depends on Ethereum's consensus layer — validator lookahead, proposer commitments, block anatomy. ePBS, FOCIL and SSLE break those assumptions, and the URC itself is not production-ready.

Goal: permissionless preconfirmation with liveness as the #1 priority, built only on primitives Ethereum cannot take away: L1 inclusion, EVM state, and bonded collateral.
Step 1 · The big idea

A perpetual standing-bid auction

A ranked list of standing bids lives on L1. No repeated auctions, no keeper, no churn:

#1 bid → WINNER
Exclusive right to propose & preconfirm each epoch until outbid, quit, expiry, or slashing
#2 bid → BACKUP
Bonded hot standby; inherits automatically if the winner leaves
… → ranked list
Up to 16 bonded bidders, ranked by amount, ties by placement order
EMPTY LIST
Total permissionless mode: bonded operators first, then anyone
  • Bids are ETH — a per-epoch rate paid from a prepaid balance (the market price of one epoch's sequencing revenue).
  • Every listed bidder stakes TAIKO — the slashable liveness bond. No yield; it is collateral, not a PoS reward.
  • The auction sells a privilege, not a necessity: chain liveness never depends on the winner.
Step 2 · The time model

Epochs & the 2-epoch lead

1 epoch = 32 slots = 384 s  ·  every transition takes effect 2 epochs after placement

Every change — bid, outbid, re-bid, renewal, signer rotation, quit — gets effectiveEpoch = placedEpoch + 2. Consequences:

  • FINALITY During epoch E, the assignments for E and E+1 are always final — nothing placed now can alter them. No boundary races.
  • HANDOVER A new or displaced winner gets ≥ 1 epoch (384 s) of notice to boot their preconf service.
  • PENDING Changes to an existing entry are pending updates materialized with the same lead — a re-bid can't forfeit an epoch you already won, a renewal can't resurrect an expired bid into a finalized epoch, a signer rotation can't rewrite slashing evidence.
epoch E−2
you place your bid
epoch E−1
assignment for E is computed & fixed
epoch E
you serve (your per-epoch fee is charged lazily)

Step 4 · Joining

Bond + bid

1. Deposit TAIKO
depositBond(amount) ≥ required bond
→
2. Deposit ETH
depositEth() prepays per-epoch fees
→
3. Bid
bid(amountInGwei, signer) ≥ reserve floor
→
4. Serve
effective 2 epochs later; one fee charged per epoch served
  • OUTBID To displace the current top, bid ≥ top × 1.05 (5% minimum increment). Lower bids enter the list normally.
  • RENEW renew() extends expiry by tenureMax — cheap, no increment, no re-ranking; applies with the 2-epoch lead.
  • QUIT quit() — effective 2 epochs later; you must keep serving until then (going dark early = stall slash). Quit wins over pending changes.
  • EXIT GUARD The fixed next-epoch winner cannot withdraw the ETH reserved for the epoch they already won.

Reserve floor = max(initialFloor, moving-average of charged bids × multiplier) — self-bids can't game it because only charged amounts feed the average.

Step 5 · Assignment & payment

Promote, don't recompute

snapshot(E) runs lazily — first proposal of the epoch (or anyone calls snapshot())
→
Promote the cached next assignment (computed last epoch) — never recomputed, so it can't be changed retroactively
→
Charge the winner one bid amount of ETH from their prepaid balance (unpayable → lapsed, backup inherits)
→
Record the epoch's winner + signer (S1 evidence), then compute next epoch's assignment
  • O(1) HOT PATH checkProposer does a constant number of SLOADs + one SSTORE — the whitelist's beacon-root scan disappears entirely.
  • FINALITY Re-bids, renewals and signer rotations only materialize for future epochs — an epoch you won is yours.
  • BACKFILL A permissionless snapshot() poke backfills up to 32 missed epochs, so even zero-proposal epochs get winners recorded and fees charged (S1 disputes keep working).
Step 6 · Serving an epoch

Propose & preconfirm

  • The winner proposes one Taiko proposal per L1 block via the normal based path — the Inbox asks the auction checkProposer(proposer, lookahead), which returns the window end.
  • The winner's sequencer signs preconfirmations (EIP-712, secp256k1) over each built block's header — for p2p authenticity, failover, and S1 disputes. Signatures are not a validity input; the ZK prover still enforces block properties.
  • Each winner proposal writes lastWinnerProposalAt = block.timestamp atomically with the proposal tx — a revert or reorg rolls the clock back with the block. The clock always describes the canonical chain.
absence = block.timestamp − max(epochStart, lastWinnerProposalAt)

Promises are epoch-bounded: the winner owns the current epoch outright, so in-flight promises complete even if they get displaced afterwards.

Step 7 · Liveness first

The fallback ladder

Rungs gate on winner absence — backup proposals do not reset the clock, so backups run at full cadence during an outage:

Rung 0 · WINNER
Always allowed in their epoch. One proposal reclaims exclusivity.
Rung 1 · BACKUP
Opens after absence > STALL_GRACE (e.g. 48 s). Full cadence.
Rung 2 · BONDED
Any operator with a bond ≥ ejection threshold, after + BACKUP_GRACE.
Rung 3 · ANYONE
After + FALLBACK_GRACE: pure based proposing.
  • ACCIDENT-SAFE The ladder opens at STALL_GRACE, but the slash escrow only fires after ESCROW_GRACE ≥ STALL_GRACE — a 4-slot L1 inclusion delay or a shallow reorg lets backups serve without ever slashing an honest winner.
  • UNAssigned Empty list → bonded operators may propose from epoch start, anyone after the grace. Liveness never depends on the auction.
  • PRICED, NOT PROVED A winner posting empty blocks (heartbeat censorship) keeps the clock alive but earns no fees; broken promises are individually slashable (P1/P2).
Step 8 · The liveness fault

Recording the stall gap

When a fallback proposer's proposal is accepted while absence > ESCROW_GRACE, the auction atomically:

1. Debit min(livenessBond, winner's bond) from the winner into contract custody
→
2. Record the fault — winner, amount, challenger, and the gap [gapStart, escrowedAt]
→
3. Accept the proposal — liveness is never blocked by the slashing machinery
One escrow per epoch (later fallback proposals don't double-debit), and the slash is not final yet — a 1-epoch refute window follows.

gapStart = the absence clock's base = max(epochStart, lastWinnerProposalAt) — the exact moment the silence began.

Step 9 · Punishment must be disprovable

The refute window

To escape the slash, the winner must disprove the recorded gap itself via refuteStall(epoch, proposal):

proposer == winner  &&  timestamp ∈ (gapStart, escrowedAt]  &&  hash(proposal) == Inbox.getProposalHash(id)

Can refute — reorged winner

Their block was reorged out but re-included inside the gap → the canonical preimage exists → escrow released.

Cannot refute — silent or strategic

A silent winner has no canonical block in the gap. A strategic staller's wake-up proposal lands after the gap — it does not disprove the recorded silence, closing the "stall 48 s, propose once, never pay" loophole.

Merely proposing again does not release the escrow — only the gap-disproof does. The evidence is plain L1 state: no beacon lookahead, no header parsing.

Step 10 · Settlement

Settle & eject

After escrowedAt + REFUTE_WINDOW (1 epoch), anyone calls settleStallSlash(epoch):

One-shot digest
same fault can never settle twice
→
Split
rewardBps (≤ 50%) to the challenger who actually proposed; remainder locked forever
→
Eject
bond below livenessBond × bondMultiplier → bid removed, withdrawal delay starts, backup inherits at the next boundary
Why ≤ 50%? Above half, a winner could self-slash through a sybil challenger and profit — stalling would be free. The cap keeps every liveness fault a net loss.
Step 11 · Safety faults

S1: operator-signed blocks

Every block the winner builds is signed (EIP-712 over the header, domain bound to the proxy address). Two cheap, provable disputes:

Invalid block

Timestamp outside the epoch window (or other self-evident property). slashInvalidBlock: recover the signer, match the per-epoch recorded signer, check the property → full slash.

Equivocation

Two different signed blocks for the same (blockNumber, parentHash). slashEquivocation: both signatures recover to the winner's recorded signer → full slash.

  • ROTATION-PROOF The epoch's signer is recorded before any pending rotation materializes — you can't escape an epoch's liability by rotating keys.
  • WINDOW Records persist forever, but slashing is economically effective while the winner's bond remains (quit + withdrawal delay). Unbonded winner → NoBondToSlash, the one-shot digest is not burned.
  • SCOPE Only properties provable from the signed payload + epoch context; everything the ZK prover checks stays on the proving path.

Step 13 · The last line of defense

Inbox escape hatch

Even a buggy checker must not halt the chain. The Inbox now:

Gas-isolates the checker: try checkProposer{ gas: 3_000_000 } — bounded, catchable
→
On revert, allows permissionless proposing while the escape hatch is open: oldest forced inclusion overdue beyond forcedInclusionDelay × permissionlessInclusionMultiplier
→
Otherwise rejects with PermissionlessProposingNotAllowed
The authorization runs before forced inclusions are consumed, so the hatch observes the very stale inclusion it is gated on. Beyond the auction: forced inclusion + based proposing remain the censorship floor — the chain inherits Ethereum's liveness end-to-end.

Recap

One-pager

Permissionless

Anyone with a bond can bid; the top bid serves, the runner-up stands by, an empty list means anyone can propose.

Liveness first

Winner-absence ladder at full cadence; slash escrow decoupled from the ladder; forced inclusion + Inbox escape hatch as the floor.

Fair punishment

Every fault is provable from L1 state; stall slashes are gap-disprovable for one epoch; reward capped at 50%; one-shot digests; ejection, not death-spirals.

Consensus-independent

No lookahead, no proposer commitments, no header parsing — safe through ePBS, FOCIL, and SSLE. FOCIL inclusion lists compose as a bonus.
