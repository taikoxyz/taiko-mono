PoS Preconfirmation Design: Etna and CometBFT

An open-stake committee gives preconfirmations in about two seconds. Anyone can submit a batch that carries DA, a valid QC and the required pair of proofs, and L1 keeps a single settled frontier. The committee is derived deterministically from fixed historical snapshots, with no per-epoch maintenance transaction. Forced inclusions are processed in fixed service slots during normal consensus. On failure, production pauses while valid batches can still be submitted; permanent failures rely on governance.

PoS fast confirmation with atomic ZK settlement. Based on Etna and CometBFT v0.40.0. Every "must" and "must not" below is a protocol requirement and needs one coordinated fork; parameters and acceptance criteria are in the appendix.

Terms

seat
A fixed-size stake unit in the registry. Voting power is counted in seats.
leader
The CometBFT proposer of a height; CometBFT rotates it.
QC
Quorum certificate: a CometBFT commit with precommit signatures from strictly more than 2/3 of the voting power. The Comet height equals the EVM block number.
settledHead
The last L2 block accepted on L1 (height, blockHash, stateRoot, contextHash): the only L1 execution frontier.
Context
The proven settlement context: configId, fiCursor, anchorId and a commitment to consensus metadata.
anchor
The L1 block an L2 block is anchored to; anchorId is the hash of its canonical header.
observation certificate
Signatures from strictly more than 2/3 of the committee weight, made with dedicated observation keys, that endorse one finalized L1 anchor tuple.
U
The back-pressure bound: a block may be at most U heights above the settled height seen at its anchor.
FI
Forced inclusion: a single-blob request in the L1 queue.
proofPolicy
The required proof combination: RISC0_RETH AND SP1_RETH.

1. Scope and guarantees

GuaranteeBoundary
Execution and ordering Every accepted batch must satisfy the dual-ZK policy of §3; the proofs cover a valid Comet QC, execution and the protocol rules. An ordinary EVM revert is still valid execution.
FI In normal operation, a due queue head that is visible in the common L1 view gets one full processing opportunity in a reserved slot; invalid skip or zk abort can discharge the item, and success of every transaction is not guaranteed. FI cannot advance on its own when the committee loses quorum.
Preconfirmation and recovery No promise that every mempool request is included within two seconds. A timeout does not automatically discard the committed suffix; valid existing batches can still be submitted. Handling the suffix after a permanent failure needs an explicit governance process.
Admission and fees Fixed bond per seat, a limited active set (about 100 seats targeted), open application, no admission within bounded time. Ordinary transactions pay for execution; there is no prepaid preconfirmation tip and no separate deadline or refund ticket for timely fair exchange.
External dependencies Depends on L1 finality and inclusion, data availability, committee quorum and full proof supply. An L1 finality stall can make anchors stale or exhaust U and pause production; this version promises neither committee-free exit nor permanent-failure recovery without governance.

Comet safety requires malicious voting power below 1/3, and a QC needs strictly more than 2/3. Observation certificates also need strictly more than 2/3: with honest observers signing only true statements, malicious or compromised observation weight of at most 2/3 cannot forge one on its own. Refreshing the view needs more than 2/3 of honest weight available and able to sign the same candidate, plus progressing L1 finality and eventual network synchrony. The weight denominator does not shrink when members are offline or abstain.

2. State and interfaces

StateMeaning
settledHead The last accepted L2 height, blockHash, stateRoot and contextHash. It is the only L1 execution frontier.
Context The settlement context: configId, fiCursor, anchorId and a commitment to consensus metadata (current and next set and pending updates). Its encoding version is fixed, and every proof binds this protocol's proof domain and configId.
lastAcceptedAt The time of the latest successful submitBatch that advanced the height, initialized at activation. It is for monitoring only and is never a reason to reject a batch or to end responsibilities automatically.
FI queue head and tail; each item has an immutable inputCommitment and single-blob reference, the actual enqueuedAt, and a serviceFee (base FI fee plus top-ups). head equals the settled Context.fiCursor.
committee[e] A committee commitment determined by consensus and recorded through normal settlement with ZK; it binds the source parent block and anchor, cutoff, root, encoding and target epoch, and H0/N fix its height range.
Registry Historical eligible-set checkpoints, bond identities, consensus and observation keys, weights, and exit/slashing state.

The interfaces are submitBatch, enqueueFI, topUpFI, and the Registry's staking, exit and double-sign reporting. There is no separate anchor registration interface or historical anchor table. Apart from the defined staking membership, there is no proposer, submitter or prover address allowlist.

configId must bind the version, L1 chainId, Inbox address, L2 chainId, protocol parameters, this protocol's proof domain and the complete proofPolicy (§3.1). Both proofs must authenticate the same batchCommitment, which binds the configuration, program version and proof domain, start and end head/context, height range, DA commitment, ordered FI input commitments, the complete, ordered and de-duplicated list of new anchor tuples, fiFeeRecipient, the epoch/committee commitment, the QC, and the next-committee record required by §5.2. The execution coinbase is fixed before proving and never derived from the final L1 msg.sender.

3. Atomic batch acceptance

submitBatch must verify:

  1. The starting blockHash and contextHash equal settledHead exactly, the start and end context fields re-hash correctly, and endHeight increases. There is no overlap and no partial acceptance; after a race, a stale start must be re-batched or re-aggregated.
  2. The actually published blob/data commitments, bounded batch size and encoding. An ordinary BlockRecord commits to the final EVM body; an FI BlockRecord references the original input and replays it; the guest binds both to the declared blocks. A proof does not replace DA.
  3. The L1 authenticity of inherited and new anchors per §4.1, the committee commitment, and the immutable input commitments of the current FIFO prefix. The mutable serviceFee is not part of that commitment, so top-ups do not invalidate existing proofs.
  4. Both proofs per §3.1. Each replays execution, ABCI consensus metadata and FI rules from the authenticated start and outputs endContext. It checks the last block's Comet header and QC — chain_id and height, the frozen root and ValidatorsHash, strictly more than 2/3 of the signing weight, the DataHash binding to the EVM hash and protocol envelope, and that AppHash matches the replayed previous state — and the parentHash chain binds the whole batch.
  5. Then atomically advance settledHead, the FI head, fees and lastAcceptedAt; the first batch of an epoch must also record the next committee of §5.2. Any failed condition reverts everything: no pending proposal is left behind, and no batch is accepted as "derives to zero blocks".

Pausing does not change acceptance. While production is paused, existing batches that satisfy these rules can still be submitted. FI due-ness is judged by each block's common anchor and canonical time; records that only become due at submission time do not retroactively rewrite committed batches.

3.1 Proof policy

The target proofPolicy is RISC0_RETH AND SP1_RETH: two different backends each provide a full proof, and L1 must verify each one and check that both cover the same batchCommitment. Repeated backends, different public inputs, unknown program versions, a single ZK proof, SGX + ZK or SGX + SGX do not satisfy this configuration.

configId fixes the two verifiers and verification keys, the guest programs and image IDs, the aggregation format and the public-input encoding. An unaudited single outer proof must not replace the two-sided AND check. Changing the combination or the programs needs a new configuration and an explicit upgrade, never a silent downgrade because proving is slow. This target is not a copy of the current mainnet combination and does not mean fast dual-ZK proving already exists.

Two zkVMs are not two independent execution implementations; a bug in the shared guest, EL logic, encoding or routing can affect both. The QC is verified inside the guest, so it is not an extra L1 safety gate against a top-level verifier bug. Launch must validate the full combination's security, public reproducibility, tail latency and cost; removing address allowlists does not replace these checks.

4. Anchors and Etna execution

4.1 Authentication at submission

anchorId is the hash of the canonical L1 header; the tuple is number/hash/stateRoot/timestamp. The ZK proofs must recompute the header hash, check that these fields come from that same header, and bind them to the execution and protocol checks that use the anchor; authenticating a hash field that is not tied to the stateRoot is not enough.

submitBatch distinguishes two cases:

  1. Inherited: only when anchorId equals the starting settled Context.anchorId may the batch reuse the accepted frontier's existing authentication; the proofs must still bind the full header and tuple. A root that appeared somewhere earlier in history does not bypass new authentication.
  2. New: the public inputs list every new tuple of the batch relative to the starting anchor — complete, de-duplicated and in order of first use. Every anchor actually used must come from the inherited one or this list, the list must match the batch's usage exactly, and MAX_ANCHORS_PER_BATCH bounds it. The contract reads each height's canonical L1 hash through BLOCKHASH or EIP-2935 and compares it with the header hash bound by the proofs; a current or future height, an unqueryable height or a mismatch is rejected.

EIP-2935 serves the most recent 8191 blocks directly, and fewer right after activation. New anchors must still be authenticatable at call time; U and fast-proving assumptions do not replace the per-anchor window check. A new anchor used several times in a batch is queried once; after success only the last anchorId is kept through endContext, with no persisted history list.

Over-age anchors or any other authentication failure revert the whole batch. A committed anchor must not be rewritten. This version does not enable the cross-window authentication of A.4, so a suffix that cannot be accepted under this version's rules goes to governance per §8. 8191 blocks is the direct query window, not a cryptographic limit on authentication; allowing valid late submission does not mean this version allows unbounded delay.

This check authenticates canonical L1; it does not prove finality at voting time, which still rests on the §5 observation certificates. Nodes must keep headers and witnesses; the L1 hash query window and RPC retention of historical eth_getProof are two separate requirements.

4.2 Common execution rules

Keep Etna's execution without an anchor transaction, the mixHash/base fee/gasLimit evolution, zk gas metering and the skip/abort/truncation semantics. parentBeaconBlockRoot is the chosen L1 execution stateRoot; the 13-byte extraData is basefeeSharingPctg(1) | fiCursor(6) | anchorNumber(6) with canonical big-endian uint48 encoding, and the old proposalId layout switches at the fork.

The anchor number never decreases, and the same number must keep the same anchorId; header fields are authenticated per §4.1 and match the tuple used in execution; L2 timestamps strictly increase and are never earlier than anchor.timestamp. At submission the last block's timestamp is not later than the current L1 block.timestamp, and no anchor comes from the future. All execution fields, the coinbase and the transaction order are fixed before proving; there is no default-manifest replacement and no rewriting after landing.

4.3 Production time and view freshness

A block uses the accepted anchor, updated or inherited per §5. Canonical time is max(parent.timestamp + 1, floor(Unix seconds of CometTime), anchor.timestamp); production checks timestamp − anchor.timestamp ≤ MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE, and pauses and refreshes observations independently when it is exceeded. BFT Time is not an unbiased random number.

This protocol drops Etna's MAX_ANCHOR_OFFSET and TIMESTAMP_MAX_OFFSET lower bounds relative to the landing origin and time. A committed block is never re-anchored or re-timed; its acceptance depends on continuity, the required proof combination and §4.1's authentication at submission. There is no separate Etna landing-age bound, but a newly accepted anchor is still limited by the history authentication window.

5. Consensus and committee

5.1 Comet and observations

The Comet height must equal EVM.number; chain_id binds configId and this protocol's consensus domain and differs from the EVM chainId. Consensus keys, ABCI observation keys and L1 sending keys are separate.

A candidate contains the EVM payload and hash, the protocol envelope, and any required observation certificates and MPT witnesses. AppHash commits to the execution head, the Context and the current and next committee state. ProcessProposal and the guests share the header, time, anchor, FI, DA and execution rules; an EL VALID alone is not enough. An ordinary Comet commit, Ethereum finality and ZK acceptance cannot stand in for each other.

Observers sign only after independently verifying the anchor's real L1 finality, chain and freshness; there is no prior registration to check. Off-chain endorsement does not replace the §4.1 L1 acceptance check. A signature binds this protocol's observation-message domain, configId, targetHeight, parentEVMHash, the valid committee commitment and the full anchor tuple. Keys and weights come from the set frozen at the time; duplicate identities never count twice, and rotation only affects future snapshots.

Updating the anchor needs a certificate from strictly more than 2/3 of the weight on the same tuple. ProcessProposal deterministically checks signatures, authorization, domain, monotonicity/ancestry and age, and never decides ACCEPT or REJECT from a node's live RPC response. Registry, FI and Inbox settled-height reads under the same root are authenticated with MPT proofs. Signatures over different tuples cannot be combined into an unsigned minimum or median.

An accepted anchor can be inherited; only an update needs a new certificate. Certificates are collected over a separate P2P/RPC channel, so startup, pauses and committee changes do not depend on new blocks. Observations are not constrained by Comet's H/R/S lock, but domains must be closed persistently, and old parents, expired authorizations and pre-signing for the future must be rejected. A member without an eligible observation may abstain.

Normal operation has no inclusion lists and no lander rotation; FI service slots follow §6. Every block has a deterministic DA cap and can land on its own. Taking the Inbox settled head authenticated under the same anchor as the frontier, the candidate chain must extend that prefix and satisfy h − settledHeight(anchor) ≤ U; U must cover the normal observation delay. Back-pressure may not be relaxed with another branch's settled height. There is no DA token bucket or daCredit. Comet keeps its time and clock constraints and the commit wait, with a minimum normal step δ_min = 1 s and a target of about 2 s; local running time never changes content validity.

5.2 Deterministic derivation and activation

H0 is the L2 height after the activation checkpoint, e(h) = floor((h − H0) / N) and H_e = H0 + eN; these are defined only for h ≥ H0, and a batch may not span epochs. The startup configuration authenticates the initial committee; later committees are derived by the rules below, with no per-epoch freeze or activation transaction.

Determined by consensus. At epoch e's first block H_e, take the anchor A_e already accepted in its parent state and use the cutoff C_e = S × floor((A_e.timestamp − D_registry) / S), with D_registry ≥ S > 0. From the Registry's historical checkpoints authenticated under A_e.stateRoot, select the end-of-block state of the last L1 block no later than C_e, including every eligibility change in that block, verified with a full snapshot or adjacent-boundary witness; missed slots do not change the rule. Startup needs enough lookback history; with missing data the first block is rejected, and no caller-chosen root is accepted.

The next committee is every active seat of that snapshot in canonical encoding, checked for unique members, positive weights, non-emptiness and the size cap; this version does not sample a sub-committee, and Comet rotates the proposer. The result goes into the protocol envelope/DataHash and the Context and is committed by this block's ordinary QC, with no extra voting round; any metadata that affects derivation must be bound by this commitment or by the continuously authenticated Context, never self-reported as an uncommitted witness. The record binds configId, targetEpoch = e+1, H_next, the source parent block hash, A_e.anchorId, C_e, the root and the encoding version; updating the anchor in the current block, the submission time and later stake changes cannot change it.

Recorded through proofs. The guests replay the derivation from the authenticated start. The first accepted batch of epoch e necessarily contains H_e and must output the full record as an authenticated public output; the contract writes committee[e+1] atomically. The same record is idempotent and a conflicting one is rejected; later batches check the commitment in the Context and cannot overwrite or re-derive it. L1 no longer computes the cutoff from the current block.timestamp; the record text is published and matched against the stored commitment.

Activated by height. The agreed new set is submitted in FinalizeBlock at H_next − 2 and takes effect at H_next. At the update point, an MPT proof under the common anchor must show that the L1 record matches the agreed value; with missing data or a mismatch the candidate is rejected, and observations can refresh independently per §5.1. This check does not re-decide membership and needs no separate L1 transaction.

The configuration must satisfy N ≥ U + 3. At the update point, settledHeight(anchor) ≥ H_e + N − 2 − U ≥ H_e + 1; strict continuous settlement and the rule that the first batch must record guarantee that the anchor's L1 state already contains committee[e+1]. So every committee that can legally activate has an L1 record, and an abandoned suffix can still be reported without special eligibility proofs.

Fixing membership does not wait for submitBatch, but actual production and rotation are still limited by U, anchor freshness and proof supply and cannot run arbitrarily far ahead of L1. The L1 record is only part of normal settlement; submitters get no power to choose the committee.

6. Mandatory FI in normal consensus

enqueueFI accepts a single blob with bounded encoding and decompressed size and records the actual call time as enqueuedAt; an old blob's publication time cannot stand in for it. inputCommitment binds configId, the queue index, enqueuedAt and the canonical blob reference. The base FI service fee is escrowed as serviceFee; topUpFI only adds to that balance and changes neither the input, the queue position nor the due time.

Fix M ≥ 2; heights with (h − H0) mod M = 0 are FI service slots. ProcessProposal and both guests must enforce:

  1. Authenticate queue[fiCursor] and tail with MPT proofs under the block's accepted anchor; an item is due only if its record is visible and block.timestamp ≥ enqueuedAt + FI_DELAY, measured on the canonical L2 timestamp.
  2. When a service slot has a due head, it must process exactly that item in a dedicated FI block with no ordinary transactions; without a due item it may be an ordinary block. Other heights consume no FI and keep their share for ordinary transactions.
  3. The original input order is fixed; timestamp, anchor, gasLimit and coinbase follow protocol rules, and the user's blob cannot override them. The item gets the full user gas and zk gas budget; authenticated malformed input or decoding over the limits is treated as empty input, and everything else follows Etna's invalid skip, zk abort and suffix truncation.
  4. Verify the body, stateRoot, receipts and zk gas, and advance the cursor by exactly one; even an empty output forms a real EVM block. Missing data, failed authentication or an omitted mandatory item make the candidate or proof invalid and cannot consume the cursor.

Each batch consumes the contiguous prefix [head, endCursor), not beyond tail or MAX_FI_PER_BATCH; the contract checks the same immutable input commitments, and only a successful acceptance advances head and pays serviceFee. Original FI data can reference the existing blob without being published again; the necessary records and envelope and a single FI block must fit into one valid submission on their own.

If a prefix of N_FI items, including the item itself, is visible and due, continuous normal production processes it within at most M × N_FI later heights, plus visibility and due time, proving and L1 acceptance. This depends on quorum, proofs and data availability, and there is no wall-clock bound during a halt. This version provides no committee-bypassing FI execution and no automatic refund on timeout.

7. Stake, penalties and fees

All active seats vote, with no sampling. Launch needs at least 4 active addresses; independent addresses do not mean independent entities. A Registry leaf permanently binds a unique bondId, consensus key, observationKey and weight; historical roots and keys cannot be overwritten, and re-entry uses a new bondId.

Double signing means the same chain_id, height, round and valid vote type with different BlockIDs (nil included). The epoch follows from the height; after verifying the signatures against the committee record already on L1 per §5.2 and a membership path, the protocol slashes and rewards the reporter. The epoch need not have any accepted batch, and a production pause or a later configuration upgrade does not cancel old responsibility. Different vote types are not double signing, and members cannot be punished only for a submitter's bad data.

An exit first removes the identity from future eligibility and records the actual L1 exitEffectiveAt, and the old bondId cannot rejoin. Before the withdrawal countdown starts, the contract checks that the old bondId is in none of the committees recorded on L1 whose end height is still above settledHeight; that set holds at most the current and the recorded next epoch, and the exiting party cannot choose it.

It must also authenticate the header A_s of the current settled Context.anchorId and check S × floor((A_s.timestamp − D_registry) / S) ≥ exitEffectiveAt, without substituting the current L1 time or a self-chosen anchor. If a derivation's first block has been accepted, its committee record is already on L1; if not, its source parent block lies after the settled frontier, and anchor monotonicity keeps its cutoff no earlier than this bound. This covers a next committee already fixed off-chain but not yet submitted, so a bond cannot be released before a missed responsibility appears.

Only after the responsibility-closing conditions hold does EVIDENCE_PERIOD start, counted from the accepted on-chain closing record; withdrawal comes after it. Reports are possible during the window, and slashing reduces the withdrawable balance. If the end of responsibility cannot be authenticated during a halt, the bond stays locked and is not released by a timeout. Unauthenticated reports do not extend the window; keys are retired only after the withdrawal is actually finalized, and an L1 reorg rolls back dependent transitions along with it.

There is no prepaid preconfirmation service fee. Ordinary EVM execution fees go to the proven coinbase. The base FI protocol fee and later top-ups form one serviceFee, escrowed at enqueue and paid once to the fiFeeRecipient bound in the proofs when a valid batch consumes the item; a copied submission cannot change the recipient. The fee buys one processing opportunity, so a valid skip or abort still earns it; missing data or an invalid proof consumes nothing and pays nothing.

The base FI fee and its congestion curve are set by configuration; today's mainnet 0.001 ETH is a reference for the base fee, not a full cost commitment for the new deployment. There is no dedicated lander reward and no proving liveness bond; normal submission, proving and DA costs must be estimated per the appendix.

At launch, the operator organizes and funds ordinary batch proving and submission, covering both proof backends, proving compute, DA, L1 gas and failed or competing retries. Batch ranges and jobs are coordinated off-chain, including re-batching or re-aggregation when a starting head becomes stale. This grants no exclusive submission right and does not change coinbase fee allocation. Long-term fee sharing or a proving market is not decided in this version; operator funding and proof supply remain liveness assumptions.

8. Pause, recovery and migration

An L1 finality stall, a stale anchor, exhausted U, or unavailable quorum or proofs can pause new production. Alerts only describe the fault; they do not change acceptance rules, revert the suffix automatically or end bond responsibilities. During a pause, keep the Comet state, WAL and H/R/S, payloads, QCs, observations and proof material; valid existing batches can still be submitted.

When the L1 view, quorum and proving recover and the existing suffix can still settle, refresh observations and continue on the same configId and signing history; never clear H/R/S or re-sign conflicting blocks. When a new anchor is past the history authentication window, data is permanently unavailable or quorum cannot return, this version has no automatic recovery path, and governance must explicitly close the old configuration and handle the suffix. A new configuration can only start from an accepted, L1-finalized execution frontier, keeping the FI cursor and queue, historical responsibilities and evidence. Closing old submission rights and retiring keys wait for the related L1 records to finalize, and a faked zero-block proof cannot stand in for missing data.

Full verifying nodes run CometBFT + ABCI + EL. Original FI data is kept until consumption and the related L1 finality, and signing and eligibility material until responsibilities and the evidence period end; EL and ABCI history must cover U plus reorg margin. The runbook must state governance latency, powers and owners; users are not promised independent execution or exit after a halt.

RPC head can reach committed blocks, safe reaches accepted blocks, and finalized reaches accepted ancestors whose L1 records are finalized. Accepted means both DA and proof are done, but it can still be affected by an L1 reorg. The UI separates waiting for finality, proof and acceptance, and a fault pause, and never marks transactions failed or refunded automatically.

The Etna relayer keeps waitProofTarget and header-anchor proof targets; normal finalized visibility is still about 13–19 minutes, plus propagation and execution delay. The RPC actually used must support historical eth_getProof at the needed age, and the root must be verified. Temporary unavailability of historical state or tries keeps the message, backs off or switches to a qualified RPC, and never sends it straight to the dead-letter queue. Historical hash authentication does not replace Merkle witnesses.

Migration starts from one settled checkpoint: settle old pending proposals, handle the old preconfirmation suffix explicitly, migrate the real FI queue and cursor, authenticate the initial committee and anchor, and initialize the Context and lastAcceptedAt. Synchronize fork routing, extraData, service slots, fee fields, BlockRecord, Context, EL and clients, both guests, the aggregator and the verification keys; retire the old asynchronous entry points and any verification path that does not meet proofPolicy, and keep evidence of old responsibilities.

Appendix: Parameters, cost and acceptance

A.1 Latency and the U/N budget

Protocol parameters, encodings and proofPolicy must be fixed before activation and bound into configId. Let F be the budget for an L1 acceptance to enter the common finalized view (including observation propagation), B the bound on how long the oldest block waits for its batch to seal, P the budget of the complete dual-proof path after sealing, I the L1 inclusion and retry budget, and δ_min the fastest allowed production step. Use L = F + B + P + I and U ≥ ceil(L / δ_min) plus a fault margin, and enforce N ≥ U + 3.

P must measure the overall tail latency and sustained throughput of witness generation, queueing, RISC0 and SP1 proving and aggregation, not one backend's p50. Parallel generation is limited by the slower path and head-of-line blocking. This version plans for P_budget = 1 min as the fast-proving target, to be accepted by measured P95/P99 and throughput under target load; if it is missed, re-choose batching, U and N and review the configuration, rather than weakening proofPolicy to pretend the target is met.

Sensitivity example only: F = 20 min, B = 5 min, I = 1 min, δ_min = 1 s, with epoch length estimated at the two-second target; no extra stall margin is included.

The 31/61-minute figures are historical p50/p90 values supplied in earlier review; they were not re-measured for this revision and are not shown to equal the new dual-proof path. At a steady two-second cadence the height difference roughly halves, but hard limits must not be configured on that basis while a one-second fastest step is allowed.

After fast proving, the main floor is the finalized-visibility delay: in the example F is about 74% of L, and even with P near zero L ≈ 26 min, U ≥ 1560 and N ≥ 1563, about 52.1 minutes per epoch at two seconds. Fast proving greatly shortens the old proof wait but cannot remove F, which still limits how fast committees rotate and eligibility changes take effect. This version keeps a single finalized view. Switching to a fresher settled view would require redesigning §5.2's committee recording and activation and the L1 reorg rules together; replacing only the back-pressure reading to shrink N is not allowed.

U bounds the gap to the settled height seen at an older anchor, which can include blocks that newer L1 has already accepted. The actually unaccepted suffix, measured against the current canonical L1 frontier, is at most U blocks; reaching U does not trigger an automatic rollback, and U × 2 s is not a wall-clock bound during a halt.

MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE must state its normal budget and the finality-stall margin it is meant to tolerate; the remaining margin is the limit minus the current reference age, and U must also cover any stall during which production should continue. Exhausting the budget may pause production while valid batches can still be submitted.

A.2 Daily cost and FI fees

daily settlement execution fee = (86400 / B_seconds) × gas_per_submit × gasPrice_gwei × 10⁻⁹ ETH. Using a planning placeholder of 750,000 gas per submission (assuming SP1 uses Groth16; about 700k–800k, not measured on the new protocol), counting L1 execution fees only:

The basis is seven sampled mainnet SGX_GETH + RISC0_RETH prove transactions at about 399k gas each, plus SP1 replacing SGX and the extra work of atomic submission; the sample was not independently re-measured, and the old prove and propose costs cannot simply be added. Before launch, measure with fixed verifier versions, real dual proofs and ordinary, epoch-boundary and FI batches, and replace the placeholder with margin.

These gas prices are scenario inputs; blobs, proving compute, failed or racing resubmissions and operations are extra. At one batch every five minutes and 0.1 gwei, settlement execution alone reaches 0.0216 ETH per day, close to the earlier historical total revenue reference of about 0.0225 ETH per day (not re-measured), so overall economic viability cannot be claimed from it. Changing B requires recomputing U and N in A.1.

The base FI fee and congestion curve are charged by configuration; the base fee and topUpFI both count toward one serviceFee, paid to fiFeeRecipient only on authenticated consumption. Quotes and the UI must separate the L1 enqueue gas and DA cost, the escrowed service fee and the actual L2 execution fee, and must not advertise one processing opportunity as guaranteed successful execution or a refund on halt.

FI pricing must use the measured worst-case processing cost from A.3, covering dual-proof work and settlement overhead. In particular, measure a first transaction that executes near the zk-gas limit, then exceeds it and aborts, leaving an empty block: both guests must still replay the original input to verify the abort, while that transaction pays no L2 execution fee. The base fee and congestion curve must account for this cost with margin, rather than estimating only successful maximal blocks, final recorded zk gas or expected execution-fee revenue.

Cost workloads must include a first transaction that executes near the zk-gas limit, exceeds it and aborts, leaving an empty block and no L2 execution fee from that transaction. Proofs and DA Fix this fork's parameters on Etna's 45M gas / 100M zk gas baseline, MAX_BLOCK_DA_BYTES, MAX_BATCH_BLOCKS, MAX_ANCHORS_PER_BATCH and blob, envelope and decoding limits. Test the dual-backend AND over the same statement, repeated, missing or single proofs, mixed programs and domains, rollback on invalid proofs, rejection of zero height progress, and no batch spanning epochs.
Pause and late submission Simulate 25-minute, 64-minute and longer finality stalls, prover outages, RPC failures and exhausted U. After a pause, valid old batches are still accepted and not rewritten by FIs due at the current time; after observations, quorum and proving recover, continue the original signing history. Over-age anchors that can no longer be accepted under this version's direct authentication must be detected and routed to the governance runbook; the A.4 extension, which is not enabled, does not count as an existing recovery capability.
Exit and evidence Test old keys and duplicate weights, double signing in unaccepted suffixes, cutoff rounding, non-membership proofs for current and future sets, and exit attacks around a set already derived off-chain but not submitted. EVIDENCE_PERIOD covers evidence propagation and L1 submission; unfinished responsibility cannot be released because of a halt.
Operations and release Keep state, QCs, FI blobs, observations and header witnesses; measure restarts, historical replay, reorgs and retention. UI and relayer waits, alerts and retries are staged per phase; do not raise every timeout uniformly. At least 4 validators complete real dual proofs and fault injection; measure gas, DA, compute, throughput and revenue before evaluating launch.

A.4 Optional extension: cross-window anchor authentication

This section is a future direction and not part of this version's acceptance rules. The contract authenticates, at submission, a recent reference hash still inside the direct query window; both guests verify an account/storage proof of the EIP-2935 history contract under that reference's full header and stateRoot to obtain an older header hash, recompute that header, and walk back hop by hop to the target anchor. The recent reference, the full authentication chain and the target tuple must bind the same batch statement; what can be replaced is the authentication witness, never a committed anchor.

Each hop must check the fixed history contract, strictly decreasing heights, the 8191-block range, ring-buffer slots and field binding, and handle the real valid range around L1 forks and right after activation; this contract alone cannot cover history from before it began recording. If the recent reference gets too old before submission, the authentication proof must be refreshed.

The extension needs no new registration transaction or permanent anchor table, but historical Merkle witnesses must be kept and a hop and proof resource budget defined; a fixed total hop count means a bounded recovery range. It only lifts the direct query limit for historical hashes and cannot recover lost FI blobs, payloads or execution witnesses. Before enabling it, update both guests, the proofPolicy configuration, public inputs and boundary tests; only with complete data and every other rule satisfied can such over-age batches become ordinary late submissions.

A.5 Baseline and scope

This version keeps atomic settlement, anchor authentication at submission, consensus-derived committees, fixed FI service slots and objective double-sign penalties. It does not include automatic suffix revocation, asynchronous pending proposals, overlapping proofs, a proving liveness bond, inclusion lists, lander rotation or misses, DA credit, special eligibility proofs or automatic generation recovery. Committee-free execution and exit and automatic recovery are left for separate follow-up design.

  • Etna #22189 · 5d35cdd7: the execution and header baseline; this document explicitly overrides its landing metadata and default-source handling.
  • EIP-2935: direct authentication of new anchors at submission, with an 8191-block direct window; there is no permanent anchor registry.
  • ZkRequiredVerifier: the current deployment, kept separate from the target policy. This specification's pure dual-ZK, open submission cannot inherit that deployment's whole policy.

  • Ethereum finality incident review: the 2023 stalls of about 25 and 64 minutes are used for fault testing, not as an upper bound on future stalls.
  • Preconfirmation Fair Exchange: this version has no separate timely-fair-exchange market with delivery by a user's target time or refunds.
