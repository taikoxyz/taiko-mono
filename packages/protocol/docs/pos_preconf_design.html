PoS Preconfirmation Design: Etna and CometBFT

Replace the preconfirmation whitelist with an open-stake CometBFT committee: about one confirmed L2 block every 2 s, batched landing on L1, and ZK proofs of execution and protocol rules.

Status: design revision, pending implementation and review. Based on Etna and CometBFT v0.40.0. Version 1 uses finalized-only anchors, a dedicated observation key, and historical committee membership authenticated by proofs. This is a design specification; it does not mean an implementation exists or has passed a security audit.

How to read it. Sections 1–6 state the mechanisms, user impact and trust boundaries. Appendix A is evidence and historical estimates, Appendix B records design trade-offs, and Appendix C is the binding implementation specification. Moving detail into a collapsed appendix does not remove a check or weaken a guarantee.

Terms

seat
A fixed-size stake unit in the registry. Voting power and rotation shares are counted in seats.
leader
The CometBFT proposer of the current height.
lander
The operator allowed to submit committed blocks to the L1 Inbox. Leader and lander are independent roles.
QC
Quorum certificate: a CometBFT commit, i.e. precommit signatures from strictly more than 2/3 of the voting power. One Comet height carries exactly one EVM block, and QC.height must equal EVM.number.
IL
Inclusion list: transactions a validator has seen and requires the next block to include, FOCIL-style.
FI
Forced inclusion: a request submitted to the L1 forced inclusion store.
acceptedL1View
The finalized L1 block that the committee has certified and that the next blocks anchor to.
generation
A CometBFT chain instance. Recovery after expired blocks starts generation g+1; the Comet string chain_id carries the protocol configuration identifier and the generation, while the numeric EVM chainId never changes.
ConsensusContext
Proven consensus state carried across proposals: configId, generation, fiCursor, daCredit and the epoch-start and selector state.
EpochRecord
The authenticated record of which committee held an epoch; L1 stores one hash per epoch identity.

1. Goals and guarantee boundaries

Replace the whitelist with an open-stake CometBFT committee that confirms an L2 block about every 2 s. Rotating landers submit batches to L1, and ZK proofs cover execution and the protocol rules. Fast confirmation is separate from final settlement; L1 still decides the final settlement order.

MechanismGuarantees and limits
Preconfirmation A commit needs strictly more than 2/3 of the voting power and confirms the block's content; transactions can still revert normally. Under honest observers and Ethereum finality, ordinary shallow L1 reorgs cannot remove an anchor. An unlanded suffix can still expire and be dropped. A reverted Ethereum finality, compromised observation keys or a malicious committee break the related guarantees. ZK protection of final settlement does not make every preconfirmation irreversible.
L1 state visibility Version 1 explicitly uses finalized-only anchors. The latest finalized checkpoint normally trails the head by about 64–96 slots (12.8–19.2 minutes), and observation propagation adds to that. L1 deposits, messages and state wait until the relevant record enters acceptedL1View, then still need relaying and execution. This range is not a hard bound under abnormal conditions.
IL A conditional append constraint for bounded, eligible light candidates. It does not guarantee that every mempool transaction is included within two seconds. Heavier transactions go through the normal mempool or FI.
FI A FIFO, one-time deterministic processing opportunity with an exclusive user execution budget in a fixed service slot. Invalid skip, zk abort and suffix truncation can discharge the obligation; it does not guarantee that every transaction succeeds or lands in the final body.
Fees and admission Ordinary transactions pay their tip only when included. Timely-fair-exchange tickets (pay only for on-time delivery, refund otherwise) are not in this version. Entry is open but the active set is limited (about 100 seats), and queuing does not guarantee admission within bounded time. Ordinary landing has no separate reward.
Liveness and recovery Production pauses when the L1 view is too old or when DA or the unproven suffix hits a hard limit. If at least 1/3 of the voting power is unavailable for a long time, version 1 relies on the DAO to recover. A proof outage does not immediately block committee rotation, but blocks cannot be produced without proofs indefinitely.

FI latency. Measured from enqueue, roughly max(FI_DELAY, time until the enqueue record enters the shared L1 view) + service-slot and queueing time. FI_DELAY = 576 s is only the due condition, not an end-to-end guarantee. With finalized-only anchors the normal 13–19 minute visibility wait is usually the bottleneck, and FI_DELAY is not added on top of it. There is no wall-clock bound during halts or recovery.

Finality stalls. MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE bounds the total age of the reference. The remaining margin is the limit minus the current reference age, not a timer that starts when finality stalls. When the normal age is already close to 19.2 minutes, a 20-minute limit leaves only about 48 seconds, before propagation overhead.

This version does not fall back automatically to shallow-confirmation anchors. If deposit latency is unacceptable, design a separate scheme — a finalized reference for the committee, exit and recovery plus a depth-d execution anchor — and re-review deep reorgs, preconfirmation invalidation and recovery; it is not a configuration switch for lower latency. Timing basis: Ethereum PoS.

2. Consensus architecture and L1 observations

The registry holds open staking, historical eligibility snapshots and responsibilities. CometBFT plus the Taiko ABCI app drive the execution client (EL). The Go and Rust clients and the ZK guest verify the same protocol rules. One Comet commit corresponds to one EVM block, and QC.height must equal EVM.number.

The builder, ProcessProposal, derivation and the guest must apply the same checks to time, anchor, header, FI, IL, DA and execution results. An EL VALID response does not replace these protocol checks. The ABCI AppHash commits to the EVM head, the ConsensusContext and the accepted L1 reference; it does not embed an Ethereum sync-committee light client.

A validator independently confirms that an L1 tuple (number, blockHash, stateRoot) is finalized, then signs it with a dedicated observationKey held by the ABCI app. acceptedL1View advances only after strictly more than 2/3 of the weight endorses the same tuple, and the block's anchor equals that accepted reference. Ordinary blocks carry the signatures in vote extensions; during startup, committee changes or pauses, validators exchange signatures in the same format over P2P/RPC without first producing a block. A validator that sees no eligible candidate may abstain.

Consensus keys, observation keys and L1 sending keys are separate. Observation keys are bound to previously authenticated historical committees and do not extend the signing duties of the consensus privval, tmkms or Horcrux. Domain separation, rotation, weight de-duplication, replay protection and generation-closing rules still apply (Appendix C.2.1).

An observation certificate is a safety-critical committee statement, not a proof of Ethereum finality. The thresholds are defined separately. Comet consensus safety still requires malicious voting power below 1/3. Certificate authenticity requires malicious or compromised observation weight of at most 2/3, with honest observers signing only tuples they have independently confirmed as finalized; because a certificate needs strictly more than 2/3 of the signatures, exactly 2/3 cannot forge one either. Refreshing the view requires more than 2/3 of honest observation weight to be available and to sign the same eligible candidate, and it also depends on L1 finality progressing and the network eventually synchronizing. Compromised weight between 1/3 and 2/3 can block refreshes but cannot forge a wrong tuple on its own. Compromise of more than 2/3 of the observation weight can induce a wrong FI or registry view and wrong later committees, which affects preconfirmation safety. The guest's binding to the real canonical L1 protects final settlement, but a real root that was not yet finalized at the time can still pass, so observation-key compromise cannot be promised to affect liveness only.

3. Inclusion, DA and batched landing

IL. Each IL is at most 8 KiB, with at most K_IL candidates and a per-transaction append-simulation budget Q. For each eligible candidate missing from an ordinary block, validators simulate it independently from the same post-block state; if it fits in the remaining gas, zk gas and DA budget, they reject the proposal that left it out. No node may skip later candidates because of local elapsed time. IL is resource-bounded protection; the full rules are in Appendix C.2.2.

FI. One service slot is reserved every M heights (M ≥ 2). When the queue head is visible and due, the slot must process exactly one item with the block's whole user execution budget; the other heights stay ordinary blocks. The authenticated original blob executes with the standard skip/abort/truncation semantics and fiCursor advances by exactly one; missing data or failed authentication cannot consume the cursor. A prefix of N_FI visible, due items, including the item itself, waits at most about M × N_FI later heights, about 2 × M × N_FI seconds at a two-second cadence. The L1 store keeps the existing single-blob submission rule.

DA. Every block must fit into one valid landing on its own. Besides gas and zk gas there is a canonical byte limit and a deterministic token bucket. Parameters must reserve room for mandatory FI and recovery overhead; a budget shortfall cannot be solved by skipping FI. Sustained load is bounded by a conservative share of L1 capacity, never by all of L1's blob bandwidth.

Landing. The rotation period is 384 s: the duty lander has the first 3/4, and the last 1/4 is open to registered operators whose responsibility bond is fully locked. Batches split by DA, block count, FI count and committee boundaries, and a period can carry several submissions when needed. Submission rights do not depend on the latest proven epoch. On-chain snapshots can be rebuilt from historical checkpoints and cached during propose, so no keeper call is needed each epoch.

Network windows. Mainnet uses 512 blocks / 6144 s and Hoodi uses 128 blocks / 1536 s today. With the anchor already about 96 slots old, Hoodi has only about 6.4 minutes of nominal anchor margin before observation, batching, inclusion and retries, so it cannot guarantee waiting a full 384 s period before submitting. This design folds raising Hoodi to 512 / 6144 into the not-yet-scheduled Etna, activated at ETNA_FORK_TIME, so no separate fork is needed for these two parameters; the spec, clients and guest must change together and pass upgrade-boundary and devnet acceptance, and nothing is live yet. Mainnet's normal margin is about 83.2 minutes, which is still not a guarantee under congestion or a finality stall. (Etna parameter baseline)

Proofs and fees. The guest authenticates the QC, the real L1 and the full execution context. Overlapping proofs must match the parent hash and context at the actual continuation point and settle only the new suffix. FIs dequeue by the proven cursor, and their fees go to the lander of the corresponding proposal. A zero-block output from an invalid proposal consumes no FI; an FI that executes to an empty EVM block still consumes it once. The DA budget does not compensate landers; fees and any subsidy still need economic validation.

4. Committee rotation and recovery

The committee runs on fixed L2 height epochs. Epoch e+1 uses the L1 registry snapshot at cutoff C_(e+1) = S × floor((T_e − Δ) / S), where T_e is the canonical EVM timestamp of epoch e's first block; the registry itself keeps only an L1 timeline and does not wait for L2 proofs. The first block authenticates the next selector; the Comet update is submitted at H_next − 2 and takes effect at H_next. Time mapping, historical lookup and selector identity must agree (Appendix C.4.1).

L1 adds no dedicated finality verifier. Snapshots, prove, eligibility registration and withdrawals follow the current canonical branch, and a reorg rolls back the transitions that depend on it. Off-chain actions — enabling a reference, permanently closing a signer domain, choosing the recovery cutoff, retiring keys and other irreversible external actions — still wait for real finality; a fixed delay is not finality.

Automatic recovery applies only to an authenticated, expired committed suffix: wait for the expiry cutoff F to finalize, process the whole Inbox prefix up to F to get P_F, then enter generation g+1 from its authenticated context. The FI cursor, resource state and valid selector are kept; the committee is not re-selected by the wall-clock time of recovery, every recovery step needs a proof, and generations cannot be skipped. Historical snapshots, data and signer-domain closing are in Appendix C.4.3.

If at least 1/3 of the voting power is permanently unavailable and no usable expiry evidence exists, version 1 cannot recover by itself and uses a DAO hard fork or configuration upgrade. Default governance and timelock delays, plus finishing the old chain, proving and finality, all add to the real halt time; no fast re-selection power is assumed. The full emergency procedure and role sign-off are in Appendix C.4.4.

5. Stake responsibility, evidence and node states

All active seats vote; limited seats and queuing can still let incumbents hold seats for a long time. The registry keeps historical bindings of validator, bond, consensus key, observation key and L1 operator, and re-registration cannot overwrite old responsibilities.

Double-sign slashing keeps historical eligibility checks. Version 1 stores on L1 a single, non-overwritable EpochRecord hash for each epoch with a complete source identity. The reporter supplies the record text and a committee-membership proof; the contract checks the hash, then verifies eligibility. It does not store records field by field and does not parse generic epoch-start QC chains. Ordinary settlement proofs register records; unsettled or abandoned epochs can be registered by recovery/retirement proofs or by eligibility-only proofs, after which conflicting signatures with the same domain, height, round and vote type can be reported. Abandoning a generation is not an exemption, and the QC of the earliest expired block B does not stand for all later epochs.

Exit and evidence window. Withdrawal requires the complete responsibility frontier to be closed, plus an evidence window counted from acceptance of the corresponding on-chain settlement or retirement record; the bond stays locked until eligibility is authenticated and responsibility is closed. MAX_UNPROVEN_AGE only stops production; it does not bound when proofs arrive and is no reason to release a bond early. An eligibility-only proof does not make abandoned blocks settleable again and does not replace a proof that responsibility has ended.

Version 1 keeps the double-sign and lander-miss penalties. Low voting participation while online is only monitored and alerted on, with no EVM system call and no automatic removal. Responsibility for a committee jointly signing an invalid block cannot be inferred from a lander data mismatch alone.

Non-voting full nodes that independently verify preconfirmations also run the CometBFT + ABCI + EL stack. The Engine head is the committed block; safe is the last ancestor published on canonical L1 and derived successfully; finalized is proven, with its L1 inclusion and proof both at Ethereum finality. proven is exposed separately, and safe can roll back with an L1 reorg.

6. Implementation and launch conditions

The registry and Inbox, ABCI and EL, clients and guest all need protocol changes; CometBFT reuses its existing consensus, and this is not a configuration-only change. The dedicated observation key needs no new consensus signer message type. Eligibility authentication reuses the proof framework with an authenticated proofKind that separates permissions, and L1 stores one record hash per epoch identity.

Before launch, fix the network windows, L1 view age, DA/IL/FI limits, evidence window, data retention and real governance powers, and run fault-injection and real-proof tests with at least 4 validators. Focus on finality stalls, Hoodi landing margin, observation-key rotation and compromise, double signing in abandoned epochs, long proof outages, overlapping proofs and full recovery. Parameters and implementation are untested and unaudited; the design claims no launch-level safety.

Product and operations acceptance. Accept Etna L1→L2 along waitProofTarget → etnaAnchorFor → eth_getProof at the L1 anchor → L2 claim; destination chains before Etna or without Etna support keep the CheckpointSaved path. The L1 RPCs used by the relayer and bridge-ui must be tested to serve historical Merkle proofs of the required age. The proof-target age must be configurable and satisfy both the protocol view age and the RPC retention window, not just a larger version of today's 96-block limit. When a historical proof is temporarily unavailable, keep the message, back off and retry, or switch to a qualified RPC; never declare the message invalid. The UI separates finality, an available anchor, proof fetching and destination execution; task deadlines, queue concurrency, alerts and per-call RPC timeouts are configured per stage. The frontend, deployment parameters, monitoring and runbooks ship together with Etna (Appendix C.6).

Appendix C: Implementation specification and operating constraints (normative)

The body and this appendix together form the design requirements. An implementation may not satisfy only the body's summary while leaving out this appendix's authentication, resource, exit or recovery conditions.

C.2 Architecture and consensus inputs

The leader is the current CometBFT proposer; the lander is the operator allowed to submit to L1; a seat is the unit of voting power and rotation share. One Comet height corresponds to one EVM block, enforced as QC.height == EVM.number. The Comet string chain_id contains the protocol configuration identifier and the generation; the numeric EVM chainId stays as the network defines it, and the two must never be mixed.

ComponentResponsibility
L1 Registry / Inbox Keep eligibility snapshots retrievable by L1 time, the operator mapping, exits and penalties; accept proposals, verify proofs and overlap-boundary contexts, and settle by FI cursor. On-chain operations follow this chain's state; no dedicated finality verifier is deployed.
CometBFT + Taiko ABCI Run in one process and handle voting, IL, L1 consensus inputs, committee updates, resource metering, statistics and recovery; validate and import through an EL interface that carries protocol context, and write forkchoice from one place.
EL Standard Etna execution, FI candidate-list filtering and bounded, isolated append simulation; verify the final EVM header, body and state. A plain newPayload VALID cannot replace the Taiko protocol checks.
Go / Rust clients and raiko2 Share one specification and test vectors, and perform candidate checks, derivation, landing, L1Origin and proving. The guest proves execution and protocol context; the host supplies authenticated witness data and cannot decide the result.

Block data still uses tx0 = EVM block hash, tx1 = payload plus the protocol verification envelope and tx2 = signed ILs. The envelope holds the needed L1 reference and authentication data, FI references and protocol actions. The ABCI checks that tx0 matches the final payload. The ABCI AppHash commits to the EVM head, the ConsensusContext and the accepted L1 reference; it embeds no Ethereum light client and no absence-count system call. State sync and recovery must verify the corresponding snapshot. Ordinary landing authenticates the last EVM block with the last block's commit, the Comet header and the two DataHash → tx0 Merkle siblings; the EVM parentHash chain and replay from the accepted parent state bind the whole segment.

C.2.1 Deterministic voting checks and L1 observation certificates

The builder, ProcessProposal, client derivation and the guest share canonical validation logic. Before voting, check the parent hash and height, extraData, mixHash and base fee, gasLimit, timestamp, anchor authenticity and monotonicity, FI cursor and execution result, IL and DA. An EL VALID cannot replace the Taiko protocol checks.

The committee endorses the L1 reference. Each validator decides finalized status through a trusted Ethereum EL + CL or an explicitly configured verification source; an unverified response from an ordinary RPC is not Ethereum finality. The ABCI keeps no BLS sync-committee light client and no fork-specific SSZ gindex state.

  1. Collect observation endorsements for a common candidate (L1 number, blockHash, stateRoot) with the matching header. A validator signs only after independently confirming that the block is finalized, on the same chain and fresh enough by configuration; otherwise it abstains. Observation signatures normally ride in ExtendVote; abstaining is valid and does not block ordinary BFT voting.
  2. The leader can advance acceptedL1View only with an observation certificate from strictly more than 2/3 of the weight for the same tuple. Observation messages use a separate fixed-encoding signing domain that binds configId, generation, targetHeight, the parent EVM hash, the valid committeeRef at that height and the L1 tuple; ordinary precommits, native extension wrappers and observation signatures cannot stand in for each other. Authorization comes from the set current at targetHeight; signatures of a retired set cannot authorize a new reference.
  3. Without a new certificate, keep the old reference and re-propose candidates. An old reference already in canonical state may stay as a historical anchor but is still subject to the production staleness limit. To avoid the deadlock where new observations need a new block and a new block needs new observations, currently authorized validators must be able to exchange observation signatures in the same format over P2P/RPC during startup, committee changes or pauses, without producing a new EVM block; the messages still bind the target height, parent block and current set. Observations are signed by the ABCI's dedicated observationKey; the consensus privval, tmkms and Horcrux keep their existing consensus duties and need no new signing entry point. In-band extensions and out-of-band P2P/RPC during a pause use the same observation encoding and verification rules. Candidate count, caches, certificate size and validity range are protocol configuration; unaccepted certificates that are expired, for a different parent or for a closed generation cannot be reused.
  4. VerifyVoteExtension and ProcessProposal verify certificates only by fixed format, signatures, weight, historical eligibility, domain, monotonicity/ancestry and age rules, and never query their own live finalized head to judge content. A node's own L1 observation decides only whether it endorses. When nodes report different latest finalized heads, their signatures cannot be treated as signatures over a minimum or median tuple.

The block's anchor uses the accepted L1 reference fixed by these rules, and the matching FI queue and registry state are authenticated by MPT proofs. The leader may not fall back to an arbitrary older reference. When the reference exceeds the configured time/height staleness, production pauses; an expired certificate cannot get around the window. Initialization uses a configured trusted checkpoint; generation recovery uses a common finalized cutoff that nodes confirm independently (Appendix C.4).

Observation keys and trust boundary. The registry records a dedicated observationKey per validator/bond identity and authenticates its historical mapping to the frozen committeeRef. A certificate takes keys and weights from the previously authenticated set valid at targetHeight; a new root under authentication cannot authorize itself. Key rotation takes effect at a defined committee boundary, historical verification uses the key valid at the time, and several keys of one validator never count weight twice. Observation signatures do not reuse Comet's one-signature-per-H/R/S lock; the same target may endorse several real finalized candidates that are ancestors of each other, but the domain, replay protection, generation closing and authorization boundary rules are mandatory.

Signatures plus MPT proofs authenticate the committee's report and the state under that root, not Ethereum finality itself. Certificate authenticity and refresh liveness are separate. Let W be the total observation weight of the historically authorized set at that height and F the malicious or compromised weight; a certificate needs signedWeight > 2W/3. With honest observers signing only real finalized tuples, F ≤ 2W/3 cannot forge on its own; this is the authenticity threshold, and it does not relax Comet's requirement of malicious voting power below W_consensus/3. To refresh while attackers refuse to sign, more than 2W/3 of honest weight must be available and willing to sign the same eligible candidate: malicious, offline and otherwise unable-to-sign weight together must be below W/3, and refresh also depends on a common candidate, L1 finality and eventual network synchrony. F between 1/3 and 2/3 can block refreshes and exhaust the staleness budget, but does not necessarily halt the chain. Total weight is fixed by the historical set; abstentions or offline validators do not shrink the denominator, and an uncompromised consensus key does not imply a trustworthy observation. Compromise of more than 2/3 of the observation weight can forge a reference or registry view and induce wrong later committees and preconfirmations; it does not only affect liveness. The guest's binding to the real canonical L1 header and state protects final settlement — fake hashes or roots cannot settle — but not every preconfirmation commitment. A real canonical block that was not yet finalized can still pass the guest, possibly without even halting the chain.

BFT Time in CometBFT v0.40 has nanosecond precision; use the unique whole-second mapping timestamp_h = max(parent.timestamp + 1, floor(Unix seconds of CometTime_h), anchor.timestamp). The mapping is used for vote admission and DA metering; the guest verifies the authenticatable monotonicity, the anchor lower bound and the final landing window. BFT Time can be influenced by which valid LastCommit signature subset is included, so it is not an unbiased random number, and the leader is not claimed to have no influence on snapshot boundaries.

Comet uses the nop mempool. Lock the time mechanism, the bounded clock-skew assumption and the commit wait; disable skipping the wait; keep normal height intervals at least 1 s, targeting about 2 s. Scheduling readiness must not become a content-validity rule based on the local wall clock. A future landing's time and origin are unknown; admission margins and back-pressure do not guarantee that L1 includes it in time.

C.2.2 IL and append-simulation budget

ExtendVote attaches candidates received at least 250 ms earlier, at most 8 KiB per IL; also configure the maximum candidate count per IL K_IL, per-transaction byte and declared-gas caps, and the append-simulation zk budget Q. Every transaction carries at least the current 243,000 intrinsic zk gas; K_IL and Q are fixed by measuring the worst-case work over all candidates. Heavier transactions outside IL eligibility can still be included from the normal mempool, with FI as the processing backstop; a single transaction above the whole-block zk gas limit can only be attempted and aborted, even through FI. VerifyVoteExtension checks format and static limits; PrepareProposal includes signed extensions from more than 2/3 of the weight; ProcessProposal checks them by the context, signature and weight rules of ValidateVoteExtensions. The first height after startup or a generation restart has no previous extensions and may skip IL.

In ordinary blocks, de-duplicate all IL candidates globally by transaction hash. For missing candidates, first run cheap checks of format, signature, nonce, balance, fees and declared resources, then execute each one independently from the same post-block state, execution environment and original remaining budget. Database changes, meters, warm state and the zk_gas_exhausted flag must not leak between candidates. An ordinary EVM revert is still a valid transaction and is not excused as invalid.

Every statically eligible candidate runs the same bounded test: if it could be included within Q, the remaining block zk gas, gas and the deterministic DA budget, omitting it means REJECT. A candidate over the protocol's per-transaction IL test budget only loses IL enforcement for this round; it is not declared permanently invalid. 8 KiB, K_IL, Q and the other static limits bound the worst case to at most activeValidatorCount × K_IL independent tests. No node may skip the remaining candidates because it reached some local elapsed time or total simulation amount. Parameters must keep the full worst-case testing within the node processing budget, or the configuration may not be enabled.

Only a dedicated block that actually processes a due FI is exempt from the ordinary IL append condition. An unused FI slot used as an ordinary block still checks IL, and candidates can be proposed again by later extensions. Honest ILs holding at least 1/3 of the weight still intersect an included set of more than 2/3, but this only constrains transactions that meet the eligibility and remaining-resource conditions above; it does not remove delays from block stuffing or an FI backlog.

C.3 FI, DA and L1 settlement

C.3.1 Dedicated FI blocks and the cursor

fiCursor marks the end of the FIFO prefix already processed by the rules (the next index to process); its initial value is the authenticated queue state at fork activation. extraData bytes 1–6 change to the uint48 cursor; byte 0 stays the fork constant basefeeSharingPctg, and bytes 7–12 hold the anchor number. The Comet height is verified directly from the header number and QC.height and is no longer duplicated in extraData. The old format must be fork-gated, and existing FIs must not be lost in migration.

Each block reads queue entries and the tail pointer from its authenticated L1 reference/anchor snapshot. The due clock is the block's canonical EVM timestamp: block.timestamp ≥ inclusion.enqueueTimestamp + FI_DELAY, and the item must already be visible in the authenticated snapshot; no second due wait based on the anchor timestamp is added. An older reference can still delay visibility, so the staleness limit of C.2.1 applies. Cursor progress is bound to the historical queue records:

  1. Fix M ≥ 2; heights with (h − H0) mod M = 0 are FI service slots. When the queue head is visible and due in a service slot, the block must process exactly queue[fiCursor] and nothing else — no ordinary or IL transactions; other heights are ordinary blocks. A service slot with no due item may be an ordinary block, with the cursor unchanged. The rule provides both an FI service floor and a reserved share for ordinary transactions; saying only "at most one FI" is not enough.
  2. Authenticate the original FI blob reference, KZG, queue identity and candidate input order, and use a version-pinned decoder. The L1 FI store rejects non-single-blob requests at submission (the existing OnlySingleBlobAllowed stays) rather than charging for multi-blob requests and treating them as empty later. One item is still limited to one FI block, with configured caps on decompressed bytes, transaction count and per-transaction encoding. Internal format errors or violations of these decoding caps in accepted, authenticated bytes complete the item under a defined empty-input rule; missing data, invalid KZG/MPT or any other authentication failure must not consume the cursor.
  3. An FI block uses protocol-defined metadata: timestamp and anchor follow the common candidate rules, the leader fixes the coinbase before voting, and gasLimit inherits the parent's valid value under Etna's cross-fork normalization and range rules; metadata in the user's blob cannot override them. The item gets a full new block's user gas and zk gas budget and executes under Etna's invalid-skip, zk-abort and suffix-truncation rules.
  4. Verify the filtered EVM body, stateRoot, receipts, zk gas and other outputs, and advance the cursor by exactly one; even an empty output is a real committed EVM block. This differs from "an invalid L1 proposal derives to zero blocks". The final body need not equal the original input list, and simple list equality cannot replace replay.

The cursor only covers a contiguous FIFO prefix: it cannot skip items, go past the authenticated tail, or reset to zero in generation recovery. Due items are processed one at a time only in reserved service slots, and the leader cannot fill an FI block with ordinary transactions to claim a resource-shortage exemption. In normal production, a prefix of N_FI visible, due items including the item itself needs at most M × N_FI later heights, about 2 × M × N_FI seconds at a two-second cadence. One item's wait from enqueue is max(FI_DELAY, time until its enqueue record enters acceptedL1View) + service-slot and queueing time; FI_DELAY is not added again after the finality wait. FI_DELAY = 576 s is not an end-to-end guarantee, and halts and recovery have no wall-clock bound. Many FIs no longer take every ordinary height in a row, but throughput drops to about one item per M blocks, and fees and queue capacity must be calibrated for that.

Ordinary landing no longer has the Inbox insert FI blocks. A new BlockRecord distinguishes ordinary and FI blocks: an ordinary block carries the canonical transaction list that matches its final body; an FI block carries the original queue index and blob reference, and the guest replays from the authenticated original input. FI input need not be copied in full into a new L1 blob, but must stay retrievable; validators and archives keep the data needed for proofs until settlement and L1 finality.

C.3.2 DA budget and segmentation

Gas and zk gas cannot replace a DA limit. The protocol configuration must give MAX_BLOBS_PER_PROPOSE, usable bytes per blob, the maximum protocol envelope and recovery-proof overhead, MAX_BLOCK_DA_BYTES, decompression and P2P size limits, and define one canonical encoding with a worst-case compression bound. The builder, voting, IL append and the guest use the same measure; every single block must fit into one valid landing on its own, fixed overhead included, without stitching one EVM block across proposals.

Each block's DA usage is fixed by authenticated block content and FI references; an ordinary block may not hide extra inputs that would be filtered out to change the measure. The FI's original blob is already published on L1, so the budget counts its canonical reference plus new proof data and metadata, with separate size limits on the original input and the execution payload. Merged proposals can benefit from compression, but verification cannot rely on optimistic compression ratios.

Sustained rate uses a deterministic DA token bucket: credit_h = min(BURST, credit_parent + RATE × (timestamp_h − timestamp_parent)) − units_h, which must be non-negative; units include the canonical record and a conservative envelope reserve. Credit is proven consensus context, and zero-block proposals and generation restarts do not refill it. The configuration must also guarantee that the next mandatory block can be paid for: define MANDATORY_MAX to cover the largest new reference and envelope of one FI block (including first-recovery overhead), and require BURST ≥ MANDATORY_MAX and RATE × MIN_TIMESTAMP_STEP ≥ MANDATORY_MAX with MIN_TIMESTAMP_STEP = 1 s; the per-block limit must also hold that value. Then even if the previous block used up the credit, the next mandatory FI block can still be committed. If this conflicts with the network's sustainable DA capacity, the parameter set may not be enabled; the conflict cannot be solved by skipping FI or silently overspending. RATE is set from the network's usable blob quota, at most one propose per L1 block and a conservative availability factor; it never claims L1's peak bandwidth.

Landers split batches by bytes, source block count, FI count and committee boundaries and may submit several times; they are not limited to one submission per 384 s. A proposal does not span committee epochs; a source holds at most 768 blocks; the number of FIs per proposal and per proof is capped so the L1 dequeue loop cannot run out of gas. The Inbox still allows one propose per L1 block. Back-pressure applies when unlanded bytes or waiting time grow or the remaining window is short; prolonged L1 congestion can still lead to the disclosed expiry recovery.

C.3.4 Lander, historical snapshots and separate back-pressure

The lander epoch stays 384 s: duty for the first 3/4 and fallback in the last 1/4, with multiple submissions under high load. Each submission binds the expected previous proposal hash and sets a conservative ProposeInput.deadline = min(earliest block time + network timestamp window, earliest anchor time + 12 × network anchor window); it does not define real expiry. Before sending, the lander may re-check the anchor's real finality through its own L1 verification source as local risk control; this takes no part in other nodes' deterministic ProcessProposal decisions. An observation certificate is a committee statement and its existence does not replace an independent finality fact; the lander bears the risk of wrong protection parameters.

L1's rotation pool comes from the registry's historical eligible-seat snapshots by a protocol-fixed L1 time slot and lookback; selection and identity mapping do not depend on the latest proven epoch and do not need to know unproven L2 epochs. The last 1/4 lets any registered operator with sufficient bond still locked submit, including old members with remaining responsibility, operators queued for exit and pending entrants with locked bonds; an exit request does not immediately remove fallback rights. This is a right to submit only, with no extra voting power; the content is still constrained by the QC and ZK.

The registry keeps historical root checkpoints when eligibility changes, and time-slot snapshots are fully determined by these records; it does not need to write a root for every L2 epoch. If an implementation needs a sealSnapshot(slot) cache, the lander can piggyback it in propose → checkProposer and anyone can call it separately; it can only rebuild a closed time slot and cannot accept a caller-supplied root. A root can be reused while historical state is unchanged. If the whole propose reverts, the cache write reverts too; the caller pays the gas, with no dedicated keeper or reward.

Full proofs only affect settlement, responsibility clearing and exit; they do not trigger ordinary committee rotation. Configure MAX_UNPROVEN_BLOCKS, MAX_UNPROVEN_AGE and an unsettled-proposal capacity alarm separately, applied as back-pressure from commonly authenticated L1 Inbox state; the limits must leave room for finality-view delay, network delay and ring-buffer capacity. Reaching an explicit hard limit still pauses production; the chain cannot run forever while provers are offline. MAX_UNPROVEN_AGE only limits further production; it does not guarantee that proofs or settlement arrive within that time and is no basis for shortening the evidence window or releasing a bond early.

C.4 Committee scheduling, finality and recovery

C.4.1 Height epochs and L1-time snapshots

configId fixes H0, the blocks per epoch N, the L1 snapshot slot width S, the lookback Δ, the network windows and the encoding. epoch(h) = floor((h − H0) / N), and QC.height must equal EVM.number; the ABCI, the guest and historical-eligibility checks use the same mapping. The bootstrap configuration defines the initial committee.

Epoch e starts at height H_e = H0 + eN, whose canonical EVM timestamp is T_e. Epoch e+1 uses the registry eligibility snapshot at cutoff C_(e+1) = S × floor((T_e − Δ) / S). rootAt(k) is the end-of-block state of the last L1 block with timestamp ≤ kS (not (k+1)S), including every eligibility change in that block. Missed L1 slots do not change the lookup rule: take the last valid historical checkpoint no later than the cutoff. Startup needs the required lookback history; a caller-chosen root cannot stand in for missing history.

The registry keeps only an L1 eligibility timeline: registration, seat changes, exit requests and removals update the eligible set and its historical root, keeping end-of-block state; it does not take L2 timestamps to decide eligibility and does not wait for prove. rootAt(slot) is determined by historical checkpoints and an adjacent-boundary proof. An exit request only affects later eligibility snapshots; the bond stays under the complete-responsibility-frontier rule of C.5.1.

T_e is determined by the canonical time mapping and the epoch's first block and cannot be reported separately by a prover. It is influenced, within bounds, by the BFT Time signature subset, especially near slot boundaries, so it is neither unmanipulable nor an unbiased random number. Registration timing, slot boundaries and Δ all need adversarial testing.

ConsensusContext records the first block's hash and T_e, the current committeeRef and the next snapshot slot. A selector's identity includes configId, generation/source chain, the source epoch-start block hash, the slot and the root. Selectors of a kept prefix stay unchanged in recovery; only selections inside a formally abandoned suffix are abandoned with that generation, and historical records cannot be overwritten. The guest recomputes the selection from the proven starting state and the chain and does not accept an unauthenticated T_e or slot.

The chosen cutoff must already be covered by the block's common acceptedL1View, and its historical root must be verifiable. The configuration requires Δ ≥ MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE + S + safety margin, so the first block of a new epoch can already fix the next snapshot instead of waiting for future L1 updates at H_next − 2. The new set is submitted at FinalizeBlock(H_next − 2) and takes effect at H_next; without the authenticated data, production pauses before the update point. L1 observations can refresh independently and do not depend on the paused next block.

Historical eligibility is registered by proofs. Version 1 uses one bytes32 record hash indexed by epoch identity. Double-sign checks take the EpochRecord text, check it against the authenticated hash and then read membership; the contract does not parse generic epoch-start QC chains. Ordinary settlement proofs output the corresponding records; records of unsettled or abandoned suffixes can be registered by recovery/retirement proofs or by eligibility-only proofs that anyone may submit. An eligibility-only proof authenticates only the authorization chain, does not need to execute a permanently invalid suffix, and does not make its blocks settleable again.

An eligibility proof starts from the trusted bootstrap or an authenticated checkpoint/recovery context and verifies, step by step, QC → Comet header → DataHash/tx0 → first EVM header/T_e → next slot/root, binding historical roots to the real L1 registry. The EpochRecord text contains at least the encoding version, configId, generation/selector source, source epoch-start block hash, epoch/slot, root, valid height range and a commitment to the historical validator/bond/key mapping. The guest computes recordHash with a fixed domain and a unique encoding, and the public output binds epochKey to recordHash; epochKey binds configId, generation, epoch and the first block's source, so a plain epoch number cannot overwrite records across generations. L1 verifies the proof's version, trusted starting point and public outputs; proofs can be split into bounded segments or aggregated recursively, and never start from a root the reporter supplies.

Storage and evidence. Each epochKey persists one bytes32 recordHash, not one storage slot for each of the record's 5–7 fields. Re-submitting the same key and hash is idempotent with no new write; a different hash for the same key is rejected. Overlapping settlement proofs register only records first seen in the new suffix, and eligibility registration is de-duplicated the same way. Settlement, recovery/retirement and eligibility proofs use the same record encoding and hash domain. A reporter first proves the record hash, then proves membership, weight, signatures and the responsibility period from its committee/key commitment; compressing storage removes no verification field.

The record text is published as calldata or an event with the registration transaction and checked against the authenticated hash; archives keep the text, committee-membership paths and the historical material needed for proofs for at least the full responsibility and evidence period plus the needed L1 finality — never just an unrecoverable hash. The number of records per registration is bounded and included in gas and calldata benchmarks. With N = 192 and a roughly two-second cadence there are about 225 records per day; at a base cost of 20k gas per new slot, 5–7 slots per record would cost about 22.5–31.5 million gas per day and one hash about 4.5 million, excluding cold access, calldata and other state logic. The old 75k-per-landing figure is only a historical estimate. A batched Merkle root is left as a later optimization after measurement; version 1 adds no batch index or membership-path specification for it.

Proof reuse and isolation. Keep the ability to authenticate eligibility in abandoned epochs. Prefer reusing the existing guest's QC/selector verification, encoding and proving backend, with an explicit proofKind that separates execution settlement from eligibility authentication; a separate guest deployment is not necessarily required. proofKind, the program version or verification key, the trusted starting point and the output type must be authenticated by public inputs, not chosen by a caller-supplied label; L1 checks state transitions by allowed proofKind. Eligibility mode can only register record hashes; it cannot advance the EVM head, ConsensusContext, FI cursor, generation or settlement fees, and cannot by itself close exit responsibility; settlement and recovery modes keep their full verification. Aggregation and version upgrades must keep the same type isolation, with tests that wrong modes are rejected.

When recovery authenticates only the first expired block B, it does not automatically authenticate all later new epochs; every epoch named in evidence needs its own eligibility proof. Abandoning a generation does not exempt double signing. An eligibility proof shows only membership and the source of responsibility, not that no further implicit responsibility exists; withdrawal still needs the complete responsibility frontier. Evidence can be saved and propagated early, and on-chain punishment waits for eligibility authentication; while eligibility and responsibility are not cleared, the bond stays locked.

C.4.2 Same-chain accounting and off-chain finality gates

This version removes the separate L1 finality verifier. Registry snapshots, Inbox prove, eligibility records and withdrawals follow the current L1 branch's state and proofs; when a dependency is reorged, the same-chain transitions roll back with it. The contracts no longer promise to reject a transition whenever Ethereum has not finalized. On-chain evidence windows start from explicit settlement or retirement records; a fixed waiting time is not called finality.

Off-chain actions that consume this state still have a finality gate: enabling a new committee or reference, permanently closing an old signer domain, choosing the automatic recovery cutoff, retiring keys after exit, and external or cross-chain payouts. Validators use a trusted Ethereum verification source when endorsing L1 observations; the ABCI handles ordinary state with consistent committee certificates, and recovery and exit operations must also check the real finality of the corresponding L1 records. Proving something against canonical L1 does not prove its past finality.

EIP-2935 can authenticate historical execution hashes; this version adds no custom on-chain finality component based on EIP-4788 and SSZ gindices. Support for Ethereum forks is the job of the Ethereum clients in use. If contracts must later reject every unfinalized operation on their own, a trusted certificate or cryptographic finality verification has to come back; the promise cannot stay once the component is removed.

During a finality stall, production must not switch automatically to a reference without a valid committee endorsement; once the allowed view staleness is used up, it pauses. During the pause, the current protected committee collects new reference certificates independently; a retired set cannot restart the chain with new off-chain signatures. Irreversible off-chain actions wait for real finality; a larger Δ or derivation window only adds margin.

C.4.3 Automatic recovery of an expired committed suffix

Let B be the first block of the current unlanded committed suffix and P its predecessor. Using the network's parameters, F is the first L1 block with F.timestamp > B.timestamp + TIMESTAMP_MAX_OFFSET or F.number − 1 > B.anchorNumber + MAX_ANCHOR_OFFSET; recovery starts only after F is finalized. Process every Inbox proposal up to F to get P_F; the proof must check that this whole prefix, the recovery parent and the currently accepted context agree. If B landed before F, recompute F from the actual first unlanded block; never recover to a stale P.

The new generation is g+1, the Comet initial height is P_F.number + 1, and chain_id carries the same configId and the new g; H0 of the epoch formula is not reset. The initial set follows P_F's authenticated epoch/selector context: within the same epoch it inherits the current committeeRef, and at a boundary it uses the next selector already fixed by the kept prefix; the committee is not re-selected by F's wall-clock time. The recovery Comet genesis_time is fixed to F's L1 timestamp; the new genesis AppHash is computed by applying the unique g+1 transition to the authenticated P_F snapshot, not by each node from its local time or its own choice of initial state. fiCursor and daCredit are inherited from P_F's authenticated context, and the ABCI snapshot at that height (including the selector and accepted L1 reference) is restored, not the state of the abandoned suffix. The AppHash in B's Comet header can authenticate the ABCI snapshot at its parent height.

The new generation's first non-zero valid proposal carries B's old Comet header, commit, DataHash → tx0 path and EVM header, plus authenticated witnesses for F, the prefix and the context. The guest verifies historical membership, signatures, the EVM header hash, B.parentHash = P_F.hash and the real expiry condition; the input g must be the currently accepted value, and each recovery step can only advance to g+1. If an intermediate generation also expired without a valid landing, later proofs must verify the full recovery chain in order, and the final g comes from those steps; generations cannot be skipped. The chain must fit the configured evidence and DA limits; if it cannot, production pauses and the defined governance recovery applies, rather than endlessly producing generations that cannot be published. An invalid proposal still outputs zero blocks and advances neither g, the cursor nor credit; a retry must carry the recovery evidence again.

Signer state is isolated per (configId, g). After confirming the real Ethereum finality of the common F, a node atomically closes the old domain, keeps the old consensus key's H/R/S records, and then creates the new state. The ABCI's dedicated observation key keeps its own persistent generation-authorization state and does not take on Comet's H/R/S double-sign protection; closing the old domain also forbids new observation signatures in it, and a crash must not restore the old domain's authorization. Before recovery starts, the new generation's valid set independently collects L1 reference certificates; the first block's proposal must carry a valid observation certificate for that generation and cannot just reuse an outdated initialization reference. The certificate is a candidate input of the first block and is applied uniformly once BFT selects it; nodes cannot write different freshly collected certificates into different genesis AppHashes. The ABCI's observation signing logic must not pre-sign for an unauthorized future generation.

Payloads, original FI data, signed observations, epoch-start material and recovery evidence are kept until the related responsibilities are cleared and the needed L1 finality is reached; landing or a zero-block proof alone is not enough to delete them. Nodes must be able to restore and authenticate the historical ABCI snapshot at P_F, or replay from an earlier authenticated snapshot; the EL's changesets and state history must support rolling back the whole allowed unlanded suffix. About 3000 blocks on Mainnet is only a two-second-cadence estimate; size it from the maximum allowed height growth plus finality and sync margin, and measure pruning and recovery.

C.4.4 Loss of quorum and the DAO emergency procedure

If at least 1/3 of the voting power is unavailable for a long time, this version can stop permanently: vote statistics cannot be produced, Comet cannot change its set by itself, and once everything has landed there is no expiry evidence for any B. Automatic generation recovery does not cover this case. This version explicitly uses a DAO emergency hard fork or configuration upgrade, adds no L1 check-in re-selection and no based fallback, and discloses the governance trust.

  1. Confirm the common height and hash, direct node state, L1 and proof progress; tell apart a network partition, missing data and a real loss of quorum; preserve proposals, payloads, FIs and double-sign evidence.
  2. Through the existing governance process, the DAO publishes a future stopTimestamp and a record stopping the old configuration, leaving margin for propagation, L1 finality and clock error. Clients and derivation explicitly reject blocks the old configuration produces after that time; "no landing for T seconds" alone does not make all old commits expire.
  3. Keep a landing grace period for old blocks covering at least stopTimestamp plus the network timestamp window; then close new proposals under the old configuration, wait for the closing record to finalize, and settle the bounded old Inbox prefix (invalid content can be proven as zero blocks). If data is unavailable, no zero-block proof may be faked: first recover the archived data or consider a separate exceptional upgrade.
  4. The final proven and L1-finalized checkpoint is the only recovery base. The DAO activates a new configId, H0, initial committee root and signing domain, keeps the original FI cursor and accepted state, and treats re-initializing resource parameters as a public upgrade rule; old roots, old-domain evidence and bond responsibilities remain.
  5. Nodes upgrade, confirm the configuration, verify the initial root and finality, and start the new consensus. Close the incident after observing height progress, matching hashes at the same height, FI service, landing and real proofs. The runbook must name the governance executor, prover, archiver and node-operations owners; having no owner is not a launchable state.

Emergency powers and latency. Version 1 uses the existing DAO and timelock by default and assumes no fast re-selection or restart authority; recovery time also includes the announcement, the old-block landing grace period, proofs, finality and node switchover. Deployment documents must fill in the real governance delay, roles and powers. A pre-authorized fast path, if needed, must define its signing threshold, allowed actions, validity period and audit separately; a pause power is not a power to change the committee or recover.

Follow-up. Fast recovery for committed blocks proven permanently invalid against finalized L1 can be studied; it must authenticate B's real commitment and the complete failure reason, rule out bad witnesses and temporary unavailability, and cannot rest on a generic "derivation failed". That path and permissionless halt recovery both need their own security review.

C.5 Stake, penalties and node roles

C.5.1 Eligibility, keys and exit

All active seats vote, with no sampling. Launch needs at least 4 active addresses (7 recommended) and no address above 1/3 of the seats. Addresses do not prove independent entities, and at least 1/3 of the weight can block quorum. When seats are full, new seats queue and incumbents may hold seats for a long time; this version adds no forced rotation or term limit, and bounded admission would need separate design.

The registry binds validator/bond identity, the consensus public key, the dedicated observationKey, the L1 operator and the payout/exit address, keeping historical mappings. Consensus keys, ABCI observation keys and L1 gas-paying keys are separate; observation keys use a fixed domain and historical authorization rules and cannot reuse any generic transaction-signing interface. The observation key affects preconfirmation safety and needs its own custody, rotation and compromise response. Re-registration uses a new bond identity; historical eligibility cannot be re-bound to a released or newly deposited unrelated bond.

An exit request removes the identity from future eligible-set checkpoints but does not release funds immediately or revoke the fallback-submission rights of an operator that still has responsibilities. Withdrawal must prove that responsibility has ended through the complete settled/retirement frontier, covering voting, landing and every generation still authorized; a favorable, lazily chosen QC chain, or a check of only the roots already registered on L1, is not enough.

The proof that responsibility has ended must show that the set currently in effect no longer contains the identity, that the cutoff of the next fixed selector is past its removal point, that later monotonic selectors cannot select the removed identity again, and that the old generation's unsettled responsibilities are cleared or ended by a valid recovery record. While proof progress is insufficient, funds stay locked, but L2 rotation does not wait for it. Unauthenticated reports cannot extend the lock indefinitely.

Withdrawal also waits for the protocol's double-sign evidence window, which starts when the corresponding on-chain settlement or retirement record closing the responsibility is accepted; no countdown that could run out early may start before eligibility authentication and responsibility closing are complete. Abandoned epochs still allow reports through eligibility proofs and cannot be excluded for being unsettled. The window must cover propagating authenticated responsibility data, generating eligibility proofs and submitting to L1; it cannot simply be MAX_UNPROVEN_AGE plus a margin, and is not counted from Ethereum finalization. A reorg rolls back the dependent withdrawal along with it. Nodes wait until the withdrawal is actually finalized before retiring keys or taking irreversible external actions. Abandoning a generation does not release the bonds of members reused by a kept selector.

Historical eligibility records are distinguished by configId, generation/selector source, epoch-start block hash, slot and root, and cannot be overwritten. Eligibility evidence that cannot be authenticated cannot be slashed immediately; and when proof lag leaves responsibility open, the bond cannot be released first while the right to report lapses.

C.5.3 Full nodes and RPC states

Voting validators run CometBFT + ABCI + EL + L1 data and consensus verification. After a long outage or restart, a node first syncs the accepted configuration and checkpoint from finalized L1 and does not accept new certificates from a retired set based only on its old local Comet state. Non-voting full nodes that independently verify and serve preconfirmations run the same verification stack without consensus private keys; nodes that only serve L1 derivation and settlement data need not follow the unsafe Comet chain, and an RPC endpoint is not a validator.

For a full preconfirmation node, the Engine head points to the committed execution head; safe points to the last ancestor fully published on canonical L1 and successfully derived; finalized points to a checkpoint that passed a valid proof, with both the proof and its L1 inclusion at Ethereum finality. An L1 reorg can move safe back; finalized must not advance on a BFT commit or an unfinalized prove alone. proven is exposed as a separate state or API rather than squeezed into the three Engine tags. One coordinator updates forkchoice for the client and the ABCI, and reorgs and recovery check ancestry first.

Configuration. H0/N, S/Δ, the L1 observation signing domain and freshness/cache limits, the L1 operator slot and lookback, M, network windows, DA RATE/BURST/MANDATORY_MAX, FI decoding/transaction/settlement caps, IL K_IL/Q/bytes/gas, unproven-suffix limits, the evidence window and the real governance delay must all be fixed and included in configId; no unlimited defaults, and no local running time decides consensus results.

Observation refresh must not depend on new EVM blocks, and initialization, recovery and committee changes must cover that path. Parameters that have not passed benchmarks and fault tests are not launch-ready.

Networks and retention. Etna's block gas limit is 45M and the current block zk gas limit is 100M. The current spec gives Mainnet 512 blocks / 6144 s and Hoodi 128 blocks / 1536 s. The finalized-only version 1 raises both Hoodi windows to 512 / 6144 as part of the not-yet-scheduled Etna, with ETNA_FORK_TIME as the common activation boundary and no separate fork. #22189 is the specification PR; the spec, clients and guest must all change and take parameters by the same fork rule, and the constant change must not apply retroactively to pre-Etna history. Test before activation, at the activation block, proposals spanning the boundary and historical replay; this is still an unimplemented configuration and cannot be called launch-ready without window and recovery tests. Wider windows also increase unlanded responsibility, rollback history and evidence-retention needs.

With the anchor already about 96 slots old, no missed slots and no extra delay, Hoodi's nominal anchor margin is only about 32 blocks / 384 s and Mainnet's about 416 blocks / 4992 s; observation refresh, batching waits, L1 inclusion and retries and the safety margin all come out of that. 384 s is the rotation period, not a guaranteed landing interval; submitting every 1–2 minutes is only a strategy to test, not a guarantee the protocol derives. Real checks use both the block-number anchor window and the timestamp window, and slot estimates are not exact execution block counts.

MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE must cover the normal age of the finalized checkpoint, observation propagation and operating margin; being only slightly above about 20 minutes does not tolerate another 20 minutes of finality stall. Require worst allowed reference age + batching/sending/inclusion/retry budget + safety margin < the conservative time budget of the anchor window, and meet the timestamp window separately; the time formula is only a conservative configuration check, and real expiry follows the block-number and time rules of C.4.3. Retention of ABCI snapshots, Comet blocks and certificates, EL changesets and original FI data must support the maximum allowed height growth, finality and recovery margin.

Economics. L2 calldata already pays gas, but that is not the real L1 blob and encoding byte cost. The DA budget only limits load and does not compensate landers. Evaluate L1 blob price pressure, fallback submissions, the opportunity cost of FI service slots, snapshot and reporting costs, and the subsidy or reimbursement needs without a separate landing reward. A dynamic DA fee, shared pool or separate compensation, if added, needs its own parameters and settlement rules; existing gas fees cannot be assumed to cover it.

Must verify. Committee rotation and landing continue while full proofs stall; the separate unproven limit triggers; T_e and slot boundaries and missed slots, recovery selector inheritance, exit attacks that hide uncommitted QCs; different honest finalized heads, abstentions, expired or old-set certificates, independent observation refresh after a long outage; many FIs alongside ordinary slots, FI consumption after a zk overflow, bad witnesses not consuming the cursor; single-block DA overflow and sustained overload, append-simulation contamination and worst-case K_IL/Q; overlapping proofs at offset = 0 and partial overlap continuing correctly, zero blocks, fees not paid twice; historical snapshots and EL rollback, L1 reorgs and finality stalls, loss of observation quorum, observation-key rotation, compromise and old-domain replay, eligibility proofs and double-sign penalties for abandoned epochs, record-hash de-duplication, text availability and membership proofs, rejection of mixed proofKinds, bond locking under unbounded proof delay, Etna parameter selection before and after the fork and historical replay across the boundary, and the real DAO recovery latency.

Bridge and relayer launch checks. After Etna, waitProofTarget / etnaAnchorFor choose the L1→L2 proof target: read the anchor number and state root from an eligible L2 header, call eth_getProof at that L1 anchor height, and set HopProof's blockId to the L2 timestamp that recorded the root, never the L1 block number. Check that the canonical L1 header and root, the L2 header and getL1StateRoot agree before building and sending the claim. Before Etna, or when the destination chain does not support Etna, waitProofTarget still uses the indexed CheckpointSaved path; test before and after the upgrade and waits that span it.

The UI splits the L1→L2 wait into finalized-reference visibility, an available Etna anchor, historical proof fetching and destination execution; about 13–19 minutes is only the normal visibility part, not a fixed arrival promise. Update the text in every language, the pending and timeout states and the help pages. A normal finalized-checkpoint age of about 64–96 slots does not imply that the age exceeds 96 blocks in the second half of every period; propagation, L2 sync and target selection add to the real age. About 6.4 minutes is only the normal checkpoint period and does not bound the extra wait during a finality stall to one period. Messages confirmed on L1 but still waiting for a valid proof must not be shown as failed or prompt a repeated deposit.

Check the relayer's waitProofTarget waiting, end-to-end task deadline, queue ack/retry/concurrency and pending retention item by item, so normal finality and proof-fetching waits do not use up permanent retry counts or drop tasks, and the relayer resumes after a restart without double execution. waitForConfirmations remains the source-transaction confirmation stage; it, the proof-target wait, single RPC timeouts and destination transaction send/receipt timeouts are configured separately, not all raised to one large timeout. Stress-test worker occupancy, backlog growth and recovery throughput during long waits.

Historical proof RPC capability. The L1 RPCs actually used by the relayer and bridge-ui must return valid account and storage Merkle proofs for allowed proof targets that verify against the expected anchor state root. Acceptance covers real public endpoints, load-balanced backends and fallbacks; an archive label, eth_syncing = false or a successful historical eth_call is not enough. A hash-based archive can keep historical tries; a Geth path-based archive's historical flat state is not historical proof capability, and v1.17.x needs an explicit --history.trienode=N setting with that history actually accumulated. Raising only --history.state, or turning trie history on just now, does not bring back already-deleted proof data; other clients are accepted by their actual version and eth_getProof capability (Geth archive and historical proofs).

Age and retention window. Replace today's hard-coded 96 with a deployment setting MAX_PROOF_TARGET_AGE_BLOCKS. Convert the protocol's MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE from time into a conservative L1 height-age budget and add L2 target selection, sync and RPC delay to get A_required. Let A_target be the relayer's allowed target age, H_rpc the verified minimum RPC historical proof depth, and M_fetch the margin for blocks produced, retries and backend differences while fetching. Require A_required ≤ A_target and A_target + M_fetch ≤ H_rpc. Actual selection still checks the height difference between the current L1 head and the anchor, never mixing seconds and blocks. When RPC capability is insufficient, reject the deployment configuration and alert, instead of silently taking a smaller value and leaving valid messages waiting forever. This parameter is a proof-fetching service constraint and does not loosen anchor validity in consensus.

MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE only limits further production; after a halt, existing anchors keep aging. Deployments must state the outage and retry budget they support and add it to the historical retention requirement. Beyond the current finite retention, switch to a verified historical-proof RPC and re-check or adjust A_target under the same window constraints before fetching proofs; otherwise keep messages waiting to choose a new anchor after recovery. Switching RPCs while the old age limit still rejects every candidate does not help. A finite history window does not promise proof fetching through an unbounded outage. Every retry re-confirms that the target, the canonical L1 root and the L2 root entry at the matching timestamp are still valid, and never reuses a proof target that has become invalid.

Error handling when proofs are unavailable. Recognized temporary unavailability of historical state or tries is a recoverable infrastructure error: keep the message, back off, re-select a target or switch to a qualified RPC, and alert; it must not use up the message's permanent failure budget or go straight to an unconsumed dead-letter queue. Configuration incompatibility must stay visible rather than look like a normal wait; malformed or invalid proofs are classified separately, and errors are not all retried forever without distinction. This must be implemented in the error branch after eth_getProof, not only in the anchor-age check before fetching.

Monitoring separately tracks the L1 finalized-checkpoint age, acceptedL1View lag, the anchor age of the chosen L2 proof target, L2 root availability, historical proof failures and retention margin, pending message age and depth, and the dead-letter count; only the legacy branch keeps monitoring CheckpointSaved sync. Distinguish normal waits, L1 finality stalls, loss of observation quorum, insufficient historical RPC capability and relayer faults. Alert thresholds come from each stage's limits and margins, not from multiplying old thresholds.

Acceptance covers waits beyond the normal 13–19 minutes, proof fetching near 96 and 128 blocks and the configured bounds, the retention race when blocks keep coming after a target is chosen, inconsistent capability across load-balanced backends, back-off and retry with no dead-letter loss when historical state or tries are unavailable, a halt beyond MAX_L1_VIEW_AGE and the long-outage fallback, and queue draining and UI recovery afterwards. Proof formats before and after Etna, waitProofTarget switching across the upgrade, and re-selection after an L2 root entry becomes invalid must also pass; the frontend, deployment parameters, alerts and runbooks ship together.

A devnet with at least 4 validators completes fault injection and real-proof verification before the roughly two-second cadence is evaluated; contracts, ABCI, EL, guest, signing uses and the upgrade and emergency procedures all need audits.
