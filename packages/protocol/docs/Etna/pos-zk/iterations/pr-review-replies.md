# PR #22262 review replies

## Comment 6011464863 (dantaik, critical issues)

## Lead adjudication of this review (comment 1 of 3)

Thanks — this is the most useful review this design has received, and I have not been able to break points 2 and 4. Point-by-point:

**1. D5 causes most of the open problems — ACCEPTED as a real consequence, escalated.** You are right that with data and proof in one transaction the data exists only off-chain between certification and landing, that this is a validium-shaped window, and that it is the root of the certified-but-unavailable failure Mode B exists to repair. D5 is a fixed decision taken by the design owner, so I cannot reverse it unilaterally. I have recorded your argument, and the alternative you propose (post data within *k* L1 slots of certification, extend the chain only once posted, prove and aggregate later, with a proving deadline well inside the 18-day blob retention window) as an open decision item with the June-2026 incident as the counter-argument it must defeat. Window quantified in the specification: certification to landing is at least the proving envelope, so 30-60 minutes at nominal.

**2. One third of the stake can freeze the chain at no slashable cost — ACCEPTED, and I could not break it.** The chain of reasoning holds as written: being offline is not an offence, epochs advance by L2 height so a stalled epoch's set cannot rotate, recovery restores the checkpoint with the same set, and there is no forced inclusion, no L1 exit and no pause lever. That makes it a ransom vector whose cost is unrelated to bridged value. Your cheaper variant also holds — with no maximum set size, adding validators until 2 s rounds stop completing is a real vector. Three v1 changes are being specified: (a) a validator-set rotation or inactivity rule keyed on **L1 time** so a stalled epoch cannot keep its set indefinitely; (b) a maximum set size, sized from the S2 timing measurement; (c) an **L1-enforced exit from the last settled state**. On (c): that is deliberately narrower than forced inclusion — it needs no inclusion path and rewrites nothing at or below the accepted checkpoint, so it is compatible with the fixed decisions and I believe it can ship in v1 rather than being deferred.

**3. Single-proof soundness with no circuit breaker — ACCEPTED in part.** Correct that "one proof from any one registered backend" makes soundness that of the weakest backend, and that the design simultaneously removed today's multi-proof and every pause lever. Two changes: (i) require k-of-n proofs for the state roots that finalize withdrawals specifically, so the cost multiplier applies only where theft is possible — the choice of k and n depends on S1's measured proving cost, which is not yet known; (ii) a protocol-native, time-limited withdrawal veto, which is not the owner-controlled pause lever this design deliberately removed. I am not promising both: (i) may be unaffordable, in which case (ii) plus disclosure is the honest fallback.

**4. Mode B's economics defeat themselves — ACCEPTED, and the sizing rule is being changed.** This is the sharpest finding in the review. Sizing the non-refundable component at `value_at_risk(D_MAX)` makes a legitimate recovery a pure public good: the triggerer pays the whole provisional value and the benefit is a live chain for everyone, so no rational actor triggers and the halt survives. Lowering it restores cheap griefing. You are right that no single value satisfies both. The fix is to stop asking the bond to do two jobs: the bond prices **spam** and is sized to the cost of a spurious trigger, while a legitimate **completion is rewarded** from the security budget the fees fund (D-8). The residual you name remains and is already disclosed in REC-03: an unfunded or below-cost pool reaches the trigger with no adversary at all, and recovery cannot repair an economics stall.

On the strategic point — that confirmation is provisional until L1 acceptance, so the design pays BFT cost for preconfirmation-strength guarantees — that is a fair challenge to the value proposition rather than a defect. The design's answer is soft-confirmation UX plus the option of a lower-latency bridge policy for bounded amounts; whether that justifies the machinery is a product question, and I am putting it to the design owner alongside D5.

**5. Process — AGREED.** Chapter-polishing stops here. Your three upstream items (D5, forced inclusion in v1, proof acceptance policy) go to the design owner with this review attached. The D2 review of the repaired recovery and the epoch-handoff model (A6) proceed in parallel, because neither depends on those outcomes.
