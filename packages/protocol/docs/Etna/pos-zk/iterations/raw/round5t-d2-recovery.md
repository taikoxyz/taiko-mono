## Finding R5T-D2-01 — an under-sized completion bundle retires less than the branch tip, leaving valid certificates at *non-retired* heights; the resumed chain cannot be re-decided and halts, at gas cost to any account, while the completion is rewarded

**Severity: Critical.** One-line rationale: the completion transition's only unverifiable input is the maximality of the certificate bundle, and maximality is exactly what makes REC-04(4)'s "no discarded block is landable" argument harmless — with a non-maximal bundle the discarded branch's certificates at heights `resumeHeight … tip` are valid forever (CONS-08(3)) and are at heights that are **not retired**, so every node that holds one must halt rather than adopt the restarted chain (CONS-09(3), CONS-15(1)–(2), CONS-05); since >2/3 of the epoch's power signed them and recovery changes no set, no quorum can form at those heights — the unbounded halt D-7 selected Mode B to eliminate, reachable permissionlessly for the price of one L1 transaction.

**Exact rule / missing rule.**
- `06:387` (REC-02, *Completion evidence*): "the completeness of the list — that it omits no higher certified height — is not checkable from L1 state, because L1 does not hold the set of certificates that exist. A bundle that omits a higher certified height is a rule violation by the completer; **its only effect is a retirement floor below the branch tip**, and REC-04 (4) shows that this leaves no discarded block landable." The rule also fixes the completion as **callable by any account** (`06:386`, `06:384`), accepting **exactly one parameter — the bundle** (`06:386`).
- `06:626` (REC-04(4)(ii)): "This holds even when the bundle proves less than the branch tip: a smaller bundle retires fewer heights, but the heights it leaves at or above `resumeHeight` are still discarded-branch heights, **not landable** ones." The argument is about *landing* only. It says nothing about certification, locks, or a node's finalized view at those heights.
- `06:388` (REC-02, *Retired heights*): retirement is defined by the floor: "A batch whose first height is below `resumeHeight` is rejected … a retired height can never be re-certified." A height `h` with `resumeHeight ≤ h ≤ tip` is **not below** `resumeHeight`, hence **not retired**.
- `10:25-26` (INV-01): the design states its own dependency explicitly: a completed recovery "deliberately produces two valid, forever-valid certificates at the same height, **one of them at a retired height**". With a non-maximal bundle the duplicate is *not* at a retired height — the sentence that makes the duplicate harmless is false of the object the rules permit.
- `02:283-284` (CONS-09(3)): "If a valid conflicting certificate exists at a height **that is not retired**, the validator halts (CONS-15(2))."
- `02:187-188` (CONS-05): "A node that instead holds a conflicting lock at H (CONS-04) or a **conflicting finalized block at H** has evidence that the assumptions failed; it halts (CONS-15) and MUST NOT silently adopt the other branch."
- `02:147-148`, `02:156` (CONS-04(1)(a) and (2)): a lock is released by "the commit at H of the locked value, **within the current generation**", or by a strictly later same-height PoLC; the carve-out that voids locks applies only where "`H` is below `resumeHeight` … it is released by the retirement itself". At `h ≥ resumeHeight` no carve-out exists.
- `02:100` (CONS-02, sign-state store): "A height at or above `resumeHeight` may be signed again, but only at a fresh round" — this grants no unlock, and expressly says "locks remain CONS-04's".
- `06:389` (REC-02, *No discretion*): "the completer chooses nothing that L1 does not verify". Literally true, fatally incomplete: L1 verifies each included certificate and cannot verify the ones left out, so the completer chooses the **floor** — and with it whether the resumed chain is decidable at all.
- **Missing rule (the repair):** either (a) make maximality checkable — resumeHeight may be written only from evidence that no higher certified height exists (an L1-recorded per-epoch certified head, or a proof attesting the branch tip); or (b) void, by rule, every lock, finalized view and certificate at heights **above the restored checkpoint but at or below the discarded branch tip**, not merely below `resumeHeight`; or (c) require `resumeHeight` to be set to the branch tip by a rule that does not depend on the completer's list. None is present.

**Assumptions.** None beyond the rules. The discarded branch is the ordinary case: since `land` is strictly sequential (`04:218-219`, `firstBlockHeight == max(lastLandedHeight + 1, resumeHeight)`), any settlement stall leaves a certified provisional chain from the checkpoint up to the depth cap `D_MAX` (`06:154-211`), i.e. up to ~900 blocks / ~1 epoch at the registered proposal `L = 900` — so a complete bundle needs `floor(D_MAX/L)+1 ≈ 2` certificates (`06:387`) and a bundle of one certificate is a valid, non-empty, maximality-violating input. The certificates themselves are public (CONS-05 *Publication* requires nodes to be able to serve them).

**Concrete attack trace.**
1. Settlement stalls for `T_STALL` (any REC-03 path, including the disclosed no-adversary case of an unfunded pool, `06:535-541`). The L2 chain has meanwhile certified provisional blocks from the checkpoint `L` up to `tip` (possibly hitting the depth cap). Any account invokes recovery and posts the bond (`06:382`).
2. `T_RECOVERY_DELAY` elapses with no accepted batch. **Any account** — not the invoker, not a validator — calls the completion transition with a bundle containing **one** certificate: the lowest certified height above `L`, i.e. `L+1`. Every check of `06:387` passes: epoch matches the L1 schedule, set root matches the staking mapping, bitmap/signature count consistent, quorum holds, height `> lastLandedHeight`. `resumeHeight := L+2`. The generation increments, the refundable balance goes to the bond's poster, and (on one reading, see R5T-D2-02) `REC_REWARD` is paid.
3. L1 now accepts only batches starting at `L+2` (`04:218-219`), whose first header must descend from the restored checkpoint at `L` (REC-04(1)). But heights `L+2 … tip` of the discarded branch each carry a valid, forever-valid commit certificate (`02:248-249`), signed by >2/3 of the same epoch set that the recovery does not change (`06:389`). Those heights are not retired (step 2 made the floor `L+2`).
4. A correct validator holding such a certificate at a height that is not retired must halt: CONS-09(3) directly, CONS-15(1)–(2) by the same reading, and CONS-05's last sentence because it holds a conflicting **finalized** block at that height. Independently, its per-height lock was released only "within the current generation" (CONS-04(1)(a)); at `h ≥ resumeHeight` the retirement carve-out does not apply, so CONS-02(b) requires it to prevote the locked (discarded) value, and a PoLC at a strictly later round cannot form because more than one third of the power is locked (`02:165-168`). Either mechanism gives the same outcome: the restarted chain cannot produce a quorum at `L+2` or at any height up to `tip`.
5. The chain halts. The stall clock was **not** reset by the completion (`06:381`: "a completed recovery changes neither `lastAcceptedBatchTime` nor the checkpoint"), so after `REC_COOLDOWN` a further recovery may be invoked; but the completion of *that* recovery is again supplied by whoever calls it, so the same account can supply the next under-sized bundle — each time at the price of one L1 transaction (`06:386`) — and each completion that moves the floor by one height also "records a retirement" and is (again, on one reading) rewarded. The honest side must find, fund and time each new recovery; the attacker must only win the completion call.
6. Net outcome: provisional history is discarded *and* the chain is left unable to finalize, which is the exact failure class D-7 was selected to end ("an unbounded halt whose only remedies are outside the protocol", `DECISIONS.md` D-7).

**Inside / outside the claimed fault model.** **Inside.** No assumption is broken: no Byzantine stake beyond the fault model, no L1 censorship, no governance action. The attacker is any L1 account with gas; steps 2 and 5 are ordinary permissionless calls the rules *require* to be permissionless (`06:386`, `06:399`). If the reader prefers the permissive reading of CONS-04/CONS-05 (old-generation certificates are "void for canonical purposes" and impose nothing), then the finding is not dismissed — it becomes the **missing rule** case: the specification's harmlessness argument for an under-sized bundle is written for landability only, and *which* reading governs a certified height above the floor is left for an implementer to invent, in the one place where the choice decides whether the recovered chain can finalize.

**Attacker resources and cost.** One in-flight (or self-invoked, reimbursed) recovery; one L1 completion transaction with a one-certificate bundle; optionally a bond that is returned on completion and a `REC_REWARD` that is calibrated to return `B_REC_KEEP + C_GAS_REC` (`07:220-223`, `09:169-170`). No stake, no bond risk beyond the returned refundable part, no `REC_WINDOW` escalation if attempts are spaced beyond the window (R5T-D2-02). The damage (chain halt plus discarding of up to `D_MAX` provisional blocks) is bounded only by the attacker's willingness to keep sending completion transactions.

**Requirement / fixed decision affected.** D-7 (Mode B: a *bounded* rollback that lets the chain keep serving; unbounded halt was the stated reason for the selection); REC-01/REC-04 (the restart linkage and the "only recovery replaces history above the checkpoint" boundary); INV-01/CONS-12 as written (the duplicate certificate is asserted to be at a retired height); REC-03's claim that the mechanism's security rests on "the retired heights below `resumeHeight`, which no generation can re-open" (`06:397-398`) — the security rests on a floor the caller chooses. D2 step 5: an unaddressed way to reach the trigger's consequences that falsifies the "no selective/unsafe rollback" half of the analysis.

**Evidence.** `06:384-389` (completion callable by any account, one-parameter bundle, completeness unverifiable, "only effect", "no discretion"), `06:626` (REC-04(4)(ii)), `06:388` (retirement defined by the floor), `10:25-26` (INV-01 "one of them at a retired height"), `02:283-284` (CONS-09(3) halt at a non-retired height), `02:187-188` (CONS-05 conflicting finalized block), `02:147-156` (CONS-04 release rule and its retired-height-only carve-out), `02:100` (CONS-02 fresh-round rule, no unlock), `02:248-249` (CONS-08(3) validity forever), `04:218-219` (L1-06 contiguity), `06:381` (clock not reset), `07:220-223` / `09:169-170` (reward).

## Finding R5T-D2-02 — the bond prices the *invoker*, not the attacker: the trigger-inducer risks nothing, the escalation is windowed, and a completion that wedges the chain is reimbursed from the fee pool

**Severity: High.** One-line rationale: REC-02/REC-03 justify the replacement of `B_REC_KEEP ≥ value_at_risk(D_MAX)` with `B_REC_KEEP ≥ C_SPURIOUS` on the ground that "the bond prices the abuse" — but the bond is paid by whoever *invokes*, the abuse is committed by whoever *induces the stall*, the two need not be the same actor, the attacker's gain is off-chain and unbounded, and `REC_REWARD` is paid to the completion's invoker whether or not the completion left the chain able to finalize — so the instrument that is supposed to price manipulation can subsidise it.

**Exact rule / missing rule.**
- `06:382` (REC-02, *Who may invoke*): "`B_REC_KEEP ≥ C_SPURIOUS` … the non-refundable component prices **spam and manipulation only** … `C_SPURIOUS := C_GAS_REC + C_REORG + C_DISRUPTION`"; "The honest trigger is paid for instead by the recovery-completion reward … not by the bond". `09:161-166` repeats the withdrawal of the value-at-risk sizing and retags `value_at_risk(D_MAX)` as "disclosure only".
- `06:465-475` (REC-03, symbolic cost): "`cost(n attempts) = Σ_{k≤n}(B_REC_KEEP(e_k)) + escalation(n) + cooldown(n) + C_GAS_REC · n + V_own`", described as the cost of "inducing the trigger". This is the **invoker's** cost. `C_REORG` and `C_DISRUPTION` are social costs the attacker does not pay; `cooldown(n)` is a delay, not a cost; `V_own` is the attacker's *own* destroyed value, not a price.
- `06:395` (REC-02, *Repeated recovery*): "escalation counts every attempt … so a second or later attempt returns at most the first attempt's cost and costs more than it returns". `attempts` counts invocations **in the preceding `REC_WINDOW`** (`06:382`); an attacker who spaces attempts by more than `REC_WINDOW` resets the exponent and pays `B_REC_BASE` again.
- `07:220-223` (ECON-02(5)(f)): "`REC_REWARD = min(REC_REWARD_CAP, floor(ALLOC_REC_PPM · pool_now / 1_000_000))` … paid to the invoker … in the completion transaction", with the cap "at the base attempt". The predicate for payment is REC-02's "when the completion records a retirement" (`06:386`) — **undefined** for a non-empty bundle that verifies heights but does not raise the floor ("a repeat completion with stale certificates is a no-op on the floor", `06:387`), and satisfied by the one-certificate bundle of R5T-D2-01.
- `06:529-541` (REC-03 residual (1)): off-chain profit is "outside any protocol bound" — disclosed. That disclosure does not repair the pricing claim; it is the reason the pricing claim is needed.
- **Missing rule:** a sizing or gating relation between what the *beneficiary of the rollback* pays and what the rollback destroys, or a completion predicate that requires the retirement to be the branch tip / the chain to be landable; and a definition of "records a retirement" for a bundle that verifies heights at or below the stored floor.

**Assumptions.** The trigger is reachable by the attacker or by a third party (R5T-D2-03, or the disclosed unfunded-pool case). `REC_WINDOW`, `B_REC_BASE`, `B_REC_KEEP`, `REC_REWARD_CAP` are all `unmeasured` (`09:160-170`), so the arithmetic below is structural, not numeric.

**Concrete trace.** (1) A stall is induced (cost to the inducer: whatever the stall path costs — see R5T-D2-03 — and *no bond*). (2) Someone — the attacker or an honest defender — invokes; that party posts `B_REC(e)` and loses `B_REC_KEEP` to the treasury. (3) The attacker completes with the one-certificate bundle: the retirement is recorded, so the invoker is paid `REC_REWARD`, calibrated to `≥ B_REC_KEEP + C_GAS_REC` whenever the pool can pay (`07:223`). If the attacker also invoked, its net cost is ≈ gas − (reward − bond costs) ≈ 0, and the chain is wedged (R5T-D2-01). (4) The attacker repeats after `REC_COOLDOWN` and `REC_WINDOW`; the escalation exponent is 0 again, and the reward pays again. On the strict reading of "records a retirement" the attacker forgoes the reward on stale bundles but still pays only gas to wedge the chain; on the permissive reading the fee-funded pool becomes a per-window subsidy for wedging it. Either way "the bond prices the abuse" is not a property of this design, and REC-03's symbolic cost function measures the wrong actor.

**Inside / outside the claimed fault model.** Inside: no assumption failure; the actor is any L1 account (or any sub-threshold coalition that can reach the trigger).

**Attacker resources and cost.** Gas for completion transactions (and, if it invokes, a bond that is returned on completion and a `REC_REWARD` reimbursement). The `C_SPURIOUS` floor is not what the attacker pays: `C_REORG`/`C_DISRUPTION` are borne by the protocol and its users.

**Requirement / fixed decision affected.** D-8 (L2 fees fund security — the pool is the security budget, and this draws on it to subsidise a manipulation); D-9 (destination rules are respected, but the treasury/pool asymmetry means the protocol's net position is negative per cycle even though the attacker nets ≈ 0); D-7 (the resistance argument D2 step 5 requires is the thing being priced); ECON-02(5)(f)'s stated purpose ("a fee-funded payment to the honest invoker, bounded so that it cannot make a spurious trigger profitable") is not met for a completion that wedges the chain.

**Evidence.** `06:382`, `06:386`, `06:387`, `06:395`, `06:465-475`, `06:529-541`; `07:220-223`; `09:160-170`; `DECISIONS.md` D-8, D-9.

## Finding R5T-D2-03 — REC-03's "exactly three ways" and its falsifier list omit the cheapest L2-only path to the trigger: a legal, certified batch whose proof is not produced inside the permitted envelope

**Severity: High.** One-line rationale: REC-03 asserts that inducing the trigger "requires breaking P3, P4 or P5, and there are exactly three ways: (i) suppress every prover, (ii) censor every landing transaction at L1, (iii) price the protocol out of the blob market" — but a single proposer slot can certify a *rule-legal* block whose proof exceeds `T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED`, falsifying P3 without suppressing any prover; because acceptance is strictly sequential, the checkpoint then cannot advance past it, and `T_STALL` elapses; no falsifier row covers this, so the review question ("can an L2-only adversary reach the trigger by any path this analysis does not cover?") must be answered **yes, unless a bound that is nowhere stated holds**.

**Exact rule / missing rule.**
- `06:456-463` (REC-03): the three-way enumeration and "Each lies outside the L2 fault model".
- `06:417-424` (P3/A-DA-2): "at least one honest, adequately-resourced prover completes **each committed batch's proof inside the envelope**". The premise is a property of every committed batch; a batch whose proof no prover can complete inside the envelope falsifies it.
- `09:139` (`T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED`): "the maximum proving latency the protocol permits a batch to consume … **MUST NOT be fixed below D6's planning envelope `T_PROOF_ENVELOPE` (1,800 s = 900 L2 blocks at 2 s)** … it is not the envelope itself, which is **the planning assumption a normal proof consumes**". Nothing in the parameter table or in any rule bounds the proving cost of a *legal* block, and nothing authorises a validator to reject a block for being proving-expensive: CONS-01's validity predicate (`02:64-73`) has no such clause, and HALT-03's cap is a *depth* bound, not a per-block cost bound (`06:154-211`).
- `09:181` (`L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT`): "Value **unmeasured** in this specification: no L2 configuration is fixed here". The envelope is a plan, not a worst case over legal blocks.
- `04:218-219` (L1-06): `firstBlockHeight == max(lastLandedHeight + 1, resumeHeight)` — one stuck certified batch at the head blocks acceptance of every later batch.
- `04:715-719` (FI-10/FI-12): the narrow forced-inclusion duty can *compel* inclusion of an adversary-published transaction in the next batches (gas-bounded by `FI_RECORD_GAS_MAX`, but not cycles-bounded), which lets a non-proposer inject proving-expensive content into batches it does not propose.
- **Missing rule:** a registered bound on the proving work of any legal batch (a cycles bound enforced in the guest/validity predicate, or a parameter relation requiring `T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED` to dominate the worst-case legal batch), or a rule that makes a batch exceeding it objectively attributable and punishable; and a falsifier row "a legal, certified batch exists whose proof is not produced inside `T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED`".

**Assumptions.** The adversary holds <1/3 of the voting power but is *selected proposer* for at least one slot (CONS-06 selection is an expectation, not a per-window bound: "a coalition with Σw_i < W/3 proposes strictly fewer than one third of the slots **in expectation**", `02:206-207`), and its block passes CONS-01's validity predicate. No L1 capability, no prover suppression, no censorship, no market power.

**Concrete trace.** (1) The adversary fills its proposer slot with a block whose execution is legal at the registered L2 gas limit but whose proof cost (cycles per L2 gas varies by orders of magnitude across opcodes/precompiles; the measurement gate F2/F4 is explicitly open, `02:685-686`) exceeds `T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED`. Honest validators execute and certify it — validity is not a function of proving cost. (2) The head of the pipeline is now an unprovable-within-envelope certified batch. `land` cannot skip it (L1-06); no admission rule may reject it (L1-04, `04:98-105`); the data is public, so this is not a withholding case. (3) No batch extending the checkpoint is accepted for `T_STALL = T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + L1_FINALITY + T_RECOVERY_MARGIN` (`06:381`, `09:157`). (4) The trigger holds; any account may invoke; the delay runs; if the bomb's proof still has not landed, the recovery completes and discards the provisional range — including honest blocks above the bomb. Cost: one L2 block's fees and waiting; the rollback's value can be `value_at_risk(D_MAX)` (disclosure-only, `09:166`). (5) Variant: the same adversary publishes a transaction whose execution is proving-expensive; CONS-01(v)/FI-11 *require* it to be included in a batch (gas-bounded, not cycles-bounded), so the cost can be injected into honest proposers' batches and repeated after each successful forced inclusion.

**Inside / outside the claimed fault model.** The **attack** is inside: the adversary is an L2-only sub-threshold coalition, and no listed assumption (A-CONS-1/2, A-DA-2, A-L1-1) is broken by *the adversary* — rather, P3 is falsified by rule-legal L2 action, which is precisely the case the analysis claims to cover by "suppress every prover". Whether a legal block exists whose proof exceeds the permitted envelope is **unmeasured**; that is the finding: the claim is not established, and the falsifier list has no row for it. This is a D2 step-5 blocker under the same standard round 4 applied ("inability to establish it is a blocker, not a disclosure item", `04-round.md` R4-MB-01).

**Attacker resources and cost.** One proposer slot (weighted selection; obtainable by a coalition below one third in expectation, and by buying/coercing a single slot — LIM-01 discloses that no rule prices slot-buying), the L2 gas of one block, and the wait. Optionally the L1 gas of one publication to add a forced-inclusion item.

**Requirement / fixed decision affected.** D-7/D2 step 5 (the resistance claim that the selection rests on); REC-03 P3 and falsifier (a) ("proving ceases to be permissionless in practice — a gate, a whitelist, or a de-facto single prover"), which does not cover "proving is permissionless but this batch is not provable in time"; HALT-03/LIVE-02's premise that a normal proof never binds the cap (this is not a normal proof, and no rule defines what happens when a legal batch is not normal).

**Evidence.** `06:417-424`, `06:456-463`, `06:557-586` (falsifiers), `06:154-211` (HALT-03), `09:139`, `09:157`, `09:181`, `04:98-105`, `04:218-219`, `02:206-207`, `02:64-73`, `04:715-719`, `02:685-686`.

## Finding R5T-D2-04 — the repaired `resumeHeight` derivation assigns to L1 a verification L1 cannot perform: Ed25519 signatures and per-signer weights are not available on L1

**Severity: High.** One-line rationale: REC-02 requires the **contract** to verify every bundle certificate "against L1 state only", including "the signer bitmap and the signature list" and quorum `3·s > 2·W` over s = the effective stake of the *distinct authenticated signers* (CONS-03) — but L1 has no Ed25519 precompile (the spec's own reason for the ECDSA heartbeat) and the per-epoch mapping stores only `(setRoot, totalVotingPower, rootCommittedAt, setVersionBlock)`, never the roster or per-index weights, so neither signature authentication nor `s` is computable on L1; the transition carries no proof and accepts no witness. Either recovery cannot complete — the unbounded halt returns — or the implementer must invent the security-relevant step the repair was supposed to remove.

**Exact rule / missing rule.**
- `06:386` (REC-02, *Completion transition*): "The transition accepts **exactly one parameter — the discarded-branch certificate bundle** … every element of the bundle is verified by the contract itself, against L1 state only, before `resumeHeight` is written." No proof argument, no witness, no signature-verification route.
- `06:387`: "(c) the signer bitmap and the signature list satisfy CONS-03's distinct-signer rules … and (d) quorum holds under exactly `checked_mul(3, s) > checked_mul(2, W)` with `W` the total voting power of that epoch read from the same mapping entry."
- `02:125-129` (CONS-03, *Distinct signers*): "bit i set exactly when one signature by `v_i`'s committed **Ed25519** key is present and verifies over the canonical vote bytes … and `s` summed over set bits only." `02:104-106`: "Voting power is taken only from that commitment — never from a vote, a certificate, a witness or a prover-supplied list".
- `02:244-251` (CONS-08(3)): the epoch's set root, total power and length must remain reconstructible from Ethereum-final L1 data — `root` and `total` are; the roster/weights are not stored (`03:491-496`, `09:124`).
- `03:531` (MEM-13(1)): "L1 has no **Ed25519 precompile** and `ecrecover` is cheap, so L1-side liveness evidence must be ECDSA." The same constraint applies to the completion transition, and the rule contradicts it.
- `04:203` (L1-05 row 25): the normal path keeps quorum **in-guest** ("checked in-guest against the signatures and quorum (PRF-04(ii)–(iii)) and recorded by L1 as the per-signer participation input").
- `06:398` (REC-02, *Authorization binding*): "the security property rests on the retired heights below `resumeHeight`, which no generation can re-open" — i.e. on this very derivation.
- **Missing rule:** how the contract obtains the authenticated `s` (per-index weights and pubkeys for the epoch) and how it verifies Ed25519 signatures — or a redesign in which the completion evidence is attested by the proof system (a proof object over the bundle) instead of verified directly on L1.

**Assumptions.** A completion is attempted with a well-formed bundle. No adversary is needed for the defect: an honest completer hits the same wall.

**Concrete trace.** (1) Recovery is invoked after a stall; `T_RECOVERY_DELAY` elapses. (2) Any caller submits the bundle. (3) For clause (c)/(d) the contract must, for each certificate, recover the canonical index order and effective stake of every set bit. The staking mapping holds a Merkle root and a total; the leaves live in the staking ledger's *current* state, which is not a per-version snapshot, and the canonical order is defined by `keccak(DOMAIN_SET_KEY, chainId, pubkey)` over the version's roster (`02:322-329`). Even if the contract were handed a roster, verifying `popcount(signers)` Ed25519 signatures in EVM bytecode is a pure-Solidity verification with no precompile, for a set whose size `N_MAX` and certificate count are unmeasured; and REC-02 forbids supplying the roster as a parameter. (4) The result is one of: completion reverts for every input (the recovery never takes effect; the chain remains stalled past `T_STALL` — the D-7 failure), or the implementer invents a trusted relayer/attester path, which reinstates exactly the "authorization is a tautology / the invoker supplies a claim" class of defect round 4 found and which REC-02 claims to have closed.

**Inside / outside the claimed fault model.** Outside the *attack* fault model but inside the *engineering* one: it needs no adversary at all. It breaks the D2 review's central question (2) — the derivation is genuine in form but not realisable as specified — and it is a normative rule that cannot be implemented.

**Attacker resources and cost.** Not applicable; the harm is that the sanctioned remedy for a stall cannot execute as written (or executes via an invented trust assumption). If an implementer invents a trusted path, the cost is a new privileged/attestation surface in a mechanism whose selling point is permissionlessness.

**Requirement / fixed decision affected.** D-7 (recovery must work when needed); REC-02 *Completion evidence* and *No discretion*; CONS-03's "same predicate everywhere … the L2 client, the zkVM guest and the L1 Inbox MUST each evaluate exactly `3·s > 2·W` over the same authenticated `s` and `W`" (`02:114-116`) is unsatisfiable at the Inbox for bundle certificates given the stored mapping; D-1's Ed25519 choice and MEM-13's ECDSA rationale collide here.

**Evidence.** `06:386-387`, `06:398`; `02:104-106`, `02:114-116`, `02:125-129`, `02:244-251`, `02:322-329`; `03:491-496`, `03:531`; `04:203`; `09:124`.

## Finding R5T-D2-05 — a completed rotation replaces provisional history outside REC-02, which REC-01/HALT-04 forbid; it is disclosed as Open (F7) but the contradiction with the "recovery is the only sanctioned replacement" rule is not resolved

**Severity: High.** One-line rationale: D-14's rotation is a second permissionless L1 transition that can strand `finalized-but-unsettled` heights above the checkpoint — the specification says so itself ("a replacement of provisional history above the checkpoint by a path other than REC-02 — a REC-01 defect") — while REC-01(a)/(d), HALT-04 and REC-02's *Permissionless, no rescue* row state as normative and Proven that recovery is the **only** way history above the checkpoint may be replaced; the two fixed decisions (D-7 and D-14) collide, and the collision is carried as an Open item rather than resolved.

**Exact rule / missing rule.**
- `02:562-570` (CONS-16(5), *Precondition this transition cannot verify from L1 — Open*): "a rotation can complete while finalized-but-unsettled heights remain above the checkpoint. Those heights would then be unlandable, which is **a replacement of provisional history above the checkpoint by a path other than REC-02 — a REC-01 defect** … Closing it needs an L1 fact this specification does not yet record: a stored last-finalized marker, a composite transition whose proof carries the head batch, or REC-02's own record and delay."
- `10:297-298` (LIM-01, F7): "A rotation could complete while finalized-but-unsettled heights remain above the checkpoint and strand them, which is a replacement of provisional history outside REC-02 — a REC-01 defect … until then the rotation's liveness bound is conditional and **the rotation may not be described as production-ready**."
- `06:37-83` (REC-01), `06:213-232` (HALT-04) and `06:399` (REC-02): recovery is "the only sanctioned way that history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint may be replaced", and "no operator, no DAO and no validator quorum controls" it.
- `02:524-528` / `02:544-550` (CONS-16(1),(3)): the rotation's trigger is the **same settlement-stall clock** as REC-02's, and it may close an epoch at the highest produced height; the stalled scenario is exactly the one in which L2 production continues (the settlement stall is not a production halt).
- **Missing rule:** the L1 fact that makes the rotation's precondition enforceable (the spec names the three candidate mechanisms and adopts none), or an amendment of REC-01/HALT-04 that admits the rotation as a second sanctioned replacement path with its own bond, delay and exposure statement.

**Assumptions.** `T_ROTATE < T_STALL` by construction (`02:526-527` vs `06:381`), so on a stalled chain the rotation becomes available first; the L2 chain has produced blocks into epochs `e+1` (etc.) under the depth cap before the rotation completes.

**Concrete trace.** (1) Settlement stalls; the L2 chain keeps finalizing provisional blocks, moving into epoch `e+1` (the unsettled-depth cap permits up to `D_MAX` ≈ one epoch at the registered `L=900`). (2) `T_ROTATE` elapses; any account invokes the rotation; the L1 clock condition and one-pending-transition rules are met. (3) At completion the rotation re-partitions: `h_first(e') = h_close(e)+1` for every epoch in `[e+1, e_r]`. The blocks already produced under `e+1`'s set now have epoch fields that disagree with the amended schedule; they are void for canonical purposes and can never land (`02:558-559`). (4) The produced-and-finalized provisional heights above the checkpoint have been replaced by a path that is not REC-02 and has no bond. No two *simultaneously* canonical histories exist (the old certificates are void, not conflicting-canonical), but a second replacement path now exists and the "only by REC-02" normative rule (`06:222`, `06:37-83`) is false of the system as specified.

**Inside / outside the claimed fault model.** Inside: no assumption failure is needed for the transition to be invocable; the precondition it cannot enforce is precisely the case that arises in an ordinary settlement stall during continued production. The rotation's trigger is objective and its invocation is permissionless (`02:530-537`).

**Attacker resources and cost.** One L1 transaction to invoke the rotation (no bond; funding of the rotation is explicitly Open, `02:571-576`). The harm is the stranding of finalized-but-unsettled heights and the misleading guarantee class; the checkpoint is preserved, so this is a provisional-history and honesty defect rather than a fund-loss or safety defect.

**Requirement / fixed decision affected.** D-7 ("the only sanctioned way history above the checkpoint may be replaced is the permissionless, bonded, delayed recovery": REC-01(d), HALT-04, REC-02 *Permissionless, no rescue*), R9/R5 (the chain keeps serving; the replacement is not the selected mechanism), and D2 step 5 (the analysis assumes recovery is the only replacement path — `06:506-528` argues reinstatement is closed "by retirement", while a second path exists that writes no retirement).

**Evidence.** `02:521-589` (CONS-16), `10:297-298` (LIM-01 F7), `06:37-83` (REC-01), `06:213-232` (HALT-04), `06:399` (REC-02), `DECISIONS.md` D-14, `iterations/05-freeze.md` item 5 (D-14 "no rule removes weight"; F8 open), `02:627` (M7 falsifier F7).

## Checked and holds (round-4 defects I could not re-break, and positive checks for this angle)

- **The retirement endpoint is genuinely evidence-derived; no claim path remains.** `resumeHeight` is L1-local and computed only from verified certificates (`04:170`, L1-05 row 33; `04:218-219`, L1-06); the invocation stores no height or hash — MIG-02 slot 270 deletes the "invoker-claimed resume height" explicitly (`08:278`); PRF-02 does not carry it (`04:170-171`). The *only* non-evidence write is the empty-bundle raise to `lastLandedHeight + 1` (`06:387`), which retires nothing that is not already below the checkpoint and cannot lower the floor. Holds.
- **Round 4's "locks survive recovery" is fixed for a *complete* bundle.** The CONS-04(2) carve-out voids locks at retired heights (`02:156`), and with a maximal bundle every certified height of the discarded branch is below the floor, so no lock or conflicting-certificate halt survives. The fix is only whole for maximal bundles (R5T-D2-01).
- **Cancellation and the in-flight window: no two canonical histories.** Cancellation is exact and same-transaction with bond transfer to the treasury (`06:384`); a completion reverts once `lastLandedHeight > H_inv`; and the retirement is adopted only at the SYS-02 depth. A restart batch cannot land before the completion exists (it reads `resumeHeight`), and any L1 reorg that removes the completion also removes every later block including the restart batch (a reorg replaces a suffix), so the mixed "completion gone, restart landed" state is unreachable. Holds.
- **Upgrade mid-recovery.** GOV-03(e) forbids an upgrade from cancelling a pending recovery, shortening or extending `T_RECOVERY_DELAY`, altering the checkpoint or the bond destination, or substituting a claimed `resumeHeight` (`08:736-738`), and MIG-02 budgets the state. Holds.
- **Rotated heights and a later recovery.** After a completed rotation, a certificate whose signed epoch was reassigned fails REC-02's epoch check (`06:387`) and would revert the whole transition; that bundle must be omitted, but the affected heights are void for canonical purposes (`02:558-559`) and are simply re-produced under `e_r` — no halt arises from the *rotation alone*; the halt risk comes from the under-sized bundle relative to heights that remain certified and non-void (R5T-D2-01).
- **D-14 heartbeat eligibility does not create an L2-only trigger path.** Eligibility is an affirmative L1 act (`03:531-535`), an L2-only adversary cannot suppress it, exclusion touches only future set versions and removes no weight within one (`03:534-535`), and REC-02's "no rule changes a member's weight within a version" (`06:389`) is consistent with CONS-16. F8 (L1 censorship of heartbeats) is a disclosed Open falsifier and is outside my angle's fault model. Holds.
- **The escalation/reward cap does make *repeated attempts inside `REC_WINDOW`* loss-making** (`06:395`, `07:222-223`); the defect is the windowed reset and the actor mismatch (R5T-D2-02), not the arithmetic.

## Answers to the five questions this review was charged with

1. **Can an L2-only adversary induce the trigger, and what does it cost now?** Yes, on one path the analysis does not cover: a single proposer slot certifying a legal block whose proof exceeds the permitted envelope (R5T-D2-03), because acceptance is strictly sequential. Cost: one L2 block plus the wait; no bond, no stake, no L1 capability. Where the adversary only induces the stall, the bond is irrelevant to it (R5T-D2-02). The disclosed no-adversary case (unfunded pool, `06:535-541`) remains reachable.
2. **Can recovery be made selective despite retirement?** The floor is genuinely derived (`resumeHeight` has no claim path), but the *evidence subset* is chosen by the completer, who is any account (`06:386`); so retirement can be made partial and, worse, the resumed chain can be made undecidable (R5T-D2-01). The bridge/replay rules themselves are sound: executed L1 withdrawals always rest on state at or below the floor (`06:392`), re-relay of rolled-back deposits is correctly stated, and the narrow forced-inclusion obligation survives retirement (`04:723-725`).
3. **Is the bond at risk at the pre-registered values, and what does an attacker gain off-chain?** The bond is at risk *for the invoker* (`B_REC_KEEP` non-refundable; whole bond to the treasury on cancellation; escalation counts attempts). It is not at risk for the beneficiary of a rollback, who need not invoke; and a completion that "records a retirement" is reimbursed up to `B_REC_KEEP + C_GAS_REC` — including the completion that wedges the chain (R5T-D2-02). The off-chain gain is unbounded by the protocol and is disclosed as residual (1) (`06:529-531`); with `value_at_risk` withdrawn as a sizing rule (`09:161-166`) nothing in the design connects the price to the damage.
4. **Can two canonical histories exist at any point?** I found none: not in flight, not across a cancellation, not across a rotation, not across an upgrade landing mid-recovery. The defects in this area are (a) a second *replacement* path (the rotation, R5T-D2-05) and (b) an ambiguity at exactly one point — whether a certificate for a discarded-but-not-retired height is a "valid conflicting certificate" that forces a halt (R5T-D2-01).
5. **Are REC-03's premises, symbolic cost and falsifiers complete?** No. The premises are now named and separable (an improvement over round 4), but P3 is falsifiable by rule-legal L2 action (R5T-D2-03); the symbolic cost prices the invoker, not the abuse (R5T-D2-02); there is no falsifier at all for a completion that retires less than the branch tip (R5T-D2-01) or for an L1-unverifiable completion evidence (R5T-D2-04); and the review requirement cites falsifiers (a)–(h) while the list is (a)–(i) (R5T-D2-07).

## Verdict on the D2-mandated review

**Not settled.** The repaired mechanism fixes the round-4 tautology: the retirement floor is derived on L1, the invoker claims nothing, completion is a named L1 event gated on Ethereum finality, cancellation is exact, and upgrades must carry a pending recovery unchanged. I could not re-break those. But the D2 step-5 claim still fails, on three independent grounds: (i) the one input L1 cannot verify — the maximality of the bundle — decides whether the resumed chain can finalize at all, and an under-sized bundle produces a halt (Critical, inside the fault model, gas-priced and rewarded); (ii) the trigger has an uncovered L2-only path that needs one proposer slot, and the bond does not price the actor who uses it (High); (iii) the completion evidence cannot be verified by the contract that must verify it (High). Per D2 step 5 and round 4's R4-MB-01, inability to establish the resistance claim is a **blocker, not a disclosure item**; the D-7 selection must therefore remain unsettled, and findings (i)–(iii) must be repaired or the procedure reopened before it is treated as settled.
