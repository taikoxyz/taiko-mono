## F1 — Critical: the total per-transaction resolution walk is absent from the artifact, so the round-1 settlement-halt attack still lands

**Severity: Critical.** Rationale: the closure summary says a position now resolves by executing the record's transactions in the record's own order, each executing or being discharged at its turn; no such rule, predicate or guest check exists in this snapshot. FI-13(1)(a) still resolves a position only when **all** of the record's transactions are in the executed payload, FI-13(4) still voids a record only when **none** of its transactions is forceable, and PRF-04(vi) still requires 'every position in [c, c + R) is resolved under FI-13(1)'s three modes' with exactly that non-total content. The consequence is the round-1 Critical itself: a live record with one forceable and one never-forceable transaction is unresolvable, and because R = min(d(A) - c, FI_MAX_PER_BATCH) is computed and every position in [c, c + R) must be resolved, one permissionless publication freezes settlement chain-wide until the record expires, repeatable at one publication per T_PROVE_DEADLINE.

**File + rule id (all at the round-2 snapshot).** spec/04 FI-13(1)(a) ('executed — the record at j is live at A and all of its transactions appear in the batch's executed payload, each exactly once, in increasing transaction index'); FI-13(1) final sentence ('A record that was forceable somewhere in the batch and was not executed is (b) or invalid'); FI-13(4) ('A record above any registered bound, or one none of whose transactions is forceable at any block's pre-state in the batch, is void'); spec/05 PRF-04(vi) ('require that every position in [c, c + R) is resolved under FI-13(1)'s three modes — executed (all of the record's transactions in the batch's executed payload, each exactly once, ...)'); FI-11(2)(4) and FI-12(2) as before; FI-12(5)'s 'why this cannot produce a permanent halt'.

**Evidence that no fix landed (this is the finding's core).** (a) The FI-13 line in the stripped spec/04 is byte-identical between the round-1 snapshot 169480a56 and ba0bb3532 (same length 4655, same content); the PRF-04(vi) resolution sentence is identical. (b) git diff 169480a56 ba0bb3532 for spec/04 is +12/-12 lines and concerns only the settlement pair (L1-03(6), the checkpoint struct, the forcedSettlementAt comment) and FI-12(5)(ii); spec/05 changed two lines (PRF-02(5) reconciliation). (c) A grep of the whole snapshot for 'per-transaction', 'own order', 'at its turn', 'non-executable', 'unaffordable', 'resulting nonce' finds none of these constructs in spec/**. (d) The delta's added review corrections are RC-1 (R is the window), RC-2 (the floor is a floor of one), RC-3 (the per-block bound) and RC-4 (L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT bound by the anchored view) — no correction touches resolution totality. (e) git log shows ba0bb3532 is HEAD and no later commit on any branch touches the FI spec or mentions resolvability/discharge.

**Assumptions.** None: publication is permissionless (DA-07) and the attacker's record is a normal record.

**Attack trace.** 1. The attacker publishes one record whose decoded payload is two transactions: t1, its own next transaction (nonce equal to its current nonce, balance covering gasLimit x maxFeePerGas + value), forceable at the batch's first pre-state; and t2, any signed transaction whose declared nonce is far above its sender's current nonce and unreachable by any transaction a batch could carry, or otherwise never forceable at any batch pre-state. 2. The record is live at A, so mode (c) fails. Mode (a) requires all transactions in the executed payload; t2 cannot execute, so (a) fails. Mode (b) requires the record to be over-bound or to have none of its transactions forceable; t1 is forceable, so (b) fails. FI-13(1) allows no other ground. 3. Once the frontier reaches the position (d(A) is monotone and the position is in the fixed window [c, c + R) whenever R spans it), FI-11(2)(4)/PRF-04(vi) reject every proof: the position is not resolved, and R cannot be chosen smaller because R = min(d(A) - c, FI_MAX_BATCH). FI-11(3)(a) demands the unconditional advance, which no compliant proof can produce. 4. Every batch's proof is invalid, land can never succeed, and settlement stops chain-wide until the record is dead (T_PROVE_DEADLINE after publication). The attacker repeats with one publication per cycle. 5. The consequence is bounded but repeatable, and it falsifies FI-12(5)'s no-halt claim; the exit remains available from the last settled state (L1-13(3)), so this is a liveness/finality freeze, not a loss of funds.

**Fault-model verdict.** Inside the fault model: no key, stake, validator or producer cooperation is needed; the only inputs are a permissionless publication and one transaction of the attacker's own account. The attacker cannot manufacture a discharge ground for someone else's transaction under the intended rule, which is exactly why the intended rule (once written) is safe — but the rule is not in the artifact.

**Attacker cost.** One L1 publication per T_PROVE_DEADLINE (plus one forceable transaction of its own).

**Requirement affected.** FI-12(5) (the no-halt argument), the increment's purpose, FI-11(3)(a) (the unconditional advance becomes unsatisfiable), and PRF-04(vi) (which cannot enforce a ground the rules do not define).

**Suggested repair (unchanged from round 1).** Write the total walk into the specification, not just the review prose: in FI-13(1)(a) replace 'all of its transactions appear in the batch's executed payload' with 'each of its transactions, in the record's own order, either appears in the executed payload or is discharged at its turn as non-executable', define the two discharge grounds exactly (the transaction's nonce not equal to the sender's nonce resulting from the record's own preceding transactions, or the sender's balance at that pre-state below gasLimit x maxFeePerGas + value), make FI-13(2)-(4) state that a position is resolved when every transaction is executed or discharged, and have PRF-04(vi) verify the same per-transaction walk in the record's own order — plus the ordering check that a transaction discharged as non-executable must indeed have been non-executable at its turn (so a proof cannot discharge an executable transaction). Until that text exists in spec/04 and spec/05, the closure is not implemented.
