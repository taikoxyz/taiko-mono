Limitations — what this design does not claim

Problem this lesson solves. It collects, in one place, the assumptions the design rests on, the numbers that are not measured, the blockers that are still open, and how to report a finding — so a learner can tell a specification from a working system.

In plain language

Nothing here is implemented, measured, audited or deployed. This is a design document. Interface sketches are not compiled code; there are no benchmarks produced by the project; the static site is a deliverable, not software (GEN-01). Every "Proven" in the specification is an argued property with named premises, not a machine-checked or tested one (LIM-01, scope row).

How to read the tags. Rules carry Proven (an argument with named premises), Assumed (a justification plus the consequence of failure) or Open (the evidence that would close it). Numbers carry derived (arithmetic on decided or sourced inputs), sourced (an external constant, cited) or unmeasured (a placeholder with the experiment that would fix it). No proposed value may be described as a benchmark (GEN-04, PARAM-01).

The assumptions. The full list is in 01 — requirements and threat model §4. The ones a reader should carry everywhere: fewer than one third of each epoch's voting power is Byzantine (A-CONS-1); message delivery among correct validators is timely often enough after a global stabilization time (A-CONS-2, A-CONS-3); correct validators hold and serve the data they voted for (A-CONS-5); at least one adequately-resourced prover completes each committed batch (A-DA-2); Ethereum includes valid transactions and does not reverse finality (A-L1-1); a new node can obtain a weak-subjectivity checkpoint within the freshness window (A-CONS-6); the zkVM is sound (A-CRYPTO-1); the economic assumption A-ECO-1 holds at the relevant time; and the DAO does not execute a malicious upgrade (A-GOV-1).

Three failure classes, kept apart. F1 — liveness-assumption failure, safety intact: a quorum offline, a partition, a prover outage, blob expiry, L1 congestion. The protocol halts, backpressures, waits or replaces provers; it never rewrites finalized history. F2 — safety-assumption failure: one third or more Byzantine stake, key compromise, a zkVM soundness break, or a governance upgrade that changes the rules. The safety claims do not apply, and two conflicting certificates can both be cryptographically valid; ZK verification cannot repair this. Detection and disclosure is the honest response. F3 — implementation-assumption failure: a client, prover or contract bug; out of scope for this design project, but the specification must not make it undetectable (01 §5, INV-01).

What is explicitly not claimed. That any attack is unprofitable; that proving throughput or the 2 s cadence has been measured; that ZK verification repairs failed consensus assumptions; that a PoS confirmation before accepted on L1 is irreversible in any mode other than Mode A's stated assumptions; that a settlement halt is bounded in duration; that users will be compensated for losses — penalties are not compensation and no universal bound on economic loss exists (specification verdict, ECON-11).

Precise definitions

  • Design-only claim. No implementation, deployment or measurement; no performance number without a source or an explicit unmeasured tag (GEN-01).
  • Evidence tags. Proven / Assumed / Open for rules; derived / sourced / unmeasured for numbers, each used once and never merged (GEN-04, GEN-08).
  • Parameter discipline. A value with no derivation and no source may not be given: it is marked unmeasured with the experiment that would fix it; changing a value is a governance action (PARAM-01).
  • Unmeasured register. The list of quantities that must be measured, with workload, hardware and methodology, before the corresponding value is fixed (PARAM-03).
  • Conditional liveness. The complete liveness claim with its clauses; liveness ends at the first clause that fails, and a safe halt is a permitted outcome (LIVE-01).
  • Sustainability inequalities. Three inequalities — rate, concurrency, data — that must be evaluated with measured inputs before launch; if any fails, the correct response is backpressure, not a longer latency assumption (LIVE-03).
  • Limitations register. The accepted limitations, stated once with their consequence, none of which may be presented as a strength or dropped from a summary (LIM-01).
  • Open blocker. An item that blocks a decision rather than being disclosed as a caveat; the Mode B cheap-trigger resistance is the named example (REC-03).
  • Finding ledger. Every adversarial finding recorded with its rule, preconditions, attack trace, fault-model position, attacker resources, harm, affected requirements and resolution or explicit acceptance (LIM-03).

Open blockers and unresolved human decisions

  • REC-03 — Mode B cheap-trigger resistance. Within the stated normal-operation assumptions, it is not established that an ordinary malicious leader or sub-threshold coalition cannot cheaply trigger a recovery that replaces honestly confirmed history. Mode B is specified and not selected; inability to establish this is a blocker, not a disclosure item (REC-03).
  • Migration audit obligations. Every upgraded contract's storage layout must be regenerated and diffed (pnpm layout); whether the deployed proxies route through a ForkRouter is unmeasured; the completeness of the privilege-removal table is a review obligation; and INV-04 is Open for the operational audit of the completed migration (MIG-02, MIG-04). The migration rehearses on Hoodi, but the rehearsal's cost and duration are unmeasured and it is a procedural mitigation, not a guarantee (MIG-06).
  • Measurement gates. The gates above (F2, F3, F4, F1) plus the three LIVE-03 inequalities and every row of PARAM-03 must be closed by measurement, with workload, hardware and methodology recorded, before launch (PARAM-03, LIVE-03).
  • Adversarial review questions. Q-A1 (is the availability counterexample reachable inside the stated assumptions?), Q-A2 (is "one certificate per batch" sound for the whole prefix?), Q-A3 (does the blob binding hold against a grinding prover?) remain open, and the red-team ledger LIM-03 is Open.
  • Human decisions. Where slashed stake goes is an unresolved human decision (ECON-06), and rewards may be unfundable because TAIKO has no mint path today — a treasury transfer or a token upgrade is a user decision, and no token-appreciation assumption may substitute for funding (LIM-01, ECON-02).

Attacks this prevents

  • Mistaking the design for a working system. GEN-01 and the scope row of LIM-01 state that there is no implementation, audit, model or measurement (GEN-01, LIM-01).
  • Reading assumptions as guarantees. Every rule carries an evidence tag and every assumption carries the consequence of its failure (GEN-04, 01 §4).
  • Quoting a proposed number as measured. PARAM-01 and PARAM-03 make that a specification violation, not a rounding error (PARAM-01, PARAM-03).
  • "The proof verifies, therefore the history is unique" and "the DAO would fix it". Both are explicit non-arguments; the invariant stops at the one-third boundary and governance capture is a separate failure class (INV-01, GOV-02).
  • "A timeout proves something does not exist". Forbidden in every mode (STATUS-10).

Attacks this does not prevent

  • F2 failures. One third or more Byzantine stake, key compromise, a zkVM soundness break or a malicious governance upgrade; the safety claims simply do not apply (INV-01, GOV-02).
  • Implementation bugs (F3). Out of scope for this project, and the specification only promises they must not be made undetectable (01 §5).
  • Liveness limits. A minority outage halts the chain; there is no degraded mode; a halt, including a permanent one, is explicitly permitted (LIM-01, LIVE-01).
  • Losses. Penalties are not compensation, no universal bound on economic loss exists, and no compensation channel is defined (ECON-11).
  • A reader who ignores this page. No document prevents someone from quoting a placeholder as a result; the defence is the tag and the finding process below.

Self-check

Is the 2 s block cadence a measured result?

No. It is a decided input (D1) that holds "under stated operating assumptions". The measurement that would support it — round completion time at 2 s with a permissionless global set — is on the unmeasured register (PARAM-03), and D1 itself says cadence is not finality, proof, settlement or withdrawal latency.

A rule is tagged "Proven". Does that mean it has been tested?

No. "Proven" means an argument with named premises. The limitations register states plainly that every "Proven" is an argued property, not a machine-checked or tested one, because there is no implementation (LIM-01, GEN-04).

Mode B is fully specified. May an implementer switch it on if the chain halts?

No. Mode B is not selected; it may not be implemented, deployed or advertised, and nothing in it may be used to describe a PoS confirmation as anything other than irreversible under Mode A. Selecting it requires the D2 evidence-and-review procedure, and REC-03 blocks selection until its cheap-trigger resistance is established (REC-02, REC-03).

How to report a finding

  • Security issues go to the project's security contact, security@taiko.xyz, not to a public issue. This design is pre-implementation, so a design-level break is still a security finding.
  • A specification finding should carry the elements the project's finding ledger requires: the exact rule (or the missing rule), its preconditions, a concrete attack trace, whether the attack is inside or outside the claimed fault model, attacker resources and cost, the harm, the affected requirements, and evidence or a worked counterexample (LIM-03, 01 §8). Findings are closed only when the attack trace is retained and the specification states why it no longer succeeds.
  • A course-versus-specification inconsistency is a finding too: R14 requires the learning site to match the specification. Quote both sentences and link the rule id; if this course ever states a stronger guarantee than the specification, the specification wins and the course is the bug (course map, 01 §7 R14).
  • Useful falsifiers to aim at. The availability counterexample reachable inside the stated assumptions (Q-A1); a number presented as measured; a rule stated more strongly than its premises; a privileged lever still live after migration (INV-04); or a violation of D2, D3, D5, D6 or D7 (fixed decisions).

Challenge this. This page relied on: (i) the register counts being read from the specification at this revision — the counts are derived from the text and will drift as the specification is edited, while the rule identifiers stay stable (GEN-03); (ii) the fault-boundary mapping of F1/F2/F3 being the requirements document's, not this page's invention; (iii) the claim that zero performance numbers of this design are measured, with only Ethereum constants tagged sourced — if a measured number has since been added, this page is stale; (iv) the blockers list being complete only up to the recorded review questions Q-A1 to Q-A3; (v) the reporting route being the repository's stated security contact and the LIM-03 finding format. Falsifier: point at any number in this course that is presented as measured without a source, or any guarantee stronger than the specification's — that is exactly the finding this page exists to make possible.
