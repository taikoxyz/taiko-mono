07 — Two seconds and thirty minutes

Problem this lesson solves. It puts the six timelines of a batch on one axis — 2-second production, PoS finality, a proof that may take up to 30 minutes, L1 acceptance, Ethereum finality and withdrawal — and makes explicit that during the proving window the batch's data is not on Ethereum and lives only with L2 nodes.

In plain language

Two seconds is a production cadence, nothing more. The fixed decision D1 sets one L2 block every 2 s under the stated operating assumptions, and SYS-03 forbids equating that cadence with finality, proof, settlement or withdrawal. A "2 s confirmation" shown before STATUS-06 must be accompanied by the statement that the batch's data and proof are not yet on Ethereum (STATUS-11, SYS-03(g)).

Thirty minutes is a planning envelope for proving, not a deadline. D6 fixes that a proving latency of a few minutes up to 30 minutes is normal operation, and the parameter table records T_PROOF_ENVELOPE = 1,800 s as decided and explicitly not a worst-case bound (D6, PARAM-01). The clock that envelope measures starts somewhere specific: the glossary defines proving latency as the wall-clock time of the proof computation for one batch, measured from the moment the batch's blocks are final and the witness is available to the moment the proof exists — and it excludes batch collection, witness generation, queueing, L1 inclusion and Ethereum finality (glossary). A proof sitting in a queue, or an L1 transaction waiting for inclusion, is outside the envelope, not evidence that the envelope was violated.

During the window, the data is not on Ethereum. Canonical data availability is the accepting L1 transaction itself (DA-01), and before that transaction exists the only copies are held by L2 participants: the validators and full or archive nodes whose retention assumption is stated in ROLE-03 and whose availability premise is A-CONS-5. That is why a node may not treat its own possession of the data as availability for anyone else, and may not report a status above STATUS-03 to another party on that basis (STATUS-09). The retention clock starts at acceptance, not at a separate proposal — under D5 there is no interval in which data is on L1 while its proof is not (DA-05).

The milestones along the axis are statuses with fixed evidence. A block is PoS-finalized when a published commit certificate plus the receiver's own data check exist (STATUS-04); a batch is proof-ready when a completed ZK proof bound to its data commitment exists off-chain (STATUS-05); it is accepted on L1 when the Inbox took (data, proof) in one transaction and advanced its checkpoint (STATUS-06); it is Ethereum-finalized when the accepting transaction is in a finalized Ethereum block (STATUS-07); and withdrawal-eligible only after the message-authentication path and the required delay (STATUS-08, MSG-03). A proof existing is not settlement: only STATUS-06 unlocks withdrawals.

What stops the queue from growing forever. The unsettled-depth cap is a consensus rule: a correct validator must not prevote or precommit a block whose unsettled depth would exceed D_MAX, so when provers fall behind, production stops and the chain is in the safe-halt state (HALT-03). The same rule says a normal proof taking the whole D6 envelope — up to 30 minutes, 900 blocks at 2 s — is not misconduct and not grounds for any recovery action. Recovery is a separate, unselected mode: Mode B is specified but not selected, and its stall trigger T_STALL is inactive and must exceed the envelope by construction (REC-02, D2).

Precise definitions

  • T1 block cadence. Proposal to next proposal, fixed at 2 s by D1; a production cadence, not a guarantee that a block at any height exists, is final, provable, settled or withdrawable (SYS-03(a)).
  • T2 PoS finality latency. Proposal to published commit certificate; the structure is owned by the consensus page and the value is unmeasured (SYS-03(b)).
  • T3 proof latency. PoS-finalized batch to STATUS-05; D6 fixes the normal-operation envelope, which is a design envelope, not a measurement (SYS-03(c)).
  • T4 L1 acceptance. Proof-ready to STATUS-06; it depends on L1 inclusion and on admission rules that contain no expiry and no discretion (SYS-03(d), L1-04).
  • T5 Ethereum finality. Accepted on L1 to STATUS-07; a property of Ethereum plus the configured finality depth F_L1 (SYS-03(e), SYS-02).
  • T6 withdrawal availability. STATUS-08, depending on T4/T5, the message-authentication path and the delay of MSG-03, derived from the whole pipeline (SYS-03(f)).
  • Proving latency (the clock of the 30-minute envelope). As defined in the glossary: from the batch's blocks being final with the witness available to the proof existing, excluding batch collection, witness generation, queueing, L1 inclusion and Ethereum finality (glossary).
  • Unsettled depth. The number of L2 blocks between the highest L1-accepted checkpoint and the block under consideration; D_MAX caps it and is unmeasured (HALT-03, PARAM-01).
  • Safe halt. A correct participant stopping rather than choosing between histories; a correct outcome in Mode A (HALT-01, glossary).

Read the last three rows as the honest limit of the timeline: the 2 s cadence, the cap and the withdrawal delay are design inputs or placeholders, and the only thing the specification says about a normal proof is that the envelope runs to 30 minutes and that a proof inside it is not misconduct. If the envelope is exceeded anyway, nothing is slashed and nothing is rolled back: the cap eventually binds, production stops and the chain is in the safe-halt state. That is a liveness limitation, disclosed as one (HALT-03, LIVE-01).

Attacks this prevents

  • Timeline conflation. Reading "2 s blocks" as 2 s finality, settlement or withdrawal; SYS-03 forbids equating any two of the six timelines, and STATUS-11 makes the disclosure an obligation (SYS-03, STATUS-11).
  • Premature withdrawal. Treating a proof-ready batch as settled: no value may leave the bridge surface on anything weaker than STATUS-06 (MSG-03, STATUS-05).
  • Pretending the data is already on Ethereum. Canonical availability is the accepting transaction; before it exists, only L2 nodes hold the data, and possession is not availability to others (DA-01, STATUS-09).
  • Slow proof treated as fault. Slashing or "recovering" because a proof took minutes: HALT-03 states a normal proof taking the whole envelope is not misconduct and not grounds for any recovery action, and WH-02 keeps a proof that has not been submitted in the local-suspicion class (HALT-03, WH-02).
  • Unbounded backlog. Letting finalized-but-unsettled history grow until its data can no longer be retrieved; the cap converts an unbounded, unrecoverable failure into a bounded, recoverable halt (HALT-03, DA-06).

Attacks this does not prevent

  • A prover outage or a proof that outruns the envelope. Settlement stalls; the cap binds; the chain halts safely. Mode A forbids discarding the finalized history instead (REC-01, HALT-01).
  • Loss of the L2 copies during the window. If correct validators cannot hold and serve the data they voted for, a finalized block can become unprovable; the design discloses this and halts rather than rolling back (A-CONS-5, LIVE-01).
  • L1 congestion or censorship of the accepting transaction. Data and proof cannot land; forced-inclusion requests cannot land either; this is a disclosed consequence of L1 failure (T-7, A-L1-1).
  • An Ethereum reorganisation above the configured finality depth. Only Ethereum-final L1 facts may enter L2 consensused state, and deeper failures are outside the protocol's control (SYS-02, L1-12).
  • Recovery. None is available in the selected mode; Mode B is specified but not selected, so no timeout here can rewrite history (REC-02, D2).

Self-check

Between the head block becoming PoS-finalized and STATUS-06, where does the batch's data live?

Only with L2 participants — validators, full and archive nodes, under the retention assumption recorded for ROLE-03 and the availability premise A-CONS-5. Canonical availability is the accepting L1 transaction itself (DA-01, ROLE-03).

When does the 30-minute proof clock start?

When the batch's blocks are final and the witness is available; it ends when the proof exists. Queueing, batch collection, witness generation, L1 inclusion and Ethereum finality are excluded from the definition (glossary).

A proof takes 29 minutes. Is that misconduct, or grounds for recovery?

No. It is inside the D6 envelope; HALT-03 says a normal proof taking up to 30 minutes is not misconduct and not grounds for any recovery action, and Mode B's trigger is not active in any case (HALT-03, REC-02).

Challenge this. The lesson used: (i) D1 and D6 as decided planning inputs, not measurements; (ii) the 900-block figure as arithmetic, 30 × 60 / 2; (iii) the glossary definition of proving latency, including that witness availability is itself an assumption (A-DA-4, ROLE-03); (iv) the claim that data lives with L2 nodes during the window, which follows from DA-01 and STATUS-09; (v) the Ethereum finality depth as a sourced external parameter whose deployment value is unmeasured; (vi) the cap D_MAX and the withdrawal delay as unmeasured parameters; (vii) Mode A's no-recovery promise as the selected mode (D2). Falsifier: find a sentence here that equates two timelines, presents 30 minutes as measured or as a bound, or puts the batch's data on Ethereum before STATUS-06 — each would be a defect under SYS-03 or DA-01.
