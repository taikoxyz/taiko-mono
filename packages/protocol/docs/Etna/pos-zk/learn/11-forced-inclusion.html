11 — No forced-inclusion path, and what that costs

Problem this lesson solves. It explains what forced inclusion was meant to solve, why this design deliberately has no forced-inclusion path, what the removal costs, and what remains: conditional statistical resistance from hash-based weighted proposer selection, plus a disclosed consequence the protocol does not remedy. The filename is kept from the earlier draft so existing links do not break; the mechanism it is named after is gone (FI-REMOVED-01).

In plain language

What forced inclusion was meant to solve. In this design L2 PoS proposers determine transaction order (D4), so a proposer can leave a transaction out. An L1-side forced-inclusion request would have been the user's recourse: the user posts a request on L1, an L2 validity rule obliges proposers to include it, and an L1 backstop rejects batches that omit it. That is a real remedy in principle — it converts "please include me" into a rule that does not depend on the proposer's goodwill.

This design deliberately does not have it. The user, as requirement owner, directed that forced inclusion be removed from the specification. There is no L1 request function, no queue, no escrow, no eligibility delay, no inclusion duty, no L1 backstop and no escape hatch, and there are no forced-inclusion parameters (FI-REMOVED-01). The protocol therefore provides no per-transaction inclusion guarantee and no L1 escape path by which a user can force a transaction into L2. The removal is recorded as a user-authorized partial relaxation of R10 in DECISIONS.md D-6, not absorbed silently.

The honest replacement — and it is not a guarantee. Proposer selection is hash-based and weighted (CONS-06). A coalition holding less than one third of the voting power that counts toward quorum is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it. Under the network assumption A-CONS-2 — a censored transaction can still reach honest validators — that is a real but conditional resistance, stated in the specification as LIVE-04. It is an expectation over proposer slots, not a deadline and not a bound on any finite window: CONS-06(b) says so in its own text.

The disclosed consequence. A network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can exclude that user's transactions indefinitely. Because a withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, such an adversary can also prevent the user from starting a withdrawal. The protocol offers no remedy, and this is a disclosed consequence, not a bug (LIVE-04, LIM-01). Removing forced inclusion is therefore not free of cost: it removes the only path that did not depend on the censored user's connectivity to honest validators.

What remains. The rules that do not depend on forced inclusion are untouched: one atomic data-plus-proof acceptance entry point (L1-01), no privileged lever in ordinary operation (INV-04), monotone checkpoint advancement (L1-06) and the ban on rewriting finalized history (REC-01). Nothing here claims the protocol is censorship-resistant without the conditional: the claim is the conditional statistical property of LIVE-04, and no more.

Precise definitions

  • No forced-inclusion path (FI-REMOVED-01). The design has no L1 request, queue, escrow, inclusion duty, L1 backstop, escape hatch or forced-inclusion parameter, and gives no per-transaction inclusion guarantee (FI-REMOVED-01).
  • Conditional statistical resistance (LIVE-04). Hash-based weighted selection means a coalition with less than one third of the voting power proposes strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it (LIVE-04, CONS-06).
  • The condition (A-CONS-2). The resistance holds only while a censored transaction can still reach honest validators; if a network-level adversary isolates a user from every honest proposer, the argument does not apply (A-CONS-2, 01 — requirements and threat model §4, LIVE-04).
  • Isolation consequence (LIVE-04). Such an adversary can exclude a user's transactions indefinitely, and because a withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, can also prevent the user from starting a withdrawal. There is no protocol remedy (LIVE-04).
  • R10, restated. "State censorship resistance honestly. The protocol provides statistical resistance from proposer rotation and gossip under A-CONS-2 and provides no L1 forced-inclusion path; no override that violates D2 may be introduced." R10 is satisfied in this relaxed form and not in its original form (01 — requirements and threat model §7, DECISIONS.md D-6).
  • User-authorized relaxation. The removal was directed by the user as the requirement owner, recorded rather than absorbed, and it changes the scope of the final verdict (DECISIONS.md D-6).
  • What is not removed. Atomic data-plus-proof acceptance (L1-01), no privileged lever in ordinary operation (INV-04), monotone checkpoint advancement (L1-06) and the ban on rewriting finalized history (REC-01) are unchanged: the removal takes away an L1 remedy, not a safety rule.
  • No unprovable accusation. With no inclusion duty there is no duty to breach, and an elapsed timeout is never evidence about what exists elsewhere (STATUS-10, WH-02).

They are exactly the rows that stop helping under network-level isolation: a user who can reach nobody in the honest set cannot benefit from a share of slots, however favorable the share is. That gap is what forced inclusion was meant to close, and this design deliberately leaves it open (LIVE-04, DECISIONS.md D-6).

Attacks this prevents

  • Selective omission by a sub-threshold coalition, while the transaction reaches honest validators. A coalition with less than one third of the voting power proposes strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and the first honest proposer with room includes what reached it (LIVE-04, CONS-06).
  • Making one firm's share the whole schedule. Selection is by committed weight through a hash over (domain, chainId, epoch, height, round); no interval is privileged by position and a sub-threshold coalition cannot claim more than its share in expectation (CONS-06(b)–(d)).
  • A privileged inclusion lever. With no request queue and no escrow, there is no owner-, DAO- or operator-reachable queue state to void or rewrite, and no selector whose removal could strand a request. The failure class the baseline's one-time init3() produced has no state to act on in this design (FI-REMOVED-01, D-6).
  • Using inclusion policy to rewrite history. The removal adds no retroactive power, and the unchanged rules require monotone checkpoint advancement and forbid invalidating PoS-finalized history (L1-06, REC-01).
  • Unprovable censorship accusations. There is no inclusion duty to breach and no evidence object that could turn an omission into a slashable offence; an elapsed timeout is never evidence of absence (STATUS-10, WH-02).

Attacks this does not prevent

  • Network-level isolation of a user from every honest proposer. The central disclosed consequence: the user can be excluded indefinitely, and there is no L1 path to force inclusion (LIVE-04, FI-REMOVED-01).
  • Preventing a withdrawal from being started. A withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, so sustained exclusion of that transaction also blocks the exit at its first step; the protocol offers no remedy (LIVE-04).
  • A coalition of one third or more of the voting power. The expectation argument stops applying there and the protocol claims nothing; the specification says so rather than claiming protection (CONS-06(b), LIM-01).
  • Predictable-slot capture and timeout forcing. Selection is a public deterministic function, and a cartel that forces timeouts on honest slots can increase its share of produced blocks; the counting property is over proposer slots, and the disclosure obligation for predictability belongs to LIM-01, not to this lesson (CONS-06).
  • L1 censorship of data, proofs or the accepting transaction. Nothing settles, and there is no forced-inclusion fallback to route around it because the design has none (T-7, LIVE-01 clause L5, LIVE-04).
  • Losses. Penalties are not compensation and no universal bound on economic loss exists (ECON-11).

Self-check

My transaction never reaches a block. Can I force it in from L1?

No. There is no forced-inclusion path: no request transaction, no queue, no duty and no escape hatch (FI-REMOVED-01). If the transaction reaches honest validators, the first honest proposer with room includes it, and a coalition with less than one third of the weight proposes strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation (LIVE-04, CONS-06). If an adversary isolates you from every honest proposer, the protocol offers no remedy (LIVE-04).

Is the protocol censorship-resistant?

Only under the conditional. The claim is statistical resistance from hash-based weighted proposer selection: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power proposes strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it — conditional on A-CONS-2, that a censored transaction can still reach honest validators. There is no unconditional claim and no per-transaction guarantee; forced inclusion was removed by user decision and the R10 relaxation is disclosed (LIVE-04, CONS-06, DECISIONS.md D-6).

My L2 transactions are being excluded and I want to withdraw. Can I start the withdrawal on L1 instead?

No. A withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, so an adversary able to isolate the user from every honest proposer can also prevent the withdrawal from being started, and with forced inclusion removed there is no L1 path to force that L2 transaction in. This is the disclosed consequence of LIVE-04; the protocol offers no remedy.

Challenge this. The lesson relied on: (i) A-CONS-2 — a censored transaction can still reach honest validators — which is a network assumption, not an enforced property; (ii) A-CONS-1 — fewer than one third of the stake that counts toward quorum is Byzantine; (iii) CONS-06(b)'s uniformity step, which is Assumed on the hash behaving as a random oracle, so the one-third statement is an expectation and not a bound on any finite window; (iv) honest proposers including what reaches them, which is a duty, not measured behaviour; (v) the disclosure obligation for a public, predictable selection function belonging to LIM-01, not to this lesson; (vi) the cost and frequency of network-level isolation being unmeasured; (vii) the removal of forced inclusion being a user-authorized partial relaxation of R10 recorded as D-6, with the withdrawal consequence disclosed. Falsifier: find a sentence here or in the specification that claims unconditional censorship resistance, promises inclusion by a deadline, offers a per-transaction inclusion guarantee, or presents the removal as free of cost.
