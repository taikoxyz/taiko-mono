Forced inclusion

This lesson teaches the one inclusion obligation the design has: once a published batch’s data has waited its inclusion delay, every batch that lands must move a settlement frontier past the oldest pending records, and the duty is checked inside the proof. It also teaches the honest half — this is not a latency guarantee. What the mechanism bounds is exclusion per unit of a censor’s L1 spending, under conditions this page states. This is a design: nothing here is implemented, measured, audited or deployed.

The idea

Any account may publish a batch’s data to L1 before its proof. Publication writes one publication record — the ordered blob versioned hashes, the claimed range, the data mode and commitment, the L1 block number and a sequence number — and accepts nothing (DA-07). That same record is the forced-data record: one register, no separate “forced” flag, no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow, no bond and no fee.

From a record’s due point — its own stored L1 block number plus the registered FI_INCLUSION_DELAY — the record is part of the due set, and every batch that lands must advance a settlement frontier past the oldest outstanding records before it can move on. The obligation is on the producer, and it is enforced inside the proof: the guest recomputes the frontier and the resolution of every position it passes (FI-11, PRF-04(vi)). It is never a gate on land(data, proof): any account may still submit, the register is not an admission condition, and a proof that does not satisfy the obligation simply does not verify (L1-04).

A published record cannot be quietly passed over. While it is live, no batch that lands may advance the settlement frontier beyond it: the proof must resolve the record — transaction by transaction, in the record’s own order, each one either run or set aside at its turn — or the batch does not settle. That is the whole force of the obligation — and it is why the honest statement is about the censor’s cost, not the user’s wait: the record can still end as dead, discharged by its own deadline rather than included. (R4R1-M-01: the walk resolves the record’s own transactions.)

A record is resolved in exactly one of three ways, and no party chooses which (FI-13). Resolution walks the record’s transactions in the record’s own order, and each transaction either runs or is set aside at its turn. (R4R1-M-01: the ground is per transaction, not per record.)

  • Executed — the record is live, and walking its transactions in the record’s own order, each one runs or is set aside at its turn, with at least one running. Every transaction that runs appears in the batch’s executed payload exactly once, in that order; each one set aside is void individually and may be re-published.
  • Void — the record is over a registered bound, or it is live and every one of its transactions is set aside at its turn, so none of them runs.
  • Dead — the record’s own deadline has passed at the batch’s anchored L1 view: its stored block number plus the registered T_PROVE_DEADLINE is at or below that view. Dead takes one stored number and nothing else: no bytes, no blobs, no execution and no L1 call.

Every position settles, whatever mix the record holds. A proof is accepted only when every position in its required window is resolved, so a rule that could not settle some record would let one publication stall the chain: the frontier could not pass that position, and no later batch could settle either. The walk closes that hole. Because each transaction is dealt with at its turn, a record with one transaction that can run and one that cannot still resolves — the runnable one runs, the other is set aside — and a record whose remaining transactions cannot run is fully set aside by that outcome rather than left standing. Every live position therefore resolves in a bounded number of batches, and no published record can pin the frontier. (R4R1-M-01: the per-transaction walk makes every position settle.)

Setting a transaction aside is a property of the turn it reaches, not of the block it met. A transaction runs at its turn only if the record’s published byte string is within its registered bounds, the chain id matches, its declared nonce is the sender’s nonce at that point, and the sender’s free balance there covers the transaction’s own declared maximum charge — gas limit × max fee per gas + value. The including block’s base fee, its gas limit, its remaining room and the producer’s ordering are not inputs. Execution follows the record’s own order, and the only things that move an account’s nonce or free balance are that account’s own signed transactions, so a producer cannot manufacture a set-aside ground for someone else’s transaction: one that can run at its turn must run, and one that genuinely cannot run at its turn is set aside in every batch and at every view (FI-13(2)–(3)). (R4R1-M-01: the walk reads the sender’s own state, never the producer’s environment.)

No new slashable offence. A validator that signs a block violating the per-block duty gains no new offence and is not slashed for it: the proof is rejected, and that rejection is the whole enforcement. There is no fee, escrow, bond or refund either; a forced transaction pays its own L2 gas if it runs, a transaction set aside at its turn may become runnable later through the sender’s own further signed transactions, and in either case the remedy is permissionless re-publication, which creates a new record with a fresh clock. (R4R1-M-01: setting a transaction aside is not a cancellation, and re-publication is the exit.)

The exit never reads it. No forced-inclusion condition attaches to a withdrawal root, to its attestations, to the withdrawal veto or to a user’s eligibility to withdraw; nothing on that path reads the register, the settlement frontier or the boundary commitment. The register is a settlement-side queue, so the obligation can delay settlement but never a release.

Two registered relations keep the obligation non-vacuous and bounded. FI_INCLUSION_DELAY + L1_FINALITY_DEPTH < T_PROVE_DEADLINE guarantees that a window always exists in which the obligation can fire while the record is still live; if it were inverted, every record would age out before it could ever be due. And FI_MAX_PER_BATCH × FI_MAX_TX_PER_RECORD × FI_RECORD_GAS_MAX ≤ MAX_BATCH_BLOCKS × L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT is what makes a compliant batch exist. The second relation has an admitted premise: it constrains a registered value, and no registered rule maintains the L2 gas-limit schedule itself (F-FI-1 below). All of these quantities are unmeasured.

The register appends, and pruning is deletion only — behind the settlement frontier, through a stored prune cursor, returning no frontier and writing no settlement record. A pruned position is always already settled, so the proof never needs it; a dead record is still a position the frontier passes, and it is discharged by reading its stored block number, not by removing it.

Not a latency guarantee

This is the headline, and it is worth stating in the design’s own words. The mechanism bounds exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, conditional on at least one honest or rational batch producer and on an L1 that includes the user’s publication. It is not a promise about time.

If you expect…What actually happens
A published record is included by some deadline.No. A record can be discharged without being executed — as void, when every one of its transactions is set aside at its turn, or as dead once its own deadline passes. Expiry is a discharge, not an inclusion, and a transaction set aside is not cancelled: it may become runnable later through the sender’s own further transactions, and re-publication is the remedy. (R4R1-M-01: the walk settles each transaction at its turn.)
A censor that refuses the front record keeps censoring.It halts its own settlement. The frontier cannot skip an outstanding live record, so no later range settles while that record stands. That is a real cost to the censor, but it is a halt of the honest pipeline, not a fee paid to the censored user.
Re-publication defeats a publishing censor.It costs the censor roughly one L1 publication per deadline window to keep a target behind its own records. That is the bound: exclusion per unit of L1 spending, not per unit of time.
Data that never reaches L1 can be forced.No. The obligation begins at publication. An adversary that censors the publication transaction, or isolates the user from every honest producer, can keep the record from existing at all — and the same adversary can prevent the user from starting a withdrawal, which is an L2 transaction.
Publication alone guarantees eventual inclusion.Only while arrivals stay within the drain the obligation can force. If livable records permanently arrive faster than FI_MAX_PER_BATCH per accepted batch, the queue grows without bound and a given record’s wait grows with it; the deadline then discharges records rather than including them (F-FI-2, F-FI-4).

The named falsifiers, stated as the design states them:

  • F-FI-1 — capacity and schedule. A registration or fork that breaks the capacity relation, or an L2 gas-limit schedule under which a maximum-length batch at the registered per-block bound cannot be produced, makes the “a compliant batch exists” premise false and can re-open a halt. No registered rule maintains that schedule premise.
  • F-FI-2 — arrivals exceed the drain. Carried open; not fixed. This is the bound on time, not on work: if the arrival rate of livable records permanently exceeds the drain the obligation forces, waits grow without bound. The mechanism still does not halt, but the deadline is then an exit, not a latency guarantee. No bound on who may publish is imposed: that would be a publish-time admission condition, and any account must be able to publish.
  • F-FI-3 — set aside now, runnable later. A transaction set aside at its turn may become runnable later, but only through the sender’s own further signed transactions: they can move the account’s nonce or spend its free balance, and nothing a producer sets can. The outcome is objective and final for the batch that resolves the position, and the remedy for a record whose transactions were set aside is re-publication; a predicate that could reopen the position would need a mutable status field. (R4R1-M-01: the discharge ground reads only the sender’s own state.)
  • F-FI-4 — expiry outruns the frontier. A settlement stall longer than the record’s deadline, or a queue that drains more slowly than records age, discharges a record by expiry rather than including it. Expiry is a discharge, not a guarantee.
  • F-FI-5 — the producer and the L1. The guarantee is conditional on at least one honest or rational producer and on an L1 that includes the publication; an L1-level adversary is outside it.
  • F-FI-6 — the anchor-age residual. A certified range whose landing is deliberately delayed past the registered anchor age, and past blob retrievability, is permanently unacceptable. The registered relations are chosen to make this impossible inside the normal envelope; the deliberate-delay residual stays disclosed.

Worked example — walking the frontier

The counts below are symbols, not values: every FI_* quantity is unmeasured. The walk is what matters, and it is per transaction: the record’s transactions are read in the record’s own order, and each one either runs or is set aside at its turn. (R4R1-M-01: resolution is a per-transaction walk.)

Two producers, one position. Suppose the record at the settlement frontier is live and at least one of its transactions can run at its turn. A producer that would rather exclude it has three options: run every transaction that can run, in the record’s own order, setting aside only those that cannot run at their turn; show it void, which requires that the record is over a registered bound or that every one of its transactions is set aside at its turn; or stop settling, so the frontier never moves and no later range lands either. There is no fourth option in which the record is skipped and settlement continues, and a producer cannot manufacture the set-aside ground: only the sender’s own signed transactions move its nonce or free balance. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why the honest statement is about the censor’s cost rather than about a user’s wait. (R4R1-M-01: a transaction that can run is only ever run.)

What this gives you

  • One unit of account for the obligation: register positions per batch. The cap, the frontier bound, the drain floor and the capacity relation are all stated in that unit, and the per-block rule is order and non-omission only.
  • A published record that cannot be silently skipped while it is live: settlement cannot continue past it, and the proof resolves it transaction by transaction, in the record’s own order, so the only ways forward are that resolution, an objective void ground or expiry.
  • A walk that is total: each transaction either runs or is set aside at its turn, so every live position settles in a bounded number of batches and no single publication can pin the frontier. (R4R1-M-01: no published record can pin the frontier.)
  • A set-aside ground no producer’s block contents can manufacture or steer, and an expiry ground that needs only the record’s own stored block number and one registered constant. (R4R1-M-01: the ground reads the sender’s own state, not the producer’s environment.)
  • Enforcement in the proof only: the admission rule of land is unchanged, there is no escrow or fee, there is no new slashable offence, and no forced-inclusion state is read by the exit.
  • An honest bound: exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, conditional on a producer that lands batches and on an L1 that includes the publication.

What it does not give you

  • No latency guarantee. A record can be void, or dead before the frontier reaches it, and arrivals beyond the drain make waits unbounded (F-FI-2, F-FI-4). The guarantee holds only while arrivals stay within the drain the obligation can force.
  • No general inclusion list. There is no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow, no fee and no L1 entry point that can force an unpublished transaction into L2 or start a withdrawal.
  • No remedy against an L1-level adversary. If the publication transaction is censored, or the user is isolated from every honest producer, the obligation never starts.
  • No inclusion without execution success. The obligation delivers a forceable transaction to L2 execution; whether it succeeds is the execution rules’ business, and a producer can order a block so a forced transaction reverts.
  • No measured parameters. Every FI_* term, the capacity relation and the schedule premise are placeholders with no measurement behind them, and nothing on this page is a value.

Check yourself

A record is published and a producer lands a batch whose anchored view has passed the record’s due point. What must the proof show about that record?

That the position is resolved. The walk reads the record’s transactions in the record’s own order, and each one either runs or is set aside at its turn — its declared nonce is not the account’s nonce at that point, or the account cannot pay the transaction’s own declared maximum charge. At least one runs, so the record resolves as executed with each set-aside transaction void individually; if none runs, the record is void, and so is a record over a registered bound; and a record is dead when its stored block number plus the registered deadline is at or below the anchored view. There is no fourth answer, and the frontier cannot pass a position that is none of the three. (R4R1-M-01: every position resolves, so no record can pin the frontier.)

Can a producer make a due record void by filling blocks and pushing the base fee above the record’s max fee per gas?

No. The walk reads the record’s own immutable bytes, the registered constants, and the sender’s nonce and free balance at the transaction’s turn, against the transaction’s own declared maximum charge. The base fee, the including block’s gas limit and its remaining room are not inputs, so a block producer cannot manufacture a set-aside ground out of the environment, and it cannot discharge a transaction that the sender has not itself superseded or defunded. A transaction that genuinely cannot run at its turn is set aside in every producer’s hands. (R4R1-M-01: the discharge ground cannot be steered.)

A record holds two transactions: one that can run at its turn and one whose declared nonce is unreachable. Does the position resolve?

Yes. The walk runs the first in the record’s own order and sets the second aside, because at its turn its nonce is not the account’s nonce there. At least one transaction runs, so the record resolves as executed, and the transaction set aside is void individually. The position is not left unresolved waiting for a mode that fits the whole record. (R4R1-M-01: a record with a mixed profile resolves by the per-transaction walk.)

A censor refuses to include the record at the front of the queue. What happens?

Settlement halts with it. The frontier cannot skip an outstanding live record, so no later range can land while the record stands, and the censor cannot set a transaction aside to dodge it: only a transaction that cannot run at its turn is set aside, so every transaction that can run must run, in the record’s own order. Its other options are to show the record void — over a registered bound, or with every transaction set aside at its turn — or to let it age out to dead, which means not settling past it for its whole deadline window. Re-publication creates a new record with a fresh clock, so keeping a target out indefinitely costs the censor roughly one L1 publication per deadline window. That is the bound — exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, not per unit of time. (R4R1-M-01: a runnable transaction cannot be set aside by the producer’s choice.)

Does the obligation delay a withdrawal?

No. Nothing on the withdrawal path — forming a root, attesting it, verifying it, the veto or exit eligibility — reads the register, the settlement frontier or the boundary commitment. A due record freezes only settlement, and it always resolves: its transactions run or are set aside at their turn, or the record is void, or it expires, so the freeze is bounded by the record’s own deadline at the outside. (R4R1-M-01: every position settles, so the freeze is bounded.)

Assumptions

  • At least one honest or rational producer lands batches; the obligation binds a producer that settles, not a chain that never settles.
  • Ethereum includes the user’s publication transaction and keeps producing and finalizing blocks; the obligation begins at publication.
  • Arrivals of livable records stay within the drain the obligation can force; if they do not, waits grow without bound and the deadline is an exit rather than a latency bound (F-FI-2).
  • The registered capacity relation holds and the L2 gas-limit schedule permits a maximum-length batch at the registered per-block bound (F-FI-1); no registered rule maintains the schedule premise.
  • Batch data and blobs stay retrievable for the window the proof needs, and the registered constants keep the normal pipeline inside the anchor-age envelope (F-FI-6).
  • Every FI_* value is unmeasured; nothing on this page is a measurement.
