Forced inclusion

This lesson teaches the one inclusion obligation the design has: once a published batch’s data has waited its inclusion delay, every batch that lands must move a settlement frontier past the oldest pending records, and the duty is checked inside the proof. It also teaches the honest half — this is not a latency guarantee. What the mechanism bounds is exclusion per unit of a censor’s L1 spending, under conditions this page states. This is a design: nothing here is implemented, measured, audited or deployed.

The idea

Any account may publish a batch’s data to L1 before its proof. Publication writes one publication record — the ordered blob versioned hashes, the claimed range, the data mode and commitment, the L1 block number and a sequence number — and accepts nothing (DA-07). That same record is the forced-data record: one register, no separate “forced” flag, no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow, no bond and no fee.

From a record’s due point — its own stored L1 block number plus the registered FI_INCLUSION_DELAY — the record is part of the due set, and every batch that lands must advance a settlement frontier past the oldest outstanding records before it can move on. The obligation is on the producer, and it is enforced inside the proof: the guest recomputes the frontier and the resolution of every position it passes (FI-11, PRF-04(vi)). It is never a gate on land(data, proof): any account may still submit, the register is not an admission condition, and a proof that does not satisfy the obligation simply does not verify (L1-04).

A record is resolved in exactly one of three ways, and no party chooses which (FI-13):

  • Executed — all of the record’s transactions appear in the batch’s executed payload, each exactly once, in order.
  • Void — the record cannot be executed because of what it is: it is over a registered bound, or none of its transactions is forceable at any pre-state of the batch.
  • Dead — the record’s own deadline has passed at the batch’s anchored L1 view: its stored block number plus the registered T_PROVE_DEADLINE is at or below that view. Dead takes one stored number and nothing else: no bytes, no blobs, no execution and no L1 call.

Void is a property of the record’s bytes, not of the block it met. A transaction is forceable only if the record’s published byte string is within its registered bounds, the chain id matches, the sender’s nonce matches, and the sender’s balance covers the transaction’s own declared maximum charge — gas limit × max fee per gas + value. The including block’s base fee, its gas limit, its remaining room and the producer’s ordering are not inputs. A producer cannot make a due record void by filling blocks or by moving the base fee, and a record that is genuinely non-forceable stays void in every batch and at every view (FI-13(2)).

No new slashable offence. A validator that signs a block violating the per-block duty gains no new offence and is not slashed for it: the proof is rejected, and that rejection is the whole enforcement. There is no fee, escrow, bond or refund either; a forced transaction pays its own L2 gas if it executes, and after a void or an expired record the user’s remedy is permissionless re-publication, which creates a new record with a fresh clock.

The exit never reads it. No forced-inclusion condition attaches to a withdrawal root, to its attestations, to the withdrawal veto or to a user’s eligibility to withdraw; nothing on that path reads the register, the settlement frontier or the boundary commitment. The register is a settlement-side queue, so the obligation can delay settlement but never a release.

Two registered relations keep the obligation non-vacuous and bounded. FI_INCLUSION_DELAY + L1_FINALITY_DEPTH < T_PROVE_DEADLINE guarantees that a window always exists in which the obligation can fire while the record is still live; if it were inverted, every record would age out before it could ever be due. And FI_MAX_PER_BATCH × FI_MAX_TX_PER_RECORD × FI_RECORD_GAS_MAX ≤ MAX_BATCH_BLOCKS × L2_BLOCK_GAS_LIMIT is what makes a compliant batch exist. The second relation has an admitted premise: it constrains a registered value, and no registered rule maintains the L2 gas-limit schedule itself (F-FI-1 below). All of these quantities are unmeasured.

The register appends, and pruning is deletion only — behind the settlement frontier, through a stored prune cursor, returning no frontier and writing no settlement record. A pruned position is always already settled, so the proof never needs it; a dead record is still a position the frontier passes, and it is discharged by reading its stored block number, not by removing it.

Not a latency guarantee

This is the headline, and it is worth stating in the design’s own words. The mechanism bounds exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, conditional on at least one honest or rational batch producer and on an L1 that includes the user’s publication. It is not a promise about time.

If you expect…What actually happens
A published record is included by some deadline.No. A record can be discharged without being executed — as void, or as dead once its own deadline passes. Expiry is a discharge, not an inclusion.
A censor that refuses the front record keeps censoring.It halts its own settlement. The frontier cannot skip an outstanding live record, so no later range settles while that record stands. That is a real cost to the censor, but it is a halt of the honest pipeline, not a fee paid to the censored user.
Re-publication defeats a publishing censor.It costs the censor roughly one L1 publication per deadline window to keep a target behind its own records. That is the bound: exclusion per unit of L1 spending, not per unit of time.
Data that never reaches L1 can be forced.No. The obligation begins at publication. An adversary that censors the publication transaction, or isolates the user from every honest producer, can keep the record from existing at all — and the same adversary can prevent the user from starting a withdrawal, which is an L2 transaction.
Publication alone guarantees eventual inclusion.Only while arrivals stay within the drain the obligation can force. If livable records permanently arrive faster than FI_MAX_PER_BATCH per accepted batch, the queue grows without bound and a given record’s wait grows with it; the deadline then discharges records rather than including them (F-FI-2, F-FI-4).

The named falsifiers, stated as the design states them:

  • F-FI-1 — capacity and schedule. A registration or fork that breaks the capacity relation, or an L2 gas-limit schedule under which a maximum-length batch at the registered per-block bound cannot be produced, makes the “a compliant batch exists” premise false and can re-open a halt. No registered rule maintains that schedule premise.
  • F-FI-2 — arrivals exceed the drain. Open, and not fixed. This is the bound on time, not on work: if the arrival rate of livable records permanently exceeds the drain the obligation forces, waits grow without bound. The mechanism still does not halt, but the deadline is then an exit, not a latency guarantee. A bound on who may publish was considered and deliberately not adopted: it would be a publish-time admission condition, and any account must be able to publish.
  • F-FI-3 — void now, forceable later. A record voided because no transaction was forceable in this batch may become forceable later. The void decision is objective and final for that record; the remedy is re-publication, because a predicate that could un-void it would need a mutable status field.
  • F-FI-4 — expiry outruns the frontier. A settlement stall longer than the record’s deadline, or a queue that drains more slowly than records age, discharges a record by expiry rather than including it. Expiry is a discharge, not a guarantee.
  • F-FI-5 — the producer and the L1. The guarantee is conditional on at least one honest or rational producer and on an L1 that includes the publication; an L1-level adversary is outside it.
  • F-FI-6 — the anchor-age residual. A certified range whose landing is deliberately delayed past the registered anchor age, and past blob retrievability, is permanently unacceptable. The registered relations are chosen to make this impossible inside the normal envelope; the deliberate-delay residual stays disclosed.

Worked example — walking the frontier

The counts below are symbols, not values: every FI_* quantity is unmeasured. The walk is what matters.

The proof reaches position…What it finds thereWhat it may do
a position below cAlready settled: executed or void in the batch that carried the frontier past it, or dead when it was discharged.Nothing; the walk starts at c, and pruning may have deleted the slot entirely.
an outstanding position whose record is live and forceableThe record’s transactions exist and can execute in this batch.Execute all of them, in order; nothing else resolves this position.
an outstanding position whose record cannot be executedOver a registered bound, or no transaction forceable at any pre-state of the batch.Record it void and move on. No re-supply of its bytes is required, and the published bytes stay on L1.
an outstanding position whose record is deadIts stored block number plus the registered deadline is at or below the anchored view.Discharge it by expiry and move on; one comparison, no bytes and no L1 call.
a position that is none of thoseA live record with a forceable transaction that the batch did not execute.Nothing: the position cannot be passed, so the proof does not verify and no settlement record is written.

Two producers, one position. Suppose the record at the settlement frontier is live and forceable. A producer that would rather exclude it has three options: execute it; show it void, which requires that no transaction of the record is forceable at any pre-state of the batch; or stop settling, so the frontier never moves and no later range lands either. There is no fourth option in which the record is skipped and settlement continues. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why the honest statement is about the censor’s cost rather than about a user’s wait.

What this gives you

  • One unit of account for the obligation: register positions per batch. The cap, the frontier bound, the drain floor and the capacity relation are all stated in that unit, and the per-block rule is order and non-omission only.
  • A published record that cannot be silently skipped while it is live: settlement cannot continue past it, so the only ways forward are execution, an objective void ground or expiry.
  • A void predicate no producer’s block contents can manufacture or steer, and an expiry ground that needs only the record’s own stored block number and one registered constant.
  • Enforcement in the proof only: no gate on landing, no new entry point, no escrow or fee, no new slashable offence, and no forced-inclusion state read by the exit.
  • An honest bound: exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, conditional on a producer that lands batches and on an L1 that includes the publication.

What it does not give you

  • No latency guarantee. A record can be void, or dead before the frontier reaches it, and arrivals beyond the drain make waits unbounded (F-FI-2, F-FI-4). The guarantee holds only while arrivals stay within the drain the obligation can force.
  • No general inclusion list. There is no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow, no fee and no L1 entry point that can force an unpublished transaction into L2 or start a withdrawal.
  • No remedy against an L1-level adversary. If the publication transaction is censored, or the user is isolated from every honest producer, the obligation never starts.
  • No inclusion without execution success. The obligation delivers a forceable transaction to L2 execution; whether it succeeds is the execution rules’ business, and a producer can order a block so a forced transaction reverts.
  • No measured parameters. Every FI_* term, the capacity relation and the schedule premise are placeholders; Phase B measures them, and nothing on this page is a value.

Check yourself

A record is published and a producer lands a batch whose anchored view has passed the record’s due point. What must the proof show about that record?

That the position is resolved: the record’s transactions were executed in the batch, or the record is void (over a registered bound, or no transaction forceable at any pre-state of the batch), or the record is dead (its stored block number plus the registered deadline is at or below the anchored view). There is no fourth answer, and the frontier cannot pass a position that is none of the three.

Can a producer make a due record void by filling blocks and pushing the base fee above the record’s max fee per gas?

No. Forceability reads the record’s own immutable bytes, the registered constants, the sender’s nonce and the sender’s balance against the transaction’s own declared maximum charge. The base fee, the including block’s gas limit and its remaining room are not inputs, so a block producer cannot manufacture a void ground out of the environment. A record that is genuinely non-forceable is void in every producer’s hands.

A censor refuses to include the record at the front of the queue. What happens?

Settlement halts with it. The frontier cannot skip an outstanding live record, so no later range can land while the record stands. The censor’s other options are to show it void or to let it age out to dead, which means not settling past it for its whole deadline window. Re-publication creates a new record with a fresh clock, so keeping a target out indefinitely costs the censor roughly one L1 publication per deadline window. That is the bound — exclusion per unit of the censor’s L1 spending, not per unit of time.

Does the obligation delay a withdrawal?

No. Nothing on the withdrawal path — forming a root, attesting it, verifying it, the veto or exit eligibility — reads the register, the settlement frontier or the boundary commitment. A due record freezes only settlement, and it can always be discharged by inclusion, void or expiry, so the freeze is bounded by the record’s own deadline at the outside.

Assumptions

  • At least one honest or rational producer lands batches; the obligation binds a producer that settles, not a chain that never settles.
  • Ethereum includes the user’s publication transaction and keeps producing and finalizing blocks; the obligation begins at publication.
  • Arrivals of livable records stay within the drain the obligation can force; if they do not, waits grow without bound and the deadline is an exit rather than a latency bound (F-FI-2).
  • The registered capacity relation holds and the L2 gas-limit schedule permits a maximum-length batch at the registered per-block bound (F-FI-1); no registered rule maintains the schedule premise.
  • Batch data and blobs stay retrievable for the window the proof needs, and the registered constants keep the normal pipeline inside the anchor-age envelope (F-FI-6).
  • Every FI_* value is unmeasured; nothing on this page is a measurement.
