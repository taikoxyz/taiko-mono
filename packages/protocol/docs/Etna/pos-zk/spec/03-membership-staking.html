03 — Membership, TAIKO staking, exits, weak subjectivity

This page owns the MEM-* rules: how existing TAIKO becomes consensus stake held on L1, how that stake becomes voting power in an epoch-scoped validator set, how validators enter and leave, how a consensus key is bound and retired, and what a node may and may not trust when it syncs. Status labels are STATUS-01–STATUS-11; timing is expressed in seconds or L1 block numbers with no L1 proposer lookahead (GEN-06); every protocol hash is one canonical abi.encode with a right-padded domain tag and the chain id first (GEN-05). Nothing on this page is implemented, deployed, benchmarked or audited (GEN-01).

What this page does not decide. Rewards, the offence catalogue, penalty sizes and the destination of slashed stake are ECON-01–ECON-12; the epoch schedule, certificate format and header fields are CONS-05, CONS-10 and CONS-13; the atomic accept path and checkpoint record are L1-01–L1-08; signature, bitmap and quorum checks in the guest are PRF-04 and epoch transitions are PRF-05. Membership fixes only the ledger, the set and the trust boundary.
Fixed decisions applied here. Staking and slashable collateral are the existing TAIKO token and nothing else; gas stays ETH (D7). SignalService, Bridge and the three vaults keep their existing addresses by in-place upgrade (D3); the staking custody contract is a new contract at a new address, which D3 does not forbid. No rule on this page can invalidate a PoS-finalized block in Mode A (D2, REC-01), no rule creates a data-first path (D5), and no rule depends on an L1 proposer or a fixed L1 slot duration (GEN-06). Every safety claim below is conditional on the Byzantine-stake assumption A-CONS-1 (< 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum in the relevant set version) and, where it needs data to be served, on A-CONS-5/A-DA-2 (01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §4). Where a rule needs an assumption, it says so at the rule.

1. Custody: one asset, one ledger

MEM-01 — L1 staking custody of TAIKO at the existing token address The staked asset is the existing L1 ERC-20 TAIKO at 0x10dea67478c5F8C5E2D90e5E9B26dBe60c54d800 (token.taiko.eth). The address is verified in taiko-baseline-contracts.md §6.1, which records TaikoToken (TKO) at that proxy from LibL1Addrs.sol:73 and TaikoToken.sol:12-13; the token has 18 decimals and a fixed supply of 1,000,000,000 ether minted once in init (TaikoToken.sol:41, sourced). No new token is created. No wrapped, bridged or L2-represented TAIKO counts as consensus stake — in particular the L2 BridgedTaikoToken has no role in consensus — and neither ETH nor any other asset is accepted as consensus collateral. Gas remains ETH (D7). Custody is a new L1 contract at a new address. New addresses are permitted: D3 preserves only the SignalService, Bridge and vault addresses by in-place upgrade, and this contract is not one of them. Bonded amounts are stored as uint256 TAIKO base units (18 decimals), never as a rounded integer multiple of gwei. The consensus ledger is not the Inbox's existing bond ledger: Bond{uint64 balance; uint48 withdrawalRequestedAt} is gwei-denominated with a ceiling of 2^64 − 1 gwei ≈ 1.845e10 TAIKO and a granularity of ≈ 18.4 TAIKO (derived, economics-raw.md §1.4, LibBonds.sol:18, IBondManager.sol:13-19), and its only slash path is a best-effort, offence-agnostic liveness debit (LibBonds.sol:148-170; economics-raw.md §1.4 point 2). Disposition of that ledger is a migration concern (MIG-03). The custody contract holds only staked TAIKO. It never lends, pledges, rehypothecates or stakes the principal elsewhere; it exposes no arbitrary-call, no owner-triggered transfer and no upgrade-triggered transfer of bonded balances, and no party other than the objective rules of this page and of ECON-01 / ECON-05 can move a bonded balance. Ownership of the contract, if any, is limited to an upgrade authority that cannot move balances (SYS-04). The token contract itself is neither replaced nor upgraded by this design; a DAO upgrade that changes the token's transfer semantics is a governance failure class (GOV-02), not a staking rule. Failure mode: a second token or a wrapped representation makes the security budget a function of a different market and breaks D7; reusing the gwei bond ledger puts rounding and a best-effort debit into the consensus path, where one base unit changes voting power and a failed debit silently converts a slash into no punishment; a transferable bonded balance reintroduces the withdrawal race that MEM-06 exists to defeat. Proven sourced gas of the deposit / exit / withdraw paths: unmeasured — measured by a gas-report run of the pinned custody contract under L1 mainnet-equivalent conditions.
MEM-02 — Effective stake, voting power, and why getVotes is not an oracle (1) For set version k (MEM-08) the ledger exposes, as a pure function of Ethereum-final L1 state at the snapshot point defined in MEM-09: effStake_k(v) = LedgerState(N(k)).active[v], where active[v] is the sum of TAIKO base units that entry v has bonded and that (i) completed activation (MEM-03), (ii) has not had an exit become effective (MEM-05), and (iii) has not been slashed away (MEM-06) before that snapshot. An entry with effStake_k(v) = 0 is not in the set. (2) Voting power is exactly the effective stake: VP_k(v) = effStake_k(v) — one TAIKO base unit is one unit of weight. There is no multiplier, no age bonus, no quadratic, time-weighted or reputation term, and no discount for common ownership. The published set carries TotalVP_k = Σ_v effStake_k(v); the quorum threshold applied to TotalVP_k is CONS-03's and is not restated here. Duplicate public keys within one set version are invalid (MEM-08); weight is counted per (pubkey, effStake) entry, and the protocol neither records nor infers beneficial ownership (MEM-10). (3) balanceOf, getVotes and getPastVotes must never be read by a consensus rule. On the existing token, votes and balance demonstrably diverge: renounceVotingPower() is irreversible and zeroes a holder's votes while the balance and total supply are unchanged (TaikoToken.sol:53-74, :92-96; economics-raw.md §1.1 line 53); four accounts are hard-coded vote-excluded — address(0), TAIKO_FOUNDATION_TREASURY 0x363e846B91AF677Fb82f709b6c35BD1AaFc6B3Da, TAIKO_DAO_CONTROLLER 0xfC3C4ca95a8C4e5a587373f1718CD91301d6b2D3 and TAIKO_ERC20_VAULT 0x996282cA11E5DEb6B5D122CC3B9A1FcAAD4415Ab (TaikoToken.sol:17-21, :130-136; economics-raw.md §1.1 line 41); vote checkpoints are timestamp-based (clock() = block.timestamp, TaikoTokenBase.sol:22-30); and delegate() is blocked when a non-voting account is either party (TaikoToken.sol:83-90). Reusing that surface would let a governance action delete consensus weight, and would count accounts that never bonded and are not slashable, breaking INV-03 ("no consensus without TAIKO at risk"), which is itself conditional on A-CONS-1. Failure mode: the protocol counts weight that is not at risk and cannot be confiscated, so a certificate can be produced by parties who lose nothing when they equivocate; or a renunciation immediately zeroes a live validator's weight and the epoch loses quorum for reasons no staking participant chose. Proven sourced

2. Activation and the validator lifecycle

MEM-03 — Activation: objective, permissionless, first-come queue (1) Entry is permissionless. Any L1 account may create an entry by bonding at least S_min TAIKO and registering a consensus key. There is no allowlist, no invitation, no operator approval, no identity requirement and no cap on the number of participants (R1). The gate is the objective predicate amount ≥ S_min; the derivation of S_min is ECON-09 and its value is a parameter subject to PARAM-01. No third-party on-ramp exists in v1: the bonded amount is credited to msg.sender, because separating the economic owner from the slashed party without a delegation contract is exactly what MEM-04 rules out. (2) Ordering key. Activation entries are ordered by the strictly increasing tuple (l1BlockNumber, txIndex, logIndex) of the L1 block that included the bonding transaction, where logIndex is the index of the entry's event in that block's log list. Ties cannot occur. Serving is FIFO by this key — first-come, first-served — and no fee, stake size, key attribute, entity or reputation can reorder, delay or front-run another entry. A per-set-version throughput limit A_max entries is permitted only as an objective FIFO quantity: it takes entries from the head of the queue in key order and never selects among them (the EIP-7514 activation-churn cap is the sourced precedent for bounding set growth; economics-raw.md §2.5). (3) Activation delay. D_activation is expressed in seconds and must satisfy D_activation ≥ T_L1_final(p_f) + E_epoch + M_act, where T_L1_final(p_f) is the p_f-quantile time for the bonding transaction's L1 block to become Ethereum-final, E_epoch is one epoch duration, and M_act is a margin; the numerical values are UNMEASURED (see the register at the end of this page). An entry becomes active, and first enters a set root, at the first set version k whose snapshot point N(k) satisfies timestamp(N(k)) ≥ timestamp(bondingBlock) + D_activation, subject to the FIFO head order and A_max of clause (2). The delay exists so that (a) an L1 reorganisation cannot reorder or erase an entry after its stake has been counted, and (b) an operator has time to bring its signing key and node online before its stake is exposed to a downtime penalty. (4) An entry that never activates. If the key is never bound, or the operator never comes online, or the entry is never reached in the FIFO head, then: the stake stays in activating; it never appears in any set root; it is never part of TotalVP_k; it is never slashable for a consensus offence, because it never signs a vote; and it accrues no reward. An entry whose key binding is incomplete is skipped for throughput accounting and keeps its position, so one unfinished entry cannot block the queue behind it. Cancellation is owner-only (cancelActivation()), and no third party can cancel, reorder or consume another entry's position (griefing defence, threat model T-13). Failure mode: if the ordering key were a fee or a stake size, entry becomes an auction and R1's permissionlessness is nominal; if D_activation were zero, an entry included in the same L1 block as a snapshot could change the set of an epoch already being voted on, breaking the agreement precondition A-CONS-4; if a stalled entry could block the head of the queue, a griefer could freeze all activation for the price of one bond. Proven unmeasured (T_L1_final(p_f), E_epoch, M_act, A_max — measured by the inclusion/finality distribution study and the consensus parameter table.)

3. Delegation

MEM-04 — No delegation in v1 (recorded decision, alternative documented) v1 has exactly one economic owner per entry: the L1 address that bonded the stake is the only address that may request exit, cancel an exit, rotate the consensus key, or receive unbonded proceeds (MEM-03, MEM-05, MEM-07). No contract, pool, custodian or third party may hold stake on behalf of another party and appear in a set root, and no rule recognises delegated, re-delegated, pooled or liquid-staked TAIKO as consensus stake. The protocol does not attempt to prevent such markets from existing; it declines to count them (MEM-10, ECON-10). This is the decision recorded in 04-architecture-decision.md §3 ("v1 supports no delegation: self-bonded operator keys only") and in DECISIONS.md; the alternative below is documented, not silently deferred. The reasons are structural, not paternalistic. (a) Slash pass-through. If slashing does not reduce delegator shares pro rata, delegators hold a free option on operator misbehaviour and the operator's own stake can be smaller than the harm caused. (b) Slashable exit and redelegation. The unbonding period only works if unbonding delegations and redelegations remain slashable for offences committed while the tokens were bonded to the source validator; Cosmos makes all three slashable, which is why its staking module is one of the largest and most intricate modules in the SDK (economics-raw.md §2.6, sourced). (c) Evidence attribution. Equivocation evidence is objective and attributable to a key, but allocating the loss across many beneficial owners requires a share accounting surface with unbonding entries and redelegations, which does not exist in v1 and cannot be improvised after an incident. (d) Polkadot's 2026 staking reform moves to self-stake-only slashing, direct evidence that the delegator/operator alignment problem is hard to solve even with the most sophisticated stake-allocation algorithm in production (economics-raw.md §2.6, sourced). If delegation is ever required — for example because the entry floor would exclude small holders — the minimum honest design is fixed now so that a future proposal cannot quietly ship less: (i) operator self-stake at least equal to its own bond floor, slashable first; (ii) explicit, documented pass-through of slashing to delegators, including unbonding delegations and redelegations; (iii) a recorded decision that the pass-through's simplicity limits are accepted, with the accepted loss distribution stated. Nothing weaker is honest (economics-raw.md §2.6, lines 386-389). Adopting it is a new user decision under GEN-02, not an implementation detail. Failure mode: shipping delegation without all three of pass-through, slashable unbonding and slashable redelegation sells an unpriced option to delegators and converts the protocol's safety claim into a claim about a contract that does not exist; or an "operator" with little of its own stake is bribed to equivocate while delegators keep the upside. Proven sourced

4. Exit, unbonding, and slashing exposure

MEM-05 — Exit request and unbonding delay derivation (1) Exit request. The owner calls requestExit(); the entry enters exit-requested and remains in every set it is already committed to, keeps voting, and remains fully slashable until the exit takes effect. This is a deliberate change from the existing bond ledger, where hasSufficientBond is false the moment a withdrawal is requested (LibBonds.sol:130-141; economics-raw.md §1.4 point 1): an exiting validator that stopped validating would silently shrink the set, and the exit delay would buy no protection at all. (2) Exit becomes effective at the first set version whose snapshot point is at or after the exit request transaction's L1 block; from that version the entry's effStake = 0 and it appears in no later set root (MEM-09). It cannot be cancelled after that point. (3) Withdrawal. The owner may withdraw only when all three hold: (a) the exit has been effective for at least D_withdraw; (b) the evidence window W_evidence has closed for every epoch the entry was in; and (c) no unsettled or collectible slashing exposure remains (MEM-06). (4) Delay formula. D_withdraw ≥ T_proof_bound + T_detect + T_evidence_submit + T_L1_include(p) + T_L1_final + T_process + M. Every term is unmeasured; the table below states what measures each one. T_L1_include(p) must be a quantile, never a mean, and may not assume a fixed L1 slot duration (GEN-06). (5) Why not nominal proving latency. D5 lands a batch's data and its proof in one L1 transaction, so there is no data-first window in which a third party could inspect a published batch and object before settlement. Detection therefore cannot rely on "the data sat on L1 for N hours before the proof arrived"; the delay must cover evidence that is generated only after settlement, or generated concurrently on L2 and then carried to L1. That makes T_L1_include(p) and T_L1_final load-bearing rather than rounding errors, and it makes 30 minutes of proving latency a lower bound on nothing: the evidence clock starts at the offence, which happens at L2 consensus time, possibly long before the batch is provable (economics-raw.md §2.5, lines 340-354). (6) Do not shorten it. If D_withdraw is judged too long for users, the correct response is to reduce the terms it depends on — for example a tighter, measured proof bound — never to set the delay below the formula. If a correlated penalty is adopted, D_withdraw must additionally exceed that penalty's application delay; the two must be chosen together or neither works (economics-raw.md §3.2 point 2; the penalty itself is ECON-05). Failure mode: an offender equivocates at epoch e, requests exit, waits out a delay no longer than the proving latency, withdraws, and is slashed on a remainder — the documented withdrawal race that both Ethereum and Cosmos engineered against (economics-raw.md §2.4). Proven unmeasured (conditional on A-CONS-1 for the safety meaning of "slashable"; the delay itself is a liveness/accounting rule.)

MEM-06 — Slashing follows stake at offence time (1) For every epoch e in which entry v appears in a set root, the ledger records SlashBase(v, e) := effStake_{setVersion(e)}(v) and retains the pair (e, SlashBase(v, e)) in L1 state until the later of (i) the end of the evidence window for e and (ii) settlement of any slash for e. An offence committed at epoch e is charged against SlashBase(v, e), never against the balance at the time the evidence is submitted. (2) Exposure accounting. Let max_exposure(v) = max{ SlashBase(v, e) : e open } over epochs whose evidence window has not closed and whose slashes are not settled. At any moment the owner may withdraw at most bonded(v) − max_exposure(v); equivalently, the contract retains at least the largest single-epoch exposure until every such epoch closes. Slashing is bounded by the bonded balance: the protocol has no claim on assets outside the custody contract, and an unpaid penalty is not collectible from the operator elsewhere (this limitation is stated for users under ECON-11). (3) The withdrawal-race defence, precisely. An offender's rational sequence is: equivocate at epoch e; request exit; wait D_withdraw; withdraw; be slashed on a remainder. It is defeated only because three rules hold together: (i) SlashBase(v, e) is fixed at epoch e and is the charge base; (ii) withdrawal is capped by the exposure of every open epoch, so the stake that could be charged cannot leave first; and (iii) D_withdraw is strictly longer than the time needed to detect, submit, include, finalise and process evidence (MEM-05 clause 4). If any one of the three fails, the race succeeds — they are jointly necessary, and none of them is a parameter that may be relaxed alone. (4) A slash for a given offence is applied at most once: the identity of an offence is (v, epoch, offence type, evidence digest), and a second submission of the same identity is a no-op. Duplicate, replay and false-accusation handling is ECON-08; the per-(v, e) charge base is this rule's. Failure mode: slashing the current balance lets an offender withdraw first and be slashed on what is left — a real, documented failure mode that both Ethereum (state.slashings[epoch] plus the withdrawable_epoch clamp) and Cosmos ("we want to 'follow the stake'", x/evidence) specifically engineered against (economics-raw.md §2.4, sourced). A short delay plus a best-effort debit makes the penalty decorative. Proven sourced (the safety effect of the penalty is conditional on A-ECO-1 and on A-CONS-1, exactly as ECON-01 states; this rule fixes attribution, not deterrence.)

5. Keys: binding, rotation, stale keys

MEM-07 — Key binding, rotation, and stale-key handling (1) One key per entry per set version. An entry has at most one consensus key at any set version: an Ed25519 public key in its canonical 32-byte compressed encoding (RFC 8032). Write pk(v, k) for the key of entry v at set version k; it is a field of the entry in the set root (MEM-08). A second key for the same entry at the same version is invalid, not an error to be resolved by preference. (2) Binding. A key enters a set root only through an L1 transaction signed by the entry's owner address, which sets the key for that entry; the transaction itself is the authentication, so no separate signature object is required. If an aggregate or threshold vote-signature scheme is ever adopted, the binding must additionally carry a proof of possession for the new key, because only then does a rogue-key attack exist (A-CRYPTO-2); with the individual Ed25519 scheme selected in 04-architecture-decision.md §2, a registered key whose secret the owner does not hold simply cannot vote, and that entry's own stake bears the consequence. (3) Rotation is forward-only and version-scoped. The owner calls rotateKey(newPk). For every set version k already published, pk(v, k) is unchanged and unchangeable. The new key applies from the first version k whose snapshot point is at or after the rotation transaction, i.e. pk(v, k) = newPk for N(k) ≥ rotationBlock and pk(v, k) = oldPk for all earlier k. The old key is retired at that moment. (4) Stale keys cannot vote. A vote for epoch e carries the signer's key (or is matched by the guest to the key of the leaf at the signer's bitmap index). It is valid if and only if the signing key equals pk(v, setVersion(e)) for the entry whose effStake is committed in R_{setVersion(e)}. A vote signed with a rotated-away key at an epoch that commits the new key is invalid: it must be discarded, must not count toward quorum (CONS-03), and its signer is not slashable for it because it is not a vote under the protocol's rules. Symmetrically, a vote signed with the old key at an epoch that commits the old key remains valid forever, no matter how many rotations happened later (CONS-08). (5) A later key must not rewrite past behaviour. No rule may validate a tallied vote against the validator's current key, and no rotation, exit or withdrawal may invalidate, reattribute or expunge a vote, vote conflict or evidence object from an epoch that committed the earlier key. Equivocation evidence under a retired key stays slashable against SlashBase(v, e) for the epochs in which that key was committed, exactly like any other evidence (MEM-06). (6) Keys are one-shot identities. A key that has ever appeared in a published set root is retired permanently: binding it again to any entry, including the same entry, is invalid. This keeps the mapping (key, epoch) → entry injective, so attribution of evidence never depends on how a key was later reused. A key may never be bound to two entries at the same time. (7) Compromise and loss. A compromised consensus key can equivocate and burn the entry's stake, but it can never move the stake: only the owner address can exit, withdraw or rotate (MEM-01, MEM-05). Rotation stops future damage but is not retroactive. A lost key is replaced by the same rotateKey path, effective only from the next set version, so votes signed under the lost key in committed epochs stand as signed. If the owner address itself is lost, the bonded stake is stranded: no rule of this specification can move it, and no DAO or emergency path exists to rescue it (SYS-04, HALT-04). Failure mode: checking signatures against the current key instead of the epoch's committed key lets a rotation launder a past equivocation (the evidence no longer matches the "current" set) and rejects valid historical votes; allowing key reuse lets a pre-signed message be attributed to a different entry's stake. Proven Assumed (the un-forgeability of Ed25519 is A-CRYPTO-2; the rule is Proven given it.)

6. The validator set as an L1-committed object

MEM-08 — Validator-set commitment construction (1) A published set is the tuple (chainId, k, R_k, TotalVP_k, n_k, entries): the chain id, the set version k, the root R_k, the total effective stake, the entry count, and the full ordered entry list [(pubkey_i, effStake_i)]. The list is published so that any party can reconstruct the tree cheaply; the root is the authoritative commitment. (2) Canonical order. Entries are sorted ascending by keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_SET_KEY, chainId, pubkey)). Public keys are unique within a version (MEM-02), so the order is total and ties cannot occur. Sorting makes the root independent of insertion order, snapshot iteration order and tree-building implementation. index is the 0-based position in this order; the signer bitmap used by PRF-04 indexes this same order, so bitmap bit i denotes entry i. (3) Leaves and interior nodes are domain-separated (GEN-05): leaf and interior tags differ, and the empty-set tag differs from both, so no node can be reinterpreted at a different position or as a different object. Odd nodes are promoted unchanged to the next level; a node is never duplicated to fill a level, which removes the duplicate-last-node ambiguity. The full encoding is in the table below. (4) What the root commits to — exactly. R_k commits to the chain id, the set version k, and the ordered list of (pubkey, effStake) pairs — and nothing else. In particular it does not commit to the epoch mapping (which version governs which epoch is L1 Inbox state, MEM-09), to the validators' owner addresses, to bond age, tenure, reputation, region or any score, or to rotation history. Adding a field that affects voting power requires a new set version and a rule change; it may not be smuggled into an existing root. (5) Total. TotalVP_k = Σ_i effStake_i is published in the same L1 transaction as the root and is recomputed by the guest from the leaves; a mismatch, or a leaf with effStake = 0, invalidates the set. A sum is not derivable from a Merkle root, so the published total is a checked field, not a convenience. (6) Authority. The root is written into L1 state by the registry contract (commitSet()) and read from L1 state by the Inbox; it is never supplied by the prover. The proof's public input "validator-set commitment" is therefore an L1 fact (L1-05), the Inbox re-derives it when verifying, and a prover cannot choose it (PRF-03). Membership in the guest is then: recompute the leaf from (pubkey, effStake), verify its Merkle path to R_k, and require that the key which verified the vote signature is the leaf's pubkey (PRF-04). Failure mode: an ambiguous or prover-chosen set commitment lets a certificate be redeemed against a set that never signed it (set substitution, the A-CONS-4 failure), and a root that silently commits extra fields lets voting power change without any consensus rule changing. MEM-09 — Set snapshot timing and one-epoch lookahead (1) Publication. Set versions are append-only and are created by commitSet(), which takes no arguments and may be called by anyone. Its contents are fixed by the contract: the root and total of the ledger state at the moment of the call, inside the L1 block N(k) that includes the call. The caller chooses nothing — not the entries, not the total, not the block's position in the block (which only affects which earlier transactions of that block are included). A ledger mutation in a later transaction of the same block does not affect R_k. (2) Which L1 block defines the set for epoch e. Let setVersion(e) be the highest k such that the L1 block N(k) is Ethereum-final in the L1 view committed by the first L2 block of epoch e. Then R_{setVersion(e)} — read from L1 state at N(k) — is the epoch's validator set, and the epoch's first block commits (setVersion(e), N(setVersion(e))). The header field carrying this tuple is CONS-10's; its value rule is this clause. Because Ethereum finality is objective and permanent, all correct nodes that can read L1 compute the same value (A-CONS-4). (3) Monotonicity. setVersion never decreases: setVersion(e) ≥ setVersion(e') for e > e', and a first block that commits a lower version than its predecessor is invalid. A version can never be reverted to, re-used after being superseded, or amended. (4) Immutability of an epoch's mapping. Once epoch e has begun, no L1 transaction may change setVersion(e) or the record at N(setVersion(e)). A later set version applies only to epochs whose first block lies after its publication; it never governs an epoch that has already started. (5) Ethereum-finality requirement and the one-epoch lookahead. No L2 consensused state may depend on a non-final L1 fact (SYS-02), so a validator must not produce the first block of epoch e unless the block N(setVersion(e)) is already Ethereum-final. If no such version exists — for example settlement or L1 inclusion has stalled — the validators do not start the epoch: they hold the current epoch open or halt (HALT-01, CONS-15). They must never fall back to "the latest root the proposer has seen". The epoch length and the handoff schedule that make this a one-epoch lookahead in steady state are CONS-13's; this clause supplies the membership-side predicate CONS-13 must satisfy: the version governing an epoch is committed and Ethereum-final strictly before that epoch starts. Failure mode: if the set for an epoch could be chosen after the epoch's blocks were voted on — by the prover, the L1 caller of commitSet(), or a version committed mid-epoch — a certificate could be judged under a set that did not sign it (set substitution), and two nodes could disagree about which set governs the same epoch (the A-CONS-4 failure). Proven Assumed (conditional on A-L1-1 for L1 finality liveness and on A-CONS-4 for set agreement; if L1 finality cannot advance, clause 5 halts the epoch boundary, which is a liveness consequence, not a safety loss.)

7. Sybil resistance and concentration

MEM-10 — Sybil resistance and the non-claim about per-address caps (1) There is no per-address, per-operator or per-validator cap on stake in v1. There is a floor S_min per entry, derived from cost recovery and permissionless entry (ECON-09), and the accounting rule that weight is counted per (pubkey, effStake) entry (MEM-02). One beneficial owner may hold many entries; the protocol does not require, verify, represent or attempt to detect beneficial ownership, and it makes no claim that splitting or merging identities is prevented. (2) What the design actually relies on. The cost of acquiring the float: AttackCost(t) = b · S_total · P(t) · (1 + σ(t)) + OC(t) + E[Slash] with b = 1/3 under A-CONS-1, P(t) the TAIKO price and σ(t) the slippage and illiquidity premium for acquiring b · S_total within the attack window — both UNMEASURED and time-varying (economics-raw.md §2.8). Splitting stake across identities does not change that term. (3) The cost-overhead argument, stated exactly. Let C be a per-address cap, S_total the total bonded stake, k = S_total / C the number of addresses needed to hold everything, and k_op the marginal cost of operating one additional validator identity (key generation and custody, monitoring, per-validator overhead, and any amortised flat bond). An adversary seeking a fraction b of stake acquires b · S_total tokens and spreads them over b · k addresses; the capital cost of the attack is unchanged, and the cap adds only b · k · k_op. The ratio (cap overhead)/(attack cost) is k_op / C: it shrinks as the cap rises and never cancels the dominant term (economics-raw.md §2.7 points 1-2). In the fuller form the acquisition term also carries P(t) · (1 + σ(t)), so the overhead is k_op / (C · P(t) · (1 + σ(t))) in the same units — the conclusion is unchanged: the cap constrains labels, not capital. (4) A cap never bounds concentration, and the only way to make it bind is to make entry expensive. k is unbounded above because addresses and keys are free to create; k_op is bounded above by the honest cost of entry, which R1 forbids making prohibitive. If the protocol caps validator slots instead, slot acquisition becomes the binding constraint and a wealthy adversary outbids honest operators for existing slots rather than acquiring tokens (a secondary market in slots); Cosmos' MaxValidators is a consensus-performance parameter, not a decentralisation mechanism, and Polkadot's 2026 self-stake minimum is a floor on operator commitment, not a cap on stake (economics-raw.md §2.7 point 3, sourced). (5) What actually constrains concentration is (a) the total cost of acquiring the float — a liquidity and slippage question, not an address question; (b) rewards that are concave in stake, so the marginal token is worth less to a large holder; and (c) transparency. The protocol can deliver (c) and does: the full entry list of every set version is public (MEM-08), so concentration is observable even when it cannot be prevented (economics-raw.md §2.7 point 5). (6) Any claim that a cap "prevents monopolisation" must be deleted or restated as the cost-overhead statement of clause (3), with k_op marked unmeasured. "Per-address caps prevent monopolization" is an explicit non-argument of this project (01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §6.4). Failure mode: a cap is presented as decentralisation, so reviewers and users believe concentration is bounded when the only effect is an additive, sub-linear overhead; or the cap makes entry costly and excludes honest small participants while a wealthy adversary splits anyway. Proven unmeasured (k_op, P(t), σ(t) and the resulting AttackCost are unmeasured; they are measured from a reference-validator cost study and from order-book depth and traded volume, respectively.)

8. Weak subjectivity and long-range attacks

MEM-11 — Weak subjectivity: the L1 checkpoint as the source, and the freshness window (1) The weak-subjectivity checkpoint is the L1 checkpoint. A node bootstraps from the checkpoint record most recently accepted by the L1 Inbox and Ethereum-final (L1-07, SYS-02): an L2 height, block hash, state root, epoch and set version. The node reads it from L1 state; no social input, no peer vote and no checkpoint provider role is introduced. The reason is that this checkpoint is Ethereum-committed and publicly auditable, and it is exactly what L1 will accept as a predecessor — a node needs an L1 view anyway to verify settlement (mode-a-feasibility-analysis.md §C5). (2) Freshness window. A node must not bootstrap from a checkpoint older than WS_max, where WS_max ≤ D_withdraw − (T_detect + T_evidence_submit + T_L1_include(p) + T_L1_final + T_process) − M_ws. The derivation: an offence committed at the checkpoint's epoch remains slashable only until the offender's stake can be released, which is at least D_withdraw after its exit (MEM-05, MEM-06); if the anchor is older than that minus the time it takes to land evidence, then a checkpoint's signers may already be beyond reach and the anchor no longer carries the slashing guarantee it is trusted for. Substituting MEM-05 clause 4 gives the derived upper bound WS_max ≤ T_proof_bound + M − M_ws. Every term is UNMEASURED; M_ws is the long-range margin. (3) It is an upper bound, not a target. WS_max is the largest age at which a checkpoint can still be defended by slashing; a smaller value is always safer, and the published value must be justified by measurement, not chosen for convenience (PARAM-03). (4) Disclosure. The freshness window, the current checkpoint's age, and the fact that a checkpoint was used to bootstrap must be visible to users of a node, in the vocabulary of STATUS-11. State synced from an anchor is never presented as verified beyond the anchor. (5) The assumption, stated at the claim. This rule rests on A-CONS-6 (new joiners can obtain a checkpoint no older than the published window) and on A-L1-1 (Ethereum liveness and safety). It is a trust requirement, not a cryptographic guarantee: if L1 cannot accept a checkpoint for longer than WS_max — an L2 halt, a proving outage, a blob-retention failure — then no fresh checkpoint exists and a new node cannot safely sync. The chain's liveness failure becomes a sync failure, and that consequence is disclosed rather than hidden. Failure mode: a node that treats an arbitrarily old anchor as authoritative accepts a history whose signers can no longer be punished — the long-range attack of MEM-12 — while believing it holds a protocol guarantee. Proven Assumed derived unmeasured (the inequality is derived from MEM-05; its inputs are unmeasured, and the conditional is A-CONS-6 + A-L1-1.)
MEM-12 — Long-range attack handling; no silent trusted checkpoint provider (1) The attack, in this design. An attacker who held at least 1/3 of the stake at some past epoch e and has since exited retains the Ed25519 secrets committed in R_{setVersion(e)} and can produce an alternative L2 history branching at or before e whose signatures are cryptographically indistinguishable from honest ones. A defensive quorum of the old set can be reconstructed from the attacker's own keys, so no signature check can detect the fork, and the attacker cannot be punished once its stake has been released (consensus-survey-raw.md §7.4; recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §10 item 10, which records that "weak subjectivity is objective/trustless" is a misconception). (2) Two defences, both required. (a) Checkpoint anchoring: a node must never adopt an L2 history that conflicts with its L1 checkpoint; the L1 checkpoint is a fork-choice revert limit, not merely a hint. (b) Freshness (MEM-11): a checkpoint older than WS_max is not usable, and the honest statement is that a very old checkpoint must not be used. Supporting membership-side defences: certificates are judged only under their own epoch's set (CONS-08); setVersion(e) is immutable once the epoch starts (MEM-09); keys are retired on rotation so (key, epoch) → entry stays injective (MEM-07); and the unbonding delay keeps the stake that signed a history slashable long enough for evidence (MEM-05, MEM-06). (3) Validating from genesis is not a substitute. The L1 staking ledger is objective, but the epoch chain is not objective by itself: a long-range attacker can produce valid signatures from a historical TAIKO-staked set, so two internally valid histories exist and only L1 says which one the protocol accepts (consensus-survey-raw.md §7.4 point 2). A node that cannot obtain a fresh L1 checkpoint cannot resolve this by re-executing more history. (4) What a node must do when it cannot obtain a fresh checkpoint. If L1 is unreachable, or no Ethereum-final checkpoint is younger than WS_max, the node must: (i) refuse to treat any L2 history as protocol-verified; (ii) not report or relay a status above STATUS-03 for such a history, and not present it as settlement-backed; (iii) not silently substitute a checkpoint obtained from a peer, an RPC endpoint, or any third-party service; and (iv) surface the condition to its operator, naming the checkpoint's age and the required bound. It may retry, and it may halt and wait (HALT-01). It must not "succeed" by trusting an unverified history. (5) No checkpoint provider role. This specification creates no registry, contract, address, role or reward for checkpoint distribution, and no node may treat one as authoritative. A node operator may configure an out-of-band anchor locally; that is a node-local trust decision outside the protocol, and it must then be labelled visibly as an operator-supplied anchor and must not be relayed to others as protocol-verified. Restart-from-checkpoint rules are HALT-02's; this rule fixes the membership-side refusal to fabricate trust. Failure mode: a node silently follows a fabricated history and reports it as verified, so users act on a chain the protocol never accepted; or an unstated third-party checkpoint provider becomes a privileged role, contradicting SYS-04 and silently re-introducing the operator R1/R2 forbid. Proven Assumed Open (the cryptographic indistinguishability of past-key signatures is Proven given A-CRYPTO-2; the freshness value is Open and unmeasured; the conditional is A-CONS-6.)

Limitations stated on this page, not hidden. (i) Every safety claim here is conditional on A-CONS-1 and, where a slash must actually be collected, on A-ECO-1 at the prevailing price.
