08 — Migration, upgrades and governance

What this page owns. The migration boundary from the Shasta deployment at revision 7718753c1 (MIG-01), the per-contract upgrade list and storage-layout constraints (MIG-02), the disposition of everything in flight across the boundary (MIG-03), removal of operational privileges (MIG-04), program-image, verifier and mixed-client rules (MIG-05), the no-replacement-deployment rule (MIG-06), and governance (GOV-01–GOV-04), where GOV-04 is a tombstone: under decision D-16 the governance stall resolution is deferred with narrow forced inclusion and aggregation, so v1 has no recovery path of any kind and clearing a settlement stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified (DEFERRED.md); heartbeat eligibility has since been revived as a live rule by increment 02, and the rotation that would consume it stays a gated tombstone (D-16; increment 02). Status labels are those of STATUS-01–STATUS-11; this page introduces no new label and no new status word.

The boundary in one picture

The migration is a single, irreversible switch from the Shasta rule set to the Etna PoS + ZK rule set over the same preserved contract addresses. It is not a chain restart: the new consensus begins at the last Shasta state that Ethereum already accepted, and every later rule must accept that state as its own past (GOV-03). History at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch — is untouchable by any protocol path, and history above it is provisional. v1 has no recovery path of any kind (D-16): the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15 and the governance stall resolution that replaced it is deferred, so no protocol path discards the provisional range and clearing a settlement stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified (DEFERRED.md §3; REC-01); the migration itself is never a recovery and never a substitute for one. (decision D-16: the boundary of REC-01 is unchanged, v1 has no recovery path, and clearing a settlement stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified.)

Normative rules

MIG-01 — migration boundary and preserved addresses The migration boundary is the last Shasta state accepted on L1 that is Ethereum-final at activation — the 4-tuple (C*, B*, H*, S*) where C* is the L2 chain id, B* the L2 block number of the last checkpoint written into the preserved L1 SignalService by a successful Inbox.prove() (Inbox.sol:364-370) before T3, H* the L2 block hash recorded for B*, and S* its L2 state root. "Last" is bounded by time, not chosen: T3 additionally requires the write to be at least L1_FINALITY_DEPTH L1 blocks old because prove() is disabled in DRAINED (MIG-03(1)(b)), so the boundary is an Ethereum-final L1 fact rather than a tip the bundle can pick. B* is identified on-chain by the equality _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].blockHash == IInbox.CoreState.lastFinalizedBlockHash and S* by _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].stateRoot; the bundle supplies (B*, S*) and T3 reverts unless both equalities hold, so the boundary is derived from the finalized tip and never chosen. Uniqueness of B* rests on keccak-256 collision resistance (A-CRYPTO-3) and on the monotonicity of L1-06. The starting point is the deployed system at revision 7718753c1: an Inbox with two separate entry points, propose() (Inbox.sol:270-290) and prove() (Inbox.sol:321-400), i.e. the data-first path D5 forbids; a disabled bond mechanism (minBond = 0, livenessBond = 0, MainnetInbox.sol:37-38) whose liveness settlement is additionally suppressed while a prover whitelist is enabled (Inbox.sol:356-359); a ProverWhitelist gating prove() (Inbox.sol:337,771-779; mainnet 0xEa798547d97e345395dA071a0D7ED8144CD612Ae); a PreconfWhitelist as IProposerChecker selecting one operator per epoch (PreconfWhitelist.sol:136-140; mainnet 0xFD019460881e6EeC632258222393d5821029b2ac); and the June 2026 blob-expiry incident, in which queued blob references outlived Ethereum's retention window and the owner-only init3() voided them with fees retained (Inbox.sol:246-258, called on mainnet in the Proposal0019 bundle, deployments/mainnet-contract-logs-L1.md:222-223) — the recorded instance of the unbounded data-first failure class. The new design has no inclusion queue and no owner-voidable data reference; it has instead D-11's permissionless publication record, whose outstanding entries are bounded by the proving deadline and discarded and re-published if unproven (MIG-02 slots 275–277). D-12's narrow forced inclusion — enforced at the proof over the published due set rather than by an admission gate or a privileged queue — is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, so v1 has no inclusion obligation and a proposer that excludes a transaction is not in breach. For unpublished transactions, and for the censorship gap this leaves, the honest statement of what inclusion resistance remains is LIVE-04. (D-11: data may be published before the proof; decision D-16: narrow forced inclusion is deferred with the other add-on mechanisms, and its register entry is DEFERRED.md §1.) State that migrates (read once at T3, written to the migration/genesis slots 258–260 and — as the genesis checkpoint record — into the L1-07 checkpoint map of slot 264 (§genesis record, MIG-02), then frozen and never re-interpreted): H*; the L1 SignalService checkpoint record for B*; the existing TAIKO ERC-20 balances at the preserved token address 0x10dea67478c5F8C5E2D90e5E9B26dBe60c54d800, which are neither moved nor rewritten (new stake custody is a new L1 staking contract holding TAIKO via transferFrom, MEM-01, D7); the Bridge state nextMessageId (Bridge slot 251) and messageStatus (slot 252); and the vault deposit maps bridgedToCanonical, canonicalToBridged, btokenDenylist, lastMigrationStart (ERC20Vault slots 301–304) and the two NFT vault maps (slots 301–302). Governance

GOV-01 — DAO governs upgrades only; the trust assumption is stated The DAO's only authorities over this protocol are (i) the ability to execute an upgrade: MainnetDAOController.execute(bytes) (onlyOwner, owned by the TaikoDAO; MainnetDAOController.sol:24-31) calling upgradeToAndCall / upgradeTo on the proxies it owns (EssentialContract.sol:207), including the migration transitions T1–T3; and (ii) nothing else — the queueing of a stall-resolution action is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, so v1 admits no runtime governance action. After ETNA_ACTIVE no other DAO-dependent runtime state may exist: no consensus rule, admission rule, proof-acceptance rule, exit rule or bridge rule may read a value the DAO can change without an upgrade, and no DAO transaction may be required for ordinary liveness — block production, voting, proving, settlement and exit must all complete without one. There is no named exception in v1: v1 has no recovery path of any kind — permissionless or governance-mediated — because the governance stall resolution is deferred with the other add-on mechanisms (DEFERRED.md). A settlement stall halts the chain: funds are safe, the boundary holds, and the exit guarantee of MEM-15 is independent of any DAO action, but clearing the stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified, and that dependency is disclosed as an assumption rather than as a v1 mechanism. (decision D-16: v1 has no recovery path; the D-15 stall-resolution authority and the governance-liveness assumption A-GOV-2 it carried are deferred with it. D-6: forced inclusion is removed, so no inclusion rule exists and none appears in this enumeration; inclusion resistance is the conditional property of LIVE-04.) The trust assumption on the DAO's conduct is explicit and is not repairable by the protocol: A-GOV-1 (01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §4.4) states that the DAO does not execute a malicious upgrade; if it does, the safety claims of this specification do not apply to the resulting rule set (failure class F2). For contrast, the prior Etna design recorded exactly two DAO-dependent states — CONFLICT after a provisional landing, and a certificate-free landing during a one-leaf outage (prior-etna-digest.md:113,119); this design has none at runtime: no DAO-dependent state is admitted in v1 (D-16). Failure modes: any runtime DAO lever is a capture target and a liveness dependency (R2); a hidden owner flag makes the no-privilege invariant (INV-04) false; describing A-GOV-1 as a guarantee is exactly the non-argument "the DAO would fix it"; and a chain whose stall remedy is a future protocol update is only as live as that update being specified and shipped (D-16). Proven Assumed (proven relative to the MIG-04 removal table being complete; the residual trust is A-GOV-1 for the DAO's conduct and A-GOV-2 for its liveness, whose failure is GOV-02)
GOV-02 — malicious governance is a separate failure class A malicious or compromised DAO is failure class F2, not a protocol failure: no rule, contract, monitor or evidence object in this design detects, prevents or repairs it, and one upgrade can rewrite any rule — including this one — reassign any storage slot, or remove the staking requirement. The honest responses are exactly three: (i) before-the-fact disclosure, (ii) comparability, i.e. every upgrade bundle is published with the target implementation addresses, the storage-layout diff and the expected post-upgrade boundary object, so any deviation is publicly detectable, and (iii) the ability of users to exit before an announced upgrade takes effect, subject to the delays of MSG-03 and ECON-03. This design does not claim that a DAO upgrade cannot invalidate finalized history; it claims that no rule of this protocol can, and that a governance rewrite is reported as a rules change rather than as a stall resolution. A captured or coerced governance can still rewrite the rules by upgrade, but the opportunistic stall-resolution surface does not exist in v1: the governance stall resolution is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §3), so no rule lets governance discard a provisional range. It is named A-GOV-2 and recorded in LIM-01. The status guarantee (STATUS-04, STATUS-06) is made relative to the rules in force — history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is untouchable by any protocol path (Ethereum finality is the separate, stronger property of STATUS-07), and history above it is provisional, with no v1 path that replaces it; a recovery mechanism is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, and a rules change by upgrade is never presented as recovery, never as an ad-hoc governance transaction, and never by an ordinary upgrade (REC-01) — and after a governance rewrite it must be re-established by the new rules. (D-15 and D-16: the rule-bound stall resolution of GOV-04 that D-15 specified is deferred and MUST NOT be implemented, so v1 has no sanctioned replacement above the checkpoint; a governance rewrite is reported as a rules change, never as recovery or a stall resolution.) This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the italic no longer presents a deferred action as the sanctioned replacement. GOV-03 — upgrades must not invalidate finalized history and are never a substitute for recovery Every upgrade, including the migration of MIG-01–MIG-06, must accept all history finalized under the rules in force before it: (a) a certificate valid under the epoch and validator-set rules of its own epoch remains valid forever (CONS-08) and no post-upgrade rule may require it to be re-derived; (b) the post-upgrade verifier route must still accept a proof of any not-yet-settled batch under the program image that was in force for that batch's epoch (MIG-05), so no batch becomes unprovable because of an upgrade; (c) checkpoint advancement stays monotone (L1-06): an upgrade may not lower the finalized tip or rewrite the checkpoint record for any height at or below the boundary with different contents; and (d) at migration the accepted history is the boundary of MIG-01, and no alternative history at or below B* may ever be adopted afterwards (HALT-02); and (e) no upgrade may be used as a substitute for recovery: an upgrade MUST accept every history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and it may not discard history above that checkpoint either, because no rule specifies a stall-clearing action — an upgrade in v1 cannot clear a stall, and clearing one requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified (D-16; DEFERRED.md §3).
