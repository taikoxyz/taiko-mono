The boundary in one picture

The migration is a single, irreversible switch from the Shasta rule set to the Etna PoS + ZK rule set over the same preserved contract addresses. It is not a chain restart: the new consensus begins at the last Shasta state that Ethereum already accepted, and every later rule must accept that state as its own past (GOV-03). History at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch — is untouchable by any protocol path, and history above it is provisional. v1 has no permissionless recovery of any kind (D-15), and the one path that may act on the provisional range above it is the rule-bound, timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04, which is live again under increment 05: it resumes settlement on the same chain, leaves the checkpoint record and everything at or below it untouched, invalidates a superseded certificate only as a batch's head evidence, and is never an upgrade and never a substitute for one (REC-01; note, however, that clearing a stall still depends on governance liveness, disclosed as F-GOV-1/A-GOV-2, so an absent or captured governance can leave the stall in place). (increment 05: the boundary of REC-01 is unchanged; the permissionless recovery stays withdrawn and the governance stall resolution is revived as the one named runtime action, with its trigger, stored timelock, consumed entry and resume-only effect. R5R2-C-01: the range above the checkpoint is not discarded or made unlandable — the resolution resumes settlement on the same chain, and only a superseded certificate's use as a batch's head evidence is invalidated.)

Normative rules

MIG-01 — migration boundary and preserved addresses The migration boundary is the last Shasta state accepted on L1 that is Ethereum-final at activation — the 4-tuple (C*, B*, H*, S*) where C* is the L2 chain id, B* the L2 block number of the last checkpoint written into the preserved L1 SignalService by a successful Inbox.prove() (Inbox.sol:364-370) before T3, H* the L2 block hash recorded for B*, and S* its L2 state root. "Last" is bounded by time, not chosen: T3 additionally requires the write to be at least L1_FINALITY_DEPTH L1 blocks old because prove() is disabled in DRAINED (MIG-03(1)(b)), so the boundary is an Ethereum-final L1 fact rather than a tip the bundle can pick. B* is identified on-chain by the equality _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].blockHash == IInbox.CoreState.lastFinalizedBlockHash and S* by _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].stateRoot; the bundle supplies (B*, S*) and T3 reverts unless both equalities hold, so the boundary is derived from the finalized tip and never chosen. Uniqueness of B* rests on keccak-256 collision resistance (A-CRYPTO-3) and on the monotonicity of L1-06. The starting point is the deployed system at revision 7718753c1: an Inbox with two separate entry points, propose() (Inbox.sol:270-290) and prove() (Inbox.sol:321-400), i.e. the data-first path D5 forbids; a disabled bond mechanism (minBond = 0, livenessBond = 0, MainnetInbox.sol:37-38) whose liveness settlement is additionally suppressed while a prover whitelist is enabled (Inbox.sol:356-359); a ProverWhitelist gating prove() (Inbox.sol:337,771-779; mainnet 0xEa798547d97e345395dA071a0D7ED8144CD612Ae); a PreconfWhitelist as IProposerChecker selecting one operator per epoch (PreconfWhitelist.sol:136-140; mainnet 0xFD019460881e6EeC632258222393d5821029b2ac); and the June 2026 blob-expiry incident, in which queued blob references outlived Ethereum's retention window and the owner-only init3() voided them with fees retained (Inbox.sol:246-258, called on mainnet in the Proposal0019 bundle, deployments/mainnet-contract-logs-L1.md:222-223) — the recorded instance of the unbounded data-first failure class. The new design has no inclusion queue and no owner-voidable data reference; it has instead D-11's permissionless publication record, whose outstanding entries are bounded by the proving deadline and discarded and re-published if unproven (MIG-02 slots 275–277). GOV-01 — DAO governs upgrades only; the trust assumption is stated The DAO's only authorities over this protocol are (i) the ability to execute an upgrade: MainnetDAOController.execute(bytes) (onlyOwner, owned by the TaikoDAO; MainnetDAOController.sol:24-31) calling upgradeToAndCall / upgradeTo on the proxies it owns (EssentialContract.sol:207), including the migration transitions T1–T3; and (ii) the queueing of a stall-resolution entry under GOV-04 — a rule-bound, resume-only runtime action whose trigger, stored timelock and effect are fixed by rule, and the only runtime governance entry point this protocol has. Nothing else is admitted: no other runtime governance action exists. After ETNA_ACTIVE no other DAO-dependent runtime state may exist: no consensus rule, admission rule, proof-acceptance rule, exit rule or bridge rule may read a value the DAO can change without an upgrade, and no DAO transaction may be required for ordinary liveness — block production, voting, proving, settlement and exit must all complete without one. There is exactly one named exception: the rule-bound, timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04 — a DAO-queued entry that any account may execute once its stored deadline passes, whose effect is fixed by L1 state alone. It is not a DAO rescue: governance chooses nothing at execution, and the discretion it retains over the values future entries will use is named F-GOV-4 rather than denied. A settlement stall is cleared only by that action: funds are safe, the boundary holds, and the exit guarantee of MEM-15 is independent of any DAO action, but clearing the stall still depends on governance liveness with no protocol bound (F-GOV-1, A-GOV-2), and that dependency is disclosed as an assumption. (increment 05: the D-15 stall-resolution authority is live again as GOV-04, with its entry consumed by execution; the governance-liveness assumption A-GOV-2 is kept and F-GOV-1/F-GOV-3/F-GOV-4 are carried with it. D-6 removed the legacy inclusion path, and the narrow rule over published data that D-12 proposed is live again (increment 04) but adds no privileged action to this enumeration: its enforcement is in the proof and its prune is permissionless and deletion-only; inclusion resistance remains the conditional property of LIVE-04.) The trust assumption on the DAO's conduct is explicit and is not repairable by the protocol: A-GOV-1 (01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §4.4) states that the DAO does not execute a malicious upgrade; if it does, the safety claims of this specification do not apply to the resulting rule set (failure class F2). For contrast, the prior Etna design recorded exactly two DAO-dependent states — CONFLICT after a provisional landing, and a certificate-free landing during a one-leaf outage (prior-etna-digest.md:113,119); this design has exactly one runtime DAO-dependent state — the queued stall-resolution entry of GOV-04, under its rule-bound trigger, stored timelock and resume-only effect — and no other (increment 05). GOV-02 — malicious governance is a separate failure class A malicious or compromised DAO is failure class F2, not a protocol failure: no rule, contract, monitor or evidence object in this design detects, prevents or repairs it, and one upgrade can rewrite any rule — including this one — reassign any storage slot, or remove the staking requirement. The honest responses are exactly three: (i) before-the-fact disclosure, (ii) comparability, i.e. every upgrade bundle is published with the target implementation addresses, the storage-layout diff and the expected post-upgrade boundary object, so any deviation is publicly detectable, and (iii) the ability of users to exit before an announced upgrade takes effect, subject to the delays of MSG-03 and ECON-03. This design does not claim that a DAO upgrade cannot invalidate finalized history; it claims that no rule of this protocol can, and that a governance rewrite is reported as a rules change rather than as a stall resolution. A captured or coerced governance can rewrite the rules by upgrade and can also act opportunistically within the live stall-resolution rule: the action of GOV-04 is rule-bound and resume-only, but a governance that waits for a genuine stall can have an unfavourable provisional range's in-flight certificates superseded, so that its signals are not carried into the settlement the resumed chain produces and must be resubmitted, and repetition is paced only by a DAO transaction plus a stored window (F-GOV-1, F-GOV-3; R5R2-C-01). Nothing in this protocol detects, prevents or repairs the capture. It is named A-GOV-2 and recorded in LIM-01. GOV-04 — stall resolution: a timelocked, resume-only governance action whose entry is consumed exactly once (revived by increment 05) The action, and what it does. A settlement stall is cleared only by the action this rule defines: one governance-queued entry, a stored timelock, a permissionless execute() that consumes the entry, and a signed generation increment. The action resumes settlement on the same chain: it leaves the checkpoint and everything at or below it untouched; it invalidates a superseded certificate only as a batch's head evidence — a superseded certificate cannot serve as the head certificate of an extending batch, which is exactly what the enforced checks say, since the head certificate, its contributing votes and the head header must carry the current generation (CONS-05(2)(a), PRF-04(i)); the range above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint remains valid history that can be extended under the new generation, and nothing in this rule invalidates or forbids a block on account of the generation in its own header (CONS-10(7): the generation is part of the header bytes, so every other header legitimately carries the generation it was produced under); and the class above the checkpoint is unprotected for a different and narrower reason than erasure: the user's signals are not carried into the settlement the resumed chain produces, so they must be resubmitted, with no compensation (ECON-11). (R5R2-C-01: the preserved preamble claimed a "full-range discard" and an "unlandable" branch; the operative checks constrain only the head evidence, so the effect is stated in the enforced form and the stronger wording MUST NOT be restored.) The former permissionless bonded recovery stays withdrawn (D-15) and MUST NOT be reintroduced under any name: there is no invoker, no bond, no escalation, no certificate bundle, no completion record and no reward. The action adds no new entry point on the exit, inclusion, staking or proof paths, and CONS-16's rotation stays deferred (D-16) and MUST NOT be implemented. (a) Trigger, read from L1 state alone. An entry may be queued only while block.timestamp − lastAcceptedBatchTime ≥ T_STALL_GOV, where lastAcceptedBatchTime is the L1 timestamp stored in the current checkpoint record (L1-07). No invoker claim, evidence object, certificate or bundle enters the predicate, and only the acceptance of an extending batch — which advances lastAcceptedBatchTime — can clear it. A queue attempt while the predicate is false MUST revert. No other trigger and no early declaration exists: a participant's local view, a halt, a missing certificate, a slow proof and an over-depth cap are not triggers.
(b) The entry, and the state machine. govResumeState takes exactly three values: none, queued, executed. There is no distinct cancelled value: a cancelled entry is behaviourally none, its cancellation is recorded by the event, and adding a state would add surface without adding a property. An entry is the tuple (govResumeQueuedAt:u64, govResumeQueuedHeight:u64, govResumeExecutableAt:u64, govResumeState:u8). Queueing is a DAO transaction through the authority of GOV-01 and is the only runtime governance entry point this protocol has. It MUST be allowed only while (i) the trigger of (a) holds and (ii) the state is none, is executed, or is queued and the entry is void under (e); a second queue attempt while a live entry is queued MUST revert. Queueing records govResumeQueuedAt = block.timestamp, govResumeQueuedHeight = lastLandedHeight, govResumeExecutableAt = block.timestamp + T_GOV_RESUME and govResumeState = queued. It names no height, state root, checkpoint, range, subset, beneficiary, generation or any other value, and no other value may be attached to it. Replacing a void entry under (e) is a new entry with fresh fields, not a revival of the void one.
(c) Execution consumes the entry, and the effect is resume-only. execute() is permissionless and takes no argument that influences the outcome. It MUST revert unless the state is exactly queued; it MUST revert before the stored govResumeExecutableAt with the error TimelockNotElapsed; it MUST revert if the entry is void under (e). On success it MUST, in one transaction: (1) increment recoveryGeneration by exactly one; (2) set govResumeState := executed; and (3) emit the resolution event, carrying at least the restored height and the new generation. It writes nothing else. resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 is derived from the current L1-accepted checkpoint, never chosen and never stored; the checkpoint record itself is left untouched; nothing at or below the checkpoint is read, written, re-judged or discarded; no retirement record and no resume record exists; the range above the checkpoint is not rewritten, re-judged or erased — it remains valid history that a later batch may settle by extending it under the new generation, and nothing in this rule invalidates or forbids a block on account of the generation in its own header (CONS-10(7)). (R5R2-C-01: the effect is resume on the same chain; the generation rule constrains only the batch's head evidence.) The generation is incremented nowhere else: no other function, initialiser, upgrade, governance call, cancellation, queue or client path may increment it in any other state or a second time. Once the state is executed, every later execute() reverts for every caller, in every block, with every calldata, and the state can only be left by a fresh DAO queue that creates a new entry with a fresh stored deadline. (e) Void on progress, with permissionless cancellation and queue-over-void. If the checkpoint advances at any time before execution — lastLandedHeight > govResumeQueuedHeight — the entry is void: execution MUST revert, and any account may cancel it, which sets none and clears the entry fields. A void entry cannot be revived and a cancelled entry's fields are not reusable; a new entry requires a fresh trigger and a fresh stored deadline. A new queue while the trigger holds MUST be permitted to replace a void entry, because a void entry is not pending and never blocks the mechanism. No account may cancel an entry whose trigger still holds and whose checkpoint has not advanced.
(f) Timelock: the notice window, and what it is not. A queued entry becomes executable no earlier than its stored govResumeExecutableAt. The window the rule can guarantee. For a user whose L2→L1 signal is included in the L2 state at or below the last accepted checkpoint at queue time, the window is a real exit window: execution leaves the checkpoint record untouched, nothing at or below it is rewritten (REC-01), and the exit of MEM-15 from that checkpoint — including the withdrawal root of L1-13, which L1-13(1)/(3) make attestable for any already-accepted checkpoint, in particular the latest one, with no new L2 block and no settlement progress, its delay measured on the L1 clock from the root's own record — remains available before, during and after execution. The required length is the registered relation T_GOV_RESUME ≥ W_root + WITHDRAWAL_DELAY + T_VETO + MARGIN, where W_root is the worst-case time to produce and record the checkpoint's k attestations; every term is unmeasured and MARGIN is a stated, unmeasured margin whose contents — including any term for the user's own L1 submission and observation latency — are left to Phase B. The relation is a constructor-time assertion under (g) and is conditional on the funded proving market and retained inputs of MEM-15(2b). What the window is not. It is not an exit window for value above the last accepted checkpoint: such value has no L1-provable claim and cannot create one (MEM-15(4)), and the execution does not carry its signals into the settlement the resumed chain produces, so they must be resubmitted; the range itself is not erased — it remains valid history that a later batch may settle by extending it under the new generation. No value of T_GOV_RESUME changes that. (R5R2-C-01: the class is unprotected because the resumed settlement does not carry its signals, not because the range is discarded.) What a holder above the checkpoint has is the boundary's prior statement that the range was provisional (REC-01), the notice the queued entry gives, and the remedy of resubmitting their transactions after the resolution; protocol penalties compensate nothing (ECON-11). Execution removes no claim at or below the checkpoint: a user who does not complete the exit inside the window can still complete it afterwards from the same checkpoint, because the root remains attestable and the delay keeps running. The window is therefore a notice-and-opportunity window; what protects that class's value is the boundary, not the window. The sentence "the timelock gives every user the exit window of MEM-15" is withdrawn wherever it appears.
(g) The window is bound to the entry, and the relations are enforced where a value is set. govResumeExecutableAt is recorded at queue time and is part of the entry: no upgrade, initialiser, parameter change, governance call or client path may move it, shorten it, extend it or recompute it, and a change to T_GOV_RESUME applies only to entries queued after it (GOV-03(e)). An implementation MUST refuse to initialise unless the registered relations hold — T_STALL_GOV > T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE (strictly larger than the D6 envelope, so a normal slow proof or one prover failure cannot make the trigger true) and T_GOV_RESUME ≥ W_root + WITHDRAWAL_DELAY + T_VETO + MARGIN (the relation of (f), whose terms are unmeasured). A change to either value is a rules change and MUST be published as one under GOV-02; it is not a silent parameter move, and the Resume-only description MUST NOT claim that governance chooses no configuration value. What governance chooses at execution is nothing; the discretion over the values future entries will use is named as F-GOV-4 rather than denied.
(h) The generation is a signed field, not a retirement record. recoveryGeneration is a field of the signed vote and block-header bytes, so validators sign the generation they are certifying under; the action of (c) increments it; validators sign the new generation. A batch extending the checkpoint MUST carry the current generation in its head certificate, in every contributing vote and in the head header — that is the whole of the requirement: it is not a range-wide generation rule, every other header legitimately carries the generation it was produced under (CONS-10(7)), and a batch whose intermediate headers were produced under an earlier generation and whose head is produced under the current one is provable and MUST NOT be rejected on that ground (CONS-05(2)(a)); a certificate for a block at or below the last accepted checkpoint MUST carry the generation of that block's own header and MUST NOT be compared with the current generation (CONS-05(2)(b), PRF-05(ii)(a)); this is what makes the first post-resolution epoch-opening batch provable, because the anchor certificate is judged under B_anchor's own (pre-resolution) generation while the batch's head is judged under the new one. The generation is not stored in the checkpoint record and no checkpoint-record field is added for it: the historical case reads the generation from the block's own header bytes, which the checkpoint record's blockHash already pins (L1-05 rows 4 and 31, L1-07). No height is retired, no retirement record exists, and no max(lastLandedHeight + 1, resumeHeight) form survives. A certificate or vote whose signed generation does not satisfy its case under CONS-05(2) is void and is rejected: a superseded certificate cannot serve as a batch's head evidence, and a superseded vote is not counted. A block is not invalid because its own header carries an earlier generation, and a batch is not rejected for carrying such a block (CONS-10(7)); this creates no new slashable offence and revives none — the rejected proof or the reverted call is the whole enforcement.
(i) Disclosed assumptions, and the churn residue. Clearing a settlement stall depends on governance liveness with no protocol bound (F-GOV-1, A-GOV-2): the trigger fixes when an entry may be queued, not that it will be. A captured or coerced governance can act opportunistically within this rule, waiting for a genuine stall and having an unfavourable provisional range's in-flight certificates superseded, so that its signals are not carried into the settlement the resumed chain produces and must be resubmitted (R5R2-C-01). Consumption bounds repetition to one generation increment per DAO transaction plus one full stored window: after an execution the trigger still holds, because execution does not advance the checkpoint, so a live governance can queue a fresh entry and increment again at that pace, voiding the in-flight certificates and proofs of the then-current provisional range each time. This residue is F-GOV-3, disclosed rather than constrained: the churn rule is consumption-only, and the "progress-earned" candidate — no new entry until the checkpoint advances past the previous execution's restore point — is not adopted, because it deadlocks the certified-but-unprovable case and L1 cannot decide whether such a range exists (the h_close referent class that gates CONS-16). None of the residue is permissionless. The assumption is recorded in LIM-01.
(j) Upgrade survival. A queued entry MUST survive an upgrade unchanged: the upgrade may not cancel it, execute it early, alter the checkpoint it resumes from, move its stored deadline, or replace it with any other action. An executed entry MUST survive an upgrade unchanged: no upgrade may set its state back to queued or none, re-open it, or make it executable again, and no upgrade may reset, lower or skip recoveryGeneration. The generation and the entry state are L1 state that carries with the L1 reorganisation (L1-12, SYS-02): an execution reorganised out never took effect, so the entry returns to queued with its stored deadline and the generation returns to its previous value; an acceptance reorganised out un-voids a live entry in the same way.
What the action does not do. It gives no account a recovery entry point (queueing is a DAO transaction; execution is permissionless only over an entry that already exists and is live and past its stored deadline); it lets no caller choose anything (execute() takes no outcome-affecting argument and the entry names no height, state, checkpoint, range, subset, set, beneficiary or generation); it does not rewrite, re-judge or delay history at or below the checkpoint; it creates no bond, reward, escrow, fee, treasury transfer or slashable offence; it reduces no validator's weight and rotates no set; it does not gate, delay or accelerate the exit, the withdrawal root, the veto, forced inclusion, settlement, staking exits or land; it does not retire a height, keep a record of any block, or erase the range above the checkpoint — it invalidates a superseded certificate only as a batch's head evidence (R5R2-C-01); and it does not bound the number of resolutions by protocol rule — the pacing is the DAO transaction plus the stored window (F-GOV-3).
