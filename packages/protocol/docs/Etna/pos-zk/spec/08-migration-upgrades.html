08 — Migration, upgrades and governance

What this page owns. The migration boundary from the Shasta deployment at revision 7718753c1 (MIG-01), the per-contract upgrade list and storage-layout constraints (MIG-02), the disposition of everything in flight across the boundary (MIG-03), removal of operational privileges (MIG-04), program-image, verifier and mixed-client rules (MIG-05), the no-replacement-deployment rule (MIG-06), and governance (GOV-01–GOV-03). Status labels are those of STATUS-01–STATUS-11; this page introduces no new label and no new status word.

Evidence base: taiko-baseline-contracts.md (the 800-line baseline, cited as path:line), prior-etna-digest.md, 04-architecture-decision.md and 01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §4–§5. Nothing on this page is implemented, deployed or measured (GEN-01).

The atomic settlement entry point is named land(data, proof) by L1-01; that name is fixed, and clients, parameters and migration scripts MUST use it.

This page is a plan, not a deployment. Every address below is as recorded in the repository's address libraries and hand-maintained deployment logs; no on-chain read was performed (baseline §10 gap 8). The preconditions in § Preconditions must be executed against the live chain before a migration bundle is built, and the bundle itself is gas-unmeasured.

The boundary in one picture

The migration is a single, irreversible switch from the Shasta rule set to the Etna PoS + ZK rule set over the same preserved contract addresses. It is not a chain restart: the new consensus begins at the last Shasta state that Ethereum already accepted, and every later rule must accept that state as its own past (GOV-03).

Normative rules

MIG-01 — migration boundary and preserved addresses The migration boundary is the last Shasta state accepted on L1 that is Ethereum-final at activation — the 4-tuple (C*, B*, H*, S*) where C* is the L2 chain id, B* the L2 block number of the last checkpoint written into the preserved L1 SignalService by a successful Inbox.prove() (Inbox.sol:364-370) before T3, H* the L2 block hash recorded for B*, and S* its L2 state root. "Last" is bounded by time, not chosen: T3 additionally requires the write to be at least L1_FINALITY_DEPTH L1 blocks old because prove() is disabled in DRAINED (MIG-03(1)(b)), so the boundary is an Ethereum-final L1 fact rather than a tip the bundle can pick. B* is identified on-chain by the equality _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].blockHash == IInbox.CoreState.lastFinalizedBlockHash and S* by _checkpoints[VERSION][B*].stateRoot; the bundle supplies (B*, S*) and T3 reverts unless both equalities hold, so the boundary is derived from the finalized tip and never chosen. Uniqueness of B* rests on keccak-256 collision resistance (A-CRYPTO-3) and on the monotonicity of L1-06. The starting point is the deployed system at revision 7718753c1: an Inbox with two separate entry points, propose() (Inbox.sol:270-290) and prove() (Inbox.sol:321-400), i.e. the data-first path D5 forbids; a disabled bond mechanism (minBond = 0, livenessBond = 0, MainnetInbox.sol:37-38) whose liveness settlement is additionally suppressed while a prover whitelist is enabled (Inbox.sol:356-359); a ProverWhitelist gating prove() (Inbox.sol:337,771-779; mainnet 0xEa798547d97e345395dA071a0D7ED8144CD612Ae); a PreconfWhitelist as IProposerChecker selecting one operator per epoch (PreconfWhitelist.sol:136-140; mainnet 0xFD019460881e6EeC632258222393d5821029b2ac); and a forced-inclusion queue already voided by the owner-only init3() via head = tail (Inbox.sol:253-258, called on mainnet in the Proposal0019 bundle, deployments/mainnet-contract-logs-L1.md:222-223). State that migrates (read once at T3, written to the migration/genesis slots 258–260 and — as the genesis checkpoint record — into the L1-07 checkpoint map of slot 264 (§genesis record, MIG-02), then frozen and never re-interpreted): H*; the L1 SignalService checkpoint record for B*; the existing TAIKO ERC-20 balances at the preserved token address 0x10dea67478c5F8C5E2D90e5E9B26dBe60c54d800, which are neither moved nor rewritten (new stake custody is a new L1 staking contract holding TAIKO via transferFrom, MEM-01, D7); the Bridge state nextMessageId (Bridge slot 251) and messageStatus (slot 252); and the vault deposit maps bridgedToCanonical, canonicalToBridged, btokenDenylist, lastMigrationStart (ERC20Vault slots 301–304) and the two NFT vault maps (slots 301–302). State that does not migrate: Shasta proposals that were proposed and never proven — they are not part of any finalized prefix, they wrote no checkpoint and have no bridge effect; pending bond balances in the Inbox ledger (slot 257), which are not carried into the new stake ledger and never count as voting power, but stay claimable (MIG-03); and blob references whose blobs have left Ethereum's retention window (A-DA-3), which cannot be proven and must not be promised. Failure mode: a migration whose bundle can pick the history splits the chain or anchors a non-finalized height; treating an unproven proposal, a legacy bond or an expired blob as migrated state strands user funds or manufactures voting power that no L1 stake backs. Proven (premises: A-CRYPTO-3 for the uniqueness of B*, and A-CONS-1 — < 1/3 Byzantine stake — for the uniqueness of the finalized prefix that the boundary inherits, A-GOV-1 for an honestly executed bundle) sourced

The preserved surfaces named in MIG-01 are the D3 set — SignalService, Bridge, ERC20Vault, ERC721Vault, ERC1155Vault — plus the settlement Inbox and the L2 Anchor, whose exact addresses and in-place-upgrade rule are stated once in MIG-06.

MIG-02 — per-contract upgrade list and storage-layout constraints Each upgraded or preserved proxy's storage is partitioned into (a) a frozen inherited prefix occupying slots 0–250, in which slot 0 holds _initialized/ _initializing, slot 51 _owner, slot 101 _pendingOwner and slot 201 __reentry/__paused, with the ranges 1–50, 52–100, 102–150, 151–200 and 202–250 byte-identical to the generated layouts at the pin; (b) the contract's own region, whose last used slot and trailing gap are fixed by the table below; and (c) nothing else. New state may be declared only after the last used slot of the contract's own region, and it consumes exactly one gap slot per 32 bytes declared (packed variables may share a slot; a declaration crossing a slot boundary consumes two); the trailing gap must shrink by exactly the number of slots consumed and keep the same final slot index (300 for the L1 contracts, 350/400/450/500 for the vaults). Deprecated slots are frozen, not free: they include Anchor._pacayaSlots (252–254), Anchor._lastProposalId (255), SignalService._slotsUsedByPacaya (251–252), Bridge.__reserved1/__ctx/__reserved2/__reserved3 (251, 253, 255, 256), PreconfWhitelist's three deprecated variables, EssentialContract.__reentry (201), and the Shasta _proposalHashes (Inbox 254) and _bondStorage (Inbox 257); a zeroed deprecated slot must not be reused, and Bridge.init2()'s recorded intent to reuse __reserved1/2/3 (Bridge.sol:197-202) is superseded by this rule. If a deployed proxy routes through a ForkRouter, slots 0–150 must remain byte-identical (ForkRouter.sol:18-20) in the router, in the old fork and in the new fork; whether the deployed proxies route through a router must be established on-chain before the bundle (§ Preconditions). The complete change list is the table below; any change not in it requires a new decision recorded in the decision log, and every upgraded contract's layout must be regenerated and diffed (pnpm layout) before and after. Failure mode: a reordered, retyped or reused slot silently corrupts the checkpoint map, the message-status map, the received-signal cache or the vault maps, which is unrecoverable without rewriting user-visible history; carving new state out of the wrong range collides with a previously deployed fork. MIG-03 — treatment of pending proposals, unproven batches, bonds, forced inclusions, bridge messages and withdrawals (1) One settlement path at a time. land(data, proof) reverts unless migrationState == ETNA_ACTIVE; prove() reverts unless migrationState is SHASTA_ACTIVE, FROZEN or DRAINED. No state exists in which both are callable, so at most one path can advance the checkpoint. FROZEN is entered by the migration bundle, which in the same transaction disables propose() and saveForcedInclusion(); states advance only in the order of T1–T3 and no transition is skippable. (2) Proposed but never proven. A proposal with no accepted prove() at the end of the drain window is void: it contributes no checkpoint, no state root and no bridge effect, and its proof path closes permanently at DRAINED. Because propose() is disabled at FROZEN, the proposal-hash ring buffer cannot be overwritten during the window (the overwrite at Inbox.sol:626 happens only inside propose()), so every unproven proposal stays provable until the deadline subject only to data availability (A-DA-2, A-DA-3). The migration announcement must state the drain deadline in L1 block numbers and that L2 transactions inside unproven proposals are not carried over. (3) Bonds. Legacy bond balances (Inbox slot 257) are not converted into stake and confer no voting power. The post-migration implementation retains exactly one legacy selector, withdrawLegacyBond(address _to, uint64 _amount), which debits that ledger under the legacy floor and delay baked at deployment from the pre-migration getConfig() (mainnet minBond = 0, withdrawalDelay = 1 weeks, MainnetInbox.sol:37,39), and it remains callable in every state. No new bond may be created after the freeze and no legacy balance may be slashed. (4) Forced inclusions. Every queue entry still present at FROZEN is either consumed by a Shasta propose() executed before the freeze, with its fee forwarded to that proposer exactly as today (Inbox.sol:710), or refunded in full (feeInGwei × 1 gwei) to the transaction sender of the ForcedInclusionSaved log for that queue index; the payer is not stored in the entry (IForcedInclusionStore.sol:10-15, LibForcedInclusion.sol:59-66) and must be recovered from L1 logs. A permissionless refundForcedInclusion(uint48 index) remains callable in every state. No entry may be voided silently, no init3()-style selector survives, and queued entries are never re-interpreted under the new FI-01 request format. (5) Bridge messages. The migration upgrade must not reset nextMessageId, messageStatus, _receivedSignals or _checkpoints; must not change the SIGNAL slot derivation (SignalService.sol:160-171,232-235); and must not change SignalService.VERSION, because one version bump invalidates the received-signal cache and hides every checkpoint simultaneously (SignalService.sol:52,63,179). An L2→L1 message sent at height ≤ B* stays provable after activation because every post-activation checkpoint is a record of a state that contains its signal; an L1→L2 message stays provable while the Anchor keeps writing L1 checkpoints with blockNumber = L1 block number and monotone _blockState.anchorBlockNumber (Anchor.sol:173-186). A message is processed at most once because its status is set by processMessage, and MIG-01's boundary rule prevents a second history from producing a second proof of receipt. (6) Withdrawals. A withdrawal that was withdrawal-eligible at the freeze remains eligible during and after the migration: eligibility depends on a stored signal and a stored checkpoint, both preserved unchanged, and no delay is extended, restarted or re-based by any transition. Failure modes: two live settlement paths produce two histories; silently voiding forced inclusions repeats the June 2026 failure class; a VERSION bump makes every in-flight message unprovable; counting a legacy bond as stake manufactures voting power no L1 stake backs. Proven sourced (the message-continuity claim also depends on A-CONS-5/A-DA-2 for the availability of the data behind the boundary state)

MIG-04 — removal of operational privileges Every lever in the table below is removed from the runtime ABI of the contract that holds it. Removal is a deliberate behaviour change of the preserved surfaces: D3 preserves addresses, not powers. After the removals, ordinary operation reads no owner, no whitelist, no resolver entry and no pause flag: block production, voting, proving, settlement, forced inclusion and exit complete with no privileged transaction. The only authority that remains is DAO upgrade authority — MainnetDAOController.execute (MainnetDAOController.sol:24-31, itself owned by the TaikoDAO) calling upgradeToAndCall through _authorizeUpgrade (EssentialContract.sol:207) — and it is never a runtime dependency (GOV-01). No runtime authority is created by the migration: the new staking contract, the new verifier route, and the forced-inclusion path are permissionless (MEM-03, L1-04, FI-01), and any owner those new contracts have is upgrade authority only. Where a removal changes incident response (no runtime pause, no runtime image rotation, no runtime token remapping), the consequence is disclosed in the table and the remedy is a DAO upgrade, not a hidden key. Failure mode: a surviving owner function is a capture target and a liveness dependency (R2), and makes the "no privilege or discretion in ordinary operation" invariant (INV-04) false; init2() in particular can rewrite lastFinalizedBlockHash, which Mode A forbids. MIG-05 — program-image and verifier upgrades; mixed-version clients and in-flight proofs The set of program images accepted for an epoch e is the value of the registry (Inbox slot 261, acceptedProgramImage[imageId] = acceptedFromEpoch) at the same L1-committed boundary that fixes the validator-set root for e (MEM-09), so an image change takes effect for epochs that start after the L1 transaction that recorded it. Registry entries are never deleted: an image that was accepted for an epoch remains accepted for that epoch forever (CONS-08). An image may be given a retiredAtEpoch marker, after which it may not be used for batches whose epoch is at or after the marker, but retirement must not invalidate a certificate, a checkpoint or a settled batch. Consequently: a proof generated before an upgrade for a batch in an epoch whose accepted set contains its image remains acceptable after the upgrade and needs no re-proving; a proof whose image is not in the accepted set of the batch's epoch is rejected by the verifier route (L1-09); and land(data, proof) must never reject a valid (data, proof) pair merely because the proof's image was superseded for later epochs. There is no mixed-version consensus window: because the accepted set is a deterministic function of L1-committed state and the upgrade is epoch-gated, all clients that have processed the boundary L1 block derive the same set, and a client that disagrees is producing invalid blocks rather than a second rule set. Both backends must implement the same statement and the same acceptance policy (PRF-09); the guest/verifier version registry itself is defined by PRF-10, which this rule uses and does not restate. Failure modes: a mid-epoch image switch splits which proofs are acceptable and lets an unsettled batch become unprovable; deleting an old image strands already-finalized history; a "mixed-version" leniency silently accepts two rule sets. Proven Assumed (premises: A-CRYPTO-1 soundness applies per image, and the epoch boundary is the L1-committed one of MEM-09; if the registry boundary and the set boundary ever diverge, the split risk in the failure mode above returns)

MIG-06 — no replacement deployments for the preserved surfaces The migration is an in-place implementation upgrade of every proxy in the table below, at the address recorded there. No new contract is deployed to a new address to replace any of them, and no new address is introduced into any interface that a user or a peer contract resolves: the SIGNAL slot derivation, the resolver results for the Bridge and vault peers, and the L1/L2 address libraries are unchanged. The settlement Inbox and the L2 Anchor are upgraded in place too, because the two immutable checkpoint-writer links are wired to those exact addresses — L1 SignalService._authorizedSyncer is the Shasta Inbox (SignalService.sol:35-37,174-175; DeployShastaContracts.s.sol:130) and L2 SignalService._authorizedSyncer is the Anchor proxy (DeployShastaL2Contracts.s.sol:46-53) — and because Anchor.blockHashes (slot 251) is a permanent L2 map that must keep answering getBlockHash. An implementer must know that these immutables are properties of the implementation code, not of proxy storage, so an implementation upgrade can change them; this rule therefore forbids changing them rather than relying on immutability to enforce it. A re-deployed SignalService implementation must be constructed with the same _authorizedSyncer, _remoteSignalService and VERSION values as the implementation it replaces. Verifiers, the L1 staking contract and the prover-reward path are not preserved surfaces: they are new deployments referenced by address from the upgraded Inbox (L1-09). TaikoToken is not deployed, moved or migrated. Failure mode: a replacement deployment changes every address users and peer contracts have integrated, breaks the checkpoint-writer link (after which the preserved SignalService can never be checkpointed again and every cross-chain message stops verifying) and duplicates bridge state across two Bridges. Governance

GOV-01 — DAO governs upgrades only; the trust assumption is stated The DAO's only authority over this protocol is the ability to execute an upgrade: MainnetDAOController.execute(bytes) (onlyOwner, owned by the TaikoDAO; MainnetDAOController.sol:24-31) calling upgradeToAndCall / upgradeTo on the proxies it owns (EssentialContract.sol:207), plus the migration transitions T1–T3 that are delivered as upgrades. After ETNA_ACTIVE no DAO-dependent runtime state may exist: no consensus rule, admission rule, forced-inclusion rule, proof-acceptance rule, exit rule or bridge rule may read a value the DAO can change without an upgrade, and no DAO transaction may be required for ordinary liveness — block production, voting, proving, settlement, forced inclusion and exit must all complete without one. The trust assumption is explicit and is not repairable by the protocol: A-GOV-1 (01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §4.4) states that the DAO does not execute a malicious upgrade; if it does, the safety claims of this specification do not apply to the resulting rule set (failure class F2). For contrast, the prior Etna design recorded exactly two DAO-dependent states — CONFLICT after a provisional landing, and a certificate-free landing during a one-leaf outage (prior-etna-digest.md:113,119); this design has zero. Failure modes: any runtime DAO lever is a capture target and a liveness dependency (R2); a hidden owner flag makes the no-privilege invariant (INV-04) false; describing A-GOV-1 as a guarantee is exactly the non-argument "the DAO would fix it". Proven Assumed (proven relative to the MIG-04 removal table being complete; the residual trust is A-GOV-1, whose failure is GOV-02)
GOV-02 — malicious governance is a separate failure class A malicious or compromised DAO is failure class F2, not a protocol failure: no rule, contract, monitor or evidence object in this design detects, prevents or repairs it, and one upgrade can rewrite any rule — including this one — reassign any storage slot, or remove the staking requirement. The honest responses are exactly three: (i) before-the-fact disclosure, (ii) comparability, i.e. every upgrade bundle is published with the target implementation addresses, the storage-layout diff and the expected post-upgrade boundary object, so any deviation is publicly detectable, and (iii) the ability of users to exit before an announced upgrade takes effect, subject to the delays of MSG-03 and ECON-03. This design does not claim that a DAO upgrade cannot invalidate finalized history; it claims that no rule of this protocol can, and that a governance rewrite is reported as a rules change rather than as a recovery. The Mode A promise (STATUS-04, D2) is made relative to the rules in force, and after a governance rewrite it must be re-established by the new rules. Failure mode: reading A-GOV-1 as a guarantee, or claiming that ZK verification, bonds or a timelock make governance capture safe; the prior Etna digest records the same conclusion ("the DAO is assumed honest; capture is out of scope", prior-etna-digest.md:113). Proven Assumed
GOV-03 — upgrades must not invalidate finalized history Every upgrade, including the migration of MIG-01–MIG-06, must accept all history finalized under the rules in force before it: (a) a certificate valid under the epoch and validator-set rules of its own epoch remains valid forever (CONS-08) and no post-upgrade rule may require it to be re-derived; (b) the post-upgrade verifier route must still accept a proof of any not-yet-settled batch under the program image that was in force for that batch's epoch (MIG-05), so no batch becomes unprovable because of an upgrade; (c) checkpoint advancement stays monotone (L1-06): an upgrade may not lower the finalized tip or rewrite the checkpoint record for any height at or below the boundary with different contents; and (d) at migration the accepted history is the boundary of MIG-01, and no alternative history at or below B* may ever be adopted afterwards (HALT-02). Compliance is checked by the published target/layout diff required by GOV-02 and, for (b), by an acceptance test that lands one already-generated pre-upgrade proof after the upgrade, on both backends (PRF-09, PRF-10). Failure mode: a "normal upgrade" silently strands a proven-but-unsettled batch or rolls the checkpoint back — the Mode A violation D2 forbids and the class recorded as the prior Etna design's XS-FIN-01 finding (prior-etna-digest.md:142). Proven Assumed (premises: CONS-08, L1-06, A-CRYPTO-3, and A-CONS-1 — < 1/3 Byzantine stake — for the uniqueness of the finalized prefix that (a) presupposes; a DAO that ignores this rule is handled by GOV-02, not by this rule)
