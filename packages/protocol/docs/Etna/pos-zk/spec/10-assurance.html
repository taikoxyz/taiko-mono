10 — Invariants, conditional liveness, limitations and rejected alternatives

Safety invariants

INV-01 — unique finalized prefix Under the stated premises, at most one block per height ever reaches STATUS-04, and every finalized block is an ancestor of every later finalized block. Premises: (P1) fewer than one third of the voting power of every epoch is Byzantine (A-CONS-1); (P2) correct validators obey the vote-uniqueness rule (CONS-02) and the lock rule (CONS-04); (P3) the commit rule requires more than two thirds of the epoch's voting power, counted over distinct authenticated signers (CONS-03); (P4) each epoch's set is the L1-committed set (CONS-10, MEM-08); (P5) the epoch-handoff rule carries locks across boundaries (CONS-09). Failure mode: if any premise fails, two conflicting certificates can both be cryptographically valid, and a ZK proof cannot detect it. Both proofs verify. The protocol's response to this class is disclosure, detection through objective evidence where it exists, and slashing — never a claim that the invariant holds. Proven Assumed (argument from the CometBFT Proof of Safety and the fork-accountability lemma, pinned in 02; P5 is review target F1)
INV-02 — no batch is accepted without its data There is no state in which an accepted, checkpoint-advancing L1 record exists without (a) the batch data being retrievable from that same L1 transaction, and (b) a valid proof binding execution to exactly that data. This holds structurally: acceptance happens in one transaction that carries both, and the data commitment is bound by DA-02 or DA-03. No recovery, outage, degraded mode, governance action or operator procedure may create an exception (REC-01 e, L1-02). Failure mode: a data-first design creates exactly the class observed in this repository — init3() (Inbox.sol:246-258) had to void forced inclusions whose blob references expired before they could be derived. That class cannot occur for a pending batch here because data is published only with its proof. Proven (inherited from D5; the in-repo incident is evidence that the alternative is not hypothetical)
INV-03 — no consensus without TAIKO at risk Voting power exists only for stake that is bonded in the L1 staking contract and active in the epoch's set; a key with no eligible stake cannot vote, and a vote is counted only if the signer's stake is in the authenticated set. Slashing follows the stake held at the time of the offence (MEM-06), so exiting after an offence does not escape the penalty. Failure modes addressed: voting power acquired without at-risk capital, and the withdrawal race in which an offender unbonds between the offence and the evidence. Proven
INV-04 — no privilege or discretion in ordinary operation Every step of ordinary operation — activation, proposing, voting, proving, forced inclusion, evidence submission, reward claiming and exit — is reachable by any address satisfying the objective rules, with no allowlist, no owner approval, no DAO vote and no operator key. A privileged lever is permitted only for upgrades, and each such lever is enumerated with its scope in MIG-04. Failure mode: a protocol that claims permissionless operation while a ProverWhitelist or an owner-only init3() can alter who may participate or what queued work survives. Proven Open (open for the operational audit of the completed migration: the rule is normative, but whether every lever has been removed is verified only at migration time)

Conditional liveness

LIVE-01 — the liveness statement and exactly where it ends The following is the complete liveness claim, and it is conditional on every clause. If (L1) more than two thirds of each epoch's voting power is online and message delay between correct validators is below the round-timeout bound for long enough to complete a round; (L2) a correct proposer is eventually selected by rotation when timeouts elapse; (L3) correct validators hold and can serve the data of the blocks they voted for, at least until settlement or the retention window ends; (L4) for every committed batch, at least one adequately-resourced prover completes its proof inside the envelope, or another prover does — this is a per-batch statement, not a mean-rate one: a sustained average that meets the throughput inequality of LIVE-03 does not imply that no individual batch exceeds the envelope (*review round 1, finding R1-09*); (L5) Ethereum includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below the finality depth; (L6) the L1 staking contract commits each epoch's set root before the epoch begins; (L7) a new node can obtain a weak-subjectivity checkpoint within the freshness window; then L2 produces a block every 2 seconds, blocks reach STATUS-04 within the round budget, and committed batches reach STATUS-06 within the proof-queue envelope. Liveness ends at the first clause that fails: (L1) fails → the chain halts rather than forks; (L3) fails → settlement halts, possibly permanently, unless a data holder returns; (L4) fails → settlement halts after the unsettled-depth cap binds; (L5) fails → nothing settles and forced inclusion is unavailable; (L6) fails → the chain cannot enter the next epoch; (L7) fails → a new node must not silently trust an unverified history. There is no clause whose failure licenses rewriting finalized history. Assumed unmeasured (the timeout bound, the retention window and the envelope are parameters whose values require measurement, 09)
LIVE-02 — backpressure and halt triggers Correct validators apply backpressure before liveness is lost, never by discarding committed history: (a) at unsettled depth D_MAX they refuse to vote for further blocks (HALT-03); (b) if the proof queue would exceed the retention window even at D_MAX, D_MAX itself is reduced by a published governance parameter change — an upgrade, not a runtime action; (c) they halt when HALT-01 conditions hold. A normal proof inside the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, 900 blocks at 2 s) must never trigger (a), (b) or any recovery action. Failure mode: backpressure implemented as "drop the oldest unsettled batch", which is a silent Mode B and violates REC-01. Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value and the retention window)
LIVE-03 — throughput sustainability conditions The proving pipeline is sustainable if and only if three inequalities hold, all evaluated with measured inputs before launch: (i) rate: the fleet's sustained proven execution rate is at least the L2's sustained execution rate; (ii) concurrency: the number of proofs in flight is at least L / Δ, where L is the proof latency bound and Δ is the batch's L2 time span (a queueing identity, not a measurement); (iii) data: the L2's data production rate does not exceed the L1 data capacity available to the batches, which for blob data is the blob target/maximum per L1 block divided by the L1 slot duration. If any inequality fails, the backlog grows without bound and the correct response is LIVE-02 backpressure — not a longer latency assumption and not a rollback. Failure modes addressed: "tolerate individual slow proofs" being mistaken for sustainable throughput, and a design that quietly assumes unlimited prover capacity. Every input to these inequalities is unmeasured in this specification; the formulas are the deliverable. Open (requires measurement of cycles per L2 gas and proven cycles per second per machine, 03 §7)

Adversarial review and findings

LIM-03 — red-team finding resolutions Every finding from the adversarial review rounds is recorded in iterations/ with its exact rule or missing rule, preconditions, concrete attack trace, whether the attack is inside the claimed fault model, attacker resources and cost, harm, affected requirements, and either a resolution or an explicit acceptance. A finding is closed only when the original attack trace is retained and the specification states precisely why it no longer succeeds.
