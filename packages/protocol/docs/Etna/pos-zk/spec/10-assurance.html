10 — Invariants, conditional liveness, limitations and rejected alternatives

The v1 guarantee class, in one place. v1 guarantees the following and nothing more. (1) The checkpoint boundary: nothing at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is ever rewritten, and a confirmation above it is provisional (L1-07). (2) The exit from the last settled state: every L2→L1 message at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint stays withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 block, no quorum and no validator (MEM-15) — while both dependencies that MEM-15(2b) assumes hold: a funded proving market produces the k−1 attestation proofs of L1-13(5), and the inputs those proofs need are still retrievable for an accepted checkpoint at or above the signal's height: the recorded certificate, the batch's data and the pre-state witness (MEM-15(2a)); an unfunded or absent market, or inputs no longer served (expired blobs and no archive at the height; A-CONS-5/A-DA-2, DA-05), leaves a user at or below the last accepted checkpoint unable to exit, which is that clause's named falsifier. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the guarantee class states the exit's proving-market dependency instead of presenting the exit as unconditional. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F4: the guarantee class names the witness dependency with the funding, so the falsifier is no longer funding-only. (3) The withdrawal root: a root requires attestations from at least k distinct registered backend families (L1-13), plus the rule-triggered, self-expiring withdrawal veto (MSG-04), and its k−1 attach proofs are funded proving work (L1-13(5)), so root formation carries the proving-market dependency of clause (2). This closes review round 7 finding F1: root formation is stated as funded proving work rather than as an unattended L1 step. What that funding actually guarantees, stated exactly: the proving share is claimed through the single ledger of L1-11, whose landing draw and attestation draw are two unranked debits of one balance; the split between landing work and attestation work is fixed by the recorded allocation policy of ECON-02 clause 5 and is unmeasured (09), and no sub-accounting, reservation or ordering rule makes it true on-chain, so either draw may be starved when the ledger is short: a burst of attestations can pay landers zero (the batch still lands), and a landing can consume the balance an attach proof expected. Both payments are best-effort and never gate (L1-13(5)), so the share funds both kinds of proving work as a policy, not as an assured budget. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F3: the guarantee class discloses that the split is fixed by the recorded policy and unmeasured, that no rule enforces it, and that either draw on L1-11's single ledger may be starved, instead of implying both are assured. Two mechanisms remain deferred and their guarantees are absent — the heartbeat's rotation (CONS-16, gated on its missing h_close referent, falsifier F7) and aggregation: the governance stall resolution is instead live as the protocol's one runtime governance action (increment 05) — the DAO may queue a single timelocked entry once settlement has stalled past its trigger, a permissionless execute() consumes it exactly once, and it discards only history strictly above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint. It is not an exit guarantee for value above that checkpoint, and no rule obliges governance to queue or execute it, so a stall it does not act on still halts the chain; and settlement accepts one proof from one registered backend, so its soundness is that of the weakest approved backend. Narrow forced inclusion is no longer absent either: increment 04 revives FI-10–FI-14 over published data — the capped FIFO prefix in positions per batch, the unconditional frontier advance and the three resolution modes (executed, void, dead), walked per transaction in the record's own order: each transaction executes or is discharged at its turn, a live record every one of whose transactions is discharged is void, a record past its deadline is dead, and the walk is total so no published record can pin the frontier — enforced in the proof and never as an admission gate. (R4R1-M-01: the resolution ground is per transaction and the walk is total, so no position in the window can be left unresolved.) The discharge ground cannot be manufactured, and the producer-set inputs that steer it are disclosed: a producer cannot alter a record's bytes or the order its transactions are walked, and only the sender's nonce is producer-immovable, but the batch's executed state is the producer's choice, so where a third-party credit lands relative to a turn can decide whether an unaffordable transaction is rescued and must execute (credit before the turn) or is discharged as unaffordable (credit after it) — the one producer-set input that can decide a discharge, disclosed as the residual F-FI-3. (correction: FI-13(3) claims non-manufacturability with its producer-set steering edges disclosed, not non-steerability or producer-independence) It is not a latency guarantee, and its guarantee is conditional: it holds only while arrivals stay within the forceable drain (F-FI-2, open and unfixed) and while at least one honest or rational producer lands batches (F-FI-5); it bounds exclusion per unit of a censor's L1 spending, and an L1-censored publication cannot be forced at all. The other falsifiers — F-FI-1, F-FI-3, F-FI-4, F-FI-6, F-FI-7 and F-FI-8 — are carried in LIM-01 and LIVE-04: F-FI-7 is the fee floor's schedule premise — the registered floor FI_MIN_EXEC_FEE_CAP (the fifth byte-invalid class of FI-13(1)(b)(E) and the (vi) requirement of FI-13(2)) is at or above the maximum execution base fee the L2 fee schedule can produce, with no registered rule maintaining it — because a schedule that can exceed the floor makes a transaction the predicate calls forceable — and the per-block duty of FI-11(4) therefore demands — one no valid block can carry: the same settlement-halt class the fee-floor repair closed, so settlement freezes until the record expires, and F-FI-8 is the enumeration residual: the predicate decides the record's own bytes and the turn pre-state it fixes, and an execution-validity rule a valid block enforces that is neither one of the enumerated classes (A)–(G) nor decided by that pre-state is carried Open rather than denied, so a transaction the predicate calls forceable and the discharge ground does not discharge at its turn that no valid block of the range can carry would leave the walk demanding an execution that cannot happen and the position unresolved — the settlement-halt class the enumerated repairs closed. (review round 4, finding F1: F-FI-7 joins the falsifier list with the fee floor and the fifth byte class.) Heartbeat eligibility is no longer absent: increment 02 revives MEM-13, so an entry that stops signing is excluded from set versions whose fixed evaluation window (MEM-13(3)) follows the window it last named by more than one (bounded by the pre-signing residual, falsifier F9, whose second consequence is an all-ineligible append window: commitSet() reverts, the append is missed and the MEM-09(5) boundary halt is reachable (MEM-13(3)/(7)(e)), a liveness failure v1 does not repair because the rotation stays gated) (INC2-RD-02: the guarantee class now names both F9 consequences.) — it is never decayed or slashed and re-attesting restores it — including for an append that is still pending — but exclusion changes only future composition: a version already committed is not repaired, a cohort that keeps signing keeps its weight (declared non-fix, falsifier F8), and a quorum-loss halt ends with no protocol change when the committed cohort returns to vote and is permanent only while it never does, because the epoch containing the next unfinalised height has an immutable committed roster and no rule may skip or re-partition a height (MEM-13 states this, and the stake/exit asymmetry, in one place). The deferred mechanisms, their findings and their revive criteria are in DEFERRED.md; the rotation CONS-16 stays a gated tombstone, the aggregation ids stay tombstones, and the governance stall resolution of GOV-04 is live (increment 05), so every remaining tombstone MUST NOT be implemented. Safety invariants

INV-01 — unique certified prefix, relative to the authenticated checkpoint Under the stated premises, at most one block per height per generation receives a valid commit certificate, and a certificate of a superseded recovery generation is void at the acceptance rule; within one generation every such block is an ancestor of every later such block, so the uniqueness of the finalized prefix holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules (CONS-02, CONS-03, CONS-04). The unconditional "at most one block per height ever" form was deleted because a stall resolution deliberately produces two valid, forever-valid certificates at the same height, one of them under a superseded recovery generation (CONS-08, GOV-04(f)). The generation-scoped form is the operative invariant (CONS-12): the resolution is live, the generation advances by exactly one per executed entry and by no other path, no rule produces a second valid certificate at a produced height within one generation, and heights above the last accepted checkpoint may be re-produced under a new generation while heights at or below it may not be changed by any path. A certificate whose signed generation has been superseded is void at the acceptance rule, and the signed recovery generation still scopes certificates, locks and uniqueness (CONS-08, PRF-02). *(increment 05: the deferred reading is withdrawn and the generation-scoped invariant is live.)* The rotation of CONS-16 would have been the second, narrower event of the same class: it would re-partition only heights that no validator produced, it would never re-judge a produced height, and each height is judged only under the epoch the L1-committed boundary record assigns it; because a valid certificate entails that its height was produced and finalized (CONS-05), no certificate can exist for a re-partitioned height, so CONS-08(3) is not weakened — but the rotation is deferred by D-16, is not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §2); the only history-moving event this design has is the executed stall resolution above the checkpoint: the rotation re-partitions nothing, no offending certificate can arise from it, and the Open precondition that no produced height is re-judged (falsifier F7) is carried in the register with the mechanism rather than as a live obligation. *(increment 05: the resolution is the one live history-moving event; the rotation stays gated.)* This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the rotation is stated as deferred, not as a completed second event of the class. (S5 §6: INV-01 is restated generation-scoped exactly as CONS-12 now is; the unconditional claim is deleted.) Relative to the authenticated checkpoint (L1-07): history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can never be changed by any protocol path, and at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it (STATUS-07); above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint every confirmation is provisional, and the one protocol path that replaces it is the executed stall resolution of GOV-04 — a DAO-queued entry under its trigger, a stored window, a permissionless execution that consumes the entry once, discarding only that provisional range — while the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15, so a stall governance does not act on persists. *(increment 05)* No timeout, rotation, admission rule, upgrade, operator action or failure of a liveness assumption may replace history above the checkpoint: the executed stall resolution is the only exception, and a stall governance does not act on halts the chain rather than rewriting it; the last settled state remains available for exit (MEM-15), subject to the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b): the exit's k−1 attestation proofs are funded proving work, and without them no root forms. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the last settled state's availability is stated with its proving-market dependency. This closes finding R2: the selective-rollback outcome claim is replaced by the wholesale form any replacement must have — the executed stall resolution discards the whole provisional range, no other replacement path exists, and no subset chosen by anyone may be discarded. *(increment 05)* Replacement is never justified by execution validity alone: the proof statement binds the configuration under which the batch was certified and carries the recovery generation (PRF-02), so an alternative history cannot be accepted merely because its execution is valid. A certificate produced under a superseded generation is void for canonical purposes but remains admissible as evidence (GOV-04(f), deferred by D-16; DEFERRED.md §3). Premises: (P1) fewer than one third of the voting power of every epoch is Byzantine (A-CONS-1); (P2) correct validators obey the vote-uniqueness rule (CONS-02) and the lock rule (CONS-04); (P3) the commit rule requires more than two thirds of the epoch's voting power, counted over distinct authenticated signers (CONS-03); (P4) each epoch's set is the L1-committed set (CONS-10, MEM-08); (P5) the per-height lock of CONS-04 is enforced, and cross-height continuity is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii) — no lock state crosses an epoch boundary, because the lock is per height (*review round 2, finding R2A-10*) (CONS-09). Failure mode: reading the certificate as the guarantee. The certificate is the same evidence in every mode, so a reader — or an interface — can conclude that a confirmation above the checkpoint is permanent; it is not, and no rule makes it permanent: the one path that replaces the provisional range is the bounded stall resolution, a stall governance does not act on leaves the chain halted, and nothing obliges governance to act. The other half matters equally: no premise failure and no other mechanism licenses a rollback, and if any premise fails, two conflicting certificates can both be cryptographically valid while a ZK proof cannot detect it. Both proofs verify. The protocol's response to that class remains disclosure, detection through objective evidence where it exists, and slashing — never a claim that the invariant holds. Proven Assumed (argument from the CometBFT Proof of Safety and the fork-accountability lemma, pinned in 02; P5 is review target F1) (increment 05: uniqueness holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules and within one recovery generation; above the last L1-accepted checkpoint every confirmation is provisional and the one path that replaces it is the executed stall resolution of GOV-04, which discards only that range and cannot touch anything at or below the checkpoint)
INV-02 — no batch is accepted without its data There is no state in which an accepted, checkpoint-advancing L1 record exists without (a) the batch data being retrievable from the transaction the accepted statement binds — the accepting transaction when the data is carried, the live publication record when it is referenced (DA-01, DA-08) — and (b) a valid proof binding execution to exactly that data. This holds structurally: acceptance happens in one transaction whose proof the Inbox verifies, and the data commitment is bound by DA-02 or DA-03. A publication advances no checkpoint and is not a batch record, so the data-first admission path remains excluded (L1-02). No outage, degraded mode, governance action or operator procedure may create an exception: the one named governance action — the executed stall resolution of GOV-04 — discards only history strictly above the latest accepted checkpoint, accepts no batch without data and manufactures no checkpoint (REC-01 e, L1-02), so this invariant has no exception. (increment 05: the bounded resolution creates no exception to INV-02.) Failure mode: a data-first admission design creates exactly the class observed in this repository — init3() (Inbox.sol:246-258) had to void queued requests whose blob references expired before they could be derived. That class is controlled here by DA-09's proving deadline: a pending batch exists only as a publication record bounded by that deadline, never as an advanceable checkpoint. Proven (D5 as relaxed by D-11: data may be published before the proof, and acceptance still happens only with a valid proof that binds it; the in-repo incident is evidence that the alternative is not hypothetical)
INV-03 — no consensus without TAIKO at risk Voting power exists only for stake that is bonded in the L1 staking contract and active in the epoch's set; a key with no eligible stake cannot vote, and a vote is counted only if the signer's stake is in the authenticated set. Slashing follows the stake held at the time of the offence (MEM-06), so exiting after an offence does not escape the penalty. Failure modes addressed: voting power acquired without at-risk capital, and the withdrawal race in which an offender unbonds between the offence and the evidence. Proven
INV-04 — no privilege or discretion in ordinary operation Every step of ordinary operation — activation, proposing, voting, proving, evidence submission, reward claiming and exit — is reachable by any address satisfying the objective rules, with no allowlist, no owner approval, no DAO vote and no operator key. A privileged lever is permitted only for upgrades, and each such lever is enumerated with its scope in MIG-04. Failure mode: a protocol that claims permissionless operation while a ProverWhitelist or an owner-only init3() can alter who may participate or what queued work survives. Proven Open (open for the operational audit of the completed migration: the rule is normative, but whether every lever has been removed is verified only at migration time)

Conditional liveness

LIVE-01 — the liveness statement and exactly where it ends The following is the complete liveness claim, and it is conditional on every clause; the exit is stated separately below with its own assumption, which is not one of (L1)–(L7). If (L1) more than two thirds of each epoch's voting power is online and message delay between correct validators is below the round-timeout bound for long enough to complete a round; (L2) a correct proposer is eventually selected by rotation when timeouts elapse; (L3) correct validators hold and can serve the data of the blocks they voted for, at least until settlement or the retention window ends; (L4) for every committed batch, at least one adequately-resourced prover completes its proof inside the envelope, or another prover does — this is a per-batch statement, not a mean-rate one: a sustained average that meets the throughput inequality of LIVE-03 does not imply that no individual batch exceeds the envelope (*review round 1, finding R1-09*); (L5) Ethereum includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below the finality depth; (L6) the L1 staking contract commits each epoch's set root before the epoch begins; (L7) a new node can obtain a weak-subjectivity checkpoint within the freshness window; then L2 produces a block every 2 seconds, blocks reach STATUS-04 within the round budget, and committed batches reach STATUS-06 within the proof-queue envelope. A settlement stall is cleared by exactly one named action: the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04 — a DAO-queued entry under its trigger, a stored window, a permissionless execution that consumes the entry exactly once — and by nothing else; the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15. When settlement stalls, the chain halts safely and funds at or below the last L1-accepted checkpoint remain withdrawable while the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b) forms the root; if governance does not queue an entry, or queues one that no one executes, the stall persists (F-GOV-1, LIM-01). *(increment 05)* Not conditional on (L1), (L2) or (L6), and its own dependencies are named. The exit needs no new L2 block, no settlement progress, no quorum and no permission — it still rides on Ethereum's own liveness (A-L1-1, the L1-inclusion half of (L5)) — so every L2→L1 message included at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint stays withdrawable on L1 with no consensus participation and no validator cooperation (MEM-15). It is not independent of proving or of retention, however: (L4) is the prover-completion assumption, the exit's k−1 attestation proofs are funded proving work (L1-13(5)) that needs the recorded certificate, the batch's data and the pre-state witness of an accepted checkpoint at or above the signal's height (MEM-15(2a)), and the exit therefore depends on both dependencies MEM-15(2b) names: the funded proving market and the retained witness — an unfunded or absent proving market, or inputs no longer retrievable (expired blobs and no archive serving the height; A-CONS-5/A-DA-2, DA-05), leaves a user at or below the last accepted checkpoint unable to exit, which is that clause's falsifier. Settlement can keep raising that checkpoint over already-certified heights while production is halted only if the data is retained and a proof is produced (MEM-15(3)). (PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2(c): the exit path is a liveness guarantee in its own right, not a conditional clause.) This closes review round 7 finding F1: the "independent of every clause" claim is replaced by the exit's exact scope — no new block, no settlement progress, no quorum, no permission, but a funded proving market. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F4: LIVE-01's named dependency list carries the witness with the funding. Liveness ends at the first clause that fails. LIVE-02 — backpressure and halt triggers Correct validators apply backpressure before liveness is lost, never by discarding committed history: (a) at unsettled depth D_MAX they refuse to vote for further blocks (HALT-03); (b) if the proof queue would exceed the retention window even at D_MAX, D_MAX itself is reduced by a published governance parameter change — an upgrade, not a runtime action; (c) they halt when HALT-01 conditions hold. A normal proof inside the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, 900 blocks at 2 s) must never trigger (a) or (b). Failure mode: backpressure implemented as "drop the oldest unsettled batch", which is an unsanctioned rollback — nothing replaces history at or below the checkpoint, and the only replacement above it is the executed stall resolution of GOV-04, which no producer can invoke and which discards the whole provisional range — and therefore violates REC-01. (increment 05: the only sanctioned replacement is the executed resolution above the checkpoint; the boundary of REC-01 stands unchanged) Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value and the retention window)
LIVE-03 — throughput sustainability conditions The proving pipeline is sustainable if and only if three inequalities hold, all evaluated with measured inputs before launch: (i) rate: the fleet's sustained proven execution rate is at least the L2's sustained execution rate; (ii) concurrency: the number of proofs in flight is at least L / Δ, where L is the proof latency bound and Δ is the batch's L2 time span (a queueing identity, not a measurement); (iii) data: the L2's data production rate does not exceed the L1 data capacity available to the batches, which for blob data is the blob target/maximum per L1 block divided by the L1 slot duration. If any inequality fails, the backlog grows without bound and the correct response is LIVE-02 backpressure — not a longer latency assumption and not a rollback. Failure modes addressed: "tolerate individual slow proofs" being mistaken for sustainable throughput, and a design that quietly assumes unlimited prover capacity. Every input to these inequalities is unmeasured in this specification; the formulas are the deliverable. Open (requires measurement of cycles per L2 gas and proven cycles per second per machine, 03 §7)
LIVE-04 — censorship resistance, stated honestly Censorship resistance has two parts, and neither is an unconditional per-transaction guarantee. (1) Published data: the narrow inclusion obligation is live. Increment 04 revives the L1-anchored forced-inclusion rules (FI-10–FI-14, decision D-12) in narrow form. From a publication record's due point (record.l1BlockNumber + FI_INCLUSION_DELAY ≤ A) every accepted batch MUST resolve the capped FIFO prefix of the due set in positions per batch, each position executed, void or dead — a live record voids from its own bytes under the enumerated classes of FI-13(1)(b): decode failure, a chain-id mismatch, an unrecoverable signature, a declared gas limit below the transaction's own intrinsic gas, a declared maxFeePerGas below the registered floor FI_MIN_EXEC_FEE_CAP (FI-13(1)(b)(E)), a malformed fee market and an oversized create initcode (FI-13(1)(b)(F)–(G)), mirrored as the predicate's own byte-decidable requirements so no demanded transaction is one no valid block can carry for a reason this predicate or the enumerated classes (A)–(G) cover — the residual the enumeration does not decide is carried Open as F-FI-8 (review round 4, finding F1: the fifth byte class is the registered fee floor, whose Open schedule premise is F-FI-7; finding S-01: the enumeration residual is F-FI-8, carried Open rather than denied); the frontier advance c' ≥ min(d(A), c + FI_MAX_PER_BATCH) is unconditional and nothing is waivable, and enforcement is in the proof (PRF-04(vi)), never an admission gate: land rejects nothing because the register is non-empty, and a validator signing a block that violates FI-11(4) is subject to no new offence — the rejected proof is the whole enforcement. This is not a latency guarantee. Resistance is the conditional statistical property described here. Proposer selection is hash-based and weighted (CONS-06), and it is a public deterministic function (CONS-06(d)), so it is fully predictable: a coalition can identify in advance the slot in which a target transaction would be included, and buying or coercing the scheduled proposer of a single slot is possible and cheap — no rule prices, detects or penalises slot-buying, and this specification assumes no cost floor that prevents it. What bounds sustained censorship is the selection share, not unpredictability: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it. That last clause is a behavioural premise, not a protocol rule, and it is the honest limit of this resistance for unpublished transactions: no inclusion duty attaches to a validator for them (ROLE-01(b)), and the omission of a transaction that was never published is neither a proposal-validity failure nor an offence (CONS-01, ECON-04), and no rule defines "room", reserves block space or constrains proposer ordering. Under A-CONS-2 the statistical half is real but conditional.
