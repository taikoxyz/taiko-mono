10 — Invariants, conditional liveness, limitations and rejected alternatives

Safety invariants

INV-01 — unique certified prefix, relative to the authenticated checkpoint Under the stated premises, at most one block per height per generation receives a valid commit certificate, and a retired height can never be re-certified; within one generation every such block is an ancestor of every later such block, so the uniqueness of the finalized prefix holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules (CONS-02, CONS-03, CONS-04). The unconditional "at most one block per height ever" form is deleted because a completed recovery deliberately produces two valid, forever-valid certificates at the same height, one of them at a retired height (CONS-08, REC-02). A completed rotation of CONS-16 is the second, narrower event of the same class: it re-partitions only heights that no validator produced, it never re-judges a produced height, and each height is judged only under the epoch the L1-committed boundary record assigns it; because a valid certificate entails that its height was produced and finalized (CONS-05), no certificate can exist for a re-partitioned height, so CONS-08(3) is not weakened by the re-partition itself; if the precondition is violated the offending certificate is void for canonical purposes and remains admissible as evidence (CONS-12, CONS-16(4); the precondition that no produced height is re-judged is Open, falsifier F7). (PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2(a): the rotation is the second, narrower event of the generation class.) (S5 §6: INV-01 is restated generation-scoped exactly as CONS-12 now is; the unconditional claim is deleted.) Relative to the authenticated checkpoint (L1-07): history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can never be changed by recovery, and at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it (STATUS-07); above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint the certified history is canonical except that it may be replaced in whole by the sanctioned recovery of REC-02 — and by nothing else, with that rule's exact objective trigger (a settlement stall of T_STALL), its bond, its delay T_RECOVERY_DELAY, and cancellation by honest progress. The exact replacement window and the restored checkpoint are owned by REC-01 and REC-02: the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is restored exactly and the contiguous range above it is rewound wholesale — the invoker has no choice about which transactions inside that range survive, so a recovery cannot be used to pick winners. That is a statement about the rewind, not a promise about the outcome: the unwound transactions are not replayed automatically and their inclusion is not guaranteed, so every party whose transaction was unwound must resubmit it, and the design does not promise that the same set of transactions will end up included. No timeout, rotation, admission rule, governance upgrade, operator action or failure of a liveness assumption may replace history. This closes finding R2: the selective-rollback outcome claim is replaced by the enforceable wholesale-rewind property and the resubmission consequence. Replacement is never justified by execution validity alone: the proof statement binds the configuration under which the batch was certified and carries the recovery authorization (PRF-02), so an alternative history cannot be accepted merely because its execution is valid. A certificate produced before a recovery is void for canonical purposes above the restored checkpoint but remains admissible as evidence (REC-02). Premises: (P1) fewer than one third of the voting power of every epoch is Byzantine (A-CONS-1); (P2) correct validators obey the vote-uniqueness rule (CONS-02) and the lock rule (CONS-04); (P3) the commit rule requires more than two thirds of the epoch's voting power, counted over distinct authenticated signers (CONS-03); (P4) each epoch's set is the L1-committed set (CONS-10, MEM-08); (P5) the per-height lock of CONS-04 is enforced, and cross-height continuity is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii) — no lock state crosses an epoch boundary, because the lock is per height (*review round 2, finding R2A-10*) (CONS-09). Failure mode: reading the certificate as the guarantee. The certificate is the same evidence in every mode, so a reader — or an interface — can conclude that a confirmation above the checkpoint is permanent; it is not, because the sanctioned recovery may replace that history. The other half matters equally: no premise failure and no other mechanism licenses a rollback, and if any premise fails, two conflicting certificates can both be cryptographically valid while a ZK proof cannot detect it. Both proofs verify. The protocol's response to that class remains disclosure, detection through objective evidence where it exists, and slashing — never a claim that the invariant holds. Proven Assumed (argument from the CometBFT Proof of Safety and the fork-accountability lemma, pinned in 02; P5 is review target F1) (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — uniqueness holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules, and above the last L1-accepted checkpoint the canonical history may be replaced only by the sanctioned recovery)
INV-02 — no batch is accepted without its data There is no state in which an accepted, checkpoint-advancing L1 record exists without (a) the batch data being retrievable from the transaction the accepted statement binds — the accepting transaction when the data is carried, the live publication record when it is referenced (DA-01, DA-08) — and (b) a valid proof binding execution to exactly that data. This holds structurally: acceptance happens in one transaction whose proof the Inbox verifies, and the data commitment is bound by DA-02 or DA-03. A publication advances no checkpoint and is not a batch record, so the data-first admission path remains excluded (L1-02). No recovery, outage, degraded mode, governance action or operator procedure may create an exception (REC-01 e, L1-02). Failure mode: a data-first admission design creates exactly the class observed in this repository — init3() (Inbox.sol:246-258) had to void queued requests whose blob references expired before they could be derived. That class is controlled here by DA-09's proving deadline: a pending batch exists only as a publication record bounded by that deadline, never as an advanceable checkpoint. Proven (D5 as relaxed by D-11: data may be published before the proof, and acceptance still happens only with a valid proof that binds it; the in-repo incident is evidence that the alternative is not hypothetical)
INV-03 — no consensus without TAIKO at risk Voting power exists only for stake that is bonded in the L1 staking contract and active in the epoch's set; a key with no eligible stake cannot vote, and a vote is counted only if the signer's stake is in the authenticated set. Slashing follows the stake held at the time of the offence (MEM-06), so exiting after an offence does not escape the penalty. Failure modes addressed: voting power acquired without at-risk capital, and the withdrawal race in which an offender unbonds between the offence and the evidence. Proven
INV-04 — no privilege or discretion in ordinary operation Every step of ordinary operation — activation, proposing, voting, proving, evidence submission, reward claiming and exit — is reachable by any address satisfying the objective rules, with no allowlist, no owner approval, no DAO vote and no operator key. A privileged lever is permitted only for upgrades, and each such lever is enumerated with its scope in MIG-04. Failure mode: a protocol that claims permissionless operation while a ProverWhitelist or an owner-only init3() can alter who may participate or what queued work survives. Proven Open (open for the operational audit of the completed migration: the rule is normative, but whether every lever has been removed is verified only at migration time)

Conditional liveness

LIVE-01 — the liveness statement and exactly where it ends The following is the complete liveness claim, and it is conditional on every clause. If (L1) more than two thirds of each epoch's voting power is online and message delay between correct validators is below the round-timeout bound for long enough to complete a round; (L2) a correct proposer is eventually selected by rotation when timeouts elapse; (L3) correct validators hold and can serve the data of the blocks they voted for, at least until settlement or the retention window ends; (L4) for every committed batch, at least one adequately-resourced prover completes its proof inside the envelope, or another prover does — this is a per-batch statement, not a mean-rate one: a sustained average that meets the throughput inequality of LIVE-03 does not imply that no individual batch exceeds the envelope (*review round 1, finding R1-09*); (L5) Ethereum includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below the finality depth; (L6) the L1 staking contract commits each epoch's set root before the epoch begins; (L7) a new node can obtain a weak-subjectivity checkpoint within the freshness window; then L2 produces a block every 2 seconds, blocks reach STATUS-04 within the round budget, and committed batches reach STATUS-06 within the proof-queue envelope. A settlement stall is no longer terminal by itself: after T_STALL the sanctioned recovery of REC-02 may replace the unsettled history above the last L1-accepted checkpoint, and the chain resumes from the restored checkpoint; until that recovery completes, the chain is halted. Independent of every clause above, and therefore not conditional on (L1), (L2), (L4) or (L6): every L2→L1 message included at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint stays withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 block, no consensus participation, no quorum and no validator cooperation (MEM-15), and settlement can keep raising that checkpoint over already-certified heights while production is halted. (PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2(c): the exit path is a liveness guarantee in its own right, not a conditional clause.) Liveness ends at the first clause that fails. Where it ends has changed (D-7): (L1) fails → the chain halts rather than forks; (L3) fails for data above the last L1-accepted checkpoint → the chain halts, and the sanctioned recovery is the only rule that restores progress, by discarding that range — no rule treats the data as available; (L4) fails → settlement halts after the unsettled-depth cap binds, and after T_STALL the recovery may replace the unsettled range, so the stall is no longer terminal by itself; (L5) fails → nothing settles and no L1 inclusion path can restore progress (LIVE-04), and recovery is not an inclusion remedy; (L6) fails → the chain cannot enter the next epoch, and recovery cannot manufacture the missing L1 set commitment, so that halt persists until the entry is appended and Ethereum-final; (L7) fails → a new node must not silently trust an unverified history. A failed or cancelled recovery returns the chain to the halted state. Offline validators specifically — the case review comment 6011464863 item 2 raised — split into two classes and the difference matters: a quorum lost to attrition (weight the L1 had already stopped observing before the stall) is bounded by the decay of MEM-13 and the rotation of CONS-16, conditionally on their Open items; a simultaneous stop of one third or more has no production-time bound, because L1 cannot tell that cohort from one that is online (falsifier F6). In both classes, value at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint remains withdrawable on L1 with no L2 liveness (MEM-15); the exact statement is LIVE-05. There is no clause whose failure licenses rewriting history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and above it a failure licenses nothing either: only the exact trigger, bond, delay and cancellation of REC-02 do. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — a settlement stall is no longer terminal; the halt conditions recovery cannot clear remain) Assumed unmeasured (the timeout bound, the retention window and the envelope are parameters whose values require measurement, 09)
LIVE-02 — backpressure and halt triggers Correct validators apply backpressure before liveness is lost, never by discarding committed history: (a) at unsettled depth D_MAX they refuse to vote for further blocks (HALT-03); (b) if the proof queue would exceed the retention window even at D_MAX, D_MAX itself is reduced by a published governance parameter change — an upgrade, not a runtime action; (c) they halt when HALT-01 conditions hold. A normal proof inside the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, 900 blocks at 2 s) must never trigger (a), (b) or any recovery action. Failure mode: backpressure implemented as "drop the oldest unsettled batch", which is an unsanctioned rollback — not the recovery of REC-02, with its objective trigger, bond, delay and cancellation — and therefore violates REC-01. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — only the sanctioned recovery may replace history, within the boundary owned by REC-01 and REC-02) Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value and the retention window)
LIVE-03 — throughput sustainability conditions The proving pipeline is sustainable if and only if three inequalities hold, all evaluated with measured inputs before launch: (i) rate: the fleet's sustained proven execution rate is at least the L2's sustained execution rate; (ii) concurrency: the number of proofs in flight is at least L / Δ, where L is the proof latency bound and Δ is the batch's L2 time span (a queueing identity, not a measurement); (iii) data: the L2's data production rate does not exceed the L1 data capacity available to the batches, which for blob data is the blob target/maximum per L1 block divided by the L1 slot duration. If any inequality fails, the backlog grows without bound and the correct response is LIVE-02 backpressure — not a longer latency assumption and not a rollback. Failure modes addressed: "tolerate individual slow proofs" being mistaken for sustainable throughput, and a design that quietly assumes unlimited prover capacity. Every input to these inequalities is unmeasured in this specification; the formulas are the deliverable. Open (requires measurement of cycles per L2 gas and proven cycles per second per machine, 03 §7)
LIVE-04 — censorship resistance, stated honestly Censorship resistance has two parts, and neither is a per-transaction guarantee. (1) Published data. The protocol provides a narrow, L1-anchored inclusion obligation: any account may publish a transaction's data (DA-07), and from the record's due point a batch must resolve it by the capped FIFO prefix of the due set or discharge it as void on FI-13's objective grounds (FI-10–FI-13), enforced at the proof (FI-11) and never by an admission gate on land. Omission of a due record within the required prefix is a proposal-validity failure (CONS-01(v)) and a breach (ECON-04 clause (6), ECON-13). The obligation is an upper bound on exclusion, never a lower bound on inclusion: it fixes no height or time by which inclusion must begin, a backlog drains over successive batches by the cap, and it reaches only data that reached L1. No general inclusion list exists: there is no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow and no forced-inclusion fee, and no L1 entry point for unpublished data (FI-PLANNED-01). (2) Unpublished data. For a transaction whose data never reaches L1, resistance is the conditional statistical property described here. Proposer selection is hash-based and weighted (CONS-06), and it is a public deterministic function (CONS-06(d)), so it is fully predictable: a coalition can identify in advance the slot in which a target transaction would be included, and buying or coercing the scheduled proposer of a single slot is possible and cheap — no rule prices, detects or penalises slot-buying, and this specification assumes no cost floor that prevents it. What bounds sustained censorship is the selection share, not unpredictability: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it. That last clause is a behavioural premise, not a protocol rule, and it is the honest limit of this resistance: no inclusion duty attaches to a validator for unpublished data (ROLE-01(b)), and omission outside the narrow rule's due prefix is neither a proposal-validity failure nor an offence (CONS-01, ECON-04), and no rule defines "room", reserves block space or constrains proposer ordering. Under A-CONS-2 the statistical half is real but conditional. A network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can prevent the data from ever being published — and so can an adversary able to censor L1 inclusion itself (A-L1-1) — and because a withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, such an adversary can also prevent the user from starting a withdrawal. The protocol offers no remedy against either, and a partial relaxation of R10 is disclosed. The recovery of REC-02 is not an inclusion remedy: it replaces unsettled history above the last L1-accepted checkpoint, it cannot include a transaction, it does not compensate an exclusion, and it gives no user an L1 path into L2. (user decisions D-12 and D-11: narrow forced inclusion over published data ships in v1 and supersedes D-6/D-10; D-11 supplies the L1 anchor the narrow rule reads. D-7: recovery is selected, and it is not a substitute for inclusion.) This closes review round 3 finding R3-CE-05: selection is stated to be fully predictable and slot-buying possible and cheap, and the resistance is bounded by the selection share plus the honest-proposer behaviour that ROLE-01(b) expressly does not impose. Failure mode: presenting statistical inclusion under A-CONS-2 as a per-transaction guarantee, or implying an L1 escape path that does not exist, would let a user size exposure on a promise the protocol never makes. Proven Assumed (the absence of an L1 path is structural; the resistance claim is conditional on A-CONS-2)
LIVE-05 — halts caused by offline validators: escapability, time bounds, and what remains unbounded This rule states, for each way validators can stop the chain without being Byzantine, exactly what a user can still do and what the protocol does not bound. It adds no mechanism: the mechanisms are MEM-13 (inactivity decay and lapse), CONS-16 (L1-time-keyed rotation), MEM-15 (exit from the last settled state) and REC-02 (recovery), and each bound below is exactly as conditional as the rule it comes from.

What is claimed, exactly. (i) A halt caused by offline validators no longer traps value that is already settled: every message at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 blocks, no quorum and no validator (MEM-15), and this part needs only Ethereum's liveness, not the L2's. (ii) That path does not depend on recovery, on the rotation, or on any validator returning. (iii) The halt's production consequence is time-bounded for the settlement-stall and attrition classes, conditionally on the Open items those rules name (F5, F6, F7 and REC-03's own reachability). (iv) It is not bounded for a simultaneous stop of one third or more (F6). What must never be written: that an offline third can no longer hold the chain; that every halt is time-bounded; that value above the last accepted checkpoint can always leave; or that the inactivity decay is a penalty — it is not, it takes no balance and creates no evidence (MEM-13(4)). This answers PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2: the liveness guarantees and limits now say which offline-validator halts are escapable and time-bounded, and what remains unbounded. Failure mode: claiming a bound the evidence cannot support in either direction — telling holders that an offline third is harmless, or leaving the impression that a halt traps everything, when the checkpoint-anchored exit path is available and is the load-bearing mitigation for the case the decay cannot reach. Proven Open (the exit guarantee is structural; the production bounds are conditional on F5/F6/F7 and on REC-03, and none of them is a measurement.)

RecoveryRecovery is itself a manipulation primitive with a price: an actor who can induce the objective trigger (T_STALL) may roll back an unfavourable provisional outcome; the bond, delay and cancellation price the attempt but do not make it impossible, and recovery is never a selective rollback: it restores the checkpoint whole and rewinds the contiguous range above it wholesale, and the invoker has no choice about which transactions inside that range survive, so it cannot be used to pick winners — but that is a statement about the rewind, not a promise about the outcome, because unwound transactions are not replayed automatically and their inclusion is not guaranteed, so a party whose transaction was unwound must resubmit it and the design does not promise that the same set of transactions will end up included. This closes finding R2: the selective-outcome guarantee is replaced by the enforceable wholesale-rewind property and the resubmission consequence.The resistance analysis of REC-03 is the only argument that the price is not cheap; it is an argument under stated premises, not a proof, and it must be independently reviewed before the selection is treated as settled (user decision D-7: Mode B selected)
RecoveryThe repeated-recovery limits are an escalating bond (B_REC(e) = B_REC_BASE · 2attempts over REC_WINDOW, where attempts counts every invocation — completed, cancelled or otherwise failed) and a cooldown (REC_COOLDOWN) (REC-02). The bond is split at invocation: the non-refundable B_REC_KEEP goes to the protocol treasury on every attempt, whether or not the recovery completes, and only the refundable balance is returned, and only on completion; the sizing target is B_REC_KEEP ≥ value_at_risk(D_MAX) — the fees and bridge value inside the provisional range, unmeasured and a Phase B output. They bound the price and the frequency of attempts, not the number of attempts a funded adversary can make, and an L1-censoring adversary is expressly outside the L2 fault modelA well-funded adversary can hold the chain in a stop-and-restart pattern; the bond schedule, its non-refundable split and the cooldown are unmeasured parameters, and a cancellation sends the whole bond to the treasury without repairing the interruption it caused (user decision D-7: Mode B selected; S5 §4: escalation counts attempts, the bond is non-refundable in part, and the sizing target is B_REC_KEEP ≥ value_at_risk(D_MAX), unmeasured.)
