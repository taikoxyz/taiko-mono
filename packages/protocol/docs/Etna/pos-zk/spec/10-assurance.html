10 — Invariants, conditional liveness, limitations and rejected alternatives

The v1 guarantee class, in one place. v1 guarantees the following and nothing more. (1) The checkpoint boundary: nothing at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is ever rewritten, and a confirmation above it is provisional (L1-07). (2) The exit from the last settled state: every L2→L1 message at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint stays withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 block, no quorum and no validator (MEM-15) — while both dependencies that MEM-15(2b) assumes hold: a funded proving market produces the k−1 attestation proofs of L1-13(5), and the inputs those proofs need are still retrievable for an accepted checkpoint at or above the signal's height: the recorded certificate, the batch's data and the pre-state witness (MEM-15(2a)); an unfunded or absent market, or inputs no longer served (expired blobs and no archive at the height; A-CONS-5/A-DA-2, DA-05), leaves a user at or below the last accepted checkpoint unable to exit, which is that clause's named falsifier. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the guarantee class states the exit's proving-market dependency instead of presenting the exit as unconditional. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F4: the guarantee class names the witness dependency with the funding, so the falsifier is no longer funding-only. (3) The withdrawal root: a root requires attestations from at least k distinct registered backend families (L1-13), plus the rule-triggered, self-expiring withdrawal veto (MSG-04), and its k−1 attach proofs are funded proving work (L1-13(5)), so root formation carries the proving-market dependency of clause (2). This closes review round 7 finding F1: root formation is stated as funded proving work rather than as an unattended L1 step. What that funding actually guarantees, stated exactly: the proving share is claimed through the single ledger of L1-11, whose landing draw and attestation draw are two unranked debits of one balance; the split between landing work and attestation work is fixed by the recorded allocation policy of ECON-02 clause 5 and is unmeasured (09), and no sub-accounting, reservation or ordering rule makes it true on-chain, so either draw may be starved when the ledger is short: a burst of attestations can pay landers zero (the batch still lands), and a landing can consume the balance an attach proof expected. Both payments are best-effort and never gate (L1-13(5)), so the share funds both kinds of proving work as a policy, not as an assured budget. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F3: the guarantee class discloses that the split is fixed by the recorded policy and unmeasured, that no rule enforces it, and that either draw on L1-11's single ledger may be starved, instead of implying both are assured. Four mechanisms are deferred by D-16 and their guarantees are absent: there is no inclusion obligation (the censorship gap; the forced-inclusion machinery is a tombstone), no recovery path of any kind — neither permissionless nor governance-mediated — so a settlement stall halts the chain until a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified, and settlement accepts one proof from one registered backend, so its soundness is that of the weakest approved backend, and no membership-liveness gate exists — heartbeat eligibility is a tombstone, so a cohort that stops participating keeps its committed weight; a quorum-loss halt ends with no protocol change when that committed cohort returns to vote, and it is permanent only while it never does, because the epoch containing the next unfinalised height has an immutable committed roster and no rule may skip or re-partition a height (MEM-13 states this, and the stake/exit asymmetry, in one place). This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the guarantee-class preamble states the heartbeat absence as the fourth deferred guarantee, not only the other three. This closes review round 8 findings R8-CC-01 and R8-CC-02: the preamble no longer says a quorum-loss halt lasts until a protocol update, and it points at the one place where the return escape, the true barrier and the asymmetry are joined. The deferred mechanisms, their findings and their revive criteria are in DEFERRED.md; their rule ids are tombstones that MUST NOT be implemented. (D-16: the four add-on mechanisms are deferred, and this page states the guarantee class that remains.)

Safety invariants

INV-01 — unique certified prefix, relative to the authenticated checkpoint Under the stated premises, at most one block per height per generation receives a valid commit certificate, and a certificate of a superseded recovery generation is void at the acceptance rule; within one generation every such block is an ancestor of every later such block, so the uniqueness of the finalized prefix holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules (CONS-02, CONS-03, CONS-04). The unconditional "at most one block per height ever" form was deleted because a stall resolution, were it live, would deliberately produce two valid, forever-valid certificates at the same height, one of them under a superseded recovery generation (CONS-08, GOV-04(f)). That mechanism is deferred by D-16, is not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §3): in v1 the generation does not advance, no rule produces a second valid certificate at a produced height, and the generation-scoped form is retained because the signed recovery generation still scopes certificates, locks and uniqueness (CONS-08, PRF-02). The rotation of CONS-16 would have been the second, narrower event of the same class: it would re-partition only heights that no validator produced, it would never re-judge a produced height, and each height is judged only under the epoch the L1-committed boundary record assigns it; because a valid certificate entails that its height was produced and finalized (CONS-05), no certificate can exist for a re-partitioned height, so CONS-08(3) is not weakened — but the rotation is deferred by D-16, is not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §2), so v1 has no second history-moving event at all: no height is re-partitioned, no offending certificate can arise from one, and the Open precondition that no produced height is re-judged (falsifier F7) is carried in the register with the mechanism rather than as a live obligation. This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the rotation is stated as deferred, not as a completed second event of the class. (S5 §6: INV-01 is restated generation-scoped exactly as CONS-12 now is; the unconditional claim is deleted.) Relative to the authenticated checkpoint (L1-07): history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can never be changed by any protocol path, and at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it (STATUS-07); above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint every confirmation is provisional, and in v1 no protocol path replaces it: the history replacement of GOV-04 is deferred by D-16 and the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15, so clearing a settlement stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified (DEFERRED.md §3). No timeout, rotation, admission rule, upgrade, operator action or failure of a liveness assumption may replace history above the checkpoint, and a settlement stall halts the chain rather than rewriting it; the last settled state remains available for exit (MEM-15), subject to the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b): the exit's k−1 attestation proofs are funded proving work, and without them no root forms. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the last settled state's availability is stated with its proving-market dependency. This closes finding R2: the selective-rollback outcome claim is replaced by the wholesale form any future replacement must have — and under D-16 there is no replacement at all, so no future update may discard a subset chosen by anyone. Replacement is never justified by execution validity alone: the proof statement binds the configuration under which the batch was certified and carries the recovery generation (PRF-02), so an alternative history cannot be accepted merely because its execution is valid. A certificate produced under a superseded generation is void for canonical purposes but remains admissible as evidence (GOV-04(f), deferred by D-16; DEFERRED.md §3). Premises: (P1) fewer than one third of the voting power of every epoch is Byzantine (A-CONS-1); (P2) correct validators obey the vote-uniqueness rule (CONS-02) and the lock rule (CONS-04); (P3) the commit rule requires more than two thirds of the epoch's voting power, counted over distinct authenticated signers (CONS-03); (P4) each epoch's set is the L1-committed set (CONS-10, MEM-08); (P5) the per-height lock of CONS-04 is enforced, and cross-height continuity is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii) — no lock state crosses an epoch boundary, because the lock is per height (*review round 2, finding R2A-10*) (CONS-09). Failure mode: reading the certificate as the guarantee. The certificate is the same evidence in every mode, so a reader — or an interface — can conclude that a confirmation above the checkpoint is permanent; it is not, and in v1 no rule makes it permanent either, because the protocol has no recovery path: a stall leaves the chain halted, and clearing it depends on a future protocol update. The other half matters equally: no premise failure and no other mechanism licenses a rollback, and if any premise fails, two conflicting certificates can both be cryptographically valid while a ZK proof cannot detect it. Both proofs verify. The protocol's response to that class remains disclosure, detection through objective evidence where it exists, and slashing — never a claim that the invariant holds. Proven Assumed (argument from the CometBFT Proof of Safety and the fork-accountability lemma, pinned in 02; P5 is review target F1) (D-16: uniqueness holds for the certificates produced under the consensus rules and within one recovery generation; above the last L1-accepted checkpoint every confirmation is provisional and no protocol path — permissionless or governance-mediated — replaces it in v1, because the stall resolution of GOV-04 is deferred and clearing a settlement stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified)
INV-02 — no batch is accepted without its data There is no state in which an accepted, checkpoint-advancing L1 record exists without (a) the batch data being retrievable from the transaction the accepted statement binds — the accepting transaction when the data is carried, the live publication record when it is referenced (DA-01, DA-08) — and (b) a valid proof binding execution to exactly that data. This holds structurally: acceptance happens in one transaction whose proof the Inbox verifies, and the data commitment is bound by DA-02 or DA-03. A publication advances no checkpoint and is not a batch record, so the data-first admission path remains excluded (L1-02). No outage, degraded mode, governance action or operator procedure may create an exception: in v1 there is no recovery path of any kind — the stall resolution of GOV-04 is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §3) — so no rule discards history, accepts a batch without data or manufactures a checkpoint (REC-01 e, L1-02). (D-16: no recovery path exists in v1, so this invariant has no exception.) Failure mode: a data-first admission design creates exactly the class observed in this repository — init3() (Inbox.sol:246-258) had to void queued requests whose blob references expired before they could be derived. That class is controlled here by DA-09's proving deadline: a pending batch exists only as a publication record bounded by that deadline, never as an advanceable checkpoint. Proven (D5 as relaxed by D-11: data may be published before the proof, and acceptance still happens only with a valid proof that binds it; the in-repo incident is evidence that the alternative is not hypothetical)
INV-03 — no consensus without TAIKO at risk Voting power exists only for stake that is bonded in the L1 staking contract and active in the epoch's set; a key with no eligible stake cannot vote, and a vote is counted only if the signer's stake is in the authenticated set. Slashing follows the stake held at the time of the offence (MEM-06), so exiting after an offence does not escape the penalty. Failure modes addressed: voting power acquired without at-risk capital, and the withdrawal race in which an offender unbonds between the offence and the evidence. Proven
INV-04 — no privilege or discretion in ordinary operation Every step of ordinary operation — activation, proposing, voting, proving, evidence submission, reward claiming and exit — is reachable by any address satisfying the objective rules, with no allowlist, no owner approval, no DAO vote and no operator key. A privileged lever is permitted only for upgrades, and each such lever is enumerated with its scope in MIG-04. Failure mode: a protocol that claims permissionless operation while a ProverWhitelist or an owner-only init3() can alter who may participate or what queued work survives. Proven Open (open for the operational audit of the completed migration: the rule is normative, but whether every lever has been removed is verified only at migration time)

Conditional liveness

LIVE-01 — the liveness statement and exactly where it ends The following is the complete liveness claim, and it is conditional on every clause; the exit is stated separately below with its own assumption, which is not one of (L1)–(L7). If (L1) more than two thirds of each epoch's voting power is online and message delay between correct validators is below the round-timeout bound for long enough to complete a round; (L2) a correct proposer is eventually selected by rotation when timeouts elapse; (L3) correct validators hold and can serve the data of the blocks they voted for, at least until settlement or the retention window ends; (L4) for every committed batch, at least one adequately-resourced prover completes its proof inside the envelope, or another prover does — this is a per-batch statement, not a mean-rate one: a sustained average that meets the throughput inequality of LIVE-03 does not imply that no individual batch exceeds the envelope (*review round 1, finding R1-09*); (L5) Ethereum includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below the finality depth; (L6) the L1 staking contract commits each epoch's set root before the epoch begins; (L7) a new node can obtain a weak-subjectivity checkpoint within the freshness window; then L2 produces a block every 2 seconds, blocks reach STATUS-04 within the round budget, and committed batches reach STATUS-06 within the proof-queue envelope. A settlement stall is not cleared by any rule of v1. The stall-resolution action of GOV-04 is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, and the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15, so there is no recovery path of any kind. When settlement stalls, the chain halts safely, funds at or below the last L1-accepted checkpoint remain withdrawable while the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b) forms the root, and clearing the stall requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified (DEFERRED.md §3). Not conditional on (L1), (L2) or (L6), and its own dependencies are named. The exit needs no new L2 block, no settlement progress, no quorum and no permission — it still rides on Ethereum's own liveness (A-L1-1, the L1-inclusion half of (L5)) — so every L2→L1 message included at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint stays withdrawable on L1 with no consensus participation and no validator cooperation (MEM-15). It is not independent of proving or of retention, however: (L4) is the prover-completion assumption, the exit's k−1 attestation proofs are funded proving work (L1-13(5)) that needs the recorded certificate, the batch's data and the pre-state witness of an accepted checkpoint at or above the signal's height (MEM-15(2a)), and the exit therefore depends on both dependencies MEM-15(2b) names: the funded proving market and the retained witness — an unfunded or absent proving market, or inputs no longer retrievable (expired blobs and no archive serving the height; A-CONS-5/A-DA-2, DA-05), leaves a user at or below the last accepted checkpoint unable to exit, which is that clause's falsifier. Settlement can keep raising that checkpoint over already-certified heights while production is halted only if the data is retained and a proof is produced (MEM-15(3)). (PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2(c): the exit path is a liveness guarantee in its own right, not a conditional clause.) This closes review round 7 finding F1: the "independent of every clause" claim is replaced by the exit's exact scope — no new block, no settlement progress, no quorum, no permission, but a funded proving market. This closes review round 8 finding R8-EBA F4: LIVE-01's named dependency list carries the witness with the funding. Liveness ends at the first clause that fails. Where it ends has changed (D-16): (L1) fails → the chain halts rather than forks, and no rule of v1 restores it; (L3) fails for data above the last L1-accepted checkpoint → the chain halts; no rule treats the data as available, and in v1 nothing discards the range either, so settlement of the affected heights waits on a future protocol update; (L4) fails → settlement halts after the unsettled-depth cap binds, and the halt persists until a future protocol update — there is no in-protocol bound; (L5) fails → nothing settles and no L1 inclusion path can restore progress (LIVE-04); (L6) fails → the chain cannot enter the next epoch and no in-protocol rule can manufacture the missing L1 set commitment, so that halt persists until the entry is appended and Ethereum-final; (L7) fails → a new node must not silently trust an unverified history. Offline validators specifically — the case review comment 6011464863 item 2 raised — have no production-time bound in v1: the L1-heartbeat eligibility of MEM-13 is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, so membership has no liveness gate and a cohort that stops attesting, or stops participating while still present, is not excluded from any future set version; no rule removes weight either, so the halt persists until that committed cohort returns to vote, and it is permanent only while it never does (MEM-13). This closes review round 8 finding R8-CC-01: the offline-validator case names the cohort-return escape. In every class, value at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint remains withdrawable on L1 with no L2 liveness (MEM-15) while the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b) produces the root's k−1 attestation proofs; the exact statement is LIVE-05. This closes review round 7 finding F1: every class's exit statement carries the proving-market condition. There is no clause whose failure licenses rewriting history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and above it a failure licenses nothing either: in v1 no protocol path replaces history, and clearing the halt requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified. (D-16: a settlement stall is not cleared by any rule of v1; the governance stall resolution of GOV-04 is deferred with the other add-on mechanisms, so the halt persists until a future protocol update, and the exit guarantee of MEM-15 is the only guarantee that survives loss of L2 production — subject to its funded proving market, MEM-15(2b). This closes review round 7 finding F1: "production-independent" is qualified by the proving dependence the exit carries.) Assumed unmeasured (the timeout bound, the retention window and the envelope are parameters whose values require measurement, 09)
LIVE-02 — backpressure and halt triggers Correct validators apply backpressure before liveness is lost, never by discarding committed history: (a) at unsettled depth D_MAX they refuse to vote for further blocks (HALT-03); (b) if the proof queue would exceed the retention window even at D_MAX, D_MAX itself is reduced by a published governance parameter change — an upgrade, not a runtime action; (c) they halt when HALT-01 conditions hold. A normal proof inside the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, 900 blocks at 2 s) must never trigger (a) or (b). Failure mode: backpressure implemented as "drop the oldest unsettled batch", which is an unsanctioned rollback — in v1 no rule replaces history at all, above the checkpoint or below it, and the replacement the stall resolution of GOV-04 would have performed is deferred by D-16 — and therefore violates REC-01. (D-16: no replacement path is normative in v1; the boundary of REC-01 stands unchanged) Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value and the retention window)
LIVE-03 — throughput sustainability conditions The proving pipeline is sustainable if and only if three inequalities hold, all evaluated with measured inputs before launch: (i) rate: the fleet's sustained proven execution rate is at least the L2's sustained execution rate; (ii) concurrency: the number of proofs in flight is at least L / Δ, where L is the proof latency bound and Δ is the batch's L2 time span (a queueing identity, not a measurement); (iii) data: the L2's data production rate does not exceed the L1 data capacity available to the batches, which for blob data is the blob target/maximum per L1 block divided by the L1 slot duration. If any inequality fails, the backlog grows without bound and the correct response is LIVE-02 backpressure — not a longer latency assumption and not a rollback. Failure modes addressed: "tolerate individual slow proofs" being mistaken for sustainable throughput, and a design that quietly assumes unlimited prover capacity. Every input to these inequalities is unmeasured in this specification; the formulas are the deliverable. Open (requires measurement of cycles per L2 gas and proven cycles per second per machine, 03 §7)
LIVE-04 — censorship resistance, stated honestly Censorship resistance has two parts, and neither is a per-transaction guarantee. (1) Published data: v1 has no inclusion obligation. The narrow, L1-anchored forced-inclusion machinery (FI-10–FI-14, decision D-12) is deferred by D-16, is not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §1). Any account may still publish a transaction's data to L1 (DA-07), and a publication advances no protocol state, but nothing requires a batch to include, resolve or prove that data: a proposer that excludes published data is not in breach, and there is no due set, prefix, deadline discharge or omission offence (CONS-01, ECON-04). No general inclusion list exists: there is no queue of arbitrary transactions, no escrow and no forced-inclusion fee, and no L1 entry point for unpublished data (FI-PLANNED-01). This is the censorship gap D-16 discloses: the protocol provides no per-transaction inclusion guarantee for any data. (2) Unpublished data. Resistance is the conditional statistical property described here. Proposer selection is hash-based and weighted (CONS-06), and it is a public deterministic function (CONS-06(d)), so it is fully predictable: a coalition can identify in advance the slot in which a target transaction would be included, and buying or coercing the scheduled proposer of a single slot is possible and cheap — no rule prices, detects or penalises slot-buying, and this specification assumes no cost floor that prevents it. What bounds sustained censorship is the selection share, not unpredictability: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it. That last clause is a behavioural premise, not a protocol rule, and it is the honest limit of this resistance: no inclusion duty attaches to a validator (ROLE-01(b)), and omission is neither a proposal-validity failure nor an offence (CONS-01, ECON-04), and no rule defines "room", reserves block space or constrains proposer ordering. Under A-CONS-2 the statistical half is real but conditional. A network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can prevent the data from ever being published — and so can an adversary able to censor L1 inclusion itself (A-L1-1) — and because a withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, such an adversary can also prevent the user from starting a withdrawal. The protocol offers no remedy against either, and R10 is satisfied only in its relaxed form: v1 states the censorship resistance it has and has no inclusion obligation. No recovery path of any kind exists in v1 (D-16), so nothing discards or rewrites history in an attempt to remedy exclusion. (D-16: the narrow forced-inclusion rule of D-12 is deferred and its guarantee is absent; D-11's permission to publish data before the proof is unchanged, and it supplies no inclusion right.) This closes review round 3 finding R3-CE-05: selection is stated to be fully predictable and slot-buying possible and cheap, and the resistance is bounded by the selection share plus the honest-proposer behaviour that ROLE-01(b) expressly does not impose. Failure mode: presenting published data as an inclusion guarantee, or letting a user believe that any L1 escape hatch exists — either would let the user size exposure on a promise the protocol never makes. Proven Assumed (the absence of an inclusion obligation is structural; the residual resistance claim is conditional on A-CONS-2 and A-L1-1)
LIVE-05 — halts and offline validators: escapability and what remains unbounded This rule states, for each way validators can stop the chain without being Byzantine, exactly what a user can still do and what the protocol does not bound. In v1 there is no production-time bound for any of them, and no mechanism is added here. The membership-liveness mechanism an earlier draft named, MEM-13's L1-heartbeat eligibility, is deferred by D-16, is not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §2); so is the stall resolution of GOV-04 (DEFERRED.md §3), and the permissionless recovery was withdrawn by D-15. What remains is the exit of MEM-15: every message at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 block, no quorum and no validator — while the funded proving market that MEM-15(2b) assumes produces the k−1 attestation proofs; an unfunded or absent proving market forms no root and leaves the exit unavailable, which is that clause's falsifier. No rule removes weight, so no halt below changes a set version's weights. This closes review round 7 finding F1: LIVE-05's exit statement carries the proving-market dependency and its falsifier.

What is claimed, exactly. (i) A halt caused by offline validators does not trap value that is already settled: every message at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is withdrawable on L1 with no new L2 blocks, no quorum and no validator (MEM-15), and this part needs only Ethereum's liveness and the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b) for the root's k−1 attestation proofs, not the L2's. This closes review round 7 finding F1: claim (i) names the proving market rather than Ethereum's liveness alone. (ii) That path does not depend on any recovery path, on the rotation, or on any validator returning. (iii) The halt's production consequence is not protocol-bounded for any class: v1 adds no membership-liveness gate and no recovery path, and for the settlement-stall, retention and missing-entry classes clearing the halt requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not specified (D-16; DEFERRED.md §§2–3). For the quorum-loss classes, the one in-v1 escape is the committed cohort returning to vote: the halt then ends with no protocol change, and it is permanent only while that cohort never returns, because the epoch containing the next unfinalised height has an immutable committed roster and no rule may skip or re-partition a height (MEM-13 states this, and the stake/exit asymmetry, in one place). This closes review round 8 findings R8-CC-01 and R8-CC-02: the claim states the cohort-return escape and the true permanence barrier. (iv) No rule removes, adds or re-weights any member of a set version, so A-CONS-1 is unaffected. (v) The deferred heartbeat would have been a declaration of presence rather than proof of consensus participation; its tombstone preserves that limit (MEM-13, DEFERRED.md §2). What must never be written: that an offline third can no longer hold the chain; that every halt is time-bounded; or that value above the last accepted checkpoint can always leave. This answers PR #22262 review comment 6011464863, item 2: the liveness limits now say which offline-validator halts are escapable and that v1 leaves every one of them without an in-protocol bound (for quorum loss the returning committed cohort is the only in-v1 escape, MEM-13). Failure mode: claiming a bound the evidence cannot support in either direction — telling holders that an offline third is harmless, or leaving the impression that a halt traps everything, when the checkpoint-anchored exit path is available — subject to the funded proving market of MEM-15(2b) — and is the load-bearing mitigation for every class above. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the failure-mode note does not present the exit as unconditionally available. Proven Open (the exit path is structural, but its k−1 attestation proofs are a disclosed market dependency (MEM-15(2b)); no production bound is claimed in v1, and none of the above is a measurement. This closes review round 7 finding F1: the exit guarantee's evidence tag carries its proving-market assumption. (D-16: the heartbeat eligibility and the stall resolution are deferred, so the bounds they supplied are withdrawn with them; the unbounded halt is the disclosed v1 position.))
