06 — Withholding, safe halt, restart, and permissionless recovery of unsettled history

A PoS confirmation above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is provisional. Mode B is selected (user decision D-7): a permissionless, bonded, delayed L1 recovery may replace history strictly above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch, which a completed recovery restores exactly (REC-01, REC-02); at or below it nothing may be changed by recovery, and Ethereum finality is the separate, stronger property of STATUS-07. The previous "never roll back" posture was replaced because an unbounded halt was judged worse than a bounded rollback: under Mode A an availability failure leaves the chain dead with no in-protocol remedy, and the requirement set also demands a chain that keeps serving users. Any interface that shows a confirmation above the last accepted checkpoint must say that it can be replaced. A completed recovery retires the discarded heights permanently: they can never be certified or landed again, at any generation, by anyone (REC-02). (S5 §1: discarded heights are permanently retired.) REC-03 states what the rollback does and does not protect against. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected; the guarantee class of a confirmation above the checkpoint is provisional.)

REC-01 — recovery boundary: no path may replace history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint No contract function, client rule, timeout, validator rotation, admission rule, governance proposal executed through the ordinary upgrade path, operational procedure, or recovery invocation may change the canonical history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch — and a completed recovery restores exactly that checkpoint. Above it, history is provisional and may be replaced only by the recovery path of REC-02, under its objective trigger, delay, bond and cancellation rules. Ethereum finality is a separate and stronger property, not the recovery floor: once the accepting L1 transaction is in a finalized Ethereum block, even an L1 reorganisation cannot undo it (STATUS-07); until then an accepted checkpoint is revocable only by an L1 reorganisation below finality (STATUS-06, L1-12), so an implementer must never read that property back as the recovery floor. Concretely: (a) no function exists whose effect is to replace an accepted block hash at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint or to discard state at or below it; the only downward movement of the canonical L2 chain is a completed recovery restoring exactly that checkpoint (REC-02); (b) no admission rule may reject a batch that extends the current L1 checkpoint (L1-04); (c) a certificate is judged under the validator set of its own epoch and stays valid as evidence forever (CONS-08), so validator rotation cannot re-judge it, and a certificate above a restored checkpoint is void for canonical purposes but remains admissible as evidence (REC-02); (d) no upgrade may be used as a substitute for recovery: an upgrade must accept every history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint (GOV-03), and history above that checkpoint may be replaced only by REC-02, never by a governance transaction; (e) no data-first path may be introduced to "work around" an unsettled batch, in recovery or otherwise, because that would let an unproven history be treated as settled (L1-02, HALT-04). (user decision D-7: the previous "never roll back" posture is replaced — the invariant is now "no recovery may replace history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint", and history above it is provisional; the Ethereum-finalized checkpoint is a separate, stronger object and is never the recovery floor.) This rule is the boundary between the two guarantee classes: no recovery may change history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and history above it is provisional. Its violation would turn the bounded rollback of REC-02 into unbounded history replacement, which no user decision authorises. Halting remains the correct local action when settlement cannot proceed; recovery is the only sanctioned way history above the checkpoint may be replaced, and it is permissionless, objective and bonded. This closes the reviewer finding that made the Ethereum-finalized checkpoint the recovery floor: the floor is the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and Ethereum finality is the separate, stronger property of STATUS-07 (D-7). Proven (structural: the boundary of the replacement window plus four positive obligations)

Safe halt

HALT-01 — safe halt obligation A correct validator halts — it stops signing and stops advancing its finalized view — rather than choose between two histories or advance on unknown state. It halts when any of the following holds and cannot be resolved by a rule-legal action: (a) it cannot obtain the data for a finalized or locked block (A-CONS-5 failed); (b) it cannot obtain a quorum for a height within the timeout ladder (A-CONS-2 failed); (c) the L1 facts it must consume are not yet Ethereum-final, or its L1 view disagrees with the consensus view (SYS-02); (d) the L1 validator-set commitment for the epoch it is about to enter is absent (CONS-13); (e) the unsettled-depth cap would be exceeded (HALT-03). Halting is a correct outcome, not a failure of the protocol: it is the action that preserves every height at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint and keeps the node inside its rules. A node that halts must keep serving what it has, and must keep its state so it can resume. Halting never authorises discarding history, and never authorises a data-first publication. A validator's halt is a local safety action and is not a recovery trigger: recovery is triggered only by the objective settlement-stall condition of REC-02, which is observable on L1. A halt caused by an unlandable batch is answered by retrying and by continuing to serve; if settlement nevertheless stalls past T_STALL, the permissionless recovery path of REC-02 is the sanctioned remedy for that stall, and it is never declared early by a participant's local view. Recovery does not clear every halt: it changes no validator set and cannot manufacture a missing epoch-set entry or an unavailable quorum, so a halt under (b) or (d) persists until the entry is appended and Ethereum-final or the quorum returns, and invoking recovery there discards the provisional range without restoring liveness (LIVE-01). This closes review round 4 finding R4-LIV-09: recovery is no longer presented as the remedy for halts it cannot clear. (user decision D-7: a halt is still correct, but it is no longer the only end state — recovery exists for the settlement-stall case and only for that case.) Failure modes: an implementer, faced with a stuck chain, invents an "unstick" action; or a participant treats its own halt as authority to roll history back. Neither is permitted — the only sanctioned replacement path is REC-02, invoked on L1 by any account, bonded, and cancelled by honest progress. Proven Assumed (the halt conditions are normative; whether they are met is assumed)
HALT-02 — restart rules After a halt, a correct participant resumes only from the highest finalized block whose data and certificate it can independently reconstruct, or — if a recovery has completed — from the checkpoint restored by that recovery (REC-02). It must never adopt an alternative history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and must never treat a timeout during the halt as evidence that a certificate does not exist (STATUS-10). On restart: (a) each validator enforces a signed-message uniqueness check over its own signing history — no message of the same type at the same height and round may be signed twice, including after a restart or a state resync, and a validator that cannot prove it has not already signed for a (height, round, type) must not sign (the amnesia case is treated as a signing fault, not as a fresh start); (b) a validator that resumes must re-verify the epoch and set for the height it is resuming at rather than trusting its cached set; (c) nodes must not accept a replayed historical message as new — every vote carries the epoch and round, and the receiving rules reject messages for heights already finalized; (d) a resumed node's first act is a data and certificate sync, not a proposal. After a completed recovery the restored checkpoint is the highest canonical checkpoint: a node must adopt it, must not treat a discarded block above it as finalized, and must apply every clause of this rule unchanged — no double signing, no replayed message, no ordering by timeout. (user decision D-7: restart follows whichever checkpoint is canonical — the highest L1-accepted one — and a discarded provisional block is not a finalized height.) Failure modes addressed: "amnesia" double-signing after a restart, replaying old votes, and a restart that silently adopts a competing branch — the classic way a safe halt becomes an unsafe restart. Proven (the signing-history rule rests on the validator's own durable state, an implementation gate)
HALT-03 — consensus-enforced unsettled-depth cap (backpressure) Define the unsettled depth at a height as the number of L2 blocks between the highest L1-accepted checkpoint and the block being considered. A correct validator must not prevote or precommit a block whose unsettled depth would exceed D_MAX − MARGIN_V. Exactly one enforcement point: the validators. land MUST NOT reject an over-depth batch, because doing so could make a legitimately finalized range permanently unlandable and would violate L1-04 (*review round 2, finding R2-LIV-07*). D_MAX is derived from the retention window, the permitted proof latency and the settlement pipeline, and not from the retention window alone: D_MAX = floor((RETENTION_WINDOW − T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED − T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL), recorded with its derivation in 09. MARGIN_V is mandatory and is at least the number of L2 blocks produced during one Ethereum finality depth, because a validator's view of the L1 checkpoint is itself only usable at that depth (SYS-02); without the margin a validator can systematically under-estimate depth and overshoot the cap. The cap is a consensus rule with a single enforcement point. Honest statement of what that costs: because the cap cannot be enforced at L1 without stranding a legitimate extension, a quorum that ignores it is not objectively punishable at L1, and a cap violation is therefore a liveness failure that is disclosed rather than a slashing offence (WH-02, LIM-01). When the cap binds, production stops and the chain is in the safe-halt state until provers catch up. Recovery does not relax the cap: a completed recovery restores the L1 checkpoint and production resumes under the same cap, the cap binding is a halt and not by itself a recovery trigger, and only the objective T_STALL condition of REC-02 can trigger recovery. (user decision D-7: the unsettled-depth cap is unchanged by the selected recovery mode; recovery may not be used to advance past it or to bypass it.) The cap must also be jointly satisfiable with the evidence window: the coupling inequality is stated normatively in ECON-07(7), and D_MAX must be reduced until it holds (*review round 2, finding P-R2-08*). It is also jointly constrained with the D6 envelope, and RETENTION_WINDOW is chosen from that constraint rather than independently: a proof that consumes the whole envelope must leave an unsettled depth at or below the depth at which validators stop, so D_MAX · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL ≥ T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL, equivalently D_MAX − MARGIN_V ≥ (T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL (the margin term is MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL, in seconds, because MARGIN_V is measured in L2 blocks). Because this rule derives D_MAX from RETENTION_WINDOW, the inequality is also a lower bound on the retention window: in particular RETENTION_WINDOW MUST be large enough that floor((RETENTION_WINDOW − T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED − T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL) − MARGIN_V ≥ (T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL (a sufficient form is RETENTION_WINDOW ≥ T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED + 2 · T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL + L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL), so the retention window is derived backwards from the inequality, never selected on its own and checked afterwards. If the inequality is violated, D_MAX − MARGIN_V can be zero or negative, in which case no block at a positive unsettled depth may be voted and production cannot advance past the current checkpoint. The envelope term is D6's planning assumption T_PROOF_ENVELOPE, not T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED (the permitted bound of 09): the promise is that a normal proof never binds the cap. This closes review round 3 finding R3-LIV-02: HALT-03 carries the joint cap/envelope inequality, so a normal 30-minute proof can never trigger the cap, and RETENTION_WINDOW is stated to be chosen from that inequality rather than independently. D_MAX is a bound on commitment creation, not on proving latency, and a normal proof taking the whole of the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, i.e. 900 blocks at 2 s) is not misconduct and not grounds for any recovery action. Failure modes addressed: unbounded growth of unsettled history (which is exactly the state in which an availability failure becomes unrecoverable), and an implementer treating a slow proof as a fault. Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value depends on measured retention and proving throughput)
HALT-04 — no emergency operator, no DAO rescue, no data-first path; recovery is the only sanctioned replacement No emergency key, guardian, DAO vote, or operational procedure may be required to preserve any invariant of this specification, and none may be introduced to "resolve" a halt. Specifically: there is no function that a privileged party can call to advance, rewind, or replace the checkpoint; no DAO intervention is permitted for liveness; and no data-first publication may be used to unblock settlement. The only sanctioned way that history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint may be replaced is the permissionless, bonded, delayed recovery of REC-02, which no operator, no DAO and no validator quorum controls, and which never replaces history at or below that checkpoint (REC-01). If progress requires information or participation that no rule-legal action can obtain, the protocol says so and stops; it does not promote a privileged fix. (user decision D-7: recovery is the only sanctioned way history above the checkpoint may be replaced, and it is discretionary in nothing — no operator, no DAO rescue, no data-first path.) Failure modes: a halt is quietly "fixed" by an operator, which both violates R2 and disguises a liveness limitation as a functioning protocol; or recovery is captured by a privileged party, which would be the same failure under a different name. Proven (enforced by the migration's removal of privileged levers, MIG-04)

WH-02 — objective evidence versus local suspicion A protocol-level penalty requires objective, self-contained, on-chain verifiable evidence: two signed messages of the same type at the same height and round with different block ids (CONS-11), or a signed message that violates a rule whose inputs are entirely on-chain. Everything else — a message that did not arrive, a height that did not advance, a proof that has not been submitted, a peer that did not answer a request — is local suspicion. Local suspicion may trigger local actions (retry, view change, reputation, peer selection, disclosure) but may never trigger a penalty, a recovery action, or a statement about what exists elsewhere. The recovery trigger is not an exception: it is the objective settlement-stall condition of REC-02 measured on L1, no participant's local view of a stall may shorten T_STALL or declare recovery early, and non-receipt of a batch, proof or certificate remains local suspicion. (user decision D-7: the trigger is objective and L1-observable; local suspicion still never triggers a penalty or a recovery action.) (user decision D-6: forced inclusion is removed, so the forced-inclusion omission offence is no longer one of this rule's evidence classes; the protocol has no inclusion duty, and LIVE-04 states what inclusion resistance remains.) Failure modes addressed: false accusations, and the more dangerous error of treating a timeout as a fault ("the sequencer was slashed after a landing deadline" is exactly the reasoning this rule forbids). Proven
WH-03 — false positives and false negatives, labelled unmeasured The specification must state, for every detection rule, both error directions: a false positive penalises a correct participant (for example, a validator whose vote was merely delayed being treated as absent), and a false negative lets a faulty participant escape (for example, a one-off selective-delivery attack that leaves no signed conflict). For each rule the specification states which direction is worse and why, and records that the rates are unmeasured with what would measure them (a fault-injection testbed with a modelled adversarial network). No rule may claim a detection rate. Failure mode: an unmeasured detection rate presented as a guarantee, or a design where the only safe path is perfect detection. unmeasured
WH-04 — reveal-later certificate: the analysis across locks, view changes and reconfiguration The protocol must resist the following schedule, which is mandatory in this specification: (1) a proposer selectively distributes a block to a subset; (2) honest validators vote according to their local views; (3) the attacker privately obtains a finality-related certificate; (4) other participants attempt a leader change, a validator change, or a recovery; (5) the attacker reveals the certificate after the alternative path has progressed. A recovery is an alternative path only above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and it restores exactly that checkpoint: a certificate revealed later for a discarded height is evidence against an equivocator, never a second canonical history (REC-02). The required conclusion is therefore that two conflicting histories cannot both be canonical at the same time: at or below the checkpoint neither conflicting history can receive a replacement guarantee at all, and above it a completed recovery leaves the restored checkpoint as the only canonical one. That conclusion rests on the conjunction of three mechanisms, never on signature counting alone: (user decision D-7: the reveal-later schedule now includes a real recovery path, and the conclusion is scoped to the provisional window above the checkpoint.)
  • Locks. A validator that precommitted a block at round R is locked on it and will not prevote a conflicting block at that height unless it sees a proof-of-lock-change at a strictly later round (CONS-04). Because a certificate implies more than one third of the voting power is correct and locked, no conflicting commit can form. This is why an unpublished certificate is harmless to safety: it is the lock, not the publication, that protects the decision.
  • View changes. A view change may change the proposer, but it may not unlock a validator that has not seen a later-round proof-of-lock-change. Therefore step (4)'s "alternative path" cannot produce a conflicting commit merely because the original certificate was hidden.
  • Reconfiguration. No lock state crosses an epoch boundary. The lock is per height (CONS-04(1)): it is released by that height's commit or by a strictly later same-height proof-of-lock-change, and CONS-09(3) states that a validator carries no lock state from a height of epoch e into e+1; a lock formed at a height of e on a value that did not finalize there is resolved at that height and is not carried forward as an independent constraint. Cross-height continuity is therefore exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii), enforced at the boundary by the anchor duty of CONS-09(1)–(2): the first block of the new epoch must descend from the last block finalized in the closing epoch. The handoff's protection is that a certificate is judged under its own epoch's set and remains valid forever (CONS-08), so the closing epoch's decision is not re-judged by the new set and a stakeholder that rotates keys or changes its stake cannot escape it; a validator that equivocates across the boundary is caught by a same-(height, round) conflicting pair, the only lock-related offence with admissible evidence (ECON-04). This closes review round 3 finding R3A-03: the retracted cross-height lock carry-over is removed from WH-04's reconfiguration mechanism, and the protection it names is the parent-validity condition plus epoch-scoped permanent certificates, not a surviving lock.
Failure mode: a design in which the alternative path discards the per-height lock within a height, or in which a validator-set change re-judges a certificate under a new set instead of under its own epoch's set (CONS-08), in which case the reveal-later schedule succeeds and two conflicting histories can both be treated as canonical without any slashable equivocation. (Under the selected mode the second history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can still be discarded by recovery, but the lock and reconfiguration mechanisms above are what prevent the conflict from arising in the first place.) Proven Assumed (premises: A-CONS-1 in every epoch; correct validators enforce CONS-02/CONS-04; the boundary argument of CONS-09, which is the named review target F1)

Mode B — the selected recovery mode (user decision D-7)

Mode B is selected and is part of the active protocol. Since user decision D-7, a permissionless, bonded, delayed L1 recovery may replace history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch, which a completed recovery restores exactly; at or below it nothing may be changed by recovery (REC-01). The selection completed the D2 procedure: Mode A was investigated first, and the specific requirement it cannot satisfy is recorded in D-7 — an availability failure under Mode A produces an unbounded halt whose only remedies are outside the protocol, while the requirement set also demands a chain that keeps serving users. The resistance analysis required by D2 step 5 is REC-03, and it requires independent review before the selection is treated as settled.

The guarantee attached to a confirmation above the last accepted checkpoint is PoS-certified / provisional (STATUS-04). Any interface that shows such a confirmation MUST say that it can be replaced; calling it irreversible is a defect. A PoS-certified block above the checkpoint may be discarded by a completed recovery, and its effects are handled by the replay rules of REC-02. (user decision D-7: Mode B is selected; the guarantee class above the checkpoint is provisional.)

Objective triggerExactly one trigger, stated as a predicate over stored L1 state: a settlement stall holds iff block.timestamp − lastAcceptedBatchTime ≥ T_STALL when the invocation transaction is evaluated, where lastAcceptedBatchTime is the stored L1 timestamp (block.timestamp of the L1 transaction) at which the most recent batch was accepted — the instant the current L1-accepted checkpoint of REC-01 was written — and it is written by the Inbox when acceptance advances the checkpoint. The predicate is equivalent to the statement that no batch extending the current L1 checkpoint has been accepted for T_STALL = T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + L1_FINALITY + T_RECOVERY_MARGIN, registered with its unit and tag in 09. The clock is L1's own and only the acceptance of an extending batch resets it — a completed recovery changes neither lastAcceptedBatchTime nor the checkpoint, so it does not restart the window, and an L1 reorganisation below finality carries the stored value back with the record it belongs to. Every term is unmeasured and is recorded with its derivation in 09, and T_STALL must be strictly larger than the D6 envelope so that a normal slow proof, or one prover failure inside the envelope, can never trigger recovery. There is no other trigger and no early declaration: a participant's local view, a halt, a missing certificate, a slow proof and an over-depth cap are not triggers. This closes review round 4 finding R4-MB-03: the trigger clock now measures a named stored quantity and requires block.timestamp − lastAcceptedBatchTime ≥ T_STALL, registered in 09.
Who may invokeAny account, permissionlessly, by posting the bond B_REC(e) = B_REC_BASE · 2attempts in ETH on L1, paid at invocation, where attempts is the number of attempts — invocations, whether they completed, were cancelled by honest progress, or failed in any other way — in the preceding REC_WINDOW. Escalation counts attempts, never completions. The bond is split at invocation into a non-refundable component B_REC_KEEP(e), transferred to the protocol treasury at invocation and never returned (ECON-06(6)), and a refundable balance B_REC(e) − B_REC_KEEP(e) returned only on a completed recovery. The invoker pays B_REC_KEEP on every attempt, whether or not the recovery completes, and REC_COOLDOWN follows every attempt. B_REC_BASE is denominated in ETH and its amount is unmeasured; the sizing rule is B_REC_KEEP ≥ value_at_risk(D_MAX) — the fees and bridge value inside the provisional range, unmeasured and a Phase B output — recorded in 09. No DAO, no operator, no validator quorum. The invocation is an L1 transaction containing only the claim, the checkpoint it is made against, and the bond. The bond is an anti-spam and anti-manipulation deposit, not validator stake or slashable consensus collateral, so D7 — staking and slashable collateral denominated in TAIKO — is unaffected by it. (user decision D-7: the bond asset is fixed as ETH on L1, paid at invocation; S5 §4: the bond is split into a non-refundable component and a refundable balance, and escalation counts attempts, not completions.)
Recovery record and concurrencyThe recovery record is a singleton held in the Inbox, in the Inbox state that holds recoveryGeneration and the accepted checkpoint (L1-05 row 31; MIG-02, slots 268–273). At most one pending invocation may exist at a time: a second invocation while one is pending MUST revert, because the record carries one bond, one H_inv and one invocation time, and the completion transition reads exactly that record. The singleton is what makes MIG-02's 17-slot Inbox budget true — the budget carries one packed recovery record with one pending-invocation entry, not a map — and permitting a queue of pending invocations would require the map variant that the budget does not carry. This closes review round 4 finding R4A-04: the holder of the recovery record is named as the Inbox, the single-pending-invocation rule is normative, and the MIG-02 budget's singleton assumption is stated.
Delay and cancellationThe invocation records the checkpoint height H_inv it was made against. No earlier than T_RECOVERY_DELAY after the invocation, the completion transition may be called by any account while the same bond remains posted; it writes resumeHeight, increments recoveryGeneration by one, and pays the refundable balance to the address that posted the bond, whoever calls it. The cancellation predicate is exact: the recovery is cancelled iff a valid batch extending the checkpoint recorded at invocation is accepted on L1 after the invocation transaction and before the completion transition is included — equivalently, iff lastLandedHeight > H_inv when the completion transition is evaluated. Acceptance of such a batch MUST cancel every pending recovery it extends past and transfer the whole posted bond to the protocol treasury in the same transaction (ECON-06(6)); the completion transition MUST revert once the predicate holds. Neither the reveal of a certificate nor of a proof for a discarded height cancels it, because those heights are by construction not yet settled. Cancellation runs to finality: the recovery takes effect only when the completion transaction is Ethereum-final under SYS-02, and L2 nodes MUST NOT act on the completion record before that depth. Before that instant no batch at a retired height can be accepted, because the completion transition writes resumeHeight and the current generation (L1-06); if the completion transaction is reorganised out below finality it never took effect, the pre-recovery history stands, and the refundable balance remains posted. There is therefore no window in which a completed recovery and a batch at a retired height can both be true. (S5 §2: completion is an L1 event whose effect is gated on Ethereum finality, with an exact cancellation predicate.)
Bond outcomeThe bond is paid in ETH on L1 at invocation and is split at that instant. B_REC_KEEP(e) is non-refundable on every attempt and is transferred to the protocol treasury whether the recovery completes, is cancelled, or fails in any other way; only the refundable balance B_REC(e) − B_REC_KEEP(e) is ever returned, and only on completion, to the address that posted it — the caller of the completion transition receives nothing by virtue of calling. When the recovery is cancelled by honest progress, the refundable balance joins B_REC_KEEP(e) and the whole bond goes to the protocol treasury, so that spamming recovery costs money; the destination is the treasury and there is no burn (ECON-06(6)). The asset is ETH and not TAIKO because the bond is an anti-spam and anti-manipulation deposit, not validator stake or slashable consensus collateral, so D7 is unaffected by it; the amounts are unmeasured. (S5 §4: the bond is at risk even when the recovery succeeds, and escalation counts attempts.)
Completion transition (named L1 event)A named transition on the recovery record, callable by any account no earlier than T_RECOVERY_DELAY after the invocation and while the same bond remains posted. It MUST revert when the cancellation predicate above holds; otherwise it writes resumeHeight, increments recoveryGeneration by exactly one, and pays the refundable balance to the address that posted the bond. The retirement and the new generation take effect for L2 nodes only when the completion transaction is Ethereum-final under SYS-02. The transition accepts no parameter that influences the restored checkpoint, the validator set or any configuration value. (S5 §2: completion is a named L1 transition, callable by any account, effective at the SYS-02 depth.)
Retired heightsThe completion transition records on L1 a resumeHeight equal to the last discarded height plus one. Discarded heights are permanently retired: they can never be certified or landed again, at any generation, by anyone. A batch whose first height is below resumeHeight is rejected by the contiguity rule of L1-06, correct validators must not sign a proposal below it (CONS-01), and a retired height can never be re-certified (CONS-12). resumeHeight is monotonically non-decreasing across generations and is zero before the first completed recovery. Retirement is bounded: resumeHeight − lastLandedHeight ≤ D_MAX. (S5 §1: discarded heights are retired, not re-opened by a new generation.)
No discretionThe invoker chooses nothing: the restored checkpoint is the current L1 checkpoint, the validator set is the one the L1 staking contract already fixes for that epoch, and no configuration value may be changed by recovery. Recovery re-enters the epoch schedule at that point. This is the rule that prevents recovery from becoming a configuration-capture mechanism.
Late certificatesA certificate for a discarded height is void for canonical purposes above the restored checkpoint, but remains admissible as evidence: if the same validator set signed conflicting certificates, the evidence rules of ECON-04 still apply, and a certificate that was revealed late is not thereby excused. Eligibility to invoke recovery must not depend on the absence of unpublished certificates.
Late proofsA proof for a height below resumeHeight is void because the height is retired: a batch may not start below resumeHeight at any generation, by anyone (L1-06). The restored checkpoint is the predecessor of the discarded range, and the proof is rejected by the retirement rule and by the current generation; the earlier justification for this rule is deleted with the claim it rested on. The data commitment may be re-batched, by any proposer, only as part of a new batch that starts at or above resumeHeight and is proved against the restored checkpoint. (S5 §1: the false claim about the predecessor is deleted and replaced by the retirement rule.)
Transaction replayL2 transactions in discarded blocks are not replayed automatically and are not guaranteed inclusion. Replay protection is expressed only in the preserved encodings of MSG-02: recovery MUST NOT introduce an additional replay key, and any change to either encoding is the migration MSG-02 already defines. (i) L1→L2 messages. The Bridge authenticates an L1→L2 message by the SIGNAL storage slot keccak256(abi.encodePacked("SIGNAL", chainId, app, signal)), and the message's execution receipt is part of L2 state. A message whose receipt lies inside the discarded range is rolled back with that range, becomes unexecuted again, and MAY be relayed again: that is the correct behaviour, not a double-spend, because the first execution no longer exists in canonical state. A message whose receipt is at or below the restored checkpoint persists and MUST NOT execute twice: the receiving Bridge's per-message status (NEW / RETRIABLE / DONE / FAILED / RECALLED) is L2 state at or below the checkpoint, so a message already DONE, FAILED or RECALLED stays terminal across the recovery. (ii) L2→L1 withdrawals. A withdrawal is executed on L1 against the bridge message hash keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_MESSAGE", message)), with srcChainId and destChainId inside the struct, proved against a stored checkpoint's state root (MSG-03). Because a completed recovery restores the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, every checkpoint against which a withdrawal had already been executed is at or below the restored checkpoint, and each such checkpoint remains canonical L2 state at or below the recovery floor. An L1-executed withdrawal therefore always remains backed by L2 state below the floor — that is why the floor is safe — and no L1-side record of an executed withdrawal is ever reversed by recovery: recovery writes no L1 bridge state. A withdrawal whose proof was never submitted is simply unexecuted and may be submitted again against the restored checkpoint. (iii) Deposits. The L1-side deposit transaction is never reversed by recovery — it is L1 state. The L2-side credit is not unconditional: a credit that lies inside the discarded range is erased with the range and MUST be re-relayed through the preserved L1→L2 path against the restored checkpoint; only a credit at or below the restored checkpoint remains credited. This closes A3: the replay rule is restated in the preserved signal-slot and bridge-message encodings (no invented key), the re-relay of a rolled-back L1→L2 message is stated as correct rather than a double-spend, the floor's safety for executed withdrawals is stated as its reason, and the unconditional "deposits remain credited" claim is corrected.
Derived rollback depthAt most D_MAX L2 blocks, because HALT-03 caps the unsettled depth, and the retirement record is correspondingly small: resumeHeight − lastLandedHeight ≤ D_MAX (L1-06). Conditional: the bound holds only while the cap is enforced; it is not a bound on loss. (S5 §1: retirement is a bounded record.)
Derived rollback durationT_STALL + T_RECOVERY_DELAY + resume time, all terms unmeasured except that T_STALL must exceed the D6 envelope by construction.
Repeated recoveryThere is no "freshness" condition, because none would be observable; what the rules enforce is a cooldown plus escalation. A new invocation is permitted only after REC_COOLDOWN has elapsed since the previous attempt — block.timestamp − lastAttemptAt ≥ REC_COOLDOWN, read from the stored attempt clock — and only while the objective trigger predicate above holds; the non-refundable component B_REC_KEEP(e) escalates with the number of attempts in the preceding REC_WINDOW (invocations, not completions, per the formula above), so the amount that is never returned rises with repetition. A repeat invocation after a completed recovery is permitted, and is a harmless no-op when nothing above the checkpoint has been certified since: retirement retires nothing new, resumeHeight is monotone so the claim cannot lower it, and the checkpoint and state are unchanged; the invoker gains nothing and pays the non-refundable component. Repetition is expensive but is neither forbidden nor bounded by these rules alone (REC-03). This closes review round 4 finding R4-MB-03: the unimplementable "fresh stall" requirement is replaced by the enforceable cooldown gate and attempts-based escalation, and the harmless repeat is stated honestly. (S5 §4: escalation counts attempts, and the cooldown follows every attempt.)
User exposureEvery L2 outcome above the checkpoint: inclusions, ordering, balances, bridge messages not yet settled, and any economic position built on them. No universal bound on economic loss exists and none may be claimed; losses are not compensated by protocol penalties (ECON-11).
D5 is unchangedRecovery is not a batch: it accepts no data, advances no checkpoint forward, and may not create a data-first path. D5 holds in full (L1-01, L1-03), and the transaction that invokes recovery is not a batch-acceptance entry point. Mode B changes the guarantee attached to unsettled confirmations; it does not relax the data-and-proof rule.
Authorization bindingThe configuration hash and an integer recovery generation are public inputs of the proof statement (PRF-02, L1-05). A batch certified under generation g is void once generation g+1 is active, and the guest MUST check the generation, so an alternative history is never accepted on execution validity alone. The generation counter stays, but only as a cheap invalidation of in-flight proofs: the security property rests on the retired heights below resumeHeight, which no generation can re-open. (S5 §1: the generation stays as a cheap invalidation; the security property rests on retired heights.)
Permissionless, no rescueRecovery remains permissionless: no operator, no DAO rescue and no discretion over the restored checkpoint or the validator set. HALT-04 stays in force.
Why this is the bounded rollback the user selected: under Mode A an availability failure produces a halt with no in-protocol remedy, and the requirement set does not accept a chain that can stay dead indefinitely. Without this specification the fallback would be improvised by an implementer — the worst outcome for both safety and honesty — so the trigger, bond, delay, cancellation, replay and exposure are stated normatively and carried with the resistance analysis of REC-03. Assumed unmeasured (the mechanism is normative; every timing and bond term is unmeasured until the economics spike, and the resistance argument of REC-03 is not yet independently reviewed)
REC-03 — resistance analysis: what it costs to induce recovery, and what cannot be bounded Premises, stated separately from the conclusion. The claim below is conditional on all six, and none may be silently dropped: (P1) A-CONS-1 — fewer than one third of the voting power is Byzantine, in every epoch; (P2) A-CONS-2 — after GST, delivery among correct validators is timely, so finalization continues with the honest supermajority (CONS-07); (P3) A-DA-2 — at least one honest, adequately-resourced prover completes each committed batch's proof inside the envelope, with the batch data obtainable (A-CONS-5, DA-01); (P4) A-L1-1 — L1 includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below finality; (P5) the landing market is funded — the fee sweep actually credits the reward pool and the reward available to a lander is not below the cost of landing, so landing is not a zero-reward race (L1-11, ECON-02 clauses 5 and 7; L1-11 deliberately permits rewardPaid = 0, so this is an economic premise and not a rule); and (P6) T_RECOVERY_DELAY is at least the time an honest prover needs to land a batch once the stall ends. P5 and P6 are premises, not measurements: every value they refer to is unmeasured until Phase B (09). (S5 §10: the old analysis described a mechanism whose authorization was a tautology; this is a re-derivation around the repaired mechanism, with the premises stated separately from the claim; round 4, R4-MB-01: the resistance claim is stated with its unmeasured inputs named as Phase B outputs, not left as a disclosure item.) The claim, restricted to what the repaired mechanism supports. Within P1–P6, a sub-threshold coalition — any set of participants holding less than one third of the voting power — cannot induce the trigger. The argument uses only the repaired mechanism: finalization continues with the honest supermajority (P1, P2); proposer selection is weighted by committed stake and the slot rotates, so a coalition selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation cannot censor indefinitely (CONS-06); data is served (P3); proving is permissionless and rewarded, and any account may land a proof and collect the reward (L1-10, L1-11); and L1 includes the landing transaction (P4). The checkpoint therefore keeps advancing: lastLandedHeight strictly increases, T_STALL is built from the permitted proof latency, the settlement pipeline, L1 finality and a margin and is strictly larger than the D6 envelope (REC-02), and no interval of length T_STALL passes without an accepted batch extending the checkpoint (L1-06), so T_STALL never elapses and the trigger of REC-02 is never reachable by that coalition. This is a claim about rule-legal behaviour under P1–P6; everything outside them is a residual or a falsifier below. What inducing the trigger would actually require. Inducing the trigger means holding the checkpoint still for the whole window, so it requires breaking P3, P4 or P5, and there are exactly three ways: (i) suppress every prover, so no proof exists to land (P3 fails); (ii) censor every landing transaction at L1, so a produced proof is never included (P4 fails); or (iii) price the protocol out of the blob market for the whole stall window, so no landing transaction is includable at a payable cost (P5 fails) — an L1 block-space and market-power condition, not an L2 action (L1-01, ECON-02 clause 1). Each lies outside the L2 fault model and each has a cost, expressed symbolically as cost(n attempts) = Σk≤n(B_REC_KEEP(ek)) + escalation(n) + cooldown(n) + gasL1(n) + V_own: B_REC_KEEP is the non-refundable bond component paid at every attempt; the posted bond escalates across attempts as B_REC(e) = B_REC_BASE · 2attempts with attempts counted in REC_WINDOW; REC_COOLDOWN follows every attempt, so attempts cannot be amortised; gasL1 is the L1 cost of invocation and completion; and V_own is the value the attacker holds inside the provisional range, which its own rollback destroys, bounded by value_at_risk(D_MAX) — the fees and bridge value the range carries (REC-02). Add what it costs to buy (i), (ii) or (iii): paying or denying every prover, censoring L1, or holding blob space for T_STALL; those resources are outside the protocol's accounting. The refundable balance is not relief: it returns only if the recovery completes, and completion is the event that destroys V_own, while cancellation sends the whole bond to the treasury (ECON-06(6)). Every value above is unmeasured and a Phase B output — B_REC_BASE, B_REC_KEEP, REC_WINDOW, REC_COOLDOWN, value_at_risk(D_MAX), the gas terms and T_STALL (09). The structure of the argument does not depend on the values: it needs only the relation B_REC_KEEP ≥ value_at_risk(D_MAX) and the ordering of the terms — a non-refundable component on every attempt, escalation across attempts, a cooldown, gas, and the attacker's own destroyed value — not any particular number. (S5 §4: the bond is non-refundable in part and escalation counts attempts, so a completed recovery is not free; round 4, "the bond is free on success" and "the landing market is unpriced" — the attacker's cost is stated instead of assumed.) Retirement, and why reinstatement is not a route. The repair also removed the reinstatement route: a completed recovery records resumeHeight on L1, and the discarded heights are permanently retired — a batch whose first height is below resumeHeight is rejected by the contiguity rule (L1-06), a correct validator must not sign at a retired height (CONS-01(vii)), and a retired height can never be re-certified under any generation, by anyone (CONS-12). A branch descending from the discarded range therefore cannot be re-proven and landed back into canonical history, whoever pays for the proof: the acceptance rule is about where a batch starts, not which proof was used. The recoveryGeneration counter remains a public input of the proof statement and invalidates in-flight proofs made before the completion (PRF-02, L1-05 row 31); it is a cheap invalidation only, and the security property rests on the retired heights. (S5 §1: the generation binds the proof, retirement binds the height; this closes round 4's R4-MB-02 and the consensus angle's Critical — a discarded but certified branch can no longer be reinstated.) (S5 §2: completion takes effect only at the SYS-02 depth, so there is no window in which a completed recovery and a batch at a retired height are both true.) Residuals, stated honestly. (1) Off-chain profit from a rolled-back state — a position closed elsewhere, a withdrawal already credited off-chain, MEV extracted around the discarded range — is outside any protocol bound, and protocol penalties are not compensation (ECON-11). (2) An adversary who can censor L1, or buy inclusion delay, can attempt repeated recoveries: the non-refundable bond and REC_COOLDOWN make each attempt expensive and escalation counts attempts, but they do not bound the number or the total cost of attempts (REC-02; 09). (3) An unfunded or below-cost reward pool reaches the trigger with no adversary at all: if the sweep does not credit the pool, or the reward is below the cost of landing, no rational prover lands, P5 fails, and the stall is economics rather than attack. Recovery cannot repair an economics stall — it restores a checkpoint and retires a range, and it changes no reward, no fee and no subsidy — so recovery must never be presented as the remedy for an unfunded reward pool. (S5 §10: the off-chain-profit residual stays outside any protocol bound; S5 §7 and §9: the sweep is protocol-constructed and the pool identity includes the proving-share outflow, which is what makes the funded-market premise checkable; round 4, "T_STALL has no inclusion-delay term" and "the landing market is unpriced" — the unfunded-pool and bought-delay cases are now residuals and falsifiers.) Recovery is therefore a manipulation primitive with a price, not a free option — and the price is only as real as the pre-registered values, which are unmeasured. Falsifiers, explicitly listed and complete. The argument fails, and this analysis must be withdrawn, if any of the following holds: (a) proving ceases to be permissionless in practice — a gate, a whitelist, or a de-facto single prover that can be pressured — so P3's prover cannot be replaced (L1-10); (b) T_RECOVERY_DELAY is shorter than the time an honest prover needs to land a batch, so a recovery can complete before honest progress cancels it (REC-02); (c) the non-refundable bond component is set below the value at risk — B_REC_KEEP < value_at_risk(D_MAX) — so a completed recovery costs the attacker less than the value it destroys (REC-02; 09); (d) the reward pool is empty, or the reward is below the cost of landing, so no rational prover lands (P5); (e) inclusion delay is purchasable for longer than T_STALL, so a funded, permissionless prover still cannot land inside the window (P4, P5); (f) retirement is not enforced on the acceptance path — the contiguity rule of L1-06 or the validators' refusal to sign below resumeHeight (CONS-01(vii)) — so a discarded height can be landed again. A reviewer who exhibits any of (a)–(f) falsifies the resistance claim and reopens the D-7 selection.
