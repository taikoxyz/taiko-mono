06 — Withholding, safe halt, restart, and governance stall resolution of unsettled history

A PoS confirmation above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is provisional. Under user decision D-15 the permissionless, bonded recovery is withdrawn from v1 and replaced by a timelocked, resume-only governance action (REC-02, owned by GOV-04): settlement may resume from the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch — and everything above it is discarded (REC-01, REC-02). At or below that checkpoint nothing may be changed, and Ethereum finality is the separate, stronger property of STATUS-07. Users are protected by the exit guarantee of MEM-15 — a withdrawal proven against the last accepted checkpoint, with no new L2 blocks, no quorum and no validator — which the timelock T_GOV_RESUME must cover, not by a recovery mechanism; whether it does is Open in REC-03. Any interface that shows a confirmation above the last accepted checkpoint must say that it can be replaced. (user decision D-15: the permissionless recovery of D-7 is withdrawn; the guarantee class of a confirmation above the checkpoint is provisional and the fallback is a timelocked, resume-only governance action that discards every height above the checkpoint.)

REC-01 — recovery boundary: no path may replace history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint No contract function, client rule, timeout, validator rotation, admission rule, governance proposal executed through the ordinary upgrade path, operational procedure, or recovery invocation may change the canonical history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch — and an executed stall resolution restores exactly that checkpoint. Above it, history is provisional and may be replaced only by the stall-resolution path of REC-02, under its objective trigger, timelock, resume-only and void-on-progress rules. Ethereum finality is a separate and stronger property, not the recovery floor: once the accepting L1 transaction is in a finalized Ethereum block, even an L1 reorganisation cannot undo it (STATUS-07); until then an accepted checkpoint is revocable only by an L1 reorganisation below finality (STATUS-06, L1-12), so an implementer must never read that property back as the recovery floor. Concretely: (a) no function exists whose effect is to replace an accepted block hash at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint or to discard state at or below it; the only downward movement of the canonical L2 chain is an executed stall resolution restoring exactly that checkpoint (REC-02); (b) no admission rule may reject a batch that extends the current L1 checkpoint (L1-04); (c) a certificate is judged under the validator set of its own epoch and stays valid as evidence forever (CONS-08), so validator rotation cannot re-judge it, and a certificate above a restored checkpoint is void for canonical purposes once its signed generation is superseded, but remains admissible as evidence (REC-02); (d) no upgrade may be used as a substitute for recovery: an upgrade must accept every history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint (GOV-03), and history above that checkpoint may be replaced only by the rule-bound stall resolution of REC-02 and never by the ordinary upgrade path (GOV-04 is that rule-bound action, and it is resume-only rather than a discretionary rescue); (e) no data-first path in the sense this rule fixes may be introduced to "work around" an unsettled batch, in recovery or otherwise, because that would let an unproven history be treated as settled (L1-02, HALT-04). Publication is not that path (D-11): a batch's data MAY be published to L1 before its proof and recorded as a publication record, because publication writes no checkpoint, advances no protocol state and becomes relevant only when a later proof references it; what D5 forbids, and what this clause forbids, is a path by which a checkpoint or any other protocol state advances on data alone. The proving deadline that bounds a published-but-unproven record, and its discard-and-republish consequence, are owned by 04 and are not restated here. (D-11: data may be published before the proof; publication writes no checkpoint, so it is not a data-first path in this rule's sense.) (user decision D-15: the boundary is unchanged — nothing at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is ever rewritten and history above it is provisional; what changes is the replacement path, now the timelocked, resume-only governance stall resolution of REC-02 rather than the permissionless bonded recovery of D-7.) This rule is the boundary between the two guarantee classes: no recovery may change history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and history above it is provisional. Its violation would turn the bounded rollback of REC-02 into unbounded history replacement, which no user decision authorises. Halting remains the correct local action when settlement cannot proceed; the stall resolution is the only sanctioned way history above the checkpoint may be replaced, and it is objective, timelocked and resume-only. This closes the reviewer finding that made the Ethereum-finalized checkpoint the recovery floor: the floor is the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and Ethereum finality is the separate, stronger property of STATUS-07 (D-15). Proven (structural: the boundary of the replacement window plus four positive obligations)

Safe halt

HALT-01 — safe halt obligation A correct validator halts — it stops signing and stops advancing its finalized view — rather than choose between two histories or advance on unknown state. It halts when any of the following holds and cannot be resolved by a rule-legal action: (a) it cannot obtain the data for a finalized or locked block (A-CONS-5 failed); (b) it cannot obtain a quorum for a height within the timeout ladder (A-CONS-2 failed); (c) the L1 facts it must consume are not yet Ethereum-final, or its L1 view disagrees with the consensus view (SYS-02); (d) the L1 validator-set commitment for the epoch it is about to enter is absent (CONS-13); (e) the unsettled-depth cap would be exceeded (HALT-03). Halting is a correct outcome, not a failure of the protocol: it is the action that preserves every height at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint and keeps the node inside its rules. A node that halts must keep serving what it has, and must keep its state so it can resume. Halting never authorises discarding history, and never authorises treating a publication as settlement: publishing a batch's data before its proof is permitted (D-11), but a publication record advances no checkpoint, so no participant may act on it as if a batch had landed (REC-01(e)). A validator's halt is a local safety action and is not a stall-resolution trigger: the action is available only while the objective settlement-stall condition of REC-02 holds, which is observable on L1. A halt caused by an unlandable batch is answered by retrying and by continuing to serve; if settlement nevertheless stalls past T_STALL_GOV, the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of REC-02 is the sanctioned remedy for that stall, and it is never declared early by a participant's local view. Recovery does not clear every halt: it changes no validator set and cannot manufacture a missing epoch-set entry or an unavailable quorum, so a halt under (b) or (d) persists until the entry is appended and Ethereum-final or the quorum returns, and invoking the stall resolution there discards the provisional range without restoring liveness (LIVE-01). One legal route can restore a quorum, and it is not the stall resolution: an entry that has not attested within HEARTBEAT_WINDOW is excluded from a future set version's root and the rotation of CONS-16 can move the resumed heights onto that version, so where (b) is caused by validators that have stopped attesting the halt is escapable at a later boundary — both rules are stated once on their own pages and are cross-referenced, not restated, here. (decision D-14: heartbeat eligibility plus the rotation, not recovery, is the route out of an unattested-quorum halt.) This closes review round 4 finding R4-LIV-09: recovery is no longer presented as the remedy for halts it cannot clear. (user decision D-15: a halt is still correct, but it is no longer the only end state — the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution exists for the settlement-stall case and only for that case, and no participant can invoke it on its own.) Failure modes: an implementer, faced with a stuck chain, invents an "unstick" action; or a participant treats its own halt as authority to roll history back. Neither is permitted — the only sanctioned replacement path is REC-02, a timelocked governance action that honest progress voids and that no participant can invoke on its own. Proven Assumed (the halt conditions are normative; whether they are met is assumed)
HALT-02 — restart rules After a halt, a correct participant resumes only from the highest finalized block whose data and certificate it can independently reconstruct, or — if a stall resolution has executed — from the checkpoint restored by that resolution (REC-02). It must never adopt an alternative history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and must never treat a timeout during the halt as evidence that a certificate does not exist (STATUS-10). On restart: (a) each validator enforces a signed-message uniqueness check over its own signing history — no message of the same type at the same height, round and recovery generation may be signed twice, including after a restart or a state resync, and a validator that cannot prove it has not already signed for a (height, round, type, generation) must not sign (the amnesia case is treated as a signing fault, not as a fresh start); (b) a validator that resumes must re-verify the epoch and set for the height it is resuming at rather than trusting its cached set; (c) nodes must not accept a replayed historical message as new — every vote carries the epoch and round, and the receiving rules reject messages for heights already finalized; (d) a resumed node's first act is a data and certificate sync, not a proposal. After an executed stall resolution the restored checkpoint is the highest canonical checkpoint: a node must adopt it, must not treat a discarded block above it as finalized, and must apply every clause of this rule unchanged — no double signing, no replayed message, no ordering by timeout. The resumed chain begins at resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1; the restored checkpoint block is its immediate predecessor, there are no skipped heights and no recovery anchor, and the ordinary contiguity rule of CONS-01(iii) and the ordinary anchor duty of CONS-09(1)–(2) apply unchanged, with B_anchor the restored checkpoint block at firstHeight − 1 (REC-04). Nothing in the discarded range is ever a header a node must obtain, and no discarded block is ever a finalized height. (user decision D-15: restart follows the highest L1-accepted checkpoint, the resumed chain has no height gap, and the withdrawn recovery linkage and recovery anchor do not apply.) Failure modes addressed: "amnesia" double-signing after a restart, replaying old votes, and a restart that silently adopts a competing branch — the classic way a safe halt becomes an unsafe restart. Proven (the signing-history rule rests on the validator's own durable state, an implementation gate)
HALT-03 — consensus-enforced unsettled-depth cap (backpressure) Define the unsettled depth at a height as the number of L2 blocks between the highest L1-accepted checkpoint and the block being considered. A correct validator must not prevote or precommit a block whose unsettled depth would exceed D_MAX − MARGIN_V. Exactly one enforcement point: the validators. land MUST NOT reject an over-depth batch, because doing so could make a legitimately finalized range permanently unlandable and would violate L1-04 (*review round 2, finding R2-LIV-07*). D_MAX is derived from the retention window, the permitted proof latency and the settlement pipeline, and not from the retention window alone: D_MAX = floor((RETENTION_WINDOW − T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED − T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL), recorded with its derivation in 09. MARGIN_V is mandatory and is at least the number of L2 blocks produced during one Ethereum finality depth, because a validator's view of the L1 checkpoint is itself only usable at that depth (SYS-02); without the margin a validator can systematically under-estimate depth and overshoot the cap. The cap is a consensus rule with a single enforcement point. Honest statement of what that costs: because the cap cannot be enforced at L1 without stranding a legitimate extension, a quorum that ignores it is not objectively punishable at L1, and a cap violation is therefore a liveness failure that is disclosed rather than a slashing offence (WH-02, LIM-01). When the cap binds, production stops and the chain is in the safe-halt state until provers catch up. The stall resolution does not relax the cap: after execution the L1 checkpoint is restored and production resumes under the same cap, the cap binding is a halt and not by itself a resolution trigger, and only the objective T_STALL_GOV condition of REC-02 can make the action available. (user decision D-15: the unsettled-depth cap is unchanged by the selected fallback; the stall resolution may not be used to advance past it or to bypass it.) The cap must also be jointly satisfiable with the evidence window: the coupling inequality is stated normatively in ECON-07(7), and D_MAX must be reduced until it holds (*review round 2, finding P-R2-08*). It is also jointly constrained with the D6 envelope, and RETENTION_WINDOW is chosen from that constraint rather than independently: a proof that consumes the whole envelope must leave an unsettled depth at or below the depth at which validators stop, so D_MAX · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL ≥ T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL, equivalently D_MAX − MARGIN_V ≥ (T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL (the margin term is MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL, in seconds, because MARGIN_V is measured in L2 blocks). Because this rule derives D_MAX from RETENTION_WINDOW, the inequality is also a lower bound on the retention window: in particular RETENTION_WINDOW MUST be large enough that floor((RETENTION_WINDOW − T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED − T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL) − MARGIN_V ≥ (T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE) / L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL (a sufficient form is RETENTION_WINDOW ≥ T_PROOF_ENVELOPE + T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED + 2 · T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + MARGIN_V · L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL + L2_BLOCK_INTERVAL), so the retention window is derived backwards from the inequality, never selected on its own and checked afterwards. If the inequality is violated, D_MAX − MARGIN_V can be zero or negative, in which case no block at a positive unsettled depth may be voted and production cannot advance past the current checkpoint. The envelope term is D6's planning assumption T_PROOF_ENVELOPE, not T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED (the permitted bound of 09): the promise is that a normal proof never binds the cap. This closes review round 3 finding R3-LIV-02: HALT-03 carries the joint cap/envelope inequality, so a normal 30-minute proof can never trigger the cap, and RETENTION_WINDOW is stated to be chosen from that inequality rather than independently. D_MAX is a bound on commitment creation, not on proving latency, and a normal proof taking the whole of the D6 envelope (up to 30 minutes, i.e. 900 blocks at 2 s) is not misconduct and not grounds for any recovery action. Failure modes addressed: unbounded growth of unsettled history (which is exactly the state in which an availability failure becomes unrecoverable), and an implementer treating a slow proof as a fault. Proven unmeasured (D_MAX's value depends on measured retention and proving throughput)
HALT-04 — no emergency operator, no discretionary DAO rescue, no data-first path; the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution is the only named exception No emergency key, guardian, or operational procedure may be required to preserve any invariant of this specification, and none may be introduced to "resolve" a halt. There is exactly one named exception: the rule-bound, timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of REC-02/GOV-04. It is not a DAO rescue: governance chooses no state, no checkpoint, no height, no validator set and no configuration value; its effect is resume-only; its timelock T_GOV_RESUME cannot be shortened for an entry already posted and is the exit window of MEM-15; and an accepted extending batch voids the entry, which any account may cancel permissionlessly. Nothing else may be required and no other DAO intervention is permitted: specifically, there is no function that a privileged party can call to advance, rewind, or replace the checkpoint; and no publication may be used to unblock settlement: publishing a batch's data before its proof is permitted (D-11), but the publication record advances no checkpoint and confers no status, so it can never substitute for a proof (REC-01(e)). The only sanctioned way that history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint may be replaced is the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of REC-02, which grants no operator, no DAO and no validator quorum any discretion, and which never replaces history at or below that checkpoint (REC-01). If progress requires information or participation that no rule-legal action can obtain, the protocol says so and stops; it does not promote a privileged fix. A stall that no valid action can clear — including one governance never queues or never executes — stays a disclosed halt rather than being "fixed" outside the rules; clearing a stall therefore depends on governance liveness with no protocol-level bound. (user decision D-15: the timelocked stall resolution is the only sanctioned way history above the checkpoint may be replaced, and it is discretionary in nothing — no emergency operator, no discretionary DAO rescue, no data-first path; the exception is named once, at the top of this rule, and the governance-liveness disclosure above is kept.) Failure modes: a halt is quietly "fixed" by an operator, which both violates R2 and disguises a liveness limitation as a functioning protocol; or recovery is captured by a privileged party, which would be the same failure under a different name. Proven (enforced by the migration's removal of privileged levers, MIG-04)

WH-02 — objective evidence versus local suspicion A protocol-level penalty requires objective, self-contained, on-chain verifiable evidence: two signed messages of the same type at the same height and round with different block ids (CONS-11), or a signed message that violates a rule whose inputs are entirely on-chain. Everything else — a message that did not arrive, a height that did not advance, a proof that has not been submitted, a peer that did not answer a request — is local suspicion. Local suspicion may trigger local actions (retry, view change, reputation, peer selection, disclosure) but may never trigger a penalty, a recovery action, or a statement about what exists elsewhere. The stall-resolution trigger is not an exception: it is the settlement-stall condition of REC-02 measured on L1, no participant's local view of a stall may shorten T_STALL_GOV or declare the stall resolved early, and non-receipt of a batch, proof or certificate remains local suspicion. (user decision D-15: the trigger remains an objective L1-observable predicate; local suspicion still never triggers a penalty or a stall-resolution action.) (D-12 supersedes D-6 here: the omission of a due published record within the capped FIFO prefix is a proposal-validity failure (CONS-01(v)) and a breach (ECON-04 clause (6)), established by the proof-enforced predicate of FI-11 and its own evidence encoding in ECON-04 clause (6), never by non-receipt or local suspicion; the obligation is bounded to a published, recorded item and survives a completed recovery unchanged (FI-14), so a rollback cannot launder an omission and the narrow rule can never reach a publication that L1 refuses to include; for unpublished transactions the protocol has no inclusion duty, and LIVE-04 states what inclusion resistance remains.) (D-12 / L1-censorship residual: this note crosses to FI-14's recovery-survival clause and to ECON-04 clause (6) rather than restating them, and states that a publication L1 refuses to include never becomes a record the narrow rule can reach.) Failure modes addressed: false accusations, and the more dangerous error of treating a timeout as a fault ("the sequencer was slashed after a landing deadline" is exactly the reasoning this rule forbids). WH-03 — false positives and false negatives, labelled unmeasured The specification must state, for every detection rule, both error directions: a false positive penalises a correct participant (for example, a validator whose vote was merely delayed being treated as absent), and a false negative lets a faulty participant escape (for example, a one-off selective-delivery attack that leaves no signed conflict). For each rule the specification states which direction is worse and why, and records that the rates are unmeasured with what would measure them (a fault-injection testbed with a modelled adversarial network). No rule may claim a detection rate. Failure mode: an unmeasured detection rate presented as a guarantee, or a design where the only safe path is perfect detection. unmeasured
WH-04 — reveal-later certificate: the analysis across locks, view changes and reconfiguration The protocol must resist the following schedule, which is mandatory in this specification: (1) a proposer selectively distributes a block to a subset; (2) honest validators vote according to their local views; (3) the attacker privately obtains a finality-related certificate; (4) other participants attempt a leader change, a validator change, or a stall resolution; (5) the attacker reveals the certificate after the alternative path has progressed. A stall resolution is an alternative path only above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, and it restores exactly that checkpoint: a certificate revealed later for a discarded height is evidence against an equivocator when it conflicts under the same generation, never a second canonical history (REC-02). The required conclusion is therefore that two conflicting histories cannot both be canonical at the same time: at or below the checkpoint neither conflicting history can receive a replacement guarantee at all, and above it a completed recovery leaves the restored checkpoint as the only canonical one. That conclusion rests on the conjunction of three mechanisms, never on signature counting alone: (user decision D-15: the reveal-later schedule now includes a rule-bound stall-resolution path, and the conclusion is scoped to the provisional window above the checkpoint.)
  • Locks. A validator that precommitted a block at round R is locked on it and will not prevote a conflicting block at that height unless it sees a proof-of-lock-change at a strictly later round (CONS-04). Because a certificate implies more than one third of the voting power is correct and locked, no conflicting commit can form. This is why an unpublished certificate is harmless to safety: it is the lock, not the publication, that protects the decision.
  • View changes. A view change may change the proposer, but it may not unlock a validator that has not seen a later-round proof-of-lock-change. Therefore step (4)'s "alternative path" cannot produce a conflicting commit merely because the original certificate was hidden.
  • Reconfiguration. No lock state crosses an epoch boundary. The lock is per height (CONS-04(1)): it is released by that height's commit or by a strictly later same-height proof-of-lock-change, and CONS-09(3) states that a validator carries no lock state from a height of epoch e into e+1; a lock formed at a height of e on a value that did not finalize there is resolved at that height and is not carried forward as an independent constraint. Cross-height continuity is therefore exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii), enforced at the boundary by the anchor duty of CONS-09(1)–(2): the first block of the new epoch must descend from the last block finalized in the closing epoch. The handoff's protection is that a certificate is judged under its own epoch's set and remains valid forever (CONS-08), so the closing epoch's decision is not re-judged by the new set and a stakeholder that rotates keys or changes its stake cannot escape it; a certificate's canonical status is generation-scoped — an executed stall resolution voids certificates of the superseded generation at the acceptance rule while leaving their evidentiary value untouched (CONS-12, REC-02) — and a validator that equivocates across the boundary is caught by a same-(height, round) conflicting pair under the same generation, the only lock-related offence with admissible evidence (ECON-04). This closes review round 3 finding R3A-03: the retracted cross-height lock carry-over is removed from WH-04's reconfiguration mechanism, and the protection it names is the parent-validity condition plus epoch-scoped permanent certificates, not a surviving lock.
Failure mode: a design in which the alternative path discards the per-height lock within a height, or in which a validator-set change re-judges a certificate under a new set instead of under its own epoch's set (CONS-08), in which case the reveal-later schedule succeeds and two conflicting histories can both be treated as canonical without any slashable equivocation. (Under the selected fallback the second history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can still be discarded by a stall resolution, but the lock and reconfiguration mechanisms above are what prevent the conflict from arising in the first place, and the generation barrier is what keeps the discarded branch out of canonical history.) Proven Assumed (premises: A-CONS-1 in every epoch; correct validators enforce CONS-02/CONS-04; the boundary argument of CONS-09, which is the named review target F1)

Governance stall resolution — the selected fallback (user decision D-15)

The governance stall-resolution action is part of the active protocol (user decision D-15). A timelocked, resume-only governance action may resume settlement from the latest L1-accepted checkpoint and discard everything above it — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch, which the action restores exactly; at or below it nothing may be changed (REC-01). The action chooses no state, no checkpoint and no height: resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 is fixed by L1 state alone (REC-02). User protection is the exit guarantee of MEM-15, not a recovery mechanism; the action exists because an unbounded halt was judged worse than a bounded, rule-fixed discard, and its execution depends on governance liveness, which has no protocol-level bound (a disclosed social-layer assumption).

The guarantee attached to a confirmation above the last accepted checkpoint is PoS-certified / provisional (STATUS-04). Any interface that shows such a confirmation MUST say that it can be replaced; calling it irreversible is a defect. A PoS-certified block above the checkpoint is discarded by an executed stall resolution, its transactions are not replayed and must be resubmitted, and its effects are handled by the replay rules of REC-02. (user decision D-15: the fallback is a timelocked, resume-only governance action; the guarantee class above the checkpoint is provisional.)

REC-02 — timelocked, resume-only governance stall resolution (user decision D-15) The permissionless, bonded recovery of D-7 is withdrawn from v1 and replaced by a single timelocked, resume-only governance action, defined here and owned by GOV-04 (stall resolution). All of the following are normative. Every timing term below is unmeasured and is recorded with its derivation in 09; none is a measurement. (user decision D-15: the permissionless recovery is withdrawn; the trigger, the timelock, the full-range discard, the resume-only effect, the void-on-progress cancellation and the governance-liveness disclosure below replace it.)

InvariantA stall resolution may only discard history strictly above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the checkpoint record written by the most recently accepted batch. Objective triggerExactly one trigger, stated as a predicate over stored L1 state: a settlement stall holds iff block.timestamp − lastAcceptedBatchTime ≥ T_STALL_GOV when the action is evaluated, where lastAcceptedBatchTime is the stored L1 timestamp at which the most recent batch was accepted — the instant the current L1-accepted checkpoint was written (L1-07). Only the acceptance of a batch extending the checkpoint resets the clock; an executed action changes neither lastAcceptedBatchTime nor the checkpoint, so it does not restart the window except by allowing production to resume. T_STALL_GOV is registered with its unit and tag in 09, is built from the permitted proof latency, the settlement pipeline, L1 finality and a margin, and MUST be strictly larger than the D6 envelope so that a normal slow proof, or one prover failure inside the envelope, can never make the action available; every term is unmeasured. There is no other trigger and no early declaration: a participant's local view, a halt, a missing certificate, a slow proof and an over-depth cap are not triggers. (user decision D-15: the trigger is an objective L1-observable predicate; the action is never available on anyone's local view of a stall.)
Who acts, and what it costsThe action is taken by governance, through the ordinary governance path of GOV-01, by queueing the transition of GOV-04; there is no permissionless invoker, no bond, no escalation, no cooldown, no completion reward and no treasury transfer. Nothing is bought or sold: the action either clears a stall or is void, and the protocol pays no one for invoking it. Because there is no permissionless entry point, there is no spam surface for a bond to price and no reward to farm; the user-protection mechanism is the timelock below, not an economic bond. (user decision D-15: the bond, its escalation, the cooldown, the completion transition, its cancellation predicate and the completion reward are withdrawn with the permissionless invoker they priced.)
Effect: full-range discard, resumeHeight fixed by L1 state aloneExecution discards everything above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — not a certified subset — and resumes from that checkpoint. The first batch accepted afterwards begins at firstHeight = resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1, where lastLandedHeight is the height of the checkpoint record: the boundary is fixed by L1 state alone. There is no bundle, no certificate submission, no maximality question and nothing for L1 to verify beyond its own record: the action accepts no calldata that names a height, a hash or a claim, and the Inbox derives the resumed height from the checkpoint it already stores (L1-06, L1-07). A batch or certificate for a discarded height cannot be landed afterwards because its signed generation is superseded (Generation fix below). (user decision D-15: the full-range discard with resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 replaces the certified-subset retirement and its derivation.)
TimelockThe queued entry MUST NOT execute before T_GOV_RESUME has elapsed since it was posted, and T_GOV_RESUME MUST be long enough that every user can exit via MEM-15 — a withdrawal proven against the last accepted checkpoint, with no new L2 blocks, no quorum and no validator — before execution. Governance cannot shorten the timelock for a queue entry already posted: the delay is fixed when the entry is queued and no later transaction may reduce it. Whether the recorded value covers the worst-case exit time is unmeasured and is carried as Open in REC-03 with its falsifier. (user decision D-15: the timelock, not a bond, is what protects users; the action cannot execute before every user has had the chance to exit.)
Resume-only; no discretionGovernance chooses nothing: no state, no checkpoint, no height, no validator set and no configuration value. The restored checkpoint is the current L1 checkpoint, resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 follows from it, and the resumed heights are judged under the L1-committed set versions the epoch schedule assigns them (MEM-09, CONS-13). No rule changes a member's weight within a set version, and the only way a set changes is the eligibility boundary of MEM-13 combined with the rotation of CONS-16 (D-14). Nothing at or below the accepted checkpoint may be touched. This is the rule that keeps the action from becoming a configuration-capture or history-editing mechanism. (user decision D-15: the action is resume-only; it cannot choose a state, a checkpoint or a height.)
Void on progress, and permissionless cancellationIf a valid batch extending the checkpoint recorded when the entry was queued is accepted on L1 before the entry executes, the action is void: it MUST revert or be cancelled, and any account may cancel the queued entry permissionlessly. There is no bond to transfer and no treasury destination on cancellation; cancellation costs only the gas of the cancellation transaction, and the queued entry carries no value. Neither the reveal of a certificate nor of a proof for a discarded height voids the action by itself; only acceptance of an extending batch does. Execution takes effect for L2 nodes only when the executing transaction is Ethereum-final under SYS-02; if it is reorganised out below that depth it never took effect and the pre-resolution history stands. (user decision D-15: progress voids the action, and cancellation is permissionless and bondless.)
Generation fix: the generation is signed into the historyExecution increments the recovery generation by exactly one. The generation is a field of the signed vote and of the header bytes (CONS-01, CONS-02, CONS-10), and a certificate carries it and is valid only under the current generation (CONS-05, CONS-12). Validators sign the new generation after the increment, so old certificates are void at the acceptance rule, not merely stale in the proof: a batch whose certificate carries a superseded generation is rejected even though its proof verifies and even though every signature in it is authentic. This closes the round-4 finding that the generation bound the proof and not the history; because there is no recovery bundle to carry, the generation needs no retirement range to be sound. (user decision D-15: the recovery generation is a field of the signed vote and header bytes; the governance action increments it and validators sign the new generation.)
Late certificatesA certificate under a superseded generation is void for canonical purposes above the restored checkpoint, but remains admissible as evidence: if the same validator set signed conflicting certificates under the same generation, the evidence rules of ECON-04 still apply, and a certificate revealed late is not thereby excused. A pair whose signed generations differ is not a same-generation conflict (CONS-11). Eligibility to queue the action must not depend on the absence of unpublished certificates. (user decision D-15: superseded-generation certificates are void canonically and remain evidence; the generation is part of the conflict predicate.)
Late proofs and stalled transactionsA proof for a batch certified under a superseded generation is void at the acceptance rule, even though its height is not permanently retired: the height may be re-produced by any proposer as part of a new batch that begins at resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1, descends from the restored checkpoint and is certified under the new generation (L1-06). Transactions in discarded blocks are not replayed automatically and are not guaranteed inclusion; they must be resubmitted by their senders under the new generation. Replay protection is expressed only in the preserved encodings of MSG-02: a stall resolution MUST NOT introduce an additional replay key. (i) L1→L2 messages. The Bridge authenticates an L1→L2 message by the SIGNAL storage slot keccak256(abi.encodePacked("SIGNAL", chainId, app, signal)) and the execution receipt is L2 state: a message whose receipt lies inside the discarded range is rolled back with that range, becomes unexecuted again, and MAY be relayed again; a message whose receipt is at or below the restored checkpoint persists and MUST NOT execute twice. (ii) L2→L1 withdrawals. A withdrawal is executed on L1 against keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_MESSAGE", message)), proved against a stored checkpoint's state root (MSG-03): everything already executed on L1 was proved against a checkpoint at or below the restored checkpoint, so it remains backed by canonical L2 state; no L1-side record of an executed withdrawal is ever reversed (the resolution writes no L1 bridge state). (iii) Deposits. The L1-side deposit transaction is never reversed; a deposit credit inside the discarded range is erased with the range and MUST be re-relayed through the preserved L1→L2 path against the restored checkpoint. (user decision D-15: discarded transactions are not replayed and must be resubmitted; the preserved encodings and the L1-backed withdrawal guarantee are unchanged.)
Derived discard depthAt most D_MAX L2 blocks, because HALT-03 caps the unsettled depth. Conditional: the bound holds only while the cap is enforced; it is not a bound on loss, and an over-depth history is discarded in full too. (user decision D-15: the discard is the whole unsettled range above the checkpoint.)
Derived stall durationT_STALL_GOV + T_GOV_RESUME + resume time, all terms unmeasured except that T_STALL_GOV must exceed the D6 envelope by construction. (user decision D-15: the duration is the trigger window plus the timelock plus resume.)
Repeated actionThere is no protocol-level cooldown and no bond to escalate: while the trigger still holds, another entry may be queued and executed after its own timelock. Each execution increments the generation again and thereby voids the in-flight proofs and certificates of the then-current provisional range; when nothing above the checkpoint has been produced, the repeat is a no-op for state but still increments the generation. Repetition is limited by governance liveness and by nothing in these rules, and it is disclosed as such; no bond paces it, so the timelock and the governance process are the only pacing. (user decision D-15: repetition is unbounded by protocol rules and is part of the governance-liveness disclosure; there is no cooldown because there is no permissionless invoker.)
User exposureEvery L2 outcome above the checkpoint: inclusions, ordering, balances, bridge messages not yet settled, and any economic position built on them. No universal bound on economic loss exists and none may be claimed; losses are not compensated by protocol penalties (ECON-11). (user decision D-15: the provisional range is at risk and its transactions must be resubmitted.)
D5 as relaxed by D-11The action is not a batch: it accepts no data, advances no checkpoint forward, and may not introduce a path by which protocol state advances on data alone. A batch's data MAY have been published to L1 and recorded in an earlier transaction (L1-01, L1-02), so what D5 fixes is that the checkpoint advances only on a valid proof; the transaction that executes the action is not a batch-acceptance entry point. The action may neither create, suppress nor re-deadline a publication record: publication is permissionless and stays available while an entry is queued, and the proving deadline that bounds a published-but-unproven record is owned by 04 (the obligation survives an executed action unchanged, FI-14). D-15 changes the guarantee attached to unsettled confirmations and the path that clears a stall; it does not relax the proof requirement. (D-11/D-12: publication writes no state that decides anything until a proof references it, and no path may suppress the forced-inclusion record.)
Authorization bindingThe configuration hash and the recovery generation are public inputs of the proof statement (PRF-02, L1-05), and under D-15 the generation is also a field of the signed vote and header bytes, so it binds the signed history and not only the proof. A batch certified under generation g is void once generation g+1 is active, at the acceptance rule and not merely in the guest; the guest MUST still check the generation it is given, and the signed-vote and header fields are what make the older certificate inadmissible in the first place. (user decision D-15: the generation binding moves from the proof alone to the signed vote and header bytes, which is what closes the round-4 finding.)
Governance liveness disclosed; no privilege beyond the ruleClearing a stall depends on governance liveness, with no protocol-level bound. This is a social-layer assumption and is recorded as one: if governance never queues or never executes a valid action, the stall persists and the chain stays halted; the halt is safe but not live without governance (HALT-01, REC-03, LIVE-01). The action grants no emergency power: no operator, DAO or governance actor may choose a state, a checkpoint, a height, a validator set or a configuration value, and HALT-04 stays in force with its exception narrowed to this rule-bound transition. (user decision D-15: the fallback is a social-layer assumption, disclosed, and the action is resume-only.)
Why this replaces the permissionless recovery: recovery failed independent review three times (authorization tautology, an invoker-claimed retirement, and a completion transition L1 could not verify that a one-certificate bundle could wedge), and each repair opened a new finding. The replacement needs no bundle, no certificate, no maximality argument and no reference to a discarded branch's tip, because it discards the whole range above the checkpoint by a boundary fixed in L1 state alone. What it costs is a disclosed dependence on governance liveness and a generation change that voids certificates rather than retiring heights. The honest residual — a stalled transaction must be resubmitted, and a range L1 censors or never publishes cannot be forced — is stated in REC-03. Assumed unmeasured Open (the mechanism is normative; every timing term is unmeasured, and whether T_GOV_RESUME covers the worst-case MEM-15 exit is carried as Open in REC-03)
REC-03 — resistance analysis, residuals and falsifiers for the governance path (user decision D-15) Premises, stated separately from the conclusion. The claim below is conditional on all four, and none may be silently dropped: (P1) A-CONS-1 — fewer than one third of the voting power is Byzantine, in every epoch; (P2) A-CONS-2 — after GST, delivery among correct validators is timely, so finalization continues with the honest supermajority (CONS-07); (P3) A-DA-2 — at least one honest, adequately-resourced prover completes each committed batch's proof inside the envelope, and the batch data is obtainable by it; under D-11 the data reaching L1 no longer waits for the proof, so this premise is that the data is either served off-chain (A-CONS-5) or published to L1 by any observer before the proving deadline, with the publication recorded on L1 while the blobs are retrievable (DA-01); (P4) A-L1-1 — L1 includes valid transactions within a bounded delay and does not reorganise below finality. All four are assumptions, not measurements, and the economic conditions they touch are unmeasured until Phase B (09). (user decision D-15: the analysis is re-derived around the timelocked governance action; the bond, reward and invoker-incentive premises of the withdrawn analysis are removed with the mechanism they priced.)
The claim, restricted to what the replacement supports. Within P1–P4, a sub-threshold coalition — any set of participants holding less than one third of the voting power — cannot make the stall-resolution trigger true. Finalization continues with the honest supermajority (P1, P2); proposer selection is weighted by committed stake and the slot rotates, so a coalition selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation cannot censor indefinitely (CONS-06); data is served (P3) and, where a holder has the body, publication is permissionless, so any account may put it on L1 in its own transaction (ECON-13); proving is permissionless and rewarded, and any account may land a proof and collect the reward (L1-10, L1-11); and L1 includes the landing transaction (P4). The checkpoint therefore keeps advancing, lastLandedHeight strictly increases, T_STALL_GOV is built from the permitted proof latency, the settlement pipeline, L1 finality and a margin and is strictly larger than the D6 envelope (REC-02), and no interval of length T_STALL_GOV passes without an accepted batch extending the checkpoint (L1-06), so the trigger never becomes true and no entry can be validly queued. This is a claim about rule-legal behaviour under P1–P4; everything outside them is a residual or a falsifier below.
What making the trigger true would actually require. It means holding the checkpoint still for the whole window, so it requires breaking P3 or P4, and there are exactly three ways: (i) suppress every prover, so no proof exists to land (P3 fails); (ii) censor every landing transaction at L1, so a produced proof is never included (P4 fails); or (iii) price the protocol out of the blob market for the whole stall window, so no landing transaction is includable at a payable cost — an L1 block-space and market-power condition, not an L2 action (L1-01, ECON-02 clause 1).
What the replacement removes, and why that matters to resistance. There is no bond to size, no escalation, no cooldown, no completion transition, no cancellation predicate to define, no certificate bundle for L1 to verify, no maximality question about which certified heights to retire, and no completion reward: the action has no calldata that influences its outcome and no value transfer at all. An adversary therefore cannot buy a trigger, cannot farm a completion, and cannot exploit an under- or over-sized bundle; the only thing it can do is what (i)–(iii) describe — keep the checkpoint still — and the only response is governance executing a transition whose entire effect is fixed by L1 state. Conversely, a selective outcome is impossible by construction: the discard is the whole range above the checkpoint, so an attacker inside that range cannot save its own effects while destroying a counterparty's; above the restored checkpoint the ordinary replay rules apply uniformly (REC-02, Late proofs and stalled transactions). (user decision D-15: removing the permissionless machinery also removes the abuse surfaces the permissionless design had to price.)
The generation fix closes the round-4 finding. A full-range discard is sound only if the discarded branch cannot be re-landed. The generation is now a field of the signed vote and header bytes (CONS-01, CONS-02, CONS-10), the action increments it, validators sign the new generation, and a certificate whose generation is not current is void at the acceptance rule (CONS-05, CONS-12). A discarded block at resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 descends from the same restored checkpoint as the resumed first block, so contiguity alone cannot separate them; the signed generation is exactly what does. No retired-height record and no recovery anchor remain, and the lock scoping follows the generation rather than a separate carve-out (CONS-04(2), REC-04).
Residuals, stated honestly. (1) Governance liveness. Clearing a stall depends on governance queueing and executing a valid action, and there is no protocol-level bound on that; this is a social-layer assumption. If governance is absent, captured or slow, the stall persists: the halt is safe but not live without governance (LIVE-01). (2) Stalled transactions are discarded. Transactions in the discarded range are not replayed and are not guaranteed inclusion; they must be resubmitted by their senders, and protocol penalties are not compensation (ECON-11). (3) An L1-censored or never-published range cannot be forced. The action cannot make data available, force a publication into L1, or land a proof L1 refuses to include; a range whose data never reaches L1 is discarded and lost, and the narrow forced-inclusion rule of FI-10 cannot reach a publication L1 censors out (LIVE-04). (4) An economics stall is not repaired. If the fee sweep does not credit the reward pool, or the landing reward is below the cost of landing, the stall can arise with no adversary at all; the action resumes from a checkpoint and changes no reward, no fee and no subsidy, so it must never be presented as the remedy for an unfunded landing market (ECON-02). (5) Off-chain profit and loss from the discarded range are outside any protocol bound, and there is no universal bound on economic loss. (6) Repetition is unbounded by these rules: no cooldown and no bond pace repeated actions, so a second entry may be queued and executed while the trigger still holds, incrementing the generation again; this is part of the governance-liveness disclosure and not a guarantee. (7) The timelock's sufficiency is unmeasured: whether T_GOV_RESUME is long enough for every user to exit via MEM-15 is not established here. (user decision D-15: the residuals are the social-layer dependence, the discarded transactions, the unforceable censored range, the economics stall, unbounded repetition and the unmeasured timelock.)
Falsifiers, explicitly listed and complete. This analysis must be withdrawn if any of the following holds: (a) any path other than the rule-bound action of GOV-04/REC-02 can replace history above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, including an upgrade (REC-01(d)); (b) the action has any discretionary parameter — a state, a checkpoint, a height, a validator set, a configuration value — or can execute before its timelock; (c) a measured worst-case time to exit via MEM-15 exceeds the recorded T_GOV_RESUME; (d) the generation is not a field of the signed vote and header bytes in an implementation, or a validator signs or an acceptance path admits a certificate under a superseded generation, so a discarded branch can be re-landed (CONS-12); (e) acceptance admits a batch whose first height is not lastLandedHeight + 1 or whose ancestry descends from a discarded block; (f) the action can change history at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint; (g) a stalled-but-published range can be carried into the new generation without a fresh batch beginning at resumeHeight and descending from the restored checkpoint; (h) governance can shorten the timelock of a queue entry already posted. A reviewer who exhibits any of (a)–(h) falsifies the resistance claim; the Open items are (c) and the registered derivation of T_STALL_GOV in 09.
Review requirement. This analysis is an argument, not a measurement and not a proof, and it requires independent review before the D-15 selection is treated as settled. Reviewers must answer three questions: (1) can any rule-legal path other than the governed action replace history above the last accepted checkpoint? (2) is the generation binding complete end to end — signed vote, header bytes, certificate, guest and acceptance rule — so that no superseded-generation certificate can be landed? (3) can governance extract any discretion from the action, or shorten its own timelock, through calldata, queueing, upgrade or execution ordering? Until all three are answered and (a)–(h) are checked, the selection is not settled. (user decision D-15: the review is re-run on the governance path, and the two Open items are named with their falsifiers.) This analysis is an argument, not a proof, and it is only as strong as P1–P4. The claim is deliberately narrow — a sub-threshold L2 coalition cannot make the trigger true while proving and publication remain permissionless — and everything outside that boundary is stated as a residual: governance liveness with no protocol bound, transactions discarded and requiring resubmission, an L1-censored or never-published range that cannot be forced, an unfunded landing market that stalls with no adversary, and repetition that no rule paces. The withdrawn analysis's bond, reward and invoker-incentive claims are not carried over with the mechanism they described. Assumed unmeasured Open (premises A-CONS-1, A-CONS-2, A-DA-2, A-L1-1; awaiting independent review, and Open on falsifier (c) — the measured MEM-15 exit time against T_GOV_RESUME — and on the registered derivation of T_STALL_GOV in 09)
REC-04 — restart linkage after an executed stall resolution: the ordinary contiguity rule applies With resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 fixed by L1 state alone (REC-02), a stall resolution leaves no height gap, so the ordinary rules apply unchanged and the former predecessor-linkage and recovery-anchor rules are withdrawn. (user decision D-15: a full-range discard with resumeHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1 replaces the max(lastLandedHeight + 1, resumeHeight) linkage and the recovery anchor.)
(1) The ordinary linkage. Let lastLandedHeight be the height of the restored checkpoint record (L1-07); the next accepted batch MUST begin at firstHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1, its prevHeight is lastLandedHeight, and prevBlockHash and prevStateRoot are the blockHash and stateRoot of that L1-07 record. The batch's first header MUST carry parent_hash = prevBlockHash, and the guest proves execution from prevStateRoot (PRF-06). This is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii) and the header walk of PRF-04(v): there is no skipped height, no substituted parent and no anchor duty that cannot be discharged.
(2) The withdrawn rules. The following existed only for the case resumeHeight > lastLandedHeight + 1 and are withdrawn, not left for a case that can no longer occur: the max(lastLandedHeight + 1, resumeHeight) contiguity form; the skipped heights of a retirement between lastLandedHeight + 1 and resumeHeight − 1; the recovery anchor keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_ETNA_RECOVERY_ANCHOR_V1", …)) and its header domain tag in CONS-10(6); and the validator substitute duty that replaced CONS-09(2). No rule may reintroduce a height gap across a stall resolution. If the restart height is the first height of its epoch, the ordinary anchor duty of CONS-09(1)–(2) applies with B_anchor the restored checkpoint block at h_last(e−1) = firstHeight − 1, a canonical block with its own epoch certificate, and set_version_commit is computed as usual from the L1 mapping (CONS-10(6)). (user decision D-15: the recovery-anchor commitment is withdrawn from CONS-09/CONS-10 rather than kept for a case that can no longer occur.)
(3) Why a discarded block still cannot be landed, and where the boundary is. A discarded block's certificate carries the superseded generation and is void at the acceptance rule (REC-02, Generation fix; CONS-05, CONS-12). For a height > resumeHeight, the canonical parent at height − 1 is a block of the resumed chain, so a discarded descendant also fails the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii). At height = resumeHeight the discarded block and the resumed first block both descend from the restored checkpoint, so contiguity alone cannot separate them and the signed generation is the binding mechanism: a new-generation block at that height is new history signed by the validators under the new generation, while the discarded block's old-generation certificate can never be accepted. That is why no retirement record is needed and why the lock scoping follows the generation (CONS-04(2)). (user decision D-15: the generation, not a retirement range, keeps the discarded branch out; the separate retired-height lock carve-out is withdrawn with it.)
(4) Validator duty at the restart. A correct validator MUST NOT prevote or precommit any block at firstHeight unless it has itself verified, from Ethereum-final L1 state, the checkpoint record at prevHeight and that firstHeight = lastLandedHeight + 1; a validator that cannot obtain them stays silent or prevotes NIL, which is a stall and not a safety loss (CONS-15, HALT-02). It MUST also verify that the header's recovery_generation is the generation the Inbox holds (CONS-10(1)). (user decision D-15: the substitute recovery-anchor duty is replaced by the ordinary checkpoint and generation checks.) Failure mode: an implementer keeps a recovery-anchor code path or a max(…) contiguity form for a case that can no longer occur, and so invents a rule the specification withdrew; or lets the first post-resolution block name a discarded block as its parent. The single checkpoint link of (1) and the signed generation of (3) are the two answers, and neither needs a retired anchor. Proven Assumed (the linkage is the ordinary contiguity rule; the validator duty rests on each validator's own Ethereum-final L1 view, SYS-02)
