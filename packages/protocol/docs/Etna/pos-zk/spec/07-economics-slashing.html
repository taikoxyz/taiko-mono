07 — Economics, offences, penalties and rewards

This page owns the ECON-* rules: the stake ledger and its accounting, the reward funding identity, the withdrawal delay's economic interactions, the closed offence catalogue and its evidence encoding, the penalty structure, the destination of slashed stake, evidence and unbonding windows, duplicate and false-accusation handling, the minimum viable stake, concentration and liquid-staking risk, the scope of user losses, and the reward budget identity. Custody, effective stake and the exit delay are MEM-01–MEM-12; the atomic accept path and on-L1 fee accounting are L1-01–L1-11; the proof-side data binding is PRF-07. Status labels are STATUS-01–STATUS-11; timing is in seconds or L1 block numbers with no L1 proposer lookahead and no fixed L1 slot duration (GEN-06); every protocol hash is one canonical abi.encode with a right-padded domain tag and the chain id first (GEN-05). Nothing on this page is implemented, deployed, benchmarked or audited (GEN-01).

Every monetary quantity on this page is UNMEASURED. The primary source is economics-raw.md (pinned revision 7718753c1, external sources retrieved 2026-10-05), which carries the file:line evidence and the external source register. No price, market capitalisation, float, depth, yield or cost figure is stated here as a measurement, and no figure is hard-coded anywhere in this specification (ECON-10 clause 5). A number below is tagged derived, sourced or unmeasured; a formula is a relation that must hold, not a value.
Fixed decisions applied here. Staking and slashable consensus collateral are the existing TAIKO token and nothing else; gas stays ETH (D7). Validator rewards are denominated and paid in ETH (ECON-02 clause 1): D7 fixes the staking asset, not the reward asset. No rule advances the checkpoint without a valid proof (D5, as relaxed by D-11: a batch's data may be published and recorded before the proof, but a publication is not settlement) — which is why the evidence window must extend past settlement (ECON-07). No stall resolution, timeout or penalty may invalidate a block at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint (D2, REC-01); above it history is provisional, penalties debit stake, and nothing ever rewrites history at or below the checkpoint. (decision D-16: the timelocked stall resolution of D-15 is deferred, v1 has no recovery path of any kind and no rule replaces history above the checkpoint; a settlement stall halts the chain and clearing it requires a future protocol update whose procedure is not yet specified — DEFERRED.md §3.) Every deterrence claim is conditional on the Byzantine-stake assumption A-CONS-1 (< 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum in the relevant set version) and on the economic assumption A-ECO-1, which is an assumption, not a guarantee. Where a claim needs an assumption, it says so at the claim. Security is funded from L2 fees (D-8, ECON-02 clause 7), and slashed stake goes to the protocol treasury with no burn option (D-9, ECON-06). (decisions D-15/D-16: the permissionless recovery, its bond and its completion reward are withdrawn, and the governance stall resolution that replaced them is deferred by D-16, so v1 has no recovery path of any kind; the fee-funded pool has no recovery-completion outflow and the treasury receives nothing from a stall resolution.)

1. The stake ledger, custody and accounting

ECON-01 — stake ledger, custody and accounting (1) One ledger, one asset. Every bonded consensus balance is recorded in the single L1 custody contract of MEM-01. The only asset it accepts is the existing TAIKO ERC-20 (0x10dea67478c5F8C5E2D90e5E9B26dBe60c54d800); amounts are uint256 TAIKO base units at 18 decimals. Bonded balances are not transferable, not delegable (MEM-04), not represented by shares, receipts, LP tokens or any bridged or wrapped form, and no ETH enters the ledger as collateral (D7). (2) Separate from the vote system. Bonded balance and voting power are read from this ledger only (MEM-02). No rule, client, contract, interface or proof statement may use balanceOf, getVotes, getPastVotes or any ERC20Votes checkpoint as stake. Reasons, sourced: renounceVotingPower() sets a holder's votes to zero irreversibly while the balance is unchanged (TaikoToken.sol:53-74; economics-raw.md §1.1), and four hard-coded accounts are excluded from votes entirely (TaikoToken.sol:17-21,130-136). A quantity a holder can unilaterally zero cannot be an oracle for at-risk capital. (3) Accounting invariants. Write b(v) for an entry's bonded balance, D, W, S for the cumulative deposits credited, withdrawals paid and slashes debited, and TOTAL for the ledger's stored bonded total. After every state-changing entry point, exactly: 
INV-A   TOTAL == Σ_v b(v)                       (exact uint256 equality, no rounding)
INV-B   TOTAL == D − W − S
INV-C   a debit applies a = min(requested, b(v)); b(v) never becomes negative;
        the event records (applied, unapplied) and no debt is carried outside the ledger
INV-D   a deposit credits at most the TAIKO balance actually received by the contract
        between the pre-call and post-call reads (no fee-on-transfer credit, no reentrancy credit)
The contract MUST expose a view checkInvariants() returns (bool) evaluating INV-A–INV-D, and the implementation's differential/fuzz suite MUST assert it after every call. A violation is a critical defect, not a degraded mode. (4) Slashing base. For every epoch e in which an entry appears in a set root, the ledger retains SlashBase(v,e) — the entry's effective stake in that set version — until the later of the close of the evidence window for e and the settlement of any slash for e (MEM-06 clauses 1–2). An offence at epoch e is charged against SlashBase(v,e), never against the balance at evidence time, and the total charge for e never exceeds it. (5) Entry points and error behaviour. deposit(uint256 amount) reverts if amount == 0 or if the received TAIKO delta differs from amount. requestExit(), cancelExit(), rotateKey(bytes32) and withdraw() are the owner-only entry points of MEM-05/MEM-07; slash(address v, uint256 epoch, bytes evidence) is callable by any address holding objective evidence (MEM-06). withdraw() reverts while any delay or exposure of MEM-05 clause 3 remains. The ledger MUST NOT expose any owner-, DAO-, operator- or emergency-triggered path that moves a bonded balance (SYS-04, INV-04); an upgrade authority may change code but must preserve INV-A–INV-D and every retained (e, SlashBase(v,e)) pair. Failure modes: reading stake from ERC20Votes makes voting power renounceable while the capital stays and lets a whale with zero votes still be counted (or the reverse); the existing gwei bond ledger caps stake near 1.845e10 TAIKO with ≈18.4 TAIKO granularity (derived, economics-raw.md §1.4), which is a rounding rule inside the consensus path; a best-effort, offence-agnostic debit makes a slash decorative; a ledger total that drifts from the sum of balances makes reward and penalty accounting unauditable and lets an offender leave with stake that should be at risk. 2. Rewards and the funding identity

ECON-02 — rewards and their funding identity; no token-appreciation assumption (1) Assets: stake in TAIKO, rewards in ETH, and exactly which terms are TAIKO. TAIKO is the staking and slashable-collateral asset and the only asset the ledger accepts (D7, ECON-01 clause 1). Validator and prover rewards are denominated and paid in ETH, collected on L1. D7 fixes the staking asset, not the reward asset, so ETH rewards are conforming and the identity below is single-currency (ETH) and evaluable. The TAIKO-denominated protocol payouts in this specification are the reporter bounty (ECON-06) and the correlated-collection keeper fee (ECON-05 clause 5), and neither is a charge on the ETH fee inflow: both are paid out of TAIKO collateral already debited from a proven offender by slash() and can be paid only when such a debit was applied — the bounty is bounded by ECON-06 clause 5 to be strictly smaller than that debit, and the keeper fee is a fraction of what the collection call collects, with the bounty and the fee together bounded by the amount collected for that offence. They therefore need no TAIKO/ETH price and do not make this identity mixed-currency; the left-hand term rewards_ETH counts only ETH-denominated payouts. This closes A5: the collection keeper fee is registered as a TAIKO payout of the same debit as the reporter bounty, so the funding identity stays single-currency (ETH). (review round 1, findings ECO-01, ECO-13) (2) No appreciation may be assumed. No rule, parameter, reward schedule, interface string, learning page or interface text may assume, require, promise or be conditioned on an increase in TAIKO's market price, market depth, float or reward-pool value. A reward is a nominal ETH amount fixed when it is awarded; the protocol states nothing about its fiat value. "Rewards are sustainable" is a claim that may be made only when the fee-only region is reached (clause 4, ECON-12), never on the strength of a reserve that still looks large (arXiv:2606.03587, quoted in economics-raw.md §2.9). (3) TAIKO has no mint path today. 1,000,000,000 ether was minted once in init (TaikoToken.sol:41) and no mint or burn exists on the L1 token or its base; the L2 bridged representation's burn does not affect L1 supply (economics-raw.md §1.1, sourced). A reward funded by new TAIKO therefore requires a token upgrade — a DAO action under GOV-01 — and is not a standing funding source. The repository's recorded objective "No Built-In PoS Reward" (tokenomics_objective_metrics.md:19) forbids paying inflationary staking rewards; it may be reversed only by a new recorded user decision (GEN-02), and until it is reversed the schedule MUST be fundable from clause 1 alone. Because rewards are paid in ETH (clause 1), a TAIKO-denominated reward would in addition require a conversion rule and a price; no such rule exists here and no price constant is permitted (ECON-10 clause 5), which is a further reason the standing schedule is the ETH identity of clause 1 and nothing else. (4) Every subsidy is bounded, expiring, and netted out. The subsidy is one line item with (a) a maximum amount in ETH (the reward asset of clause 1 — a mixed TAIKO/ETH maximum is not a line item, it is two unaggregated assets), (b) a start and an end expressed in unix seconds, (c) a pre-funded account that no rule of this specification may top up or borrow against, and (d) no reward obligation: at every period boundary, accrued payouts may not exceed the realised net inflow of clause 1. The subsidy account is capitalised by the draw D_subsidy that clause 1 subtracts, so a subsidy is a transfer of the protocol's own inflow, not a second funding source: it can smooth timing within its term and can never make an unfunded schedule funded. When the subsidy is exhausted or expires, the protocol pays the fee-funded amount only and must remain coherent: the validator count is reduced through the objective exit path of MEM-05 (never by confiscation) or the chain halts safely (HALT-01). A design that is coherent only while the subsidy lasts is non-conforming. The validator reward pool is an ETH balance held in a segregated account of the L1 staking contract of MEM-01 — not in the Inbox, whose L1-11 ledger (the prover reward) is not touched by validator payouts. A reservation is released only by discharge: each claimValidatorReward reduces the epoch's outstanding amount by exactly the ETH it pays, the epoch's last claim also releases the allocation's floor remainder as (5)(d) states, the permissionless proving-share transfer of (5)(e) reduces the epoch's un-transferred proving share to zero, and the epoch leaves reserved_before when its outstanding amount reaches zero; an epoch whose frozen participant set is empty has no lawful claimant, so its allocation is released at the freeze. v1 defines no expiry and no cancellation because no rule closes a claim (5)(c) or cancels a fixed allocation; a future rule that closes or cancels one MUST release the reservation in the same recorded step. Participation is attested by the only object that can carry it to L1: the authenticated signer bitmap of the head certificate, exposed as the public input signerBitmap (PRF-02) and verified in-guest against the certificate's signatures, the canonical set order and the quorum predicate (PRF-04(ii)–(iii)) before the batch can land. (c) Who may claim. The staking contract MUST expose claimValidatorReward(uint64 epoch, uint32 index) (name indicative), callable by any address, so that no operator depends on a keeper. A claim pays the entry's recorded owner, never the caller, and each (epoch, index) may be claimed at most once. Claims for epoch e open at evidenceClose(e) (ECON-07), the instant at which the epoch's allocation may be fixed and its participant set and participating stake are frozen (5)(d); a claim before then reverts. The claim is the freeze's backstop. The first claim of e MUST execute the freeze of (5)(d) as its first step if e is not yet frozen, so the party claiming is the party with the incentive to fix the allocation and no epoch depends on a separate keeper. This closes review round 8 finding R8-DPE-02: the permissionless claim is the freeze's guaranteed trigger, with the claimant's own payout as the incentive. A successful claim is paid from the epoch's own fixed allocation only and reduces that epoch's outstanding reservation by exactly the amount paid, in the same call (5)(a), so the ETH behind a claim can never be drawn by another epoch. (review round 7, finding R7-DPE-05: the claim is the reservation's release event, recorded where the payment is made.) (d) Accounting identity per epoch, with the participant set frozen at the window's close. Let Alloc(e) be the ETH the recorded allocation policy assigns to epoch e's validator payout. Let P(e) be the frozen participant set: the canonical signer indices whose participation bit for e was written by land at or before evidenceClose(e) (ECON-07(1)), and let PS(e) = Σ_{u ∈ P(e)} effStake(u,e) be the participating stake frozen at that same instant. Neither P(e) nor PS(e) may be read from mutable state at claim time: the staking contract MUST compute and store both at the freeze, and the frozen denominators are what the identity below uses. Cutoff rule. A batch of epoch e that lands after evidenceClose(e) is legal — no admission rule may reject it (L1-04, L1-06) — but it MUST NOT change P(e) or PS(e): its validators are recorded in the epoch's late-participation record on L1 (5)(b) and are not paid from epoch e's allocation and not charged to any later epoch's allocation — the claim for e is closed by the freeze. Consequence, stated rather than assumed away: validators of an epoch whose batches settle only after that epoch's window closes are unpaid for that epoch, because no rule may reopen a final allocation or create a retroactive claim; any account may land a batch at any time (L1-04), so the remedy is timely settlement, not retroactivity. A participation bit written for a different epoch e′ whose own evidenceClose(e′) has not yet passed is unaffected by e's freeze and enters P(e′) normally; for e itself, only bits written at or before evidenceClose(e) ever enter P(e) (5)(b). For each index in P(e) the staking contract MUST resolve the committed entry and its effStake from the set root stored for e (MEM-09(2)), so the accumulator's key is the canonical signer index and v below denotes the entry committed at that index; an entry that did not sign is paid nothing, however much stake it holds. Alloc(e) MUST be fixed before the epoch's first claim, MUST NOT exceed the pool's realised net inflow for the period — the ETH that has actually arrived by the sweep of clause 7 plus POOL_TOPUP (clause 1) — and MUST fit inside the free balance at that instant; the floor-rounding remainder stays in the pool and is never minted. The freeze's trigger and order, and why the amount is objective. An epoch e becomes freezable exactly when evidenceClose(e) has passed and MUST NOT be frozen before then; a call that would freeze it earlier MUST revert. The freeze is permissionless and needs no keeper: the staking contract MUST expose freezeEpoch(uint64 epoch) (name indicative), callable by any address once the epoch is freezable, and every call that can change the pool's balance or fix a later epoch's amounts — an inflow credit, a validator claim, a proving-share transfer, or the freeze of a later epoch — MUST first execute the freeze of every freezable epoch not yet frozen, in ascending epoch order, before applying its own effect. Consequently no caller chooses when or whether an epoch's amounts are fixed: only an inflow changes the free balance (a claim and a proving-share transfer discharge a reservation against the same balance, so free is unchanged by them), and an inflow cannot credit before it has executed the pending freezes, so Alloc(e) and ProvingShare(e) are the amounts the free balance supports at the instant evidenceClose(e) passed — a function of L1 state and of no caller's timing, which is what the objective-reward requirement demands. A later epoch can never freeze before an earlier one, so reserved_before(e) counts every earlier epoch's outstanding and no later freeze dilutes e; an epoch cannot be skipped by inaction, because its own first claim executes its freeze (5)(c) and any address may submit that claim; and while no pool-touching call is ever made, no allocation has been promised and no claim exists — the ordinary no-claim case, not a lost reward. A direct ETH credit that no call of the staking contract mediates (a forced credit) is the one thing that can raise the free balance without executing the cascade; because it can only increase the balance, it is a donation that raises every participant's allocation alike and is never a dilution, a denial or a caller's capture. frozenAt records the block time at which the freeze executes; the amounts it fixes are the close-time amounts above. The identity Σ_v payout(v,e) ≤ Alloc(e) ≤ free_before(e) ≤ pool_before(e), evaluated at the frozen denominators, is a checked condition, not an intention: the staking contract MUST persist the per-epoch allocation record allocRecord[e] = (frozenAt, reservedBefore(e), Alloc(e), ProvingShare(e), participantCount(e), claimedCount(e), paid(e), provingTransferred(e)) (name indicative) from which outstanding(e), reserved_before(e) and free_before(e) are computed — the freeze MUST write the epoch's fixed amounts, its outstanding reservation and frozenAt in one step — and MUST expose views that evaluate the identity above and the exact decomposition pool_balance = free + Σ_e outstanding(e) for every epoch with an open claim or an outstanding reservation — exactly as ECON-01(3) requires the checkInvariants() view for INV-A–INV-D — and the implementation's differential/fuzz suite MUST assert both after every claim and after every freeze. Releasing the floor remainder, so a reservation can actually end. Floor payouts mean Alloc(e) − Σ payout(v,e) cannot reach zero by claims alone: it is a remainder smaller than participantCount(e) wei that no claim amount pays, so a reservation released only by exact discharge would stay reserved forever and the decomposition view would iterate a set that never shrinks. The release is completed by the epoch's last claim: claimedCount(e) counts the claims paid, and the claim that brings it to participantCount(e) MUST, in the same call, release the remaining Alloc(e) − paid(e) to the free balance; an epoch whose frozen participant set is empty has no lawful claimant, so its whole allocation is released at the freeze (5)(a). After that release the epoch's outstanding is exactly its un-transferred proving share, which the permissionless transfer of (5)(e) reduces to zero, so clause 5(a)'s "the epoch leaves reserved_before when its outstanding amount reaches zero" is reachable. The staking contract MUST maintain the live reserved total as stored state, updated by exactly these steps — the freeze, a claim including its last-claim release, the proving-share transfer and the empty-set release — and MUST NOT compute reserved_before(e) or the decomposition view by iterating the set of epochs; reservedBefore(e) is the running total captured at the freeze. (e) What fixes Alloc(e): the recorded allocation policy, its owner, its formula and the proving-share outflow. The mechanism that moves L2 fee revenue to the L1 pool is clause 7. The allocation policy is recorded in the L1 staking contract's storage as one policy record, written by the activation transaction, and it may be changed only by an upgrade under GOV-01 that is recorded in the decision log before it takes effect and applies to future epochs only — never to an epoch already entered and never to an allocation already fixed. (6) Exit churn is capped per epoch. CHURN_LIMIT is the maximum fraction of an epoch's voting power that may leave the set at one epoch boundary: the L1 staking contract MUST NOT make more than CHURN_LIMIT · TotalVP(e) of effective stake inactive at any single epoch e. Deferred exits become effective in ascending order of the request coordinates (l1BlockNumber, txIndex, logIndex) of the requestExit() transaction — the objective ordering key owned by MEM-03(2) — so no later request takes effect before an earlier deferred one, and the cap never reorders the queue by stake, key, identity, reputation or fee. A deferred entry remains in every set version it is already committed to and remains fully slashable (clause 4). The cap exists because otherwise a coordinated exit — every operator calling requestExit() in one L1 block, or the public end of the subsidy (ECON-02 clause 4) or a price move making exit rational for many operators at once — drops the stake that deters attack in a single step, after which an attacker acquires or rents a set that no longer satisfies the deterrence relation of ECON-09 clause 2 and A-ECO-1. Deferral is a delay of an exit, never a denial of it and never a confiscation: withdraw() remains available once the delay and the exposure gates of MEM-05 are satisfied. The two are one rule — MEM-05 owns the transition, this clause bounds how much stake may leave in one epoch — and an implementation that makes an exit effective regardless of the cap is non-conforming, because it voids the cap. A deferred exit keeps the entry in every committed set and fully slashable (clause 4) until its effective version arrives. 5. Penalty structure

ECON-05 — penalty structure and the convex correlated penalty (1) Parameters. IMM_FRAC(c) is the immediate penalty fraction for offence class c, a parameter with 0 < IMM_FRAC(c) ≤ 1 so that every proven offence has a non-zero immediate cost (value unmeasured, PARAM-01; Ethereum's precedent is a fixed fraction of the offender's effective balance, sourced in economics-raw.md §2.3). F_SAT = 1/3 is the safety threshold, numerically equal to the b of A-CONS-1. CORR_Q ∈ {1, 2} selects the penalty shape. CORR_DELAY is a duration in seconds (clause 5). (2) Implicated fraction. For epoch e and offence class c, let O(e,c) be the set of entries with at least one applied offence of class c at e, each counted once, and let TotalVP(e) be the published total voting power of that set version (MEM-02): 
f(e,c)  =  ( Σ_{v ∈ O(e,c)} SlashBase(v,e) ) / TotalVP(e)
c(f)    =  min( (f / F_SAT)^CORR_Q , 1 )            with F_SAT = 1/3
P(v,e)  =  min( SlashBase(v,e) · ( IMM_FRAC(c) + c(f(e,c)) ) , SlashBase(v,e) )
charge  =  min( P(v,e) , bonded(v) , remaining_exposure(v,e) )
CORR_Q = 1 reproduces Ethereum's min(3f, 1); CORR_Q = 2 reproduces Polkadot's min((3x/n)^2, 1) (economics-raw.md §2.3, sourced). Both are convex in f and both saturate at f = F_SAT: c(F_SAT) = 1 by construction, so the confiscation threshold and the safety threshold coincide — a coalition that reaches the 1/3 of stake at which A-CONS-1 stops holding loses 100% of its epoch stake in the correlated component, in addition to the immediate component. (3) Bounded debit. The charge is bounded by the bonded balance and by the per-epoch charge base; the applied amount is debited, the unapplied remainder is recorded in the event and is not carried as a debt outside the ledger (MEM-06 clause 2, ECON-11). No entry's balance may go negative (ECON-01 INV-C). (4) Immediate component. The immediate component is debited when the accepted evidence transaction executes, through the same slash() call that sets the offence identity. (5) Correlated component and its delay. f(e,c) cannot be final before the evidence window for e closes, so the correlated component is computed once at corrApplyAt(e,c) = evidenceClose(e) + CORR_DELAY and debited by a permissionless applyCorrelated(e, c) call; until then the amount is locked against the exposure accounting of MEM-06. CORR_DELAY must satisfy ECON-07's inequality with D_WITHDRAW — a long delay is the price of a correlated penalty (Ethereum applies its correlated component up to EPOCHS_PER_SLASHINGS_VECTOR/2, ≈36.4 days after the slash; economics-raw.md §2.3, §3.2 point 2, sourced). Who is paid to collect it. The collection call is permissionless and MUST pay its caller: of the amount the call collects, the caller receives the keeper fee floor(CORR_KEEPER_PPM · collected / 1_000_000), a separate evidence submitter entitled to the reporter bounty for that identity under ECON-06 clause (5) receives that bounty, and the remainder goes to the protocol treasury (user decision D-9: no burn). The keeper fee and the bounty together MUST NOT exceed the amount collected, and the registered parameters MUST be set so that this holds for every identity the call can collect. A caller MAY collect several identities in one call, so that one keeper fee covers the L1 gas of the batch. Honest limit. The protocol MUST NOT rebate below the L1 gas cost from the treasury: a collection whose keeper fee would be below its L1 gas cost may simply not be worth making, so collection is weaker for tiny penalties than for large ones. That weakness is disclosed rather than subsidised — the treasury never tops the fee up, and no rule may promise a subsidy for a collection. This closes A5: the correlated penalty names its collector's keeper fee, the entitled reporter's bounty and the treasury remainder, permits batching several identities in one call, and discloses the unsubsidised tiny-penalty case. (6) Premise, stated at the claim. This structure deters only if the slashed stake has value and is actually confiscated. Both halves are assumptions: A-ECO-1 (the dollar loss exceeds the attack profit at the relevant time) is assumed, and confiscation is possible only while the evidence window of ECON-07 holds and the exposure accounting of MEM-06 is enforced. If TAIKO's market value or liquidity falls, the same token amount is a weaker deterrent; the specification states the derived price at which the design stops being secure rather than asserting security at an assumed price (see TAIKO price volatility). (7) Honest-mistake tail. On a small validator set, a correlated penalty makes one operator's duplicated key a correlated loss for a non-trivial fraction of the set. The mitigation is operational (slashing-protection databases, remote signers, key custody); no parameter choice removes the tail. The specification MUST disclose it and MUST NOT describe the correlated penalty as harmless. (Polkadot's documented incidents, all cloned keystores, are the sourced example; economics-raw.md §2.3.) Failure mode: a linear-only penalty makes the bribe for a coalition linear in its size and the confiscation threshold unrelated to the safety threshold, so "Byzantine stake < 1/3" remains an assumption about intent rather than a statement about loss given attack; an unbounded or negative-capable debit breaks the ledger invariants; a correlated penalty whose application delay exceeds the withdrawal delay is uncollectible and therefore decorative. Proven sourced unmeasured (the saturation argument is conditional on A-CONS-1 and on the coincidence of F_SAT with the quorum denominator; the deterrence claim is conditional on A-ECO-1.)

7. Evidence window versus unbonding period

ECON-07 — the evidence window is strictly shorter than the unbonding delay (1) The window, anchored to the offence epoch's own set version, with the anchor stored on L1. W_evidence(e) is a duration in seconds per epoch e. Evidence for an offence at epoch e is admissible only if the L1 block that includes the slash() transaction has block.timestamp ≤ evidenceClose(e), where evidenceClose(e) opens at t_root(e) and closes no earlier than max(t_root(e), t_observed(e)) + W_evidence(e) (the production-anchored close and the Open anchor t_observed(e) are defined below). What t_root(e) is, concretely. It is the run-time-visible block.timestamp of the L1 transaction whose call to commitSet() appended epoch e's entry to the staking contract's per-epoch mapping (MEM-09 clause 2, the transaction N(k)). The staking contract MUST persist that timestamp — the anchor this window needs — with the entry, which is the exhaustive four-field tuple epoch → (setRoot, totalVotingPower, rootCommittedAt, setVersionBlock) of MEM-09(2): rootCommittedAt is the stored block.timestamp, and setVersionBlock = N(k) is the L1 block number of the same commitSet() call, a uint64 packed with rootCommittedAt in the entry's third slot, so the tuple still consumes three slots. The entry is written once in the same call and immutable thereafter, and the Inbox and slash() MUST read the anchor from that stored field. It MUST NOT be recomputed from blockhash, reconstructed from an event log after the EVM's 256-block window, or taken from the L2 header field set_version_commit (which L1 cannot read); a missing entry means no window exists and a slash for e reverts EpochNotCommitted(e). The anchor is the commit block's timestamp, not the later moment at which that block became Ethereum-final: the finality requirement is enforced where it belongs — MEM-09 clause 5 requires mapping[e] to be Ethereum-final before any block of epoch e is finalized — and clause (4)'s 3 · E_EPOCH term is derived for exactly this anchor (under the two-epoch lookahead of CONS-13(3) the commit precedes the epoch's first height by up to 2 · E_EPOCH). Defining the anchor as "became final" would make it un-storable on L1 and would shorten the admissible interval by the finality lag; the stored commit timestamp is the value the non-emptiness inequality of clause (4) already assumes, and it is registered as t_root(e) in 09. (review round 2, finding E-R2-07: the anchor is a stored, run-time-visible L1 value rather than an event-log reconstruction, and the window is computable.) The close may not precede the epoch's production. The commit timestamp is an L1 wall clock, and the two clocks diverge across a halt: L1 time and commitSet() keep advancing while L2 height production stops (MEM-09(1), CONS-13), so after a halt longer than W_evidence(e), t_root(e) + W_evidence(e) can fall before any height of epoch e exists. A conforming window therefore opens when the epoch's set root is committed — t_root(e) — but MUST NOT close before the epoch has been produced on L2 and its batches have had the full window to land: it closes no earlier than max(t_root(e), t_observed(e)) + W_evidence(e), where t_observed(e) is the L1-observable instant of the event that makes an offence of epoch e observable and starts the epoch's full landing window. The anchor is the event that makes the offence observable, never L1 wall-clock alone; no rule, implementation or interface may treat the commit-anchored instant as sufficient, and clause (4)'s non-empty-interval requirement is applied to this later close. The later close is fixed by this rule from the start from stored facts: it is not a re-opening, extension or waiver after the fact by any party, and the prohibition below on re-anchoring a window to a later or unrelated set-commit block is unaffected. Open anchor. Which L1 fact carries t_observed(e), and where it is stored, is not fixed by this specification: MEM-09(2)'s stored tuple carries the commit timestamp rootCommittedAt and the commit block number setVersionBlock, and the Inbox checkpoint record of L1-07 carries the accepting L1 block number rather than that block's timestamp, so no existing stored value carries the epoch's production or landing time. Candidate anchors, to be fixed by a recorded decision before launch, are the L1 timestamp at which the checkpoint record for the epoch's last height h_last(e) is first written, or a production/landing marker written into the per-epoch entry once the epoch's heights are known to have been produced. Consequence until that anchor is defined and stored: the commit-anchored close remains the only enforceable condition, and after a halt longer than W_evidence(e) the resumed epochs' offences are inadmissible — their heights are produced after evidenceClose(e) — while the ledger's release gate can already have opened (MEM-05 clause 3, clause (5) below), so stake exposed to those epochs can be withdrawn between the offence and the moment its evidence would become admissible, and INV-03's premise that the stake counting toward quorum is slashable is void for every such height. The liveness consequence is the same defect: an epoch with more than one third of its committed weight withdrawn and offline can never reach quorum, so the chain can wedge permanently although the L1 ledger holds a healthy bonded set. That is clause (4)'s empty interval — a defect, not a tuning choice — and it MUST be closed before launch. This closes review round 3 finding R3-LIV-01: the window's close is anchored to the event that makes the offence observable rather than to L1 wall-clock alone, and the L1 anchor that carries it is recorded as Open with its consequence instead of being assumed. The anchor is the offence's epoch and its set version, never an arbitrary set-commit block: set versions may be created permissionlessly (MEM-09), and a later or unrelated commitment MUST NOT be used to compute, re-anchor, extend or shorten the window of e. No window may be re-opened, extended or waived after the fact, by any party (SYS-04); a later inclusion reverts EvidenceWindowClosed. (review round 1, findings ECO-04, ECO-05) (2) Strict inequality. 8. False accusations, duplicates and replay

ECON-08 — false accusations, duplicate evidence, replay and post-exit evidence (1) Cost of an invalid submission. A submission that fails any check of ECON-04 clause 2 reverts entirely; the submitter pays exactly the L1 gas. No bond, deposit or stake is consumed, no state is written, and the accused has nothing to answer. The protocol MUST NOT require the accused to respond within any deadline: a missed response deadline that can consume stake converts the availability assumption A-CONS-5 into a penalty, and a party that is offline is not thereby guilty. A reporter bond or deposit may be introduced only by a recorded decision, and then only if (a) it is returned exactly on a valid submission, (b) it is forfeited only on an objectively decidable condition — a duplicate identity, or an object whose signatures do not verify — never on "the accusation was not proven", and (c) its size does not deter reporting a real offence. Its calibration is unmeasured. (2) Idempotence. For a valid submission the identity is id = (v, epoch, offenceType, keccak256(evidence)) (MEM-06 clause 4); the first application sets applied[id] permanently, and a second submission of the same id is a no-op as defined in ECON-04 clause 4 — no additional debit, no second bounty, no window extension. (3) Distinct offences in one epoch. A distinct valid offence by the same v at the same epoch is a distinct id and is recorded. The total charge for the epoch remains bounded by SlashBase(v,e) (MEM-06 clause 2); once that cap is reached the remainder is recorded as unapplied and is not collected (ECON-05 clause 3). The correlated fraction f(e,c) counts each offender once and is applied to every offender of the class at corrApplyAt(e,c), so later distinct offences still raise the correlated component for the whole class. (4) Replay. Every evidence object carries the domain tag and the chain id (GEN-05, ECON-04 clause 2); an object is invalid under any other chain id, and signatures are checked against the set root of the epoch named in the evidence, so an object from another epoch or set version reverts. applied[id] is permanent: no expiry, no upgrade, no migration and no recovery path may clear it, and an upgrade must preserve it (GOV-03). (5) Evidence that arrives after the offender has exited is impossible by construction. The construction has two halves, and an implementation that weakens either is non-conforming. (a) The ledger's release gate (MEM-05 clause 3, MEM-06 clause 2) makes the state "the offender has withdrawn stake exposed to a still-open epoch" unreachable: withdraw() reverts until every epoch the entry was in has a closed window and no unsettled exposure. (b) Because every epoch in which the entry appears has evidenceClose(e) ≤ the withdrawal time, any evidence still inside its window is necessarily submitted before the withdrawal; after the withdrawal the window is closed, so a later submission reverts EvidenceWindowClosed and cannot debit a released balance. There is therefore no ordering of (exit, evidence) in which in-window evidence is unenforceable, and none in which late evidence touches released funds. Implementers MUST NOT add a "late evidence" path, and the constants must satisfy ECON-07 clause 2 or this construction fails. (6) Disclosure. A reverted or duplicate submission produces no on-chain accusation record and MUST NOT be displayed as a finding: an interface may show an applied offence (with its transaction) and must not present an unproven or failed accusation as fact (WH-02). Failure modes: a bond forfeited on "not proven" suppresses honest reporting, which kills the detection half of A-ECO-1; a challenge-response deadline lets an attacker slash an offline validator; a non-permanent replay marker lets one evidence object be applied twice or on a forked chain; a "late evidence" path would break the withdrawal gate. 9. Minimum viable stake

ECON-09 — minimum viable stake is set by operator economics, not attack cost (1) Derivation, in ETH-revenue terms. The minimum bonded amount for one validator S_min must satisfy the operator break-even relation 
S_min  ≥  C_op_annual / ( r_gross − ρ_ops )        equivalently  r_be = C_op_annual / S + ρ_ops
where C_op_annual is hardware, bandwidth, operations and L1 gas for one validator per year, expressed in ETH; r_gross is the annualised ETH reward rate actually paid per unit of bonded TAIKO out of the budget of ECON-12, in ETH per TAIKO per year; and ρ_ops is the operator's required margin in the same unit. The relation is dimensionally closed (ETH/year ÷ ETH per TAIKO per year = TAIKO) and contains no price term, so no TAIKO price is assumed silently by the algebra; the price enters through the operators' required margin, which is exactly why ρ_ops is unmeasured and time-varying rather than a constant. If the pool cannot pay r_gross > ρ_ops, the denominator is zero or negative and there is no finite S_min. Entry is then closed in practice: the admission predicate amount ≥ S_min (MEM-03 clause 1) either admits no operator or is unimplementable, so R1's permissionless entry is unsatisfiable in that regime — the consequence must be recorded as an open conflict between R1 and the funding identity, never smoothed by lowering the minimum until operators appear or by assuming a price recovery. The honest statement is that the design is unfunded. (review round 1, findings ECO-02, ECO-03) (2) The binding constraint, stated. Attack cost bounds the aggregate stake and never the individual validator: 
S_total  ≥  Λ · ( EV_extractable + OV_option ) / ( b · P(t) · (1 + σ(t)) )
This inequality contains no individual S; no rule may derive S_min from it (economics-raw.md §3.1 constraint A). The parameter Λ ≥ 1 is unmeasured, and the claim is conditional on A-ECO-1. (3) Participation. S_min ≤ S_affordable(median intended participant), or entry is permissionless only in name (R1). The resulting count n = S_total / S_min is not bounded by any consensus rule: CONS-06 and CONS-10 are well defined for every n ≥ 1 and impose no maximum (the invalid-set conditions are n = 0, a zero effective stake, and a malformed or duplicate key, not a large n), so there is no normative set-size window; what bounds n in practice is the reward budget Budget_ETH(n) of ECON-12 and the measured consensus performance at the target set size, which is unmeasured (04 §7, falsifier F3). The resulting Budget(n) must be fundable (ECON-12); if it is not, S_min and the participation target are in conflict and the conflict must be recorded, not smoothed. This closes review round 2 finding E-R2-11(b): the undefined "consensus set-size window" is deleted and re-expressed as the budget bound plus the unmeasured consensus performance limit; no rule of this specification defines such a window, and none is implied. (4) Values. The numeric value of S_min is a PARAM-01 parameter and is unmeasured with respect to C_op_annual, r_gross and ρ_ops; C_op_annual must be measured for a reference host under the 2 s cadence, L2 state size, gossip bandwidth and L1 gas costs of this design, and must not be copied from Bitcoin or Ethereum validator costs (economics-raw.md §3.1). A published S_min may not be described as measured until that study exists. (5) Activation ordering and the rate limit (economics side). The ordering argument is owned by MEM-03 clause 2 and is not restated here: ordering by L1 inclusion is not proof-of-fairness under adversarial L1 ordering — priority fees and builders can move a bonding transaction ahead of a target — and the protection this design gives is that inclusion is delayed, never denied, under an objective key that no third party can cancel or pass. This rule fixes the economics consequence. ACTIVATION_RATE_LIMIT is that rule's per-epoch activation cap (called A_max there; it is one parameter, and the parameter table registers it as ACTIVATION_RATE_LIMIT). It is stated per epoch, not per set version, because set versions may be created permissionlessly (MEM-09) and a per-set-version cap would bound nothing. It is what makes the set growth assumed by Budget_ETH(n) (ECON-12) an input the protocol at least bounds; it bounds the rate and is not an anti-front-running mechanism, and it MUST NOT be described as one or as a fairness guarantee. ACTIVATION_RATE_LIMIT is unmeasured and must be derived from the set growth that consensus and the budget can absorb. (review round 1, finding ECO-09) Failure mode: deriving a minimum from attack cost produces a number no operator can live on; the set then consists of the operators with the lowest outside option — possibly the attacker's — and the security argument silently rests on participants who are losing money. Conversely, choosing the minimum from participation goals alone without checking the budget produces a set that cannot be paid to operate. Proven unmeasured (aggregate attack-cost bound conditional on A-ECO-1; safety meaning conditional on A-CONS-1.)

10. Concentration, borrowing and liquid staking

ECON-10 — caps constrain labels, borrowing changes cost, liquid staking is unpriced (1) A per-address or per-validator cap constrains labels, not capital. Let C be the cap per address, S_total the total bonded stake, k = S_total / C the number of identities needed to hold it, and k_op the marginal annual cost of operating one additional identity (key generation, custody, monitoring, per-identity overhead). An adversary seeking fraction b of stake acquires b · S_total TAIKO and splits it across b · k identities: the dominant capital term is unchanged, and the cap adds at most b · k · k_op. The ratio of cap overhead to acquisition cost is k_op / C, which shrinks as the cap rises and never cancels the dominant term. Therefore no rule, interface or document may claim that a cap "prevents monopolisation"; a design that keeps a cap MUST state this arithmetic, with k_op unmeasured. A cap on validator slots is worse than a cap on addresses, because it also caps the honest set size and turns slot acquisition itself into the scarce resource (economics-raw.md §2.7; MEM-10's non-claim is not weakened here). (2) Borrowing. The attacker must control b · S_total, not own it. Borrowed or leveraged stake changes the cost floor to the financing cost over the attack window plus the loss of the pledged collateral; the protocol cannot observe leverage, so no rule may assume a maximum leverage ratio, and no claim may be phrased as "the attacker must own the tokens". Leverage ratios are unmeasured (economics-raw.md §2.6, citing arXiv:2401.08610). (3) Liquid-staking derivatives are an unpriced attack surface. A low-stake adversary can degrade a target pool's consensus performance and monetise at the application layer through leveraged shorts — "profitable with over one-half probability for LSTs of major staking pools" — because the profit is booked against an LST repricing rather than against the consensus layer (arXiv:2605.01025, *Your Loss is My Gain: Low Stake Attacks on Liquid Staking Pools*, 2026-05-01, retrieved 2026-10-05; economics-raw.md §2.6, §2.8). This is the OV_option term, and standard cost-of-attack accounting — which assumes the attacker must be a large stakeholder — does not bound it. v1 recognises no delegated, pooled or liquid-staked TAIKO as consensus stake and counts weight per (pubkey, effStake) entry without recording beneficial ownership (MEM-02, MEM-04). That removes the delegation surface; it does not remove the derivative market, because a derivative issuer can still bond TAIKO through its own entry. Hence (a) no claim on this page may be read as bounding attack profitability while a TAIKO liquid-staking or derivative market exists, and (b) every published concentration statistic MUST say that it is a statistic over ledger entries, not over beneficial owners. (4) What actually constrains concentration. The cost of acquiring the float (a liquidity and slippage question, σ(t), unmeasured), reward schedules that are concave in stake, and transparency (entry-level stake is public). None is a guarantee; transparency makes concentration observable, not impossible (economics-raw.md §2.7 point 5). (5) No hard-coded market datum. No price, market capitalisation, float, order-book depth, traded volume, slippage or yield figure may appear as a constant, immutable, constructor argument, interface string or normative value anywhere in this specification or the interfaces it defines. Economics-raw.md §3.5 records every one of these as UNVERIFIED in this pass; a constant would be a claim that the value is stable, which is false, and each one is an input to σ(t), S_total or AttackProfit — that is, each one can invalidate A-ECO-1. Statements of the form "an attack costs X" are prohibited for the same reason; the permitted statement is the derived price at which the design stops being secure, computed from measured inputs and dated. Failure modes: a cap presented as an anti-monopoly mechanism is a false security claim (threat model §6.4 non-argument); ignoring borrowing understates attack cost; ignoring liquid-staking derivatives leaves the OV_option term out of the accounting, so A-ECO-1 appears satisfied when it is not; a hard-coded price makes a market observation into a protocol rule that fails with no rule change. Proven sourced unmeasured

11. Scope of user losses

ECON-11 — what protocol penalties do and do not cover (1) What a penalty does. A penalty debits the bonded stake of an entry proven to have committed a listed offence, up to the charge base of MEM-06, and dispatches the amount per ECON-06. That is its entire effect. No penalty reverses a PoS-finalized block, returns a bridge message, re-orders history, or repairs a failed consensus assumption (D2, REC-01). (2) What penalties do not cover. Protocol penalties are not compensation and do not cover: value extracted by an attacker before detection (EV_extractable); losses caused by F2 failures — ≥1/3 Byzantine stake (A-CONS-1 failing), key compromise, a zkVM soundness break, or a malicious governance upgrade — where the design's safety claims do not apply and no slashing rule repairs the failure; losses caused by F1 failures — halt, congestion, prover outage, blob expiry, unavailable data — where the honest response is to halt safely, not to redistribute; losses inside third-party contracts, bridges, DEXes and lending markets; losses from user error; losses from L1 reorganisation; and the loss of the fiat value of TAIKO itself. (3) No universal bound. No rule, document, interface, learning page or marketing text may state or imply a universal bound on user economic loss. The threat model already forbids inventing one (D2 obligation 4), and economics-raw.md §4.1 L1 records why: loss depends on EV_extractable, which depends on the value bridged, DEX depth and detection latency — none of which the protocol controls. The status labels of STATUS-01–STATUS-11 remain the only progress vocabulary: a slash is not a confirmation, and a penalty is not insurance. (4) No compensation channel is defined here. If the recorded ECON-06 destination includes no compensation policy, users must expect none; a future compensation or insurance scheme is a new design with its own funding identity under ECON-02 and its own decision record, and may not be assumed by this specification (the "insurance" allocation discussed in arXiv:2401.05797 is an example of such a scheme, not a rule of this one). (5) The honest commitment. The protocol's commitment to users is disclosure, the objective exit path of MEM-05, and the ability to halt safely; it is not a promise of reimbursement. Failure mode: the most damaging economic failure is a user who reads "slashing" as "my loss will be covered" and sizes exposure on a guarantee the protocol never made. Proven Assumed (the exclusion of F2 losses restates the fault boundary: 01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §5.)

12. Budget identity and the cost of decentralisation

ECON-12 — Budget(n) and the linear cost of splitting stake (1) Identity, in ETH. With n validators each bonded at S TAIKO (S_total = n · S), per-validator annual operating cost C_op in ETH and required ETH return on locked capital ρ_capital per unit of bonded TAIKO per year (risk-free rate + illiquidity premium for D_WITHDRAW + expected slashing loss + TAIKO price-risk premium): 
Budget_ETH(n)   =  n · ( C_op + ρ_capital · S )                     [ETH per year]
NetETHInflow    =  F_exec_L2 + F_priority_L2 + POOL_TOPUP − C_L1_data − C_L1_verify − C_bridge_ops − D_subsidy
n_max_fundable  =  NetETHInflow / ( C_op + ρ_capital · S )
Runway          =  R_reserve / ( Budget_ETH(n) − NetETHInflow )     while NetETHInflow < Budget_ETH(n)
fee-only region reached  ⇔  NetETHInflow ≥ Budget_ETH(n)
with NetETHInflow the ETH identity of ECON-02 clause 1 and R_reserve the reserve valued in ETH at a dated price — a TAIKO-denominated reserve is not a fixed ETH quantity. ρ_capital is not a constant: it is an unmeasured, time-varying function of the TAIKO/ETH price and of D_WITHDRAW, so no numeric Budget_ETH(n) is computable without a dated price, and stating the identity assumes none. Every term is unmeasured. (review round 1, findings ECO-01, ECO-02, ECO-03, ECO-12) (2) Structural result. The capital component scales with S_total however it is split; the operating component scales with n. Splitting the same stake across more validators therefore raises the reward bill linearly in n while fee revenue does not scale with n. Decentralisation is bought with operating-cost funding, and any rule that lowers S_min to admit small participants forces n up and the budget up proportionally. If n > n_max_fundable, the difference cannot be paid: the bounded, expiring subsidy of ECON-02 clause 4 is netted out of the identity (it is a transfer of the protocol's own inflow, not a second funding source), so the validator count is reduced through MEM-05's objective exit path or the chain halts; no rule pays unfunded rewards (ECON-02 clause 5). (3) Runway, not yield. The honest deliverable is a runway calculation, not a yield: "A protocol can have a large nominal reserve and still be close to security failure after adverse price or demand shocks" (arXiv:2606.03587, *Reserve Depletion and Security Runway in Proof-of-Stake Systems*, 2026-06-02, retrieved 2026-10-05, quoted in economics-raw.md §2.9). A design is "reward-sustainable" only when the fee-only region is reached before the reserve depletes; until then the correct description is "reserve-dependent". (4) Price reflexivity. Costs and rewards are ETH-denominated while the locked capital is TAIKO-denominated, so a fall in TAIKO's ETH price raises the ETH reward per unit of locked stake needed to hold operator economics constant and raises ρ_capital (price-risk plus expected slashing-loss premia): the same net ETH inflow then funds fewer validators. This is the reflexive loop of economics-raw.md §4.3. It must be disclosed and stress-tested, not parameterised away; paying rewards in ETH removes the mixed-currency identity of ECON-02 clause 1 but does not remove the loop, which is a property of the capital asset. (review round 1, finding ECO-03) (5) The launch table. Before launch, the following table must be filled with measurements, not estimates: for each candidate n — S_min, r_req(n) in ETH per TAIKO per year, Budget_ETH(n)/yr, NetETHInflow/yr, POOL_TOPUP/yr, D_subsidy/yr and the runway (economics-raw.md §3.4). No value in it may be published as measured until the measurement exists (PARAM-03). Failure mode: fixing S_min from participation goals without checking Budget(n) produces a set that either cannot be paid or silently centralises to the cheapest operators; a reward bill that grows linearly in n while revenue is flat is an unfunded decentralisation promise. Proven derived unmeasured
ECON-13 — publication of data before the proof: permissionlessness and who pays (the forced-inclusion deadline is deferred by D-16) (1) Publication is permissionless and decides nothing by itself. Under D-11 a batch's data (and a transaction's data, which in v1 creates no inclusion obligation because D-16 defers the narrow forced-inclusion rule) MAY be published to L1 in a transaction of its own, by any account — the batch's proposer, the transaction's sender, a relayer, a prover or an unrelated observer — and recorded in the Inbox's publication record. No permission, allowlist, proposer status, validator quorum, bond, stake or DAO action is required; no rule may reject a publication because of who published it, and no rule may require the publisher to be the party the data concerns. Publication is not a batch: it accepts no proof, advances no checkpoint, changes no validator set and creates no claim on L2 state, and the record becomes relevant only when a proof references it (L1-01, L1-02). (2) Who pays, and whether it is rewarded. The publisher pays the L1 gas and blob cost of its own publication transaction, C_PUBLISH. Publication is not a protocol payout: it has no term in the funding identity of ECON-02 clause 1, it is not paid from the reward pool, the treasury, a bond or a validator allocation, and no rule may budget or promise a publication reward that has no arrived funding. Whether the protocol ever pays for publication is Open, with a named falsifier: if no party publishes a censored batch's data while some observer holds it — because the act is unpaid while it costs gas — then the D-11 collapse of the withholding window does not hold in practice, the withholding residual stands (it was carried by REC-03, a D-16 tombstone), and the honest response is a recorded, fee-funded publication reward whose funding identity is re-derived and whose spam surface is answered explicitly (paying for arbitrary data publication is an unbounded outflow unless the reward is conditioned on data that is later proven), not an assertion that publication is incentive-compatible. Closing the Open requires a recorded decision, not a measurement alone. (3) The forced-inclusion due point and prefix — DEFERRED by D-16 (tombstone). Not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented. The due point, the proving deadline, the due set derived from the publication record, the capped FIFO prefix and the guest's check were owned by the L1-integration and proof-statement rules (FI-10–FI-14, PRF-04(vi)), which are tombstones. v1 has no inclusion obligation: no due point exists, no prefix is required, and a publication record is never read as a duty to include anything (FI-REMOVED-01; DEFERRED.md §1). The publication record's own deadline (deadlineBlock = record.l1BlockNumber + T_PROVE_DEADLINE, DA-09) is unchanged and is a property of the record, not an inclusion clock. (decision D-16: the forced-inclusion deadline is deferred with the machinery, and publication creates no obligation.) (4) The forced-inclusion breach — DEFERRED by D-16 (tombstone). Not normative in v1 and MUST NOT be implemented. This clause formerly made a proposal whose block omits the required prefix past its due point the forced-inclusion breach of ECON-04 clause (6) and assigned it the ordinary penalty and the reporter bounty. Both are deferred with the machinery, and v1 has no inclusion offence: a proposer that excludes a transaction — published or not — is not in breach, no evidence object exists, no penalty applies and no bounty is payable (FI-REMOVED-01; DEFERRED.md §1). The censorship gap is disclosed instead of penalised: a network-level adversary able to keep a user's data off L1 has no protocol remedy against it (LIVE-04). A-ECO-1 can fail with no rule change. P(t) and σ(t) move for reasons the protocol does not control; a design that passes A-ECO-1 at one price can fail at a lower one while every rule stays identical. The specification therefore never states "an attack costs X". It states the price at which the design stops being secure as a derived quantity, recomputed from measured inputs at review time (economics-raw.md §2.8 warnings 1–2, §4.3). Until σ(t) is measured from order-book depth and traded volume, any statement of the form "an attack costs X million" is UNVERIFIED and must not appear.

If the price falls. Attack cost falls roughly proportionally; the ETH-denominated reward per unit of locked TAIKO must rise to hold operator economics constant; ρ_capital rises (price risk plus expected slashing loss); honest operators exit first; voting power declines by at most CHURN_LIMIT per epoch (ECON-03 clause 6) and a balance cannot leave before D_WITHDRAW (ECON-03 clause 4) — D_WITHDRAW alone delays only the payout, not the loss of voting power, so the churn cap is what prevents a one-step drop; slashed stake is worth less exactly when deterrence is needed most; and a repricing event is precisely what makes the liquid-staking monetisation of ECON-10 clause 3 profitable (economics-raw.md §4.3). (review round 1, findings ECO-03, ECO-07)

The available responses, stated so that no rule invents a price guarantee: (a) disclose the derived price at which the design stops being secure; (b) treat D_WITHDRAW as a two-sided trade-off — long protects against late evidence, short reduces exposure to a falling price — and state the trade-off; (c) diversify the security budget so the fee-only region is reached earlier (ECON-12); (d) disclose the deterioration and change a governance parameter: there is no objective on-chain signal for the security-budget ratio S_total / (Λ · (EV_extractable + OV_option)), because P(t), σ(t), EV_extractable and OV_option are all off-chain, so no rule can halt on a price signal and "monitor and halt" is withdrawn here (ECON-02 clause 7). No parameter makes a PoS chain secured by a volatile token robust to that token's collapse, and the specification must not imply otherwise. (review round 1, finding ECO-16)
