07 — Economics, offences, penalties and rewards

This page owns the ECON-* rules: the stake ledger and its accounting, the reward funding identity, the withdrawal delay's economic interactions, the closed offence catalogue and its evidence encoding, the penalty structure, the destination of slashed stake, evidence and unbonding windows, duplicate and false-accusation handling, the minimum viable stake, concentration and liquid-staking risk, the scope of user losses, and the reward budget identity. Custody, effective stake and the exit delay are MEM-01–MEM-12; the atomic accept path and on-L1 fee accounting are L1-01–L1-11; the proof-side data binding is PRF-07. Status labels are STATUS-01–STATUS-11; timing is in seconds or L1 block numbers with no L1 proposer lookahead and no fixed L1 slot duration (GEN-06); every protocol hash is one canonical abi.encode with a right-padded domain tag and the chain id first (GEN-05). Nothing on this page is implemented, deployed, benchmarked or audited (GEN-01).

Every monetary quantity on this page is UNMEASURED. The primary source is economics-raw.md (pinned revision 7718753c1, external sources retrieved 2026-10-05), which carries the file:line evidence and the external source register. No price, market capitalisation, float, depth, yield or cost figure is stated here as a measurement, and no figure is hard-coded anywhere in this specification (ECON-10 clause 5). A number below is tagged derived, sourced or unmeasured; a formula is a relation that must hold, not a value.
Fixed decisions applied here. Staking and slashable consensus collateral are the existing TAIKO token and nothing else; gas stays ETH (D7). Validator rewards are denominated and paid in ETH (ECON-02 clause 1): D7 fixes the staking asset, not the reward asset. No rule creates a data-first path (D5) — which is why the evidence window must extend past settlement (ECON-07). No recovery, timeout or penalty may invalidate a PoS-finalized block (D2, REC-01); penalties debit stake, they never rewrite history. Every deterrence claim is conditional on the Byzantine-stake assumption A-CONS-1 (< 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum in the relevant set version) and on the economic assumption A-ECO-1, which is an assumption, not a guarantee. Where a claim needs an assumption, it says so at the claim. Security is funded from L2 fees (D-8, ECON-02 clause 7), and slashed stake goes to the protocol treasury with no burn option (D-9, ECON-06).

1. The stake ledger, custody and accounting

ECON-01 — stake ledger, custody and accounting (1) One ledger, one asset. Every bonded consensus balance is recorded in the single L1 custody contract of MEM-01. The only asset it accepts is the existing TAIKO ERC-20 (0x10dea67478c5F8C5E2D90e5E9B26dBe60c54d800); amounts are uint256 TAIKO base units at 18 decimals. Bonded balances are not transferable, not delegable (MEM-04), not represented by shares, receipts, LP tokens or any bridged or wrapped form, and no ETH enters the ledger as collateral (D7). (2) Separate from the vote system. Bonded balance and voting power are read from this ledger only (MEM-02). No rule, client, contract, interface or proof statement may use balanceOf, getVotes, getPastVotes or any ERC20Votes checkpoint as stake. Reasons, sourced: renounceVotingPower() sets a holder's votes to zero irreversibly while the balance is unchanged (TaikoToken.sol:53-74; economics-raw.md §1.1), and four hard-coded accounts are excluded from votes entirely (TaikoToken.sol:17-21,130-136). A quantity a holder can unilaterally zero cannot be an oracle for at-risk capital. (3) Accounting invariants. Write b(v) for an entry's bonded balance, D, W, S for the cumulative deposits credited, withdrawals paid and slashes debited, and TOTAL for the ledger's stored bonded total. After every state-changing entry point, exactly: 
INV-A   TOTAL == Σ_v b(v)                       (exact uint256 equality, no rounding)
INV-B   TOTAL == D − W − S
INV-C   a debit applies a = min(requested, b(v)); b(v) never becomes negative;
        the event records (applied, unapplied) and no debt is carried outside the ledger
INV-D   a deposit credits at most the TAIKO balance actually received by the contract
        between the pre-call and post-call reads (no fee-on-transfer credit, no reentrancy credit)
The contract MUST expose a view checkInvariants() returns (bool) evaluating INV-A–INV-D, and the implementation's differential/fuzz suite MUST assert it after every call. A violation is a critical defect, not a degraded mode. (4) Slashing base. For every epoch e in which an entry appears in a set root, the ledger retains SlashBase(v,e) — the entry's effective stake in that set version — until the later of the close of the evidence window for e and the settlement of any slash for e (MEM-06 clauses 1–2). An offence at epoch e is charged against SlashBase(v,e), never against the balance at evidence time, and the total charge for e never exceeds it. (5) Entry points and error behaviour. deposit(uint256 amount) reverts if amount == 0 or if the received TAIKO delta differs from amount. requestExit(), cancelExit(), rotateKey(bytes32) and withdraw() are the owner-only entry points of MEM-05/MEM-07; slash(address v, uint256 epoch, bytes evidence) is callable by any address holding objective evidence (MEM-06). withdraw() reverts while any delay or exposure of MEM-05 clause 3 remains. The ledger MUST NOT expose any owner-, DAO-, operator- or emergency-triggered path that moves a bonded balance (SYS-04, INV-04); an upgrade authority may change code but must preserve INV-A–INV-D and every retained (e, SlashBase(v,e)) pair. Failure modes: reading stake from ERC20Votes makes voting power renounceable while the capital stays and lets a whale with zero votes still be counted (or the reverse); the existing gwei bond ledger caps stake near 1.845e10 TAIKO with ≈18.4 TAIKO granularity (derived, economics-raw.md §1.4), which is a rounding rule inside the consensus path; a best-effort, offence-agnostic debit makes a slash decorative; a ledger total that drifts from the sum of balances makes reward and penalty accounting unauditable and lets an offender leave with stake that should be at risk. 2. Rewards and the funding identity

ECON-02 — rewards and their funding identity; no token-appreciation assumption (1) Assets: stake in TAIKO, rewards in ETH, and exactly which terms are TAIKO. TAIKO is the staking and slashable-collateral asset and the only asset the ledger accepts (D7, ECON-01 clause 1). Validator and prover rewards are denominated and paid in ETH, collected on L1. D7 fixes the staking asset, not the reward asset, so ETH rewards are conforming and the identity below is single-currency (ETH) and evaluable. The one TAIKO-denominated protocol payout in this specification is the reporter bounty (ECON-06), and it is not a charge on the ETH fee inflow: it is paid out of TAIKO collateral already debited from a proven offender by slash(), it can be paid only when such a debit was applied, and ECON-06 clause 5 bounds it to be strictly smaller than that debit. It therefore needs no TAIKO/ETH price and does not make this identity mixed-currency; the left-hand term rewards_ETH counts only ETH-denominated payouts. Over any accounting period, the sum of those ETH-denominated payouts to validators, provers and any other role MUST satisfy

(review round 1, findings ECO-01, ECO-13) (2) No appreciation may be assumed. No rule, parameter, reward schedule, interface string, learning page or interface text may assume, require, promise or be conditioned on an increase in TAIKO's market price, market depth, float or reward-pool value. A reward is a nominal ETH amount fixed when it is awarded; the protocol states nothing about its fiat value. "Rewards are sustainable" is a claim that may be made only when the fee-only region is reached (clause 4, ECON-12), never on the strength of a reserve that still looks large (arXiv:2606.03587, quoted in economics-raw.md §2.9). (3) TAIKO has no mint path today. 1,000,000,000 ether was minted once in init (TaikoToken.sol:41) and no mint or burn exists on the L1 token or its base; the L2 bridged representation's burn does not affect L1 supply (economics-raw.md §1.1, sourced). A reward funded by new TAIKO therefore requires a token upgrade — a DAO action under GOV-01 — and is not a standing funding source. The repository's recorded objective "No Built-In PoS Reward" (tokenomics_objective_metrics.md:19) forbids paying inflationary staking rewards; it may be reversed only by a new recorded user decision (GEN-02), and until it is reversed the schedule MUST be fundable from clause 1 alone. Because rewards are paid in ETH (clause 1), a TAIKO-denominated reward would in addition require a conversion rule and a price; no such rule exists here and no price constant is permitted (ECON-10 clause 5), which is a further reason the standing schedule is the ETH identity of clause 1 and nothing else. (4) Every subsidy is bounded, expiring, and netted out. The subsidy is one line item with (a) a maximum amount in ETH (the reward asset of clause 1 — a mixed TAIKO/ETH maximum is not a line item, it is two unaggregated assets), (b) a start and an end expressed in unix seconds, (c) a pre-funded account that no rule of this specification may top up or borrow against, and (d) no reward obligation: at every period boundary, accrued payouts may not exceed the realised net inflow of clause 1. The subsidy account is capitalised by the draw D_subsidy that clause 1 subtracts, so a subsidy is a transfer of the protocol's own inflow, not a second funding source: it can smooth timing within its term and can never make an unfunded schedule funded. When the subsidy is exhausted or expires, the protocol pays the fee-funded amount only and must remain coherent: the validator count is reduced through the objective exit path of MEM-05 (never by confiscation) or the chain halts safely (HALT-01). A design that is coherent only while the subsidy lasts is non-conforming. (review round 1, finding ECO-01) (5) Payment mechanism: the pool, the per-signer input, the claim, and the per-epoch identity. (a) Custody. The validator reward pool is an ETH balance held in a segregated account of the L1 staking contract of MEM-01 — not in the Inbox, whose L1-11 ledger (the prover reward) is not touched by validator payouts. (user decision D-6: forced inclusion removed; no forced-inclusion escrow, fee or refund term remains in this accounting) ETH in the pool is never collateral, never stake and never slashable: it is not counted in INV-A–INV-D (ECON-01), no slash may debit it, and no rule may borrow against it. Its inflows are the terms of clause 1 (L2 fee revenue, arriving by the sweep of clause 7, and POOL_TOPUP); the pool never carries a negative balance. Its outflows are exactly two, and they are disjoint draws on the same balance: the validator payout Alloc(e) of (5)(d) and the proving share ProvingShare(e) of (5)(e), which is credited to the Inbox prover reward ledger of L1-11 only by a transfer that debits the pool in the same transaction. No inflow may be assigned to both: Alloc(e) + ProvingShare(e) ≤ pool_before(e), checked at the (5)(d) freeze, so one inflow cannot be allocated twice. (S5 §9: the pool identity includes the proving-share outflow.) (b) Per-signer input. Participation is attested by the only object that can carry it to L1: the authenticated signer bitmap of the head certificate, exposed as the public input signerBitmap (PRF-02) and verified in-guest against the certificate's signatures, the canonical set order and the quorum predicate (PRF-04(ii)–(iii)) before the batch can land. After verification, land(data, proof) MUST write that bitmap into the staking contract's per-epoch participation accumulator — participated[e][index] = true for every set bit, for every epoch the batch's heights cover — and nothing else may set it. A payout MUST NOT be made on presence in the set root, on bonded balance, or on any unauthenticated claim. (c) Who may claim. The staking contract MUST expose claimValidatorReward(uint64 epoch, uint32 index) (name indicative), callable by any address, so that no operator depends on a keeper. A claim pays the entry's recorded owner, never the caller, and each (epoch, index) may be claimed at most once. Claims for epoch e open only after evidenceClose(e) (ECON-07), when the epoch's allocation is fixed and its participant set and participating stake are frozen (5)(d); a claim before then reverts. (d) Accounting identity per epoch, with the participant set frozen at the window's close. Let Alloc(e) be the ETH the recorded allocation policy assigns to epoch e's validator payout. Let P(e) be the frozen participant set: the canonical signer indices whose participation bit for e was written by land at or before evidenceClose(e) (ECON-07(1)), and let PS(e) = Σ_{u ∈ P(e)} effStake(u,e) be the participating stake frozen at that same instant. Neither P(e) nor PS(e) may be read from mutable state at claim time: the staking contract MUST compute and store both at the freeze, and the frozen denominators are what the identity below uses. Cutoff rule. A batch of epoch e that lands after evidenceClose(e) is legal — no admission rule may reject it (L1-04, L1-06) — but it MUST NOT change P(e) or PS(e): its validators are recorded as participants on L1 and are not paid from epoch e's allocation and not charged to any later epoch's allocation — the claim for e is closed by the freeze. Consequence, stated rather than assumed away: validators of an epoch whose batches settle only after that epoch's window closes are unpaid for that epoch, because no rule may reopen a final allocation or create a retroactive claim; any account may land a batch at any time (L1-04), so the remedy is timely settlement, not retroactivity. Participation bits written for an epoch whose own freeze has not yet passed are unaffected and enter that epoch's P normally. For each index in P(e) the staking contract MUST resolve the committed entry and its effStake from the set root stored for e (MEM-09(2)), so the accumulator's key is the canonical signer index and v below denotes the entry committed at that index; an entry that did not sign is paid nothing, however much stake it holds. Then, exactly: 
pool_after(e)   = pool_before(e) + inflow(e) - Σ_{v ∈ P(e)} payout(v,e) - ProvingShare(e)
payout(v,e)     = floor( Alloc(e) · effStake(v,e) / PS(e) )   // PS(e) frozen at evidenceClose(e)
Σ_v payout(v,e)  ≤  Alloc(e)                        // no borrowing, no negative pool
Alloc(e) + ProvingShare(e)  ≤  pool_before(e)           // one inflow is never allocated twice
Alloc(e) MUST be fixed before the epoch's first claim and MUST NOT exceed the pool's realised net inflow for the period — the ETH that has actually arrived by the sweep of clause 7 plus POOL_TOPUP (clause 1); the floor-rounding remainder stays in the pool and is never minted. The identity Σ_v payout(v,e) ≤ Alloc(e) ≤ pool_before(e), evaluated at the frozen denominators, is a checked condition, not an intention: the staking contract MUST expose a view that evaluates it for every epoch with an open claim — exactly as ECON-01(3) requires the checkInvariants() view for INV-A–INV-D — and the implementation's differential/fuzz suite MUST assert it after every claim. This closes review round 3 finding R3-CE-03: P(e) and the participating stake are frozen at evidenceClose(e), and a batch of epoch e landing after that instant cannot change the payout denominator. This closes review round 3 finding R3-PRF-03: the per-epoch payout identity is computable from frozen state, the accumulator key is the canonical signer index resolved against the stored set root, and the identity is checked rather than asserted. (e) What fixes Alloc(e): the recorded allocation policy, its owner, its formula and the proving-share outflow. The mechanism that moves L2 fee revenue to the L1 pool is clause 7. The allocation policy is recorded in the L1 staking contract's storage as one policy record, written by the activation transaction, and it may be changed only by an upgrade under GOV-01 that is recorded in the decision log before it takes effect and applies to future epochs only — never to an epoch already entered and never to an allocation already fixed. The policy is two non-negative integer shares in parts per million, ALLOC_VAL_PPM and ALLOC_PRV_PPM, with ALLOC_VAL_PPM + ALLOC_PRV_PPM ≤ 1_000_000. The per-epoch quantities are computed from the pool's realised balance and fixed at the freeze of (5)(d): Alloc(e) = floor(ALLOC_VAL_PPM · pool_before(e) / 1_000_000) and ProvingShare(e) = floor(ALLOC_PRV_PPM · pool_before(e) / 1_000_000), with Alloc(e) + ProvingShare(e) ≤ pool_before(e); the rounding remainder stays in the pool. ProvingShare(e) is the proving-share outflow: it is credited to the Inbox prover reward ledger of L1-11 only by a permissionless transfer that debits the pool in the same transaction, and it is the only credit that ledger may receive from the pool (5)(a). Because both shares are computed from one balance under one sum bound, one inflow cannot be allocated twice. An empty pool pays nothing: while no ETH has arrived, Alloc(e) = 0 and ProvingShare(e) = 0, the pool has no inflow, and no validator reward can be paid at all — the security budget A-ECO-1 compares against attack profit is zero, the schedule of clause 1 is unsatisfiable, and no rule may promise, budget or advertise a reward beyond what has arrived. That consequence is stated, not assumed away. (6) Rewards are not a safety claim. Paying rewards deters nothing by itself; deterrence is ECON-05's argument and is conditional on A-ECO-1. A reward rate parameter is unmeasured until its inputs are measured (PARAM-03). (7) Funding path: the L2 fee vault, the permissionless sweep through the preserved Bridge, and the arrival identity. (user decision D-8: security is funded from L2 fees; this clause is the path and the honest consequences of it) (a) Collection — the L2 fee vault. One L2 account, the L2 fee vault (L2FeeVault, a new deployment whose address is recorded before launch; it is not one of the preserved addresses of D3/MIG-06), accumulates the L2 fee revenue of clause 1: the execution base-fee component and the priority-fee component of every L2 transaction (F_exec_L2, F_priority_L2). The L2 fee policy MUST route both components to this vault; a component that is destroyed, paid to a block beneficiary, or otherwise diverted is not collected revenue, MUST NOT be counted in clause 1, and MUST NOT be promised as a reward. The vault holds ETH only: it is never collateral, never stake, never slashable, and never part of the ledger of ECON-01. It has no discretion: no caller, operator, validator, DAO action or upgrade path may redirect its balance to any destination other than the sweep of (b). (b) The sweep: protocol-constructed, no destination argument, and the correct fee arithmetic. The vault MUST expose one sweep entry point that moves its accumulated ETH to L1 through the preserved Bridge (MSG-01). The sweep takes no argument that influences the destination: it builds the bridge message itself from protocol constants — the canonical bridge invocation selector as its method data, the vault as sender, the reward-pool address of clause 5(a) as destOwner, and to set to that same pool — and nothing else. The destination is a protocol constant recorded before launch, never a parameter the caller supplies; the sweep is the only route by which L2 fee revenue reaches L1, and there is no direct L1 claim, no accounting entry against future settlement, no escrow, and no second destination. Who may sweep: any address, at any time, with no gate, allowlist, reservation or operator action; the caller chooses only when, never where or how much is credited to whom. Fee arithmetic, stated correctly. The preserved Bridge requires value + fee == msg.value. Here value is the whole swept amount and is credited to the pool in full; fee is additional ETH supplied in addition to the swept amount by the sweep transaction that carries it (the same caller that already pays the sweep's L2 gas), and it MUST NOT be deducted from value. A sweep that reduces the credited amount to cover the fee is non-conforming. The L1 release cost of the message is part of C_bridge_ops of clause 1 and is not a second charge on the pool. One shot and replay. Each sweep emits exactly one bridge message, is not batchable with any other message, and carries the vault's monotonic sweep counter as its nonce; a replayed or duplicated message is rejected by the domain- and nonce-scoped replay protection of MSG-02, and a message that does not authenticate credits nothing. Cadence, and the zero-balance no-op: there is no protocol-mandated schedule and no keeper duty — the sweep MUST be callable whenever the vault's balance is non-zero, and the vault MUST NOT hold a balance that no rule-legal call can move; a sweep of a zero balance is a no-op and MUST NOT block anything, and no argument exists by which a caller could route or partially divert a non-zero balance. The sweep credits nothing by assertion: the L1 release credits the pool only when the preserved Bridge has authenticated the message (MSG-02). (S5 §7: the sweep is protocol-constructed, takes no destination argument, and the Bridge fee is additional ETH rather than a deduction from the swept amount.) (c) Arrival identity, with the corrected fee arithmetic. Fees are revenue when they arrive at the pool, not when they are collected on L2 and not when a sweep is sent. Let s range over the sweeps whose bridge message releases to the pool in the period. The credited amount is the whole swept amount; the message fee fee(s) is additional ETH supplied with the sweep (clause (b)) and is not deducted from it (unmeasured; a cost to the sweep caller, registered in §Unmeasured inputs). Then, exactly:

                                                        // covered by an L1 checkpoint at the period's end

The right-hand side is an upper bound on the left, never a promise: collection precedes arrival by at least the settlement pipeline, so a period's inflow cannot exceed the fees collected up to the last settled L2 block of that period, and the same ETH is counted exactly once — the identity of clause 1 is evaluated on the pool's realised inflow, never on fees in flight. fee(s) and every timing term here are unmeasured and are registered in §Unmeasured inputs. (S5 §7: the fee is additional ETH, not a deduction from the swept amount; the arrival identity is corrected accordingly.) (d) Honest consequences, stated at the claim. (i) Rewards lag the settlement pipeline. The fee ETH is on L2 and crosses only inside a bridge message that L1 releases after a checkpoint covering the sweep's own L2 block is accepted on L1 (STATUS-06) and the message is proven against that checkpoint (MSG-03). The lag is therefore at least the settlement pipeline of ECON-03 plus message proving and L1 release, and it can be longer; no reward may be paid, promised, budgeted or advertised from fees that have not arrived. (ii) An empty pool pays nothing. If arrival is zero — fees are zero, the vault has not been swept, or the bridge has not delivered — then Alloc(e) = 0, the per-epoch identity of clause 5(d) pays nothing, and no rule may borrow against future arrival, reopen a closed allocation, or present the fee stream as a reserve; the security budget A-ECO-1 compares against attack profit is then zero, and saying so is part of the rule. (iii) A bridging failure stops rewards rather than creating a claim on future revenue. If the Bridge cannot carry a sweep — the L2 transaction fails, the message ends FAILED or RECALLED (MSG-02), it is never proven, or no batch covering its L2 block is ever accepted — the ETH remains on L2 or is returned by the Bridge's own status handling, and it is revenue of no period. The protocol creates no receivable, no debt, no insurance and no promise of future revenue: providers are unpaid for the affected epochs, and the honest response is to disclose the outage and repair the path, never to pay from a balance the pool does not hold. No rule may halt on a price, budget or fee signal as a substitute for this path: no objective on-chain signal for the security-budget ratio exists (§TAIKO price volatility: the inputs P(t), σ(t), EV_extractable and OV_option are off-chain), so "monitor and halt" is withdrawn as a response and the honest deliverable is disclosure plus a governance parameter change. 3. Withdrawal delay and the settlement pipeline

ECON-03 — withdrawal delay derived from the complete settlement pipeline (1) The withdrawal delay D_WITHDRAW is the quantity derived in MEM-05, clause 4. That formula is normative there and is not restated here (GEN-03); this rule fixes the economic interactions that must hold around it. The canonical registered name is D_WITHDRAW (09); the spelling D_withdraw used by MEM-05 and elsewhere is a deprecated alias for the same parameter, and clients, parameters and migration text MUST use D_WITHDRAW. (review round 2, finding E-R2-11(c): the alias pair is resolved to one registered name, as the round-1 T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED/ACTIVATION_RATE_LIMIT/WS_MAX deprecations did.) (2) D_WITHDRAW is a function of the complete settlement pipeline that MEM-05 defines — proof completion, detection, evidence submission, an L1-inclusion quantile T_L1_include(p), L1 finality, processing and a margin. A delay derived from nominal proving latency alone is a defect: D5 lands data and proof in one transaction, so there is no data-first window in which a third party could object before settlement, and the evidence clock starts at the offence on L2, which may precede provability by hours (economics-raw.md §2.5). (3) One clock, three consumers. The same D_WITHDRAW must simultaneously cover (a) the evidence window W_evidence of ECON-07, (b) the correlated-penalty application delay CORR_DELAY of ECON-05, and (c) L1 processing of the slash. If any of the three is longer than D_WITHDRAW minus the others, the design is non-conforming; the two must be chosen together or neither works (economics-raw.md §3.2 point 2). (4) Exit does not stop duty or exposure. A validator that has called requestExit() remains in every set version it is already committed to, keeps voting, and remains fully slashable until the exit becomes effective (MEM-05 clause 1). This is a deliberate change from the current bond ledger, in which hasSufficientBond becomes false the moment a withdrawal is requested (LibBonds.sol:130-141; economics-raw.md §1.4 point 1). (5) Representation. D_WITHDRAW is a duration in seconds; the conditions of MEM-05 clause 3 are evaluated against L1 state (block timestamps and L1 block numbers), never against a wall clock during validation (GEN-06), and T_L1_include(p) is a quantile measured from inclusion-delay data, never a mean or a nominal constant. (6) Exit churn is capped per epoch. CHURN_LIMIT is the maximum fraction of an epoch's voting power that may leave the set at one epoch boundary: the L1 staking contract MUST NOT make more than CHURN_LIMIT · TotalVP(e) of effective stake inactive at any single epoch e. Exits beyond the cap are neither cancelled nor penalised; they are deferred. How a deferred exit is ordered. Deferred exits become effective in ascending order of the request coordinates (l1BlockNumber, txIndex, logIndex) of the requestExit() transaction — the objective ordering key owned by MEM-03(2) — so no later request takes effect before an earlier deferred one, and the cap never reorders the queue by stake, key, identity, reputation or fee. A deferred exit is never cancelled by the protocol: it remains pending until the first snapshot the cap does not exclude, and only the owner's cancelExit() before effectiveness removes it (MEM-05(2), §2.1 transition 3b). A deferred entry remains in every set version it is already committed to and remains fully slashable (clause 4). The cap exists because otherwise a coordinated exit — every operator calling requestExit() in one L1 block, or the public end of the subsidy (ECON-02 clause 4) or a price move making exit rational for many operators at once — drops the stake that deters attack in a single step, after which an attacker acquires or rents a set that no longer satisfies the deterrence relation of ECON-09 clause 2 and A-ECO-1. Deferral is a delay of an exit, never a denial of it and never a confiscation: withdraw() remains available once the delay and the exposure gates of MEM-05 are satisfied. CHURN_LIMIT is unmeasured; it must be derived from the deterrence relation, not chosen for convenience. Which rule wins when the cap binds. MEM-05(2) states the same condition — the exit becomes effective at the first set version at or after the request that the cap does not exclude — and §2.1 lifecycle row 4 carries the same qualification. The two are one rule — MEM-05 owns the transition, this clause bounds how much stake may leave in one epoch — and an implementation that makes an exit effective regardless of the cap is non-conforming, because it voids the cap. A deferred exit keeps the entry in every committed set and fully slashable (clause 4) until its effective version arrives. This closes review round 2 finding R2A-06: the ordering key and the never-cancelled property of a deferred exit are fixed here, and MEM-05(2) and §2.1 row 4 state the cap's power to defer effectiveness. 5. Penalty structure

ECON-05 — penalty structure and the convex correlated penalty (1) Parameters. IMM_FRAC(c) is the immediate penalty fraction for offence class c, a parameter with 0 < IMM_FRAC(c) ≤ 1 so that every proven offence has a non-zero immediate cost (value unmeasured, PARAM-01; Ethereum's precedent is a fixed fraction of the offender's effective balance, sourced in economics-raw.md §2.3). F_SAT = 1/3 is the safety threshold, numerically equal to the b of A-CONS-1. CORR_Q ∈ {1, 2} selects the penalty shape. CORR_DELAY is a duration in seconds (clause 5). (2) Implicated fraction. For epoch e and offence class c, let O(e,c) be the set of entries with at least one applied offence of class c at e, each counted once, and let TotalVP(e) be the published total voting power of that set version (MEM-02): 
f(e,c)  =  ( Σ_{v ∈ O(e,c)} SlashBase(v,e) ) / TotalVP(e)
c(f)    =  min( (f / F_SAT)^CORR_Q , 1 )            with F_SAT = 1/3
P(v,e)  =  min( SlashBase(v,e) · ( IMM_FRAC(c) + c(f(e,c)) ) , SlashBase(v,e) )
charge  =  min( P(v,e) , bonded(v) , remaining_exposure(v,e) )
CORR_Q = 1 reproduces Ethereum's min(3f, 1); CORR_Q = 2 reproduces Polkadot's min((3x/n)^2, 1) (economics-raw.md §2.3, sourced). Both are convex in f and both saturate at f = F_SAT: c(F_SAT) = 1 by construction, so the confiscation threshold and the safety threshold coincide — a coalition that reaches the 1/3 of stake at which A-CONS-1 stops holding loses 100% of its epoch stake in the correlated component, in addition to the immediate component. (3) Bounded debit. The charge is bounded by the bonded balance and by the per-epoch charge base; the applied amount is debited, the unapplied remainder is recorded in the event and is not carried as a debt outside the ledger (MEM-06 clause 2, ECON-11). No entry's balance may go negative (ECON-01 INV-C). (4) Immediate component. The immediate component is debited when the accepted evidence transaction executes, through the same slash() call that sets the offence identity. (5) Correlated component and its delay. f(e,c) cannot be final before the evidence window for e closes, so the correlated component is computed once at corrApplyAt(e,c) = evidenceClose(e) + CORR_DELAY and debited by a permissionless applyCorrelated(e, c) call; until then the amount is locked against the exposure accounting of MEM-06. CORR_DELAY must satisfy ECON-07's inequality with D_WITHDRAW — a long delay is the price of a correlated penalty (Ethereum applies its correlated component up to EPOCHS_PER_SLASHINGS_VECTOR/2, ≈36.4 days after the slash; economics-raw.md §2.3, §3.2 point 2, sourced). (6) Premise, stated at the claim. This structure deters only if the slashed stake has value and is actually confiscated. Both halves are assumptions: A-ECO-1 (the dollar loss exceeds the attack profit at the relevant time) is assumed, and confiscation is possible only while the evidence window of ECON-07 holds and the exposure accounting of MEM-06 is enforced. If TAIKO's market value or liquidity falls, the same token amount is a weaker deterrent; the specification states the derived price at which the design stops being secure rather than asserting security at an assumed price (see TAIKO price volatility). (7) Honest-mistake tail. On a small validator set, a correlated penalty makes one operator's duplicated key a correlated loss for a non-trivial fraction of the set. The mitigation is operational (slashing-protection databases, remote signers, key custody); no parameter choice removes the tail. The specification MUST disclose it and MUST NOT describe the correlated penalty as harmless. (Polkadot's documented incidents, all cloned keystores, are the sourced example; economics-raw.md §2.3.) Failure mode: a linear-only penalty makes the bribe for a coalition linear in its size and the confiscation threshold unrelated to the safety threshold, so "Byzantine stake < 1/3" remains an assumption about intent rather than a statement about loss given attack; an unbounded or negative-capable debit breaks the ledger invariants; a correlated penalty whose application delay exceeds the withdrawal delay is uncollectible and therefore decorative. Proven sourced unmeasured (the saturation argument is conditional on A-CONS-1 and on the coincidence of F_SAT with the quorum denominator; the deterrence claim is conditional on A-ECO-1.)

7. Evidence window versus unbonding period

ECON-07 — the evidence window is strictly shorter than the unbonding delay (1) The window, anchored to the offence epoch's own set version, with the anchor stored on L1. W_evidence(e) is a duration in seconds per epoch e. Evidence for an offence at epoch e is admissible only if the L1 block that includes the slash() transaction has block.timestamp ≤ evidenceClose(e), where evidenceClose(e) opens at t_root(e) and closes no earlier than max(t_root(e), t_observed(e)) + W_evidence(e) (the production-anchored close and the Open anchor t_observed(e) are defined below). What t_root(e) is, concretely. It is the run-time-visible block.timestamp of the L1 transaction whose call to commitSet() appended epoch e's entry to the staking contract's per-epoch mapping (MEM-09 clause 2, the transaction N(k)). The staking contract MUST persist that timestamp with the entry — epoch → (setRoot, totalVotingPower, rootCommittedAt) — written once in the same call and immutable thereafter, and the Inbox and slash() MUST read the anchor from that stored field. It MUST NOT be recomputed from blockhash, reconstructed from an event log after the EVM's 256-block window, or taken from the L2 header field set_version_commit (which L1 cannot read); a missing entry means no window exists and a slash for e reverts EpochNotCommitted(e). The anchor is the commit block's timestamp, not the later moment at which that block became Ethereum-final: the finality requirement is enforced where it belongs — MEM-09 clause 5 requires mapping[e] to be Ethereum-final before any block of epoch e is finalized — and clause (4)'s 3 · E_EPOCH term is derived for exactly this anchor (under the two-epoch lookahead of CONS-13(3) the commit precedes the epoch's first height by up to 2 · E_EPOCH). Defining the anchor as "became final" would make it un-storable on L1 and would shorten the admissible interval by the finality lag; the stored commit timestamp is the value the non-emptiness inequality of clause (4) already assumes, and it is registered as t_root(e) in 09. (review round 2, finding E-R2-07: the anchor is a stored, run-time-visible L1 value rather than an event-log reconstruction, and the window is computable.) The close may not precede the epoch's production. The commit timestamp is an L1 wall clock, and the two clocks diverge across a halt: L1 time and commitSet() keep advancing while L2 height production stops (MEM-09(1), CONS-13), so after a halt longer than W_evidence(e), t_root(e) + W_evidence(e) can fall before any height of epoch e exists. A conforming window therefore opens when the epoch's set root is committed — t_root(e) — but MUST NOT close before the epoch has been produced on L2 and its batches have had the full window to land: it closes no earlier than max(t_root(e), t_observed(e)) + W_evidence(e), where t_observed(e) is the L1-observable instant of the event that makes an offence of epoch e observable and starts the epoch's full landing window. The anchor is the event that makes the offence observable, never L1 wall-clock alone; no rule, implementation or interface may treat the commit-anchored instant as sufficient, and clause (4)'s non-empty-interval requirement is applied to this later close. The later close is fixed by this rule from the start from stored facts: it is not a re-opening, extension or waiver after the fact by any party, and the prohibition below on re-anchoring a window to a later or unrelated set-commit block is unaffected. Open anchor. Which L1 fact carries t_observed(e), and where it is stored, is not fixed by this specification: MEM-09(2)'s stored tuple carries only the commit timestamp rootCommittedAt, and the Inbox checkpoint record of L1-07 carries the accepting L1 block number rather than that block's timestamp, so no existing stored value carries the epoch's production or landing time. Candidate anchors, to be fixed by a recorded decision before launch, are the L1 timestamp at which the checkpoint record for the epoch's last height h_last(e) is first written, or a production/landing marker written into the per-epoch entry once the epoch's heights are known to have been produced. Consequence until that anchor is defined and stored: the commit-anchored close remains the only enforceable condition, and after a halt longer than W_evidence(e) the resumed epochs' offences are inadmissible — their heights are produced after evidenceClose(e) — while the ledger's release gate can already have opened (MEM-05 clause 3, clause (5) below), so stake exposed to those epochs can be withdrawn between the offence and the moment its evidence would become admissible, and INV-03's premise that the stake counting toward quorum is slashable is void for every such height. The liveness consequence is the same defect: an epoch with more than one third of its committed weight withdrawn and offline can never reach quorum, so the chain can wedge permanently although the L1 ledger holds a healthy bonded set. That is clause (4)'s empty interval — a defect, not a tuning choice — and it MUST be closed before launch. This closes review round 3 finding R3-LIV-01: the window's close is anchored to the event that makes the offence observable rather than to L1 wall-clock alone, and the L1 anchor that carries it is recorded as Open with its consequence instead of being assumed. The anchor is the offence's epoch and its set version, never an arbitrary set-commit block: set versions may be created permissionlessly (MEM-09), and a later or unrelated commitment MUST NOT be used to compute, re-anchor, extend or shorten the window of e. No window may be re-opened, extended or waived after the fact, by any party (SYS-04); a later inclusion reverts EvidenceWindowClosed. (review round 1, findings ECO-04, ECO-05) (2) Strict inequality. For every epoch, W_evidence + T_process + CORR_DELAY + M_ev < D_WITHDRAW, where T_process is the L1 processing time of a slash (MEM-05 clause 4), CORR_DELAY is ECON-05 clause 5's delay and M_ev > 0 is a margin. Equivalently, the first moment at which any stake exposed to e may be released is strictly later than the last moment at which in-window evidence can be applied. Equality is non-conforming: it turns the deadline into an L1-ordering race. The two bounds are simultaneous: a conforming W_evidence MUST satisfy clause 4's lower bound and this strict inequality at the same time, and if no value does, D_WITHDRAW (MEM-05 clause 4) must be raised; the parameters are chosen together (ECON-03 clause 3), never one against the other. (3) Why the window exists. The unbonding period is not a user-experience choice; it is the window in which late-arriving evidence must still be able to confiscate the stake that caused the offence: "there can be a delay between an infraction occurring, and evidence of the infraction reaching the state machine (this is one of the primary reasons for the existence of the unbonding period)" (Cosmos SDK x/slashing/README.md, retrieved 2026-10-05, quoted in economics-raw.md §2.4). (4) The window must not be empty, and it must cover the whole pipeline. For every offence at epoch e the admissible interval [t_offence, evidenceClose(e)] MUST be non-empty and MUST contain the whole settlement pipeline from the offence to the earliest possible detection of it — permitted proof latency, L1 inclusion, L1 finality, then detection and submission. 8. False accusations, duplicates and replay

ECON-08 — false accusations, duplicate evidence, replay and post-exit evidence (1) Cost of an invalid submission. A submission that fails any check of ECON-04 clause 2 reverts entirely; the submitter pays exactly the L1 gas. No bond, deposit or stake is consumed, no state is written, and the accused has nothing to answer. The protocol MUST NOT require the accused to respond within any deadline: a missed response deadline that can consume stake converts the availability assumption A-CONS-5 into a penalty, and a party that is offline is not thereby guilty. A reporter bond or deposit may be introduced only by a recorded decision, and then only if (a) it is returned exactly on a valid submission, (b) it is forfeited only on an objectively decidable condition — a duplicate identity, or an object whose signatures do not verify — never on "the accusation was not proven", and (c) its size does not deter reporting a real offence. Its calibration is unmeasured. (2) Idempotence. For a valid submission the identity is id = (v, epoch, offenceType, keccak256(evidence)) (MEM-06 clause 4); the first application sets applied[id] permanently, and a second submission of the same id is a no-op as defined in ECON-04 clause 4 — no additional debit, no second bounty, no window extension. (3) Distinct offences in one epoch. A distinct valid offence by the same v at the same epoch is a distinct id and is recorded. The total charge for the epoch remains bounded by SlashBase(v,e) (MEM-06 clause 2); once that cap is reached the remainder is recorded as unapplied and is not collected (ECON-05 clause 3). The correlated fraction f(e,c) counts each offender once and is applied to every offender of the class at corrApplyAt(e,c), so later distinct offences still raise the correlated component for the whole class. (4) Replay. Every evidence object carries the domain tag and the chain id (GEN-05, ECON-04 clause 2); an object is invalid under any other chain id, and signatures are checked against the set root of the epoch named in the evidence, so an object from another epoch or set version reverts. applied[id] is permanent: no expiry, no upgrade, no migration and no recovery path may clear it, and an upgrade must preserve it (GOV-03). (5) Evidence that arrives after the offender has exited is impossible by construction. The construction has two halves, and an implementation that weakens either is non-conforming. (a) The ledger's release gate (MEM-05 clause 3, MEM-06 clause 2) makes the state "the offender has withdrawn stake exposed to a still-open epoch" unreachable: withdraw() reverts until every epoch the entry was in has a closed window and no unsettled exposure. (b) Because every epoch in which the entry appears has evidenceClose(e) ≤ the withdrawal time, any evidence still inside its window is necessarily submitted before the withdrawal; after the withdrawal the window is closed, so a later submission reverts EvidenceWindowClosed and cannot debit a released balance. There is therefore no ordering of (exit, evidence) in which in-window evidence is unenforceable, and none in which late evidence touches released funds. Implementers MUST NOT add a "late evidence" path, and the constants must satisfy ECON-07 clause 2 or this construction fails. (6) Disclosure. A reverted or duplicate submission produces no on-chain accusation record and MUST NOT be displayed as a finding: an interface may show an applied offence (with its transaction) and must not present an unproven or failed accusation as fact (WH-02). Failure modes: a bond forfeited on "not proven" suppresses honest reporting, which kills the detection half of A-ECO-1; a challenge-response deadline lets an attacker slash an offline validator; a non-permanent replay marker lets one evidence object be applied twice or on a forked chain; a "late evidence" path would break the withdrawal gate. 9. Minimum viable stake

ECON-09 — minimum viable stake is set by operator economics, not attack cost (1) Derivation, in ETH-revenue terms. The minimum bonded amount for one validator S_min must satisfy the operator break-even relation 
S_min  ≥  C_op_annual / ( r_gross − ρ_ops )        equivalently  r_be = C_op_annual / S + ρ_ops
where C_op_annual is hardware, bandwidth, operations and L1 gas for one validator per year, expressed in ETH; r_gross is the annualised ETH reward rate actually paid per unit of bonded TAIKO out of the budget of ECON-12, in ETH per TAIKO per year; and ρ_ops is the operator's required margin in the same unit. The relation is dimensionally closed (ETH/year ÷ ETH per TAIKO per year = TAIKO) and contains no price term, so no TAIKO price is assumed silently by the algebra; the price enters through the operators' required margin, which is exactly why ρ_ops is unmeasured and time-varying rather than a constant. If the pool cannot pay r_gross > ρ_ops, the denominator is zero or negative and there is no finite S_min. Entry is then closed in practice: the admission predicate amount ≥ S_min (MEM-03 clause 1) either admits no operator or is unimplementable, so R1's permissionless entry is unsatisfiable in that regime — the consequence must be recorded as an open conflict between R1 and the funding identity, never smoothed by lowering the minimum until operators appear or by assuming a price recovery. The honest statement is that the design is unfunded. (review round 1, findings ECO-02, ECO-03) (2) The binding constraint, stated. Attack cost bounds the aggregate stake and never the individual validator: 
S_total  ≥  Λ · ( EV_extractable + OV_option ) / ( b · P(t) · (1 + σ(t)) )
This inequality contains no individual S; no rule may derive S_min from it (economics-raw.md §3.1 constraint A). The parameter Λ ≥ 1 is unmeasured, and the claim is conditional on A-ECO-1. (3) Participation. S_min ≤ S_affordable(median intended participant), or entry is permissionless only in name (R1). The resulting count n = S_total / S_min is not bounded by any consensus rule: CONS-06 and CONS-10 are well defined for every n ≥ 1 and impose no maximum (the invalid-set conditions are n = 0, a zero effective stake, and a malformed or duplicate key, not a large n), so there is no normative set-size window; what bounds n in practice is the reward budget Budget_ETH(n) of ECON-12 and the measured consensus performance at the target set size, which is unmeasured (04 §7, falsifier F3). 10. Concentration, borrowing and liquid staking

ECON-10 — caps constrain labels, borrowing changes cost, liquid staking is unpriced (1) A per-address or per-validator cap constrains labels, not capital. Let C be the cap per address, S_total the total bonded stake, k = S_total / C the number of identities needed to hold it, and k_op the marginal annual cost of operating one additional identity (key generation, custody, monitoring, per-identity overhead). An adversary seeking fraction b of stake acquires b · S_total TAIKO and splits it across b · k identities: the dominant capital term is unchanged, and the cap adds at most b · k · k_op. The ratio of cap overhead to acquisition cost is k_op / C, which shrinks as the cap rises and never cancels the dominant term. Therefore no rule, interface or document may claim that a cap "prevents monopolisation"; a design that keeps a cap MUST state this arithmetic, with k_op unmeasured. A cap on validator slots is worse than a cap on addresses, because it also caps the honest set size and turns slot acquisition itself into the scarce resource (economics-raw.md §2.7; MEM-10's non-claim is not weakened here). (2) Borrowing. The attacker must control b · S_total, not own it. Borrowed or leveraged stake changes the cost floor to the financing cost over the attack window plus the loss of the pledged collateral; the protocol cannot observe leverage, so no rule may assume a maximum leverage ratio, and no claim may be phrased as "the attacker must own the tokens". Leverage ratios are unmeasured (economics-raw.md §2.6, citing arXiv:2401.08610). (3) Liquid-staking derivatives are an unpriced attack surface. A low-stake adversary can degrade a target pool's consensus performance and monetise at the application layer through leveraged shorts — "profitable with over one-half probability for LSTs of major staking pools" — because the profit is booked against an LST repricing rather than against the consensus layer (arXiv:2605.01025, *Your Loss is My Gain: Low Stake Attacks on Liquid Staking Pools*, 2026-05-01, retrieved 2026-10-05; economics-raw.md §2.6, §2.8). This is the OV_option term, and standard cost-of-attack accounting — which assumes the attacker must be a large stakeholder — does not bound it. v1 recognises no delegated, pooled or liquid-staked TAIKO as consensus stake and counts weight per (pubkey, effStake) entry without recording beneficial ownership (MEM-02, MEM-04). That removes the delegation surface; it does not remove the derivative market, because a derivative issuer can still bond TAIKO through its own entry. Hence (a) no claim on this page may be read as bounding attack profitability while a TAIKO liquid-staking or derivative market exists, and (b) every published concentration statistic MUST say that it is a statistic over ledger entries, not over beneficial owners. (4) What actually constrains concentration. The cost of acquiring the float (a liquidity and slippage question, σ(t), unmeasured), reward schedules that are concave in stake, and transparency (entry-level stake is public). None is a guarantee; transparency makes concentration observable, not impossible (economics-raw.md §2.7 point 5). (5) No hard-coded market datum. No price, market capitalisation, float, order-book depth, traded volume, slippage or yield figure may appear as a constant, immutable, constructor argument, interface string or normative value anywhere in this specification or the interfaces it defines. Economics-raw.md §3.5 records every one of these as UNVERIFIED in this pass; a constant would be a claim that the value is stable, which is false, and each one is an input to σ(t), S_total or AttackProfit — that is, each one can invalidate A-ECO-1. Statements of the form "an attack costs X" are prohibited for the same reason; the permitted statement is the derived price at which the design stops being secure, computed from measured inputs and dated. Failure modes: a cap presented as an anti-monopoly mechanism is a false security claim (threat model §6.4 non-argument); ignoring borrowing understates attack cost; ignoring liquid-staking derivatives leaves the OV_option term out of the accounting, so A-ECO-1 appears satisfied when it is not; a hard-coded price makes a market observation into a protocol rule that fails with no rule change. Proven sourced unmeasured

11. Scope of user losses

ECON-11 — what protocol penalties do and do not cover (1) What a penalty does. A penalty debits the bonded stake of an entry proven to have committed a listed offence, up to the charge base of MEM-06, and dispatches the amount per ECON-06. That is its entire effect. No penalty reverses a PoS-finalized block, returns a bridge message, re-orders history, or repairs a failed consensus assumption (D2, REC-01). (2) What penalties do not cover. Protocol penalties are not compensation and do not cover: value extracted by an attacker before detection (EV_extractable); losses caused by F2 failures — ≥1/3 Byzantine stake (A-CONS-1 failing), key compromise, a zkVM soundness break, or a malicious governance upgrade — where the design's safety claims do not apply and no slashing rule repairs the failure; losses caused by F1 failures — halt, congestion, prover outage, blob expiry, unavailable data — where the honest response is to halt safely, not to redistribute; losses inside third-party contracts, bridges, DEXes and lending markets; losses from user error; losses from L1 reorganisation; and the loss of the fiat value of TAIKO itself. (3) No universal bound. No rule, document, interface, learning page or marketing text may state or imply a universal bound on user economic loss. The threat model already forbids inventing one (D2 obligation 4), and economics-raw.md §4.1 L1 records why: loss depends on EV_extractable, which depends on the value bridged, DEX depth and detection latency — none of which the protocol controls. The status labels of STATUS-01–STATUS-11 remain the only progress vocabulary: a slash is not a confirmation, and a penalty is not insurance. (4) No compensation channel is defined here. If the recorded ECON-06 destination includes no compensation policy, users must expect none; a future compensation or insurance scheme is a new design with its own funding identity under ECON-02 and its own decision record, and may not be assumed by this specification (the "insurance" allocation discussed in arXiv:2401.05797 is an example of such a scheme, not a rule of this one). (5) The honest commitment. The protocol's commitment to users is disclosure, the objective exit path of MEM-05, and the ability to halt safely; it is not a promise of reimbursement. Failure mode: the most damaging economic failure is a user who reads "slashing" as "my loss will be covered" and sizes exposure on a guarantee the protocol never made. Proven Assumed (the exclusion of F2 losses restates the fault boundary: 01-requirements-and-threat-model.md §5.)

The capital component scales with S_total however it is split; the operating component scales with n. Splitting the same stake across more validators therefore raises the reward bill linearly in n while fee revenue does not scale with n. Decentralisation is bought with operating-cost funding, and any rule that lowers S_min to admit small participants forces n up and the budget up proportionally. (3) Runway, not yield. The honest deliverable is a runway calculation, not a yield: "A protocol can have a large nominal reserve and still be close to security failure after adverse price or demand shocks" (arXiv:2606.03587, *Reserve Depletion and Security Runway in Proof-of-Stake Systems*, 2026-06-02, retrieved 2026-10-05, quoted in economics-raw.md §2.9). A design is "reward-sustainable" only when the fee-only region is reached before the reserve depletes; until then the correct description is "reserve-dependent". (4) Price reflexivity. Costs and rewards are ETH-denominated while the locked capital is TAIKO-denominated, so a fall in TAIKO's ETH price raises the ETH reward per unit of locked stake needed to hold operator economics constant and raises ρ_capital (price-risk plus expected slashing-loss premia): the same net ETH inflow then funds fewer validators. This is the reflexive loop of economics-raw.md §4.3. It must be disclosed and stress-tested, not parameterised away; paying rewards in ETH removes the mixed-currency identity of ECON-02 clause 1 but does not remove the loop, which is a property of the capital asset.
