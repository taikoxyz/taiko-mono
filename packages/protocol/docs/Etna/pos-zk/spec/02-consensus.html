02 — Consensus: proposals, votes, locks, finality, epochs

This page states CONS-01–CONS-15 in full, and CONS-16 as a tombstone (deferred by D-16 — the MEM-13 heartbeat eligibility it was to consume is revived by increment 02 and stated in 03, but the rotation itself stays deferred because its closing height has no L1 referent); they are listed once and referenced elsewhere by identifier (GEN-03). This closes review round 8 finding R8-AC-04: the page contract no longer claims to state the deferred rotation in full. The selected protocol is Tendermint/CometBFT-class BFT with one block per height, single-slot finality, Ed25519 per-validator vote signatures, and validator sets that are derived from L1-committed TAIKO stake and quantized into epochs (04-architecture-decision §1–§2; survey: consensus-survey-raw.md §3). The published lock / proof-of-lock-change rule of the CometBFT specification, pinned at commit 709fd12b…, is retained unchanged and quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1.

Scope of the claims on this page. Safety statements are conditional on A-CONS-1 (Byzantine stake strictly below 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum, in every epoch) and on correct validators executing the rules below. Liveness statements are conditional on A-CONS-2 (partial synchrony) and A-CONS-3 (the advisory delay bound holds often enough). Where a statement depends on 01 §4.2 or on the availability assumptions A-CONS-5 / A-DA-2, it says so at the claim. Nothing on this page is measured, implemented or audited (GEN-01).

Objects on the wire, and the round

Every object below is encoded in exactly one canonical way; hashing, domain separation and the ban on abi.encodePacked are fixed by GEN-05. Signature algorithms are Ed25519 (RFC 8032; 32-byte public key, 64-byte signature sourced (survey §8.4, from the RFC)). A signature is made over the exact encoded bytes below, with no pre-hash. A signature outside its domain tag is invalid.

Rules

CONS-01 — proposal validity A proposal at height H and round R is P = (chain_id, epoch, height, round, proposer_index, block, pol_round, signature) with the fields of the encoding table. A correct validator accepts P as valid at (H,R) if and only if (i)–(viii) hold. (i) P.chain_id is this chain, P.height = H, P.round = R, and P.epoch = epoch_of(H) under the L1-committed epoch schedule (CONS-13). (ii) P.proposer_index = proposer(H,R) (CONS-06) and P.signature verifies under the consensus public key committed at that index in set_root(P.epoch). (iii) block.parent_hash is the hash of the block this validator has finalized at height H-1 (CONS-05); for H = H_0 the parent is the L1-committed genesis anchor (CONS-14). v1 has no resumed chain: the stall resolution of REC-02 and its restart linkage REC-04 are deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, so no rule discards unsettled history, no height is re-produced and no restored checkpoint exists; the ordinary H-1 rule stated above is the whole of this clause, with no substituted anchor, no resumeHeight case and no discarded block named as parent (HALT-02). A settlement stall halts the chain and v1 has no path that restarts it (INV-01, DEFERRED.md §3). This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the parent rule no longer reads an executed stall resolution as a live contingency. (iv) Local execution of block against the post-state of that parent succeeds and reproduces every header field it commits to (state root, receipts root, gas used, logs bloom, transaction root) — this is STATUS-02. (v) Forced-data inclusion — the per-block order and non-omission duty, scoped to the transaction's turn. For every block h of the range, at every pre-state of h: if a forced transaction t of a record j that is due at the batch's anchored view A, live at A, and not dead at A is at its turn at that pre-state and forceable there — t's turn is the pre-state FI-13(1)(a) pins by position: the pre-state immediately before t's own position in the executed payload when t appears, the pre-state immediately before the record's next transaction in the record's own order that appears in the executed payload when t does not appear, and the pre-state at the end of the batch's execution when no later transaction of the record appears — and t has not been executed in an earlier block of the range, then h's body MUST contain t before it contains any forced transaction of a record k > j, and MUST contain t if its remaining gas at that point is at least the gas limit t declares. The duty applies at the transaction's turn and only there, so it applies to exactly the transactions the proof-side walk of FI-13(1)(a) would execute and never to one the walk discharges: at the turn the walk reaches t with every earlier transaction of j already resolved, its verdict on t is executable exactly when t is forceable there under FI-13(2) — whose predicate carries the same byte-decidable requirements the void limb enumerates, the intrinsic-gas floor of (2)(ii) and signature recovery in (2)(iv), so a transaction this clause demands is one a valid block can carry — and discharged otherwise, and this clause fires only in the first case. A transaction the walk has discharged at its turn is not demanded here at any later pre-state, even when it has become forceable there, and no transaction of a record resolved (b) void is ever demanded, because the walk does not reach it. The per-block duty and the proof-side walk MUST share one predicate — a disagreement is a protocol defect — and a duty that demanded a transaction the walk has discharged would be unsatisfiable by construction, because a discharge is fixed by the record's own order and the batch's own execution, never by a block's contents. The "increasing transaction index" condition of FI-13(1)(a) governs only the transactions the walk executes: a discharged transaction does not appear at all, so it cannot violate that order and no block is ever required to include one. The clause has no per-block record count, no per-block gas quota and no per-block capacity constant: the number of records a batch must resolve is FI-12's cap, in positions per batch. A block that can carry forced work and carries none is invalid; a block that cannot is not. The obligation itself is enforced in the proof, never in the admission rules of land(data, proof) (FI-11(1), PRF-04(vi)), and no new slashable offence is created: a validator that signs a block violating this clause commits no new offence, the forced-inclusion offence rows of ECON-04 stay tombstoned and MUST NOT be implemented, and the rejected proof is the whole enforcement. (increment 04: the clause is revived as the order-and-non-omission duty, in one unit with FI-12; the per-block count that R6-D12-03 found unpayable is deleted, and no per-block gas floor is added.) (review round 3, R4R3-T-01/R4R3-NR-01: the duty is scoped to the transaction's turn, so the per-block check and the proof-side walk are one predicate; the seam that made a nonce-descending record unprovable — a duty demanding a transaction the walk had already discharged — is closed, and the "increasing transaction index" condition constrains only the transactions the walk executes.) (vi) Every remaining validity rule of this specification holds (block gas limit, block size, DA bounds; see 04 and 09). (vii) Current recovery generation. The block's header MUST carry the recovery generation the Inbox holds (CONS-10(1), (7)); a proposal whose header generation is not the current generation is invalid. The generation is a signed field and in v1 no live rule increments it — the stall resolution that would have is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented — so the current generation is the L1-held activation value and this clause compares a header against it (REC-02 tombstone; DEFERRED.md §3). This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the rule no longer asserts that a discarded-branch event happened; the check is stated against the generation v1 has. A proposal at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint is invalid: the only canonical history there is the accepted one, and no rule re-opens it. (viii) Fee-vault credit. Local execution of block must credit the L2 fee vault (ECON-02 clause 7(a)) with the execution base fees and priority fees that the block's own execution collects; a proposal whose block diverts any part of those fees to any other beneficiary is invalid. (S5 §1 and §8: superseded-generation heights may not be proposed, and fee diversion is a validity condition; D-15 replaces retirement by the signed generation.) A correct validator MUST NOT sign a prevote or a precommit for a proposal that fails any of (i)–(viii); its only legal alternative in that round is NIL (CONS-02). Slashing scope. Voting for an invalid proposal is an offence only where objective evidence exists: a deterministic checker whose inputs are (a) the signed object, (b) Ethereum-final L1 data, and (c) public block bodies must be able to reach the verdict. Objectively decidable under this rule: (ii) a wrong proposer or a bad signature; (iii) a parent that contradicts the finalized chain; and (vii) a proposal at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint, which is read from L1 state; the superseded-generation half of (vii) has no case in v1, because no rule advances the generation. Clause (v) is deliberately excluded from the offence set: a signer of a block that violates the forced-data order-and-non-omission duty is subject to no new slash, and the rejected proof is the whole enforcement (increment 04, owner decision 6 on the open items). This closes review round 8 finding R8-CC-04: the offence scope lists only the checkpoint case v1 can reach. Execution validity (iv) is decidable only by re-execution, which is public but expensive, and fee diversion (viii) is decidable the same way: it is listed in the offence catalogue ECON-04 (S5 §8). Whether execution validity (iv) is slashable is fixed by the same offence catalogue ECON-04, and the protocol MUST NOT claim deterrence for an offence that catalogue does not list. (increment 04: clause (v) is live as the per-block order-and-non-omission duty and is not a slashable offence — its rows in ECON-04 stay tombstoned; the objectively decidable offence set of this rule remains (ii), (iii) and (vii), and no deterrence may be claimed for an offence the catalogue does not list.) Mere non-receipt of a proposal is never misconduct (threat T-15). Failure mode: an implementer invents its own validity predicate — most likely by dropping one of (i)–(iv) and (vi)–(viii) or by adding a condition of its own — or a slashable offence is defined that is not objectively checkable, which turns slashing into a censorship or griefing tool. Proven (the predicate is a deterministic function of signed data, L1 state and public bodies)
CONS-02 — vote phases and vote uniqueness per (height, round) Phases. A height H proceeds through rounds R = 0, 1, 2, …; each round has a propose, a prevote and a precommit step, and then either a commit at H or a transition to R+1 (CONS-07). A vote is the Vote object of the encoding table, with type ∈ {PREVOTE, PRECOMMIT}; V.epoch MUST equal epoch_of(V.height), V.recovery_generation MUST equal the recovery generation the Inbox holds, and a vote whose epoch or generation field disagrees is invalid regardless of its signature (CONS-08, CONS-10(7)). (user decision D-15: the signed vote carries the recovery generation.) validator_index is the position of the signer in the canonical order of set_root(V.epoch) (CONS-10).
Prevote step at (H,R), evaluated in this order: (a) apply the unlock check of CONS-04; (b) if still locked on a block, sign PREVOTE for the locked value; (c) else if a proposal valid at (H,R) is known, sign PREVOTE for its block; (d) else sign PREVOTE for NIL.
Precommit step at (H,R): (a) if a PoLC for a block B at (H,R) is known, (re)lock on B at this height, setting (locked_height, locked_value, locked_round) := (H, B, R) and sign PRECOMMIT for B; (b) else if a PoLC for NIL at (H,R) is known, unlock and sign PRECOMMIT for NIL; (c) else keep the lock unchanged and sign PRECOMMIT for NIL.
Uniqueness (the signing rule). Once a validator signs a PRECOMMIT for a given (chain_id, height, round) under a generation g, it MUST NOT sign any other message of the same type for that same (chain_id, height, round, g) — this includes votes for NIL. The identical obligation applies to PREVOTE and to PROPOSAL. Mechanically: before signing a message m of type t at (H,R), the signer consults a persistent sign-state store that holds, per (chain_id, H, t, g), the set of (round, value) pairs already signed; if (R, value') is present with value' ≠ value(m), the signer MUST refuse to sign. The store MUST be durably written before the signature is broadcast and MUST survive process restart; if the store is missing, unreadable or corrupt, the validator MUST NOT sign, and MUST NOT "start fresh" (CONS-15) — re-signing after a restart is the amnesia attack.
The store under generation scoping (dormant in v1). Entries are scoped to the generation they were signed under. No generation is superseded in v1: the stall resolution that would increment it is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented (REC-02 tombstone; DEFERRED.md §3), so the generation stays at its activation value, every entry remains under the current generation, and this scoping is a keying discipline rather than a recovery contingency. This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the store paragraph states the generation keying and that nothing supersedes a generation in v1. The store MUST NOT be cleared, truncated, rewritten or restarted from empty by any rule — a restart, a resync or a revived stall resolution included — and no v1 rule clears it: the stall resolution is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, and were it revived it would not be an exception, because a resolution's scoping change only disregards superseded-generation entries for new signing and never erases the record of what was signed. This closes review round 9 finding R9-CC-02: the prohibition names every rule and the withdrawn resolution explicitly, so the dormancy note cannot be read as licence for a revival to clear the store. A validator that discards its record of what it signed is in breach (HALT-02(a), CONS-15(4)). The uniqueness rule is scoped to (chain_id, height, round, generation), and a pair is a conflict only under the same generation (CONS-11); because the generation is constant, that scoping is exactly the ordinary uniqueness obligation it always was — it grants no new signature right, no discarded height is ever re-signed under a new generation, and locks remain CONS-04's while the halt triggers remain CONS-15's, unchanged. (historical, not a live rule: user decision D-15 scoped the signing store and the uniqueness rule to the signed generation, so a resumed chain would be producible without deleting the record of what was signed, and the fresh-round requirement of the retired-height design was withdrawn with it; D-16 defers the resumed chain, so no v1 rule reads this.) This closes review round 8 finding R8-CC-04: the store paragraph no longer describes a stall resolution clearing the store or a discarded height being re-signed. Failure mode: a node restarts without a durable sign-state store, signs a conflicting vote it did not intend, and becomes slashable while also making two conflicting certificates possible without any party holding 1/3 of the stake. The uniqueness rule is also what makes equivocation evidence a two-object proof (CONS-11). Proven (signing obligation: CometBFT signing spec, quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1)
CONS-03 — quorum: more than 2/3 of epoch voting power, distinct signers, stake-weighted Let epoch e have the committed set v_0 … v_{n-1} in canonical index order with integer voting powers w_i ≥ 1 and W = Σ_i w_i (CONS-10; MEM-08 owns the L1-side construction). Voting power is taken only from that commitment — never from a vote, a certificate, a witness or a prover-supplied list (PRF-03), and the same integers w_i are used for quorum and for proposer selection (CONS-06); a design with two weight vectors has two different security thresholds and is a defect. For a set Q of distinct validator indices and a value x at (H,R) with e = epoch_of(H):
s = power(Q) = Σ_{i∈Q} w_i, the sum over the distinct authenticated signers in Q only
quorum(Q) ⇔ checked_mul(3, s) > checked_mul(2, W)
quorum_block(Q, B) ⇔ quorum(Q) and every i ∈ Q signed PRECOMMIT for BLOCK(B) at (H,R)
polc(Q, x) ⇔ quorum(Q) and every i ∈ Q signed PREVOTE for x at (H,R), where x ∈ BLOCK ∪ {NIL}.
Exactly one predicate. The rule above is the whole quorum predicate, and it is the same predicate everywhere: the L2 client, the zkVM guest and the L1 Inbox MUST each evaluate exactly 3 * s > 2 * W over the same authenticated s and W, with checked arithmetic. checked_mul MUST abort on overflow, or the comparison MUST be evaluated in a type wide enough that 3·W cannot wrap. Division, floating point, a stored or pre-computed threshold, a "threshold + 1" form and a "strictly greater than the threshold" form are all forbidden: the comparison is exactly 3·s > 2·W, so a set with 3·s = 2·W does not reach quorum. A strict/non-strict mismatch, or a threshold compared instead of recomputed, is a protocol defect because it splits chain acceptance from L1 acceptance: a certificate the L2 accepts can be rejected by the guest or by the L1 contract, or the reverse, so a legitimately finalized block becomes unsettleable or a non-quorum certificate lands (survey §3.2 records that the published CometBFT text does not expand its "+2/3" into a comparison). This closes review round 1 finding CS-09: the client already accepted 3s > 2W while the L1 threshold row and the guest predicate required s strictly greater than floor(2W/3)+1; L1-05 row 12 and PRF-04(iii) must be amended to this single predicate.
Distinct signers. A certificate carries (i) the signers bitmap of the encoding table and (ii) a list of exactly popcount(signers) signatures. Verification MUST require: popcount(signers) = len(signatures); bit i set exactly when one signature by v_i's committed Ed25519 key is present and verifies over the canonical vote bytes for (H,R); no index counted twice; and s summed over set bits only. A duplicate signature, a signature whose bit is clear, a set bit with no signature, or a non-zero pad bit makes the certificate invalid — never "partially counted". NIL votes never contribute to quorum_block. Failure mode: a threshold computed by division accepts exactly 2/3 and lets a coalition of exactly one third block or forge finality; a signer counted twice inflates power; weight read from the certificate or the witness lets a prover mint its own quorum. Proven (quorum intersection: two sets each > 2W/3 share more than W/3; see CONS-12)
CONS-04 — lock rule and unlocking by proof-of-lock-change The rule is the published CometBFT rule, quoted verbatim from the specification at pinned commit 709fd12b… (recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1, source S1) and retained unchanged:
• "A set of +2/3 of prevotes for a particular block or <nil> at (H,R) is called a proof-of-lock-change or PoLC for short."
• Unlock rule (Prevote step): "First, if the validator is locked on a block since LastLockRound but now has a PoLC for something else at round PoLC-Round where LastLockRound < PoLC-Round < R, then it unlocks. If the validator is still locked on a block, it prevotes that. Else, if the proposed block from Propose(H,R) is good, it prevotes that. Else, if the proposal is invalid or wasn't received on time, it prevotes <nil>."
• Lock rule (Precommit step): "If the validator has a PoLC at (H,R) for a particular block B, it (re)locks (or changes lock to) and precommits B and sets LastLockRound = R." / "Else, if the validator has a PoLC at (H,R) for nil, it unlocks and precommits nil." / "Else, it keeps the lock unchanged and precommits nil."
• Proposal hint: "A proposal at (H,R) is composed of a block and an optional latest PoLC-Round < R which is included iff the proposer knows of one. This hints the network to allow nodes to unlock (when safe) to ensure the liveness property."
Normatively: (1) a validator's lock is a persistent local triple (locked_height, locked_value, locked_round) with locked_value ∈ BLOCK ∪ {NIL} and locked_round a non-negative integer or NONE, and it is per height and per generation: the lock at height H under generation g constrains votes at H under g only, survives restart while H is unresolved, and is released by (a) the commit at H of the locked value, within g (CONS-05) — once the locked value is finalized the lock has done its work and no later height may be constrained by it. A commit at H of a different value does not release the lock: the validator that also holds a conflicting certificate halts under CONS-15(2), except in the generation-scoped case of (2), which is dormant in v1. A commit certificate produced under a superseded generation would have no canonical effect at that height, but no v1 rule supersedes a generation: the stall resolution that would increment it is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented, so this release never occurs. This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the lock release no longer treats a generation change as a live event. Release is also by (b) the unlock of (2). (S5 §3: (1)(a) is qualified — the commit of the locked value within the current generation.) The two readings of a lock MUST NOT coexist. The normative reading is this per-height lock, whose cross-height effect is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii): a validator's vote at a later height must build on the block it finalized at the previous one. The competing reading — a lock that persists across heights and is never cleared at a height boundary — is not normative and MUST NOT appear in any client, contract or later text: with unlocking possible only at (H, p), a lock formed at H could never be released once the height had ended, every honest lock would be permanent, and a third of the set in that state would halt the chain after the first locked height. An implementer that found both readings would have to invent which one is normative; this clause removes the choice. (2) At (H,R), unlock happens only against a PoLC at (H, p) for a value different from locked_value with locked_round < p < R — strictly later, same height, different value. Generation scoping — the retired-height carve-out is withdrawn. A lock is scoped to the generation under which it was formed. A stall resolution would increment the generation (REC-02), but that rule is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented: v1 has no recovery path of any kind, nothing increments the generation, and no lock is ever formed under a superseded generation. Were one superseded, a lock formed under it would be void at every discarded height: it would neither constrain a vote under the new generation nor be carried anywhere, no PoLC would be required, and it would not be a halt condition. This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the generation-change release is stated as dormant, not as a live contingency. The former retired-height carve-out is withdrawn as a live mechanism: it addressed locks at discarded heights, and in v1 no height is discarded and no lock can be formed under a superseded generation, because the generation change that would create either case is deferred by D-16 and MUST NOT be implemented (DEFERRED.md §3). Locks are released only by (1)(a) and (2) as stated, and a revival of the discard must reinstate the carve-out together with the findings recorded for it; it is not kept here as a rule v1 can discharge. This closes review round 8 finding R8-CC-04: the withdrawn carve-out is justified by the deferral, not by a discard v1 cannot perform. A conflicting signed pair under the same generation remains admissible evidence under ECON-04, and that catalogue owns the evidentiary element; no element of the tombstoned REC-02 is needed to state it. This closes review round 8 finding R8-AC-02: the live evidentiary element is owned by ECON-04, not by the tombstoned REC-02. (historical, not a live rule: user decision D-15 made lock scoping follow the signed generation and withdrew the separate retired-height carve-out; D-16 defers the generation change, so no discard case arises in v1.) A PoLC at the same round, at an earlier round, or for the value already locked does not unlock; a PoLC at another height neither unlocks the lock at H nor creates a lock at that other height. A validator that holds a lock at H and obtains a valid commit certificate at H for a different block has evidence that the assumptions failed: it halts (CONS-15(2)) and MUST NOT treat the certificate as an unlock. (3) The pol_round field of a proposal is advisory only: a validator MUST verify the PoLC itself before unlocking and MUST NOT treat the field as evidence. (4) A validator MUST NOT precommit a block B at (H,R) without holding a PoLC for B at (H,R), and MUST NOT prevote anything other than its locked value while locked at that height and not unlocked by (2). This closes review round 2 finding R2A-10: the lock is per height, released by the commit at that height or by a strictly later same-height PoLC, and the cross-height carry-over is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii); the two readings must not coexist, because an implementer would otherwise have to invent which one is normative.
Why the lock makes a withheld certificate harmless to safety. Quoting the Proof of Safety (S1): "If a validator commits block B at round R, it's because it saw +2/3 of precommits at round R. This implies that 1/3+ of honest nodes are still locked at round R' > R. These locked validators will remain locked until they see a PoLC at R' > R, but this won't happen because 1/3+ are locked and honest, so at most -2/3 are available to vote for anything other than B." A certificate that exists but has not been published therefore can only delay a node that has not seen it; it cannot displace a decision already taken unless 1/3 or more of the epoch's power violates this rule or the signing rule, which is exactly the published fork-accountability lemma ("the union of the JSets for both commits … must include double-signing by at least 1/3+ of the validator set", S1); that lemma's conclusion is conditional on the JSets being compilable, and its evidentiary reach is narrowed in CONS-09(4)(a). This claim is conditional on A-CONS-1 and on correct validators executing (1)–(4). Failure mode: an implementation that unlocks on a same-round PoLC, on a later round at another height, or on the proposal's pol_round field alone, admits a conflicting commit with no slashable signer; a validator that loses its lock on restart can vote for a conflicting block. Proven Assumed (A-CONS-1; liveness is not claimed here — a permanently withheld PoLC stalls a locked validator by design, "cannot make progress" is the honest outcome, not a bug)
CONS-05 — commit rule: more than 2/3 precommits finalize the block A commit certificate at (H,R) for block id B is C = (chain_id, epoch, recovery_generation, H, R, B, set_root(epoch), signers, signatures) — the tuple of the encoding table, with the signed recovery_generation the validity predicate below reads. This closes review round 9 finding R9-CC-01: the tuple now carries the generation the same page's encoding table and CONS-08(1)/CONS-10(6)–(7) already give it, so the object and its predicate agree. It is valid iff epoch = epoch_of(H), set_root(epoch) equals the L1-committed set root for that epoch (MEM-08), recovery_generation equals the generation the Inbox holds at acceptance and the generation signed by every contributing vote (CONS-01(vii), CONS-10(7)), and quorum_block(Q, B) holds per CONS-03 with exactly popcount(signers) verifying signatures. CONS-06 — leader selection: deterministic stake-weighted selection Let epoch e have committed weights w_0 … w_{n-1} (w_i ≥ 1) in the canonical order of set_root(e) (MEM-08), W = Σ w_i, and cumulative sums cum_i = w_0 + … + w_i with cum_{-1} = 0. For a height H in epoch e and a round R, the proposer is selected uniformly in weight by a hash:
pos(H,R) = uint256(keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_PROPOSER, chainId, epoch, H, R))) mod W
proposer(H,R) = the unique i with cum_{i-1} ≤ pos(H,R) < cum_i.
DOMAIN_PROPOSER is the right-padded ASCII tag "TAIKO_ETNA_PROPOSER_V1" with chainId as the second field, encoded by the one canonical abi.encode of GEN-05 (typed u256, u64, u64, u32 after the tag); the tag is never used for another object. The intervals are half-open, so exactly one index matches whenever every w_i ≥ 1; there are no ties, and the half-open convention at an exact boundary is the whole tie-breaking rule: the lower index wins. pos is reduced modulo W, so it always lies in [0, W). A set with w_i = 0, n = 0 or W = 0 is invalid (CONS-10).
(a) Pure function of the committed inputs. The proposer of (H,R) is a pure function of (chainId, epoch_of(H), H, R) and the authenticated set set_root(epoch_of(H)) — never of a node's local round history, arrival order, proposal or witness. Every correct node computes the same proposer for the same (H,R) regardless of the path by which it arrived, so a round cannot be split between two proposers.
(b) Uniform in weight; the fairness claim is Assumed. Exactly w_i of the W residues in [0, W) lie in validator i's interval, so if pos is uniform on [0, W) then validator i is proposer with probability exactly w_i / W, the residue structure is uniform (no interval is privileged by its position, so the lowest-sorted validator has no systematic advantage), and a coalition C with Σ_{i∈C} w_i < W/3 proposes strictly fewer than one third of the slots in expectation. The counting argument is exact in the committed integers; the step that turns it into a probability statement — that pos is uniform — rests on keccak256 behaving as a random oracle, which A-CRYPTO-3 (collision resistance) does not supply, so the fairness claim is Assumed, not proven, and it is an expectation, not a bound on any finite window: unlike the superseded residue rotation, this rule does not claim that every W-height window contains exactly w_i proposer slots for validator i.
(c) Modulo bias is negligible and bounded. The reduction mod W makes the probability of each residue differ from the uniform 1 / W by at most 2^-256, so validator i's selection probability deviates from w_i / W by at most w_i / 2^256 ≤ W / 2^256: for any W ≪ 2^256 — the case for every TAIKO base-unit total, where W is far smaller than the 256-bit hash range — the bias is negligible. No second quantization of the weights is introduced to "help" the reduction: the w_i here are exactly the integers used for quorum (CONS-03).
(d) Publicly verifiable from the set root alone. Anyone — a validator, a light client, a full node, an L1 contract, a user — holding the L1-committed set_root(epoch_of(H)) and the tuple (chainId, epoch_of(H), H, R) can recompute pos, locate the interval and name the proposer, with no witness, certificate, proposal, participation proof or cooperation from any validator.
What this does not claim. The property is over proposer slots, not over produced blocks: a cartel that forces timeouts on honest slots can increase its share of produced blocks. That is a liveness and ordering surface (threat T-6), handled by the timeout ladder (CONS-07), which moves the proposer slot on when a round fails; it is not a safety surface. Censorship resistance is not a guarantee but the conditional statistical property of that rotation: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, so a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it — real but conditional resistance under A-CONS-2, since a network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can prevent that user's data from reaching L1 at all, and the protocol offers no remedy against that. For data that is published, the narrow obligation of FI-10–FI-14 adds the proof-enforced resolution duty (increment 04), so this rotation is the conditional resistance for unpublished data and for the disclosed L1-censorship case, while published data also carries the narrow obligation — itself conditional on a non-censoring L1, on at least one honest or rational producer (F-FI-5) and on arrivals within the drain (F-FI-2). (increment 04: the rotation is the conditional resistance for unpublished data and for the L1-censorship case; published data also carries the narrow obligation of FI-10–FI-14, which is itself conditional, and the censorship gap of LIVE-04 stands as disclosed for the unpublished case.) The full statement, including the disclosed partial relaxation of R10, is LIVE-04. Selection is a public deterministic function, so the next proposer is predictable far in advance; the disclosure obligation for that predictability, and any mitigation, belong to LIM-01.
Proposer privilege. A proposer may broadcast at most one proposal valid at (H,R) (two distinct signed proposals at one round are equivocation, CONS-11). It has no other power: it cannot finalize anything, cannot choose the validator set, and every validator checks CONS-01 itself.
Unit. w_i are the same integers used for quorum, and PARAM-01 must fix their unit with MEM-02. The stake-to-unit mapping, its quantization error and its tag are MEM-02's and are unmeasured here. No modest value of W is required: the rule is well defined for every W ≥ 1, and the bound of (c) is what makes a large W safe. Failure mode: a proposer rule that depends on local state splits a round between two proposers; a rule with undefined tie-breaking lets two implementations disagree at a boundary; a rule that weights by anything other than the committed set (e.g. by hash without weights, or by arrival order) lets a sub-threshold coalition capture more than its share of slots; a residue rotation whose index is in weight units makes the first-sorted validator the proposer of effectively every height. Proven Assumed (determinism, well-definedness and the interval counting are proven in the committed integers; the uniform-weight fairness of (b) is Assumed on the hash's uniformity and is an expectation, not a per-window bound — see the modifications table, M6. CONS-07 — round timeouts, view change and the adaptive ladder (1) Every timeout is a duration in seconds. No timeout is expressed in L1 slots, no rule depends on Ethereum proposer lookahead or on a fixed L1 slot duration, and no validity rule reads a wall clock (GEN-06, R12); wall clocks pace local signing and timeout actions only. (2) Parameters, fixed by PARAM-01: TIMEOUT_MIN (base round budget) and TIMEOUT_MAX (ceiling) — the registered spellings of 09, which this rule reads. Proposed values: TIMEOUT_MIN = 3 s derived from round_trip = 4·Δ_max + X_max with TIMEOUT_MIN ≥ 2·round_trip, and TIMEOUT_MAX = 30 s derived as a bound small relative to the 30-minute proving envelope of D6 sourced (D6) so that a stall is detected long before settlement deadlines bind. Δ_max (advisory one-way delay between correct validators) and X_max (local execution time for one block) are UNMEASURED; the measurement that would fix them is telemetry of the p99 one-way message delay and of block execution time at the target validator count, topology and block size. Both TIMEOUT_MIN and TIMEOUT_MAX are therefore unmeasured in practice. (3) The ladder. Let R_progress be the highest round ≤ R at which this node received a PoLC or a commit certificate for height H (0 if none), and let R_cap = ceil(log2(TIMEOUT_MAX / TIMEOUT_MIN)). Then
T(H,R) = min(TIMEOUT_MAX, TIMEOUT_MIN · 2^min(R - R_progress, R_cap)) seconds,
evaluated in integer arithmetic only (with the proposed values the ladder is 3, 6, 12, 24, 30, 30, … s). The exponential term is the response to round failures; the R_progress reset is the adaptation to observed round completion: a round that produced a PoLC or a commit resets the backoff to base, so a transient stall does not permanently inflate the timeouts. Timeouts are local and have no normative effect on any other node. (4) Advancement. A correct validator at (H,R) MUST advance to (H,R+1) when either (a) T(H,R) expires, or (b) it has received a PoLC at (H,R) and has completed its own precommit step for that round — after a PoLC no further signature of that type can change the outcome of the round. On advancing it MUST update R_progress if it saw a PoLC or a commit certificate. It MUST NOT advance past a commit: if it holds a valid commit certificate for H it commits and enters H+1. A locked validator advances rounds normally; its votes remain constrained by CONS-04. (5) A timeout changes only local round state. It is never evidence that a certificate, proposal or block does not exist (STATUS-10), never invalidates anything, and never licenses an existential claim about what other nodes hold. (6) Independence from safety. The timeout values have no effect on CONS-12: safety holds under asynchrony, and A-CONS-2 requires partial synchrony for liveness only. If the assumed delay bound Δ_max is violated, the consequences are view-change churn, slower confirmation and eventually a stall — liveness only, never safety (A-CONS-3). A stall is a legitimate outcome (CONS-15); the protocol MUST NOT respond to it by weakening a safety rule. Backpressure beyond the unsettled-depth cap is HALT-03 and is not owned here. Failure mode: timeouts expressed in L1 slots break when Ethereum changes slot timing (R12); a fixed timeout with no backoff turns a partition into a permanent stall of the round machinery even after the network heals; a timeout read as evidence of absence is the exact non-argument 01 §6.4 forbids. Proven Assumed (liveness part: A-CONS-2 / A-CONS-3; the safety part is unconditional on timing)

CONS-11 — equivocation: definition, evidence and who may submit Definition. Validator v equivocates at (H,R) if it produces two signed messages m1 ≠ m2 with type(m1) = type(m2) ∈ {PROPOSAL, PREVOTE, PRECOMMIT}; identical chain_id, height = H, round = R and validator_index = v; and the same signed recovery_generation (a pair whose generations differ is not a conflict: an object under a superseded generation would belong to a discarded history, and in v1 no generation is superseded because the resolution of REC-02 is deferred by D-16); and either different signed values — for votes block_id(m1) ≠ block_id(m2), where NIL differs from every BLOCK(x) by construction of the tagged union, and for proposals block_hash(m1) ≠ block_hash(m2) — or different signed epoch fields. A pair of that second form is a conflict under the epoch-agreement check: at one (H,R) at most one epoch value can equal epoch_of(H) (CONS-08(1)), so a pair whose signed epochs differ is a conflict even when the block values coincide, and an epoch-mismatched pair MUST NOT be treated as a non-conflict. The generation check runs the other way: a pair whose signed generations differ is a re-signing across a generation change and MUST NOT be treated as a conflict; no generation change occurs in v1 (the resolution is deferred by D-16), so every admissible pair is within one generation and the existing rules apply unchanged (CONS-02). (user decisions D-15 and D-16: the conflict predicate is generation-scoped; the action that would advance the generation is deferred.) This closes review round 7 finding R7-RB-06: the conflict predicate is stated against the one generation v1 has. Signing a prevote for X and a precommit for Y ≠ X at the same (H,R) is not equivocation: it is the normal step sequence. Signing at different rounds or different heights is not equivocation.
Evidence. The evidence object is the pair (m1, m2) with both signatures, plus e = epoch_of(H). It is self-authenticating: a checker verifies each signature against the public key committed for v at its index in the set that the object's own signed epoch field names (MEM-07(4)), with each named epoch's root reconstructible from L1 (CONS-08(3)); checks that the objects are distinct and agree on (chain_id, H, R, type, validator_index); applies the epoch-agreement check — if the two signed epoch fields are equal, both MUST equal epoch_of(H), and if they differ the pair is admitted as evidence and the object whose epoch field is not epoch_of(H) is itself an invalid vote under CONS-08(1) — and concludes the offence. No further witness, no P2P receipt, no participation proof and no other validator's cooperation is needed. Conversely, failure to receive a message is never evidence (threat T-15; STATUS-09).
Who may submit. Any party — a validator, a full node, a user, a third party, or a contract — may assemble and submit evidence. The process MUST NOT require the submitter to be a validator, to have staked, to reveal an identity, or to have participated in the round. Reward, collateral, duplicate handling and false-accusation cost are ECON-04/ECON-08.
Lifetime. Evidence remains checkable for as long as set_root(e) is reconstructible from L1 (CONS-08). Whether a slash can still be executed is a function of the unbonding and evidence windows (ECON-07): evidence age must be less than the unbonding period so the offending stake is still available ("for Proof of Stake chains where validators are bonded, evidence age should be less than the unbonding period so validators still can be punished", CometBFT evidence spec, quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1).

Failure mode: an under-specified definition — for example treating a prevote/precommit pair as a conflict, or requiring the two messages to be of different types — either makes honest validators slashable or makes real equivocation unprovable; requiring a validator as submitter leaves the offence unpunished by default.
