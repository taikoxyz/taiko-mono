02 — Consensus: proposals, votes, locks, finality, epochs

This page states CONS-01–CONS-15 in full; they are listed once and referenced elsewhere by identifier (GEN-03). The selected protocol is Tendermint/CometBFT-class BFT with one block per height, single-slot finality, Ed25519 per-validator vote signatures, and validator sets that are derived from L1-committed TAIKO stake and quantized into epochs (04-architecture-decision §1–§2; survey: consensus-survey-raw.md §3). The published lock / proof-of-lock-change rule of the CometBFT specification, pinned at commit 709fd12b…, is retained unchanged and quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1.

Scope of the claims on this page. Safety statements are conditional on A-CONS-1 (Byzantine stake strictly below 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum, in every epoch) and on correct validators executing the rules below. Liveness statements are conditional on A-CONS-2 (partial synchrony) and A-CONS-3 (the advisory delay bound holds often enough). Where a statement depends on 01 §4.2 or on the availability assumptions A-CONS-5 / A-DA-2, it says so at the claim. Nothing on this page is measured, implemented or audited (GEN-01).

Rules

CONS-01 — proposal validity A proposal at height H and round R is P = (chain_id, epoch, height, round, proposer_index, block, pol_round, signature) with the fields of the encoding table. A correct validator accepts P as valid at (H,R) if and only if all of the following hold. (i) P.chain_id is this chain, P.height = H, P.round = R, and P.epoch = epoch_of(H) under the L1-committed epoch schedule (CONS-13). (ii) P.proposer_index = proposer(H,R) (CONS-06) and P.signature verifies under the consensus public key committed at that index in set_root(P.epoch). (iii) block.parent_hash is the hash of the block this validator has finalized at height H-1 (CONS-05); for H = H_0 the parent is the L1-committed genesis anchor (CONS-14). (iv) Local execution of block against the post-state of that parent succeeds and reproduces every header field it commits to (state root, receipts root, gas used, logs bloom, transaction root) — this is STATUS-02. (user decision D-6: the forced-inclusion duty is removed from this rule and from the specification; proposal validity imposes no inclusion condition and has no replacement clause.) What replaces the removed clause: nothing on L1 — the following is a disclosure, not a validity condition. The protocol provides no per-transaction inclusion guarantee and no L1 forced-inclusion path. Censorship resistance is the conditional statistical property described in LIVE-04: proposer selection is hash-based and weighted (CONS-06), so a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, and a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it. Under A-CONS-2 that is a real but conditional resistance. A network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can exclude that user's transactions indefinitely, and because a withdrawal is initiated by an L2 transaction, such an adversary can also prevent the user from starting a withdrawal. The protocol offers no remedy, and a partial relaxation of R10 is disclosed. (vi) Every remaining validity rule of this specification holds (block gas limit, block size, DA bounds; see 04 and 09). (vii) No retired height. A proposal at a height below resumeHeight — the first height that may be certified or landed after a completed recovery (REC-02, L1-06) — is invalid: discarded heights are permanently retired and no generation re-opens them. (viii) Fee-vault credit. Local execution of block must credit the L2 fee vault (ECON-02 clause 7(a)) with the execution base fees and priority fees that the block's own execution collects; a proposal whose block diverts any part of those fees to any other beneficiary is invalid. (S5 §1 and §8: retired heights may not be proposed, and fee diversion is a validity condition.) A correct validator MUST NOT sign a prevote or a precommit for a proposal that fails (i)–(iv), (vi), (vii) or (viii); its only legal alternative in that round is NIL (CONS-02). Slashing scope. Voting for an invalid proposal is an offence only where objective evidence exists: a deterministic checker whose inputs are (a) the signed object, (b) Ethereum-final L1 data, and (c) public block bodies must be able to reach the verdict. Objectively decidable under this rule: (ii) a wrong proposer or a bad signature; (iii) a parent that contradicts the finalized chain; (vii) a proposal at a retired height, which is read from L1 state. Execution validity (iv) is decidable only by re-execution, which is public but expensive, and fee diversion (viii) is decidable the same way: it is listed in the offence catalogue ECON-04 (S5 §8). Whether execution validity (iv) is slashable is fixed by the same offence catalogue ECON-04, and the protocol MUST NOT claim deterrence for an offence that catalogue does not list. Mere non-receipt of a proposal is never misconduct (threat T-15). Failure mode: an implementer invents its own validity predicate — most likely by dropping one of (i)–(iv) or (vi), or by adding a condition of its own — or a slashable offence is defined that is not objectively checkable, which turns slashing into a censorship or griefing tool. Proven (the predicate is a deterministic function of signed data, L1 state and public bodies)
CONS-02 — vote phases and vote uniqueness per (height, round) Phases. A height H proceeds through rounds R = 0, 1, 2, …; each round has a propose, a prevote and a precommit step, and then either a commit at H or a transition to R+1 (CONS-07). A vote is the Vote object of the encoding table, with type ∈ {PREVOTE, PRECOMMIT}; V.epoch MUST equal epoch_of(V.height), and a vote whose epoch field disagrees is invalid regardless of its signature (CONS-08). validator_index is the position of the signer in the canonical order of set_root(V.epoch) (CONS-10).
Prevote step at (H,R), evaluated in this order: (a) apply the unlock check of CONS-04; (b) if still locked on a block, sign PREVOTE for the locked value; (c) else if a proposal valid at (H,R) is known, sign PREVOTE for its block; (d) else sign PREVOTE for NIL.
Precommit step at (H,R): (a) if a PoLC for a block B at (H,R) is known, (re)lock on B at this height, setting (locked_height, locked_value, locked_round) := (H, B, R) and sign PRECOMMIT for B; (b) else if a PoLC for NIL at (H,R) is known, unlock and sign PRECOMMIT for NIL; (c) else keep the lock unchanged and sign PRECOMMIT for NIL.
Uniqueness (the signing rule). Once a validator signs a PRECOMMIT for a given (chain_id, height, round), it MUST NOT sign any other message of the same type for that same (chain_id, height, round) — this includes votes for NIL. The identical obligation applies to PREVOTE and to PROPOSAL. Mechanically: before signing a message m of type t at (H,R), the signer consults a persistent sign-state store that holds, per (chain_id, H, t), the set of (round, value) pairs already signed; if (R, value') is present with value' ≠ value(m), the signer MUST refuse to sign. The store MUST be durably written before the signature is broadcast and MUST survive process restart; if the store is missing, unreadable or corrupt, the validator MUST NOT sign, and MUST NOT "start fresh" (CONS-15) — re-signing after a restart is the amnesia attack. Failure mode: a node restarts without a durable sign-state store, signs a conflicting vote it did not intend, and becomes slashable while also making two conflicting certificates possible without any party holding 1/3 of the stake. The uniqueness rule is also what makes equivocation evidence a two-object proof (CONS-11). Proven (signing obligation: CometBFT signing spec, quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1)
CONS-03 — quorum: more than 2/3 of epoch voting power, distinct signers, stake-weighted Let epoch e have the committed set v_0 … v_{n-1} in canonical index order with integer voting powers w_i ≥ 1 and W = Σ_i w_i (CONS-10; MEM-08 owns the L1-side construction). Voting power is taken only from that commitment — never from a vote, a certificate, a witness or a prover-supplied list (PRF-03), and the same integers w_i are used for quorum and for proposer selection (CONS-06); a design with two weight vectors has two different security thresholds and is a defect. For a set Q of distinct validator indices and a value x at (H,R) with e = epoch_of(H):
s = power(Q) = Σ_{i∈Q} w_i, the sum over the distinct authenticated signers in Q only
quorum(Q) ⇔ checked_mul(3, s) > checked_mul(2, W)
quorum_block(Q, B) ⇔ quorum(Q) and every i ∈ Q signed PRECOMMIT for BLOCK(B) at (H,R)
polc(Q, x) ⇔ quorum(Q) and every i ∈ Q signed PREVOTE for x at (H,R), where x ∈ BLOCK ∪ {NIL}.
Exactly one predicate. The rule above is the whole quorum predicate, and it is the same predicate everywhere: the L2 client, the zkVM guest and the L1 Inbox MUST each evaluate exactly 3 * s > 2 * W over the same authenticated s and W, with checked arithmetic. checked_mul MUST abort on overflow, or the comparison MUST be evaluated in a type wide enough that 3·W cannot wrap. Division, floating point, a stored or pre-computed threshold, a "threshold + 1" form and a "strictly greater than the threshold" form are all forbidden: the comparison is exactly 3·s > 2·W, so a set with 3·s = 2·W does not reach quorum. A strict/non-strict mismatch, or a threshold compared instead of recomputed, is a protocol defect because it splits chain acceptance from L1 acceptance: a certificate the L2 accepts can be rejected by the guest or by the L1 contract, or the reverse, so a legitimately finalized block becomes unsettleable or a non-quorum certificate lands (survey §3.2 records that the published CometBFT text does not expand its "+2/3" into a comparison). This closes review round 1 finding CS-09: the client already accepted 3s > 2W while the L1 threshold row and the guest predicate required s strictly greater than floor(2W/3)+1; L1-05 row 12 and PRF-04(iii) must be amended to this single predicate.
Distinct signers. A certificate carries (i) the signers bitmap of the encoding table and (ii) a list of exactly popcount(signers) signatures. Verification MUST require: popcount(signers) = len(signatures); bit i set exactly when one signature by v_i's committed Ed25519 key is present and verifies over the canonical vote bytes for (H,R); no index counted twice; and s summed over set bits only. A duplicate signature, a signature whose bit is clear, a set bit with no signature, or a non-zero pad bit makes the certificate invalid — never "partially counted". NIL votes never contribute to quorum_block. Failure mode: a threshold computed by division accepts exactly 2/3 and lets a coalition of exactly one third block or forge finality; a signer counted twice inflates power; weight read from the certificate or the witness lets a prover mint its own quorum. Proven (quorum intersection: two sets each > 2W/3 share more than W/3; see CONS-12)
CONS-04 — lock rule and unlocking by proof-of-lock-change The rule is the published CometBFT rule, quoted verbatim from the specification at pinned commit 709fd12b… (recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1, source S1) and retained unchanged:
• "A set of +2/3 of prevotes for a particular block or <nil> at (H,R) is called a proof-of-lock-change or PoLC for short."
• Unlock rule (Prevote step): "First, if the validator is locked on a block since LastLockRound but now has a PoLC for something else at round PoLC-Round where LastLockRound < PoLC-Round < R, then it unlocks. If the validator is still locked on a block, it prevotes that. Else, if the proposed block from Propose(H,R) is good, it prevotes that. Else, if the proposal is invalid or wasn't received on time, it prevotes <nil>."
• Lock rule (Precommit step): "If the validator has a PoLC at (H,R) for a particular block B, it (re)locks (or changes lock to) and precommits B and sets LastLockRound = R." / "Else, if the validator has a PoLC at (H,R) for nil, it unlocks and precommits nil." / "Else, it keeps the lock unchanged and precommits nil."
• Proposal hint: "A proposal at (H,R) is composed of a block and an optional latest PoLC-Round < R which is included iff the proposer knows of one. This hints the network to allow nodes to unlock (when safe) to ensure the liveness property."
Normatively: (1) a validator's lock is a persistent local triple (locked_height, locked_value, locked_round) with locked_value ∈ BLOCK ∪ {NIL} and locked_round a non-negative integer or NONE, and it is per height: the lock at height H constrains votes at H only, survives restart while H is unresolved, and is released by (a) the commit at H of the locked value, within the current generation (CONS-05, REC-02) — once the locked value is finalized the lock has done its work and no later height may be constrained by it. A commit at H of a different value does not release the lock: the validator that also holds a conflicting certificate halts under CONS-15(2), except at a retired height, where the carve-out of (2) applies. A commit certificate produced under a previous generation does not release a lock at a retired height either — the retirement itself does. Release is also by (b) the unlock of (2). (S5 §3: (1)(a) is qualified — the commit of the locked value within the current generation.) The two readings of a lock MUST NOT coexist. The normative reading is this per-height lock, whose cross-height effect is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii): a validator's vote at a later height must build on the block it finalized at the previous one. The competing reading — a lock that persists across heights and is never cleared at a height boundary — is not normative and MUST NOT appear in any client, contract or later text: with unlocking possible only at (H, p), a lock formed at H could never be released once the height had ended, every honest lock would be permanent, and a third of the set in that state would halt the chain after the first locked height. An implementer that found both readings would have to invent which one is normative; this clause removes the choice. (2) At (H,R), unlock happens only against a PoLC at (H, p) for a value different from locked_value with locked_round < p < R — strictly later, same height, different value. Carve-out — a lock at a retired height is void. If H is below resumeHeight (REC-02, L1-06) the height is retired, the lock at it is void, and it neither constrains any vote nor is carried anywhere: it is released by the retirement itself, no PoLC is required, and a locked value at a retired height is not a halt condition and is not by itself evidence of equivocation — a conflicting signed pair remains admissible evidence under ECON-04, exactly as REC-02's late-certificate element states. Without this carve-out a third or more of locked power could wedge the new chain at a discarded height, which is the unbounded halt that HALT-03 and CONS-15(2) exist to avoid. (S5 §3: a lock at a retired height is void.) A PoLC at the same round, at an earlier round, or for the value already locked does not unlock; a PoLC at another height neither unlocks the lock at H nor creates a lock at that other height. A validator that holds a lock at H and obtains a valid commit certificate at H for a different block has evidence that the assumptions failed: it halts (CONS-15(2)) and MUST NOT treat the certificate as an unlock. (3) The pol_round field of a proposal is advisory only: a validator MUST verify the PoLC itself before unlocking and MUST NOT treat the field as evidence. (4) A validator MUST NOT precommit a block B at (H,R) without holding a PoLC for B at (H,R), and MUST NOT prevote anything other than its locked value while locked at that height and not unlocked by (2). This closes review round 2 finding R2A-10: the lock is per height, released by the commit at that height or by a strictly later same-height PoLC, and the cross-height carry-over is exactly the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii); the two readings must not coexist, because an implementer would otherwise have to invent which one is normative.
Why the lock makes a withheld certificate harmless to safety. Quoting the Proof of Safety (S1): "If a validator commits block B at round R, it's because it saw +2/3 of precommits at round R. This implies that 1/3+ of honest nodes are still locked at round R' > R. These locked validators will remain locked until they see a PoLC at R' > R, but this won't happen because 1/3+ are locked and honest, so at most -2/3 are available to vote for anything other than B." A certificate that exists but has not been published therefore can only delay a node that has not seen it; it cannot displace a decision already taken unless 1/3 or more of the epoch's power violates this rule or the signing rule, which is exactly the published fork-accountability lemma ("the union of the JSets for both commits … must include double-signing by at least 1/3+ of the validator set", S1); that lemma's conclusion is conditional on the JSets being compilable, and its evidentiary reach is narrowed in CONS-09(4)(a). This claim is conditional on A-CONS-1 and on correct validators executing (1)–(4). Failure mode: an implementation that unlocks on a same-round PoLC, on a later round at another height, or on the proposal's pol_round field alone, admits a conflicting commit with no slashable signer; a validator that loses its lock on restart can vote for a conflicting block. Proven Assumed (A-CONS-1; liveness is not claimed here — a permanently withheld PoLC stalls a locked validator by design, "cannot make progress" is the honest outcome, not a bug)
CONS-05 — commit rule: more than 2/3 precommits finalize the block A commit certificate at (H,R) for block id B is C = (chain_id, epoch, H, R, B, set_root(epoch), signers, signatures). It is valid iff epoch = epoch_of(H), set_root(epoch) equals the L1-committed set root for that epoch (MEM-08), and quorum_block(Q, B) holds per CONS-03 with exactly popcount(signers) verifying signatures.
Block B at height H is PoS-finalized (STATUS-04) at a node when all of: (i) the node holds a valid certificate for (H,R,B); (ii) the node has performed its own data-availability check for B's body (it holds the body, can serve it, and the parent-hash chain from its last finalized block to B links without gaps) — STATUS-09, DA-01; and (iii) parent(B) is the block the node finalized at H-1.
The local decision is a function of the certificate, the node's own data-availability check and the node's own finalized prefix — and of nothing else. No rule may condition a node's commitment on having seen, or on not having seen, any other certificate. In particular the decision MUST NOT be "commit only if no conflicting certificate exists", and MUST NOT be "wait for a duration T and commit if nothing else arrives" (STATUS-10). A node that instead holds a conflicting lock at H (CONS-04) or a conflicting finalized block at H has evidence that the assumptions failed; it halts (CONS-15) and MUST NOT silently adopt the other branch.
Publication. On reaching (i)–(iii) the node MUST gossip C and treat H as finalized, and it MUST be able to serve C to a node that is behind in height (S1: "Nodes gossip to nodes lagging in blockchain height with block commits for older blocks."). Publication is a liveness aid: no safety argument on this page depends on any receiver having seen a certificate at a particular time. Failure mode: a commit rule conditioned on the absence of other certificates makes the local decision non-monotone and makes it depend on an unprovable negative; a withheld certificate then appears to invalidate a decision already taken, which is the failure these rules forbid — the node's decision stays a function of the certificate, its own data check and its own finalized prefix, while replacement of unsettled history above the last accepted checkpoint remains the separate, rule-bound L1 path of REC-02. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — recovery never conditions the local commit rule; the rule of this clause is unchanged.) Proven (the >2/3 boundary is CONS-03; data availability is checked locally and is non-transferable)
CONS-06 — leader selection: deterministic stake-weighted selection Let epoch e have committed weights w_0 … w_{n-1} (w_i ≥ 1) in the canonical order of set_root(e) (MEM-08), W = Σ w_i, and cumulative sums cum_i = w_0 + … + w_i with cum_{-1} = 0. For a height H in epoch e and a round R, the proposer is selected uniformly in weight by a hash:
pos(H,R) = uint256(keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_PROPOSER, chainId, epoch, H, R))) mod W
proposer(H,R) = the unique i with cum_{i-1} ≤ pos(H,R) < cum_i.
DOMAIN_PROPOSER is the right-padded ASCII tag "TAIKO_ETNA_PROPOSER_V1" with chainId as the second field, encoded by the one canonical abi.encode of GEN-05 (typed u256, u64, u64, u32 after the tag); the tag is never used for another object. The intervals are half-open, so exactly one index matches whenever every w_i ≥ 1; there are no ties, and the half-open convention at an exact boundary is the whole tie-breaking rule: the lower index wins. pos is reduced modulo W, so it always lies in [0, W). A set with w_i = 0, n = 0 or W = 0 is invalid (CONS-10).
(a) Pure function of the committed inputs. The proposer of (H,R) is a pure function of (chainId, epoch_of(H), H, R) and the authenticated set set_root(epoch_of(H)) — never of a node's local round history, arrival order, proposal or witness. Every correct node computes the same proposer for the same (H,R) regardless of the path by which it arrived, so a round cannot be split between two proposers.
(b) Uniform in weight; the fairness claim is Assumed. Exactly w_i of the W residues in [0, W) lie in validator i's interval, so if pos is uniform on [0, W) then validator i is proposer with probability exactly w_i / W, the residue structure is uniform (no interval is privileged by its position, so the lowest-sorted validator has no systematic advantage), and a coalition C with Σ_{i∈C} w_i < W/3 proposes strictly fewer than one third of the slots in expectation. The counting argument is exact in the committed integers; the step that turns it into a probability statement — that pos is uniform — rests on keccak256 behaving as a random oracle, which A-CRYPTO-3 (collision resistance) does not supply, so the fairness claim is Assumed, not proven, and it is an expectation, not a bound on any finite window: unlike the superseded residue rotation, this rule does not claim that every W-height window contains exactly w_i proposer slots for validator i.
(c) Modulo bias is negligible and bounded. The reduction mod W makes the probability of each residue differ from the uniform 1 / W by at most 2^-256, so validator i's selection probability deviates from w_i / W by at most w_i / 2^256 ≤ W / 2^256: for any W ≪ 2^256 — the case for every TAIKO base-unit total, where W is far smaller than the 256-bit hash range — the bias is negligible. No second quantization of the weights is introduced to "help" the reduction: the w_i here are exactly the integers used for quorum (CONS-03).
(d) Publicly verifiable from the set root alone. Anyone — a validator, a light client, a full node, an L1 contract, a user — holding the L1-committed set_root(epoch_of(H)) and the tuple (chainId, epoch_of(H), H, R) can recompute pos, locate the interval and name the proposer, with no witness, certificate, proposal, participation proof or cooperation from any validator.
What this does not claim. The property is over proposer slots, not over produced blocks: a cartel that forces timeouts on honest slots can increase its share of produced blocks. That is a liveness and ordering surface (threat T-6), handled by the timeout ladder (CONS-07), which moves the proposer slot on when a round fails; it is not a safety surface. Censorship resistance is not a guarantee but the conditional statistical property of that rotation: a coalition with less than one third of the voting power is selected for strictly fewer than one third of slots in expectation, so a transaction that reaches honest validators is included by the first honest proposer with room for it — real but conditional resistance under A-CONS-2, since a network-level adversary able to isolate a user from every honest proposer can exclude that user's transactions indefinitely and the protocol offers no remedy. (user decision D-6: forced inclusion removed — the rotation above is the remaining, conditional, resistance.) The full statement, including the disclosed partial relaxation of R10, is LIVE-04. Selection is a public deterministic function, so the next proposer is predictable far in advance; the disclosure obligation for that predictability, and any mitigation, belong to LIM-01.
Proposer privilege. A proposer may broadcast at most one proposal valid at (H,R) (two distinct signed proposals at one round are equivocation, CONS-11). It has no other power: it cannot finalize anything, cannot choose the validator set, and every validator checks CONS-01 itself.
Unit. w_i are the same integers used for quorum, and PARAM-01 must fix their unit with MEM-02. The stake-to-unit mapping, its quantization error and its tag are MEM-02's and are unmeasured here. No modest value of W is required: the rule is well defined for every W ≥ 1, and the bound of (c) is what makes a large W safe. Failure mode: a proposer rule that depends on local state splits a round between two proposers; a rule with undefined tie-breaking lets two implementations disagree at a boundary; a rule that weights by anything other than the committed set (e.g. by hash without weights, or by arrival order) lets a sub-threshold coalition capture more than its share of slots; a residue rotation whose index is in weight units makes the first-sorted validator the proposer of effectively every height. Proven Assumed (determinism, well-definedness and the interval counting are proven in the committed integers; the uniform-weight fairness of (b) is Assumed on the hash's uniformity and is an expectation, not a per-window bound — see the modifications table, M6. This closes review round 1 finding CS-02: the superseded residue rotation in weight units left every position inside the first weight interval, so one minimum-bond key proposed every height.)
CONS-07 — round timeouts, view change and the adaptive ladder (1) Every timeout is a duration in seconds. No timeout is expressed in L1 slots, no rule depends on Ethereum proposer lookahead or on a fixed L1 slot duration, and no validity rule reads a wall clock (GEN-06, R12); wall clocks pace local signing and timeout actions only. (2) Parameters, fixed by PARAM-01: T_min (base round budget) and T_max (ceiling). Proposed values: T_min = 3 s derived from round_trip = 4·Δ_max + X_max with T_min ≥ 2·round_trip, and T_max = 30 s derived as a bound small relative to the 30-minute proving envelope of D6 sourced (D6) so that a stall is detected long before settlement deadlines bind. Δ_max (advisory one-way delay between correct validators) and X_max (local execution time for one block) are UNMEASURED; the measurement that would fix them is telemetry of the p99 one-way message delay and of block execution time at the target validator count, topology and block size. Both T_min and T_max are therefore unmeasured in practice. (3) The ladder. Let R_progress be the highest round ≤ R at which this node received a PoLC or a commit certificate for height H (0 if none), and let R_cap = ceil(log2(T_max / T_min)). Then
T(H,R) = min(T_max, T_min · 2^min(R - R_progress, R_cap)) seconds,
evaluated in integer arithmetic only (with the proposed values the ladder is 3, 6, 12, 24, 30, 30, … s). The exponential term is the response to round failures; the R_progress reset is the adaptation to observed round completion: a round that produced a PoLC or a commit resets the backoff to base, so a transient stall does not permanently inflate the timeouts. Timeouts are local and have no normative effect on any other node. (4) Advancement. A correct validator at (H,R) MUST advance to (H,R+1) when either (a) T(H,R) expires, or (b) it has received a PoLC at (H,R) and has completed its own precommit step for that round — after a PoLC no further signature of that type can change the outcome of the round. On advancing it MUST update R_progress if it saw a PoLC or a commit certificate. It MUST NOT advance past a commit: if it holds a valid commit certificate for H it commits and enters H+1. A locked validator advances rounds normally; its votes remain constrained by CONS-04. (5) A timeout changes only local round state. It is never evidence that a certificate, proposal or block does not exist (STATUS-10), never invalidates anything, and never licenses an existential claim about what other nodes hold. (6) Independence from safety. The timeout values have no effect on CONS-12: safety holds under asynchrony, and A-CONS-2 requires partial synchrony for liveness only. If the assumed delay bound Δ_max is violated, the consequences are view-change churn, slower confirmation and eventually a stall — liveness only, never safety (A-CONS-3). A stall is a legitimate outcome (CONS-15); the protocol MUST NOT respond to it by weakening a safety rule. Backpressure beyond the unsettled-depth cap is HALT-03 and is not owned here. Failure mode: timeouts expressed in L1 slots break when Ethereum changes slot timing (R12); a fixed timeout with no backoff turns a partition into a permanent stall of the round machinery even after the network heals; a timeout read as evidence of absence is the exact non-argument 01 §6.4 forbids. Proven Assumed (liveness part: A-CONS-2 / A-CONS-3; the safety part is unconditional on timing)
CONS-08 — epoch-scoped certificates remain valid forever (1) Every certificate, vote and proposal names an epoch, and that value is not free: it MUST equal epoch_of(height) under the L1-committed epoch schedule (CONS-13). An object whose epoch field disagrees with epoch_of(height) is invalid regardless of its signatures, because otherwise its signers could choose which set judges them. (2) A certificate for epoch e MUST be judged under set_root(e) — the set committed at L1 for epoch e — and under no other set. The current epoch, the current head, wall-clock time, and the identity or stake of later validators MUST NOT enter the judgement. (3) Once valid, a certificate is valid forever. An implementation MUST NOT apply an expiry, a maximum age, a "current set" check, or a re-verification under a later set. The epoch's set root, total power and length MUST remain reconstructible from Ethereum-final L1 data for the lifetime of the chain; MEM-08/MEM-09 must therefore store every epoch's root in storage that is never overwritten. Retention of set roots is a storage-layout obligation, not a soft policy. (4) Consequently, validator rotation cannot invalidate finality. A validator that joins in epoch e+k can never cause a block finalized in epoch e to be re-judged, re-signed or discarded; rotation changes who may finalize future heights only. This is the mechanism by which a certificate's validity travels with the certificate, not with the current set: the judgement is fixed for ever, even though the canonical status of the history above the last accepted checkpoint is governed by the recovery rules (REC-02), and a certificate voided there remains admissible as evidence. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — recovery never re-judges a certificate under another set; it only replaces unsettled history above the accepted checkpoint.) (5) The security of an old certificate is exactly the security of the set that signed it, at the stake that counted in that epoch. The current set's stake neither strengthens nor weakens it. Slashing of an old offence follows the stake at offence time (MEM-06). Failure mode: an implementation that verifies certificates against "the current validator set" makes rotation equivalent to a rollback — a later set can restate history — and the epoch rule of this clause silently disappears (a violation of CONS-08 and GOV-02). Proven (premise: the epoch roots stay available from L1, which MEM-08 owns)
CONS-09 — epoch handoff and cross-height carry-over (the delicate modification, F1) Let h_last(e) = H_0 + (e+1)·L - 1 be the last height of epoch e, h_first(e+1) = h_last(e) + 1, and B_anchor the block at h_last(e) finalized under epoch e's set. The first block of epoch e+1 MUST carry in its header the epoch_anchor field defined in CONS-10(6): the canonical commitment under domain tag "TAIKO_ETNA_ANCHOR_V1" to the tuple (h_last(e), hash(B_anchor), cert_hash(e)), where hash(B_anchor) is the hash of the last block finalized in the closing epoch e and cert_hash(e) is the canonical certificate hash of B_anchor's epoch-e commit certificate, with the exhaustive field list of CONS-10(6). Its parent MUST be B_anchor. (2) Validator duty. A correct validator MUST NOT prevote or precommit any block of epoch e+1 unless it has itself verified a valid epoch-e commit certificate for B_anchor (CONS-03, CONS-05) and the parent linkage of (1). The certificate may be obtained by gossip from any peer; the check is on the object, not on the sender. A validator that cannot obtain it stays silent or prevotes NIL; the consequence is a stall at the boundary, which is a liveness failure, not a safety one. (3) Cross-height rules at the epoch boundary. Locks are per height (CONS-04(1)): the lock at a height is released by that height's commit, so a validator carries no lock state from a height of epoch e into e+1. What carries across the boundary is the parent-validity condition of CONS-01(iii), enforced by the anchor duty of (1)–(2): the first block of e+1 must descend from B_anchor, the last block finalized in e. For a validator that joins in e+1 and holds no vote history, the anchor check of (1)–(2) is what takes the parent rule's place — it must build on a block finalized by the previous set rather than on a block it merely received. A lock formed at a height of e on a value that did not finalize there is resolved at that height by the operative release rule of CONS-04(1) — the commit of the locked value within the current generation, a strictly later same-height PoLC under CONS-04(2), or, where the height has been retired by a completed recovery, the retirement itself — and is not carried forward as an independent constraint. If a valid conflicting certificate exists at a height that is not retired, the validator halts (CONS-15(2)). (S5 §3: "resolved at that height" gets the operative release rule it lacked.) This closes review round 2 finding R2A-10: the boundary protection is the parent-validity condition plus the anchor duty, not a persistent lock. (4) The argument. Suppose two conflicting blocks are finalized across the boundary. Heights are partitioned by epoch and a certificate for a height in epoch e can only be an epoch-e certificate (CONS-08(1)), so there are two cases. (a) The conflict is a pair of epoch-e certificates for one height. Two quorums of epoch e then signed conflicting precommits at that height, so by CONS-03 the quorums intersect in more than one third of the epoch's power. What that intersection proves is exactly this and no more: a signer that signed conflicting values at the same (H,R) has produced a same-(height, round) conflicting signed pair, which is always slashable and is the only lock-related offence with admissible evidence (CONS-11; ECON-04's lock row). A signer that signed the two values at different rounds R1 < R2 may be fully compliant: CONS-04(2) permits unlocking against a PoLC at a round p with R1 < p < R2 and CONS-04(4) requires exactly such a PoLC before precommitting the second value, so the two certificates alone do not prove misconduct. The published fork-accountability lemma's conclusion — "the union of the JSets for both commits, if they can be compiled, must include double-signing by at least 1/3+ of the validator set" (S1) — is conditional on that compilation, and a withholding adversary (T-4) that reveals only the two certificates prevents it. Therefore a cross-round or cross-epoch divergence may leave no admissible evidence under withholding, and the statement "at least one third is punishable" is Assumed, not established; it MUST NOT be presented as a deterrent guarantee, and the limitation row for it is owed to the LIM-01 register (page 10). This closes review round 1 finding CS-07: objective slashability is narrowed to the same-(height, round) conflicting pair, and cross-round divergence under withholding is Assumed, not established. (b) The conflict is between B_anchor and a block B' at h_first(e+1) that does not descend from it. B' needs more than 2/3 of epoch e+1's precommits, and every correct signer among them verified an epoch-e certificate for B''s parent B'' at h_last(e) by (2). Now B'' ≠ B_anchor and both are at the same height with epoch-e certificates: that is case (a) again. Case (b) therefore offers no escape, and a conflicting history across the boundary requires at least one third of epoch e's power to have violated the signing or lock rules — though, as (a) states, only the same-(height, round) part of that violation is objectively evidenced. (5) Status: ASSUMED-WITH-ARGUMENT, not proven. The published CometBFT Proof of Safety assumes the validator set for a height is known to all correct nodes and that application-driven updates are visible before use; the epoch-quantized, L1-authenticated handoff replaces that (04 §2.1, M2) and 04 §7 records it as open falsifier F1. CONS-10 — validator-set authentication in block headers (1) Every block header carries two set commitments, validators_hash, the commitment to the set of epoch_of(H), and next_validators_hash, the commitment to the set of epoch_of(H)+1. Both are the L1-committed validator-set root of MEM-08: validators_hash = R_{setVersion(epoch_of(H))} and next_validators_hash = R_{setVersion(epoch_of(H)+1)}. The header fields add no encoding of their own (*review round 1, finding B R1-05*). In addition to these two set commitments, the header carries the two epoch-transition fields defined in (6): epoch_anchor and set_version_commit. (2) Single owner of the encoding. MEM-08 is the sole owner; there is no second, flat or differently-typed encoding of a validator set in this specification, and this rule may not define one. In full:
A leaf is keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_SET_LEAF, chainId, k, index, pubkey, effStake)); an interior node is keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_SET_NODE, chainId, k, left, right)); entries are ordered ascending by keccak256(abi.encode(DOMAIN_SET_KEY, chainId, pubkey)); odd nodes are promoted unchanged and never duplicated; the domain tags, the empty-set tag, the field types and the invalid-set conditions are exactly MEM-08's. What the root commits to is MEM-08(4)'s "exactly": the chain id, the set version k and the ordered (pubkey, effStake) pairs, where effStake is TAIKO base units as a uint256 — never a truncated u64 — and not owner addresses, bond age or any score. A set with n = 0, a duplicate or missing index, a zero effective stake, or a malformed key is invalid. Below, set_root(e) denotes the root this encoding produces for the set version governing epoch e, read from the L1 per-epoch mapping of MEM-09(2). (3) Transitions. The validators_hash of the first block of epoch e and the next_validators_hash of the last block of epoch e-1 MUST both equal the L1-committed set_root(e) (MEM-08, MEM-09). The staking contract computes set_root(e) itself from its own state and stores it in the L1 per-epoch mapping (MEM-09(2)); no proposer, signer, relayer, witness or prover supplies it (PRF-03). Because the encoding is fixed, a mismatch is detectable by anyone, and two different values for one epoch are a conflict that halts the L2 (CONS-15) rather than a choice. (4) Authentication procedure for a light client or a zkVM guest. Starting from an anchored header, walk the header chain by parent hash; at an epoch change require the new header's validators_hash to equal its predecessor's next_validators_hash; take the epoch-e set as a witness, recompute the MEM-08 Merkle root from its leaves and interior nodes, and require equality with (i) the head's validators_hash and (ii) the epoch's L1-committed root exposed as a public input. Only after both equalities hold may the certificate's signer keys and weights be used (PRF-05). A verifier that skips the comparison is trusting the witness. (5) The commitment is over the whole set, not over individual stakes; a verifier that recomputes the root from a different set and matches it has found a collision (assumption A-CRYPTO-3). A second encoding is forbidden, not merely discouraged: if a flat hash or any other preimage were also admissible, one of the two bindings — the header-field equality of (1)/(3) or the Merkle membership proof of MEM-08(6)/PRF-03 — could be satisfied while the other failed silently, and a set substitution would pass whichever check the implementation happened to keep.
(6) Epoch-transition header fields and their canonical encodings. In this clause e is the epoch being closed and e+1 the epoch being opened, as in CONS-09. The first block of epoch e+1 MUST carry the two fields below; every other block header MUST carry both as zero, so the header hash stays a pure function of the block's own content. All three hashes are single canonical GEN-05 abi.encodes with their own right-padded ASCII domain tag, and each tag is used for exactly one object.
epoch_anchor = keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_ETNA_ANCHOR_V1", chainId, h_last(e), hash(B_anchor), cert_hash(e))) — the canonical commitment to the tuple of CONS-09(1), where hash(B_anchor) is the hash of the block at h_last(e), the last block finalized in the closing epoch e. A verifier that holds B_anchor and its certificate recomputes hash(B_anchor) and cert_hash(e) and compares; a header whose epoch_anchor does not recompute from the certificate it presents is invalid.
cert_hash = keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_ETNA_CERT_V1", chainId, epoch, height, round, block_id_kind, block_id_value, set_root, signers, signatures)) over exactly the fields of the CommitCertificate object of the encoding table: block_id_kind/block_id_value the tagged-union encoding of the certificate's block id, set_root the certificate's epoch set root, signers the bytes validator bitmap, and signatures the bytes[] list ordered by ascending signer index. The field list is exhaustive: a hash over any other field set, any other field order, or any other signature order is a different object and MUST NOT be accepted as cert_hash.
set_version_commit = keccak256(abi.encode("TAIKO_ETNA_SETVERSION_V1", chainId, epoch, set_version, N)) for the epoch the first block belongs to — epoch = e+1 — where (set_version, N) is the pair (setVersion(e+1), N(setVersion(e+1))) of that epoch's L1 mapping entry, N being the L1 block number of the commitSet() call whose state produced set_root(e+1) (MEM-09(2), which owns the value rule; this clause owns the encoding).
The shortcut is forbidden. The handoff of CONS-09 is sound only if implementations actually carry and check the anchor: omitting epoch_anchor, carrying a value that is never recomputed from B_anchor's certificate, carrying a non-zero set_version_commit that does not recompute from the L1 mapping entry, or letting epoch e+1's set sign without the check, removes the entire safety argument of CONS-09(4) — the shortcut named in CONS-09(5)(i) — and is therefore forbidden. Failure mode: a prover-supplied or proposer-supplied set lets the attacker define the quorum that judges it; the L1 comparison must be checkable from a header field, not inferred from the certificate. CONS-11 — equivocation: definition, evidence and who may submit Definition. Validator v equivocates at (H,R) if it produces two signed messages m1 ≠ m2 with type(m1) = type(m2) ∈ {PROPOSAL, PREVOTE, PRECOMMIT}; identical chain_id, height = H, round = R and validator_index = v; and either different signed values — for votes block_id(m1) ≠ block_id(m2), where NIL differs from every BLOCK(x) by construction of the tagged union, and for proposals block_hash(m1) ≠ block_hash(m2) — or different signed epoch fields. A pair of that second form is a conflict under the epoch-agreement check: at one (H,R) at most one epoch value can equal epoch_of(H) (CONS-08(1)), so a pair whose signed epochs differ is a conflict even when the block values coincide, and an epoch-mismatched pair MUST NOT be treated as a non-conflict. Signing a prevote for X and a precommit for Y ≠ X at the same (H,R) is not equivocation: it is the normal step sequence. Signing at different rounds or different heights is not equivocation.
Evidence. The evidence object is the pair (m1, m2) with both signatures, plus e = epoch_of(H). It is self-authenticating: a checker verifies each signature against the public key committed for v at its index in the set that the object's own signed epoch field names (MEM-07(4)), with each named epoch's root reconstructible from L1 (CONS-08(3)); checks that the objects are distinct and agree on (chain_id, H, R, type, validator_index); applies the epoch-agreement check — if the two signed epoch fields are equal, both MUST equal epoch_of(H), and if they differ the pair is admitted as evidence and the object whose epoch field is not epoch_of(H) is itself an invalid vote under CONS-08(1) — and concludes the offence. No further witness, no P2P receipt, no participation proof and no other validator's cooperation is needed. Conversely, failure to receive a message is never evidence (threat T-15; STATUS-09).
Who may submit. Any party — a validator, a full node, a user, a third party, or a contract — may assemble and submit evidence. The process MUST NOT require the submitter to be a validator, to have staked, to reveal an identity, or to have participated in the round. Reward, collateral, duplicate handling and false-accusation cost are ECON-04/ECON-08.
Lifetime. Evidence remains checkable for as long as set_root(e) is reconstructible from L1 (CONS-08). Whether a slash can still be executed is a function of the unbonding and evidence windows (ECON-07): evidence age must be less than the unbonding period so the offending stake is still available ("for Proof of Stake chains where validators are bonded, evidence age should be less than the unbonding period so validators still can be punished", CometBFT evidence spec, quoted in recovery-and-withholding-raw.md §1.1).
Proposer equivocation. Two distinct signed proposals at one (H,R) are an offence of the same class, and it is the only proposer misbehaviour that is objectively evidenced (CONS-01). Failure mode: an under-specified definition — for example treating a prevote/precommit pair as a conflict, or requiring the two messages to be of different types — either makes honest validators slashable or makes real equivocation unprovable; requiring a validator as submitter leaves the offence unpunished by default. Proven (evidence check needs only the two signed objects and the L1-committed set. This closes review round 1 finding CS-11: the evidence predicate did not require the two objects to agree on the signed epoch field, so an epoch-mismatched pair could escape the evidence rule.)
CONS-12 — the safety invariant: generation-scoped uniqueness of the certified prefix Invariant (generation-scoped). Under the premises below, at most one block per height per generation receives a valid certificate, and a retired height can never be re-certified. Formally, fix a generation g: if certificate C1 finalizes block B1 at height H1 under generation g and certificate C2 finalizes block B2 at height H2 ≤ H1 under the same generation (each valid per CONS-03 and CONS-05), then the ancestor of B1 at H2 is B2: within one generation the certified prefix is unique. The unconditional "at most one block per height ever" claim is deleted: a completed recovery deliberately violates it by discarding provisional history above the restored checkpoint and retiring the discarded heights (REC-02). The generation is the boundary of the uniqueness claim, and retirement makes the claim for a height permanent: below resumeHeight a height is never certified again, under any generation, by anyone (L1-06, CONS-01(vii)). (S5 §6: CONS-12 is generation-scoped, a retired height is never re-certified, and the unconditional claim is deleted.) The invariant is about the certificates produced under the rules — the objects CONS-01–CONS-09 let honest validators produce — and it is relative to the authenticated checkpoint: it is anchored at the last L1-accepted checkpoint (MEM-11, L1-04), and every claim below is a claim about the chain of such certificates that extends that checkpoint. Above the last accepted checkpoint the invariant is conditional on the recovery rules: a certificate at a height above that checkpoint is a claim on provisional history, and the permissionless, bonded recovery of REC-02 may replace that history without any certificate being forged or re-judged. At and below the last accepted checkpoint no recovery path may change the history at all. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — the invariant is restated relative to the authenticated checkpoint and made conditional on the recovery rules for history above it.)
Premises (all of them; none may be silently dropped). (a) A-CONS-1: Byzantine stake is strictly below 1/3 of the stake that counts toward quorum, in every epoch — the premise is per epoch because a certificate is judged under its own epoch's set (CONS-08). (b) Correct validators execute CONS-02 (persistent vote uniqueness), CONS-04 (lock and unlock) and CONS-01's validity rule. (c) The set and its weights are L1-authenticated: set_root(e) is computed from L1-committed TAIKO stake (CONS-10, MEM-08) and cannot be chosen by a proposer, a signer or a prover (PRF-03). (d) The epoch handoff and its cross-height carry-over hold (CONS-09: per-height locks released at their commit, CONS-04(1), and the parent-validity condition, CONS-01(iii)) — ASSUMED-WITH-ARGUMENT, not proven. The cross-epoch component of this invariant therefore inherits that status.
Argument within an epoch. Two quorums at one height each satisfy 3·s1 > 2·W and 3·s2 > 2·W, hence 3·(s1+s2) > 4·W and the intersection of the two signer sets exceeds W/3. The published Proof of Safety then shows that one third or more of honest, locked validators prevent a conflicting commit unless one third or more violate the lock or signing rules. That violation is not automatically evidenced. The fork-accountability lemma concludes that the union of the JSets includes double-signing by at least 1/3+ only if the JSets can be compiled (S1; survey §3.2), and the admissible evidence predicate of CONS-11 accepts only same-(height, round) pairs, so a cross-round or cross-epoch divergence under a withholding adversary may be unpunishable; the reading "at least one third is objectively slashable" is Assumed, not established (CONS-09(4)(a); the limitation row is owed to LIM-01). This argument transfers unchanged from CometBFT for a fixed epoch set, and the safety invariant itself does not depend on slashing: it needs only that fewer than one third are faulty.
When the premises fail. If 1/3 or more of one epoch's power is Byzantine, or keys are compromised, or clients mis-implement CONS-02/CONS-04, then two conflicting commit certificates can both be cryptographically valid. The ZK proof does not repair this: the proof attests that a certificate is internally consistent with a stated validator set and quorum rule — it cannot attest that no other certificate exists (01 §6.5; INV-01). Every uniqueness claim in this specification is therefore relative to (i) an L1-authenticated checkpoint (MEM-11), (ii) A-CONS-1 in the relevant epochs, and (iii) the recovery rules for heights above that checkpoint, since a certificate there is provisional (REC-02). Under failure the honest response is detection, evidence publication and disclosure (fault class F2) — never a rule that pretends to resolve it, and never a path that rewrites history at or below the last accepted checkpoint. Above that checkpoint the only sanctioned replacement is the bonded recovery of REC-02, which discards unsettled history by rule and grants no party discretion over the restored checkpoint. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — the uniqueness claim is conditional on the recovery rules above the last accepted checkpoint, and the only replacement path is permissionless and bonded.)
One certificate per batch. For a batch whose head block is H, verifying the certificate of H alone, plus the parent-hash linkage of the batch's headers back to the previously L1-accepted checkpoint, extends the invariant to every block of the batch, because finality of B1 at H1 implies its ancestors by the same invariant (04 §2). The premise — correct validators enforce the parent relation (CONS-01(iii)) and the lock rule — is stated as such, and a violation that takes the form of a same-(height, round) conflicting pair is objectively slashable (CONS-11); other lock violations are not independently provable (CONS-09(4)(a)). Failure mode: reading the quorum rule as a guarantee of uniqueness without its premises, or claiming that the proof system upgrades a two-thirds assumption into a safety guarantee; both produce a system that misstates its own failure boundary. Proven Assumed (cross-epoch component, per CONS-09/F1)
CONS-13 — epoch length, set-commitment timing and two-epoch lookahead (1) L is the epoch length in heights, a fixed positive integer parameter (PARAM-01). Proposed value L = 900 heights derived: at the D1 cadence of one block per 2 s sourced (D1) an epoch is 30 minutes of L2 history, matching the project's arithmetic convention that 30 min = 900 L2 blocks (01 §3) and the D6 proving envelope sourced (D6). Suitability at the target set size is unmeasured. L is fixed, together with H_genesis and the epoch offset e_0, by the L1-committed epoch schedule recorded in the activation transaction (CONS-14); none of the three may be taken from a peer, a witness or the proof. The L1-side epoch clock. The schedule above is the L2-side schedule: it fixes epoch boundaries by L2 height. The L1 side has its own schedule, expressed in L1 blocks and recorded in the same activation record: L1_0 is the L1 block at which epoch e_0 begins — for the migration specified here, the L1 block at which epoch 0 begins — and EPOCH_LEN_L1 is the number of L1 blocks per L1-side epoch, so epoch e begins on the L1 side at L1_first(e) = L1_0 + (e - e_0) · EPOCH_LEN_L1 (PARAM-01). The L1 staking contract derives the epoch it is in, and the target epoch of every append, from this L1-block schedule alone — never from a wall clock, from an L2 view, or from any L2-derived progress signal. This closes review round 3 finding R3A-01: the two-epoch lookahead referred to the epoch the L1-committed schedule had reached without defining that L1-side clock or anchoring epoch 0, so an implementer had to invent a security-relevant rule; both are now fixed by named L1 blocks recorded in the activation record (CONS-14). (2) epoch_of(H) = floor((H - H_genesis) / L) + e_0, h_first(e) = H_genesis + (e - e_0)·L, h_last(e) = H_genesis + (e - e_0 + 1)·L - 1, with H_genesis the genesis L2 height (H_0 of CONS-14). epoch_of is a pure function of the height and of L1-committed constants: no L2-derived view, no header, no witness and no proof participates in it. The two schedules are reconciled by one entry rule: the L2 MUST NOT enter epoch e — no proposer may propose and no validator may prevote or precommit a block at a height ≥ h_first(e) — until the L1-side clock of (1) has reached L1_first(e). The boundary is still defined by L2 height; the L1 clock only forbids entering an epoch early. A faster-than-nominal L2 cadence therefore waits at the boundary for the L1 clock instead of outrunning it — no boundary halt can arise from cadence drift — and a slower cadence never has to wait. This closes review round 3 finding R3A-01: the L2-side height schedule and the L1-side block schedule are now related by an explicit entry rule, so the height-derived epoch_of and the L1-side clock can no longer drift apart at a realised cadence other than the nominal one. The L1 staking contract stores an append-only per-epoch mapping epoch → (setRoot, totalVotingPower); an entry is immutable once its epoch starts, and the epoch key is not chosen by any caller (MEM-09(1)). The L1 Inbox resolves epoch_of over the batch's heights from its own schedule and reads that mapping; it MUST reject any batch whose claimed epoch differs from epoch_of of its heights before verifying the proof, and it MUST resolve the epoch to a set root from L1 state without trusting the proof, the witness or the prover (L1-05, PRF-02) (*review round 1, finding B R1-03*). (3) Two-epoch lookahead. The L1 staking contract MUST append the per-epoch mapping entry for epoch e+2 — its setRoot computed with the MEM-08 encoding (MEM-08) and the corresponding totalVotingPower — during the L1-side epoch e of (1), within the append-obligation window (no later than one EPOCH_LEN_L1 after the entry becomes coverable), and that entry MUST be an Ethereum-final L1 fact before any block of epoch e+2 may be voted for or finalized (SYS-02). The commitment is an obligation of the L1 staking contract on its own schedule and MUST NOT be keyed to the Inbox's last accepted L2 height or to any other L2-derived progress signal: under D5 the accepted height lags production by the whole proving pipeline, so a gate keyed to it would stop production at every epoch boundary. The L2 MUST NOT wait on an L1 transaction to continue voting inside an epoch; it reads the epoch's set from Ethereum-final L1 state at the boundary, and the entry for the epoch being entered is already an Ethereum-final L1 fact because it was committed two epochs earlier. The obligation is not merely passive. The entry for the next uncovered epoch MUST be appended no later than one EPOCH_LEN_L1 after it becomes coverable — it is coverable once the L1-side clock has reached the earliest epoch whose entry the append rule permits, in steady state the start of epoch e — commitSet() MUST remain permissionless so that anyone may perform the obligation, and a missed append MUST be restorable by a later call: the lowest-missing rule of MEM-09(1) refills a skipped epoch and any later call may restore the full lead, so a single missed call cannot permanently reduce the design to a one-epoch lead. The operative steady-state inequality is E_EPOCH_L1 ≥ L1_FINALITY + margin, where E_EPOCH_L1 = EPOCH_LEN_L1 · L1_BLOCK_INTERVAL: an entry appended at the last permitted moment still has one full L1-side epoch to become Ethereum-final before the L2 may enter the epoch it governs, and the two-epoch rule of this clause gives a full epoch of slack on top of that. Who pays for the append call and whether a keeper bounty exists is Open: the consequence of no one calling is that the lead degrades to one epoch, the obligation window is missed, and the boundary halt of (5) becomes reachable — every entry remains appendable and restorable, so the state is recoverable without a rule change, but production stops at the boundary until a caller appends the missing entry and it becomes Ethereum-final. This closes review round 3 finding R3A-02: the append was an obligation of a contract that has no schedule, with no deadline, no performer and no restoration rule; the deadline, the permissionless performance and the self-healing lowest-missing rule are now normative, and the enforcement question is marked Open with its consequence.
With E_EPOCH the epoch duration in seconds, the lookahead MUST satisfy the normative inequality
2 * E_EPOCH ≥ T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED + T_SETTLE_PIPELINE + L1_FINALITY + margin,
where T_PROOF_MAX_PERMITTED is the longest proving time permitted for a batch, T_SETTLE_PIPELINE is the time for the batch's data and proof to be included and accepted on L1, L1_FINALITY is the time for an L1 block to become Ethereum-final, and margin is a safety allowance. Every input to the inequality is unmeasured: the measurement that would fix them is telemetry of proving latency, settlement-pipeline latency and L1 inclusion-to-finality time at the target deployment. For the proposed L and the D1 cadence the two-epoch window is roughly 60 minutes derived, but whether that suffices is exactly what the unmeasured inputs decide. This closes review round 2 finding R2A-01: the superseded one-epoch lookahead keyed the commitment for epoch e+1 to the Inbox's last accepted height, which under D5 lags production by the whole proving pipeline and therefore stalled production at every epoch boundary. (4) At most one entry per epoch. The mapping is append-only: the L1 staking contract MUST reject any write whose epoch key already exists, and no later transaction, reorganisation or upgrade may overwrite an entry. A chain that nevertheless observes two different roots for one epoch is facing a contract defect or a consensus failure and MUST halt (CONS-15) rather than choose; it never uses two sets for one epoch, and it never re-derives the set from a non-final L1 block. The membership-side detail of the append is MEM-09's. (5) Halt condition. If, when the L2 reaches h_first(e+1) and the L1-side clock of (1) has reached L1_first(e+1) — that is, at the moment of entry — no Ethereum-final mapping entry for epoch e+1 is available, then correct validators MUST NOT propose, prevote or precommit any block at heights ≥ h_first(e+1), MUST NOT continue into epoch e+1 with epoch e's set, and MUST halt at the highest finalized block of epoch e (CONS-15, HALT-01(d), HALT-02). The halt condition is evaluated at the moment of entry and at no earlier moment. A missing entry for a future epoch is not a halt condition: while the L2 produces an epoch, an absent or not-yet-final entry for an epoch later than the one being entered changes nothing, and with the two-epoch lookahead of (3) the entry for the epoch being entered is committed two epochs before it is needed; production MUST NOT stop for a commitment it does not yet require. The halt applies only when the entry for the epoch being entered is absent. This closes review round 3 finding R3A-01: the halt is evaluated at the moment of entry on the anchored L1-block clock, and a missing future entry never halts. A late entry may only be used to resume at h_first(e+1) if no block of epoch e+1 was ever produced; restart rules are HALT-02's and never roll back a finalized block. Continuing with the old set would put a height into an epoch whose set cannot be authenticated against L1 and would violate CONS-08(1); taking a set from an unfinalized L1 block would violate SYS-02 and assumption A-CONS-4 (all correct nodes agree on the set version for an epoch). A halt at the boundary is a liveness failure (class F1), and no recovery may roll the L1-accepted prefix back: heights at or below the last accepted checkpoint are untouchable, while above it the permissionless, bonded recovery of REC-02 may replace unsettled history under its own trigger, bond and delay. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — the boundary halt stands, and the only replacement path above the checkpoint is REC-02.) Failure mode: a set chosen inside the epoch it governs makes L2 progress depend on an L1 transaction that can be censored or reorged — the chain either stalls unpredictably, or worse, two nodes judge one certificate under two different sets after a reorg, which is a set-substitution attack with no slashable party. Proven (premise: SYS-02, Ethereum-final L1 facts only)
CONS-14 — genesis, activation and the first epoch (1) Activation is a single L1 transaction against the existing Inbox (in-place upgrade, D3/MIG-01) which records in contract storage: chain_id, H_0 (the genesis L2 height H_genesis), the epoch schedule's offset e_0, the L1-side clock's anchor L1_0 — the L1 block at which epoch e_0 begins, which for the migration specified here is the L1 block in which the activation transaction is included — the L1-side epoch length EPOCH_LEN_L1 (CONS-13(1)), genesis_block_hash, genesis_state_root, L (CONS-13), and the first per-epoch mapping entry e_0 → (set_root(e_0), W_0). The transaction MUST compute the entry's setRoot itself, from the L1 staking contract's state at that block, with the MEM-08 encoding (MEM-08); it MUST NOT accept a caller-supplied root, a peer-supplied root or a proof-supplied root. This closes review round 3 finding R3A-01: the activation record now names the L1 block at which epoch 0 begins and the L1-side epoch length, so the L1-side clock of CONS-13(1) has an anchored origin instead of an assumed one. (2) Epoch e_0 covers heights [H_0, H_0 + L - 1]; epoch_of(H_0) = e_0, and the activation record fixes e_0 explicitly (for the migration specified here e_0 = 0, so the first epoch is epoch 0 and epoch 0 begins on the L1 side at L1_0); and the proposer of (H_0, 0) is determined by CONS-06's hash-based selection over the epoch-0 set (there is no special initial position: pos(H_0,0) is whatever the canonical hash yields). The genesis block is the anchor of epoch 0 and has no certificate: it is trusted only through the L1 activation record. (3) The first epoch's set is authenticated exactly like every later one: every header of epoch 0 carries validators_hash = set_root(0) and next_validators_hash = set_root(1), and a verifier checks both against L1 (CONS-10). For the first boundary the two-epoch schedule necessarily starts one epoch short, because there is no epoch e_0 - 1: the entry for epoch 1 MUST be committed and final during epoch 0, and from the boundary at h_last(0) onward the two-epoch lookahead of CONS-13(3) governs in full. If the entry for epoch 1 is missing, epoch 0 finalizes up to h_last(0) and the chain halts at the boundary, as in CONS-13(5). (4) No block of epoch 0 may be finalized before the activation transaction is Ethereum-final (SYS-02). A client MUST NOT start from a genesis record obtained only from a peer, and any bootstrap that starts later than H_0 requires an L1-authenticated checkpoint (MEM-11). An activation with n = 0 or W_0 = 0 is invalid: no proposer exists (CONS-06) and no quorum can form (CONS-03). (5) genesis_state_root is the root of the state migrated from the current Taiko chain; the migration boundary and the treatment of pending proposals, bonds and messages are MIG-01/MIG-03, not this page. Failure mode: a genesis or first set supplied out of band, or by a peer, lets an attacker start a node on a fabricated history that no L1 fact contradicts; a caller-supplied genesis set root is a direct set-substitution hole in the first epoch, which is the epoch with the least evidence available to a new node. Proven (premise: the activation transaction is Ethereum-final before epoch 0 finalizes)
CONS-15 — halt on loss of the assumptions (pointer to HALT) A correct validator halts rather than choosing between two histories. Halting means: stop signing (CONS-02); stop advancing the finalized checkpoint; keep serving the blocks, votes and certificates already held; publish the conflicting evidence assembled under CONS-11; and wait. It MUST NOT choose a branch, MUST NOT roll back, and MUST NOT delegate the choice to an operator. A client that replaces history on its own is not performing recovery: the only sanctioned way history above the last L1-accepted checkpoint may be replaced is the permissionless, bonded recovery of REC-02, which is invoked on L1 under its own trigger, bond, delay and cancellation rules, restores the L1-accepted checkpoint, and leaves no choice of checkpoint, validator set or configuration to any operator, DAO or invoker. The halt obligations themselves — what a halt is, restart rules, the consensus-enforced unsettled-depth cap, and the ban on an emergency operator, a DAO rescue and a data-first recovery — are stated once in HALT-01–HALT-04, and the recovery rules are stated once in REC-01–REC-03; a correct node never substitutes its own rollback for them. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — recovery is a permissionless, bonded L1 path, and CONS-15 points at the recovery rules as well as the halt rules.) The consensus conditions on this page that trigger a halt are: (1) two valid but conflicting commit certificates at one height (CONS-05, CONS-12); (2) a valid commit certificate for a block conflicting with the validator's own lock at a height that is not retired (CONS-04): a lock at a retired height is void (CONS-04(2)), so such a certificate is not a halt trigger on the strength of that lock, while a conflicting signed pair at a retired height remains admissible evidence (REC-02, ECON-04) (S5 §3: retired-height locks are void and are not halt or equivocation evidence); (3) an epoch boundary whose set commitment is missing or ambiguous (CONS-13(4)–(5)); (4) loss or corruption of the persistent sign-state store (CONS-02), where signing MUST stop and MUST NOT be restarted from an empty store; (5) exceeding the unsettled-depth cap (HALT-03); (6) any observation irreconcilable with CONS-08's epoch rule, such as two set_root(e) values or a certificate whose epoch field contradicts the epoch schedule. A halt is a legitimate outcome and not a defect: it is the honest response to a detected assumption failure (01 §5, class F1), and it never licenses an inference that some object does not exist (STATUS-10). Failure mode: an implementation that "resolves" a fork by choosing the longer, heavier or operator-preferred branch converts a detected assumption failure into a silent rollback — the exact violation of CONS-15 and HALT-04 this specification exists to prevent, since replacing history above the last accepted checkpoint is the business of the bonded recovery of REC-02 and of nothing else. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected — a client-side or operator-side rollback remains forbidden; only the rule-bound recovery path may replace unsettled history.) Proven (the halt action is unconditional; the triggers are assumption failures, whose avoidance is not claimed)
