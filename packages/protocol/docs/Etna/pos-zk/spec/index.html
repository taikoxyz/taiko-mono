Etna PoS + ZK — reference specification

Verdict (Phase 3, 2026-10-05; guarantee class revised by change order 04): a permissionless, TAIKO-staked L2 PoS consensus (Tendermint/CometBFT-class, single-slot finality) combined with ZK proofs of consensus finality and EVM execution can satisfy the requirements in 01-requirements-and-threat-model.md under the stated assumptions, in Mode B: the certificate and its evidence are unchanged, but a PoS confirmation above the last L1-accepted checkpoint is provisional — it MAY BE REPLACED by the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04 if settlement has stalled for T_STALL_GOV. At or below the last L1-accepted checkpoint no stall resolution, timeout, rotation, admission rule or published upgrade may change history, and a failure that the stall resolution does not cover still halts the chain rather than acting without a rule. Clearing a settlement stall depends on governance liveness with no protocol bound — the assumption A-GOV-2, recorded in LIM-01 — while the exit guarantee of MEM-15 is independent of it. This is a specification-level verdict: nothing here is implemented, benchmarked or deployed. (D-15: v1 has no permissionless recovery; the replacement is a rule-bound governance action.)

This is a PoS-sequenced validity rollup. Transaction order is determined by L2 PoS, not by Ethereum proposers. Ethereum provides data availability, proof verification, settlement, and bridge anchoring. It does not sequence L2 blocks.

Status labels are normative. Every deliverable — specification, learning site, user interface text — must use the exact labels of STATUS-01–STATUS-11. No stronger word ("final", "irreversible", "confirmed") may be substituted for a weaker status. Above the last L1-accepted checkpoint no confirmation is stronger than PoS-certified / provisional, and any interface that shows one must say that it can be replaced (STATUS-11), and no interface may promise that a stalled chain resumes without governance: that depends on the governance-liveness assumption A-GOV-2 (LIM-01). (D-15: the replacement is the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04.)

General rules

GEN-01 — what this document is This specification defines a protocol design. It contains no implementation, no deployment, and no measurement. No claim in any deliverable may describe a proposed parameter as a measured result, and no performance number may appear without a source or an explicit unmeasured tag. Failure mode: an unaudited design is mistaken for a working system; reviewers and users cannot tell which claims are evidence-backed. Proven derived
GEN-02 — fixed decisions bind the design D1–D7 are constraints. A design element that conflicts with one of them is a defect, not a tradeoff. Changing one requires a new user decision recorded in the decision log. Failure mode: silent relaxation of D5 or D7, which the user expressly forbade. A recorded project decision may relax or supersede a fixed decision only where the decision log records it and names what survives: D-11 relaxes D5 (data may be published before the proof; the checkpoint still advances only on a valid proof), D-12 supersedes D-6/D-10 (narrow forced inclusion over published data; what remains absent and deferred is the general inclusion list, not inclusion itself) and D-15 supersedes D-7 (the permissionless bonded recovery is withdrawn; the boundary of REC-01 is kept, and the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04 replaces the mechanism, which makes clearing a settlement stall depend on governance liveness — the assumption A-GOV-2). Any other change to D1–D7 is a defect. (D-11, D-12, D-15: the recorded relaxations and supersessions are named here so that GEN-02 and the decision log cannot diverge.) Proven
GEN-03 — one rule, one place, stable identifiers Every normative rule has exactly one stable identifier of the form PREFIX-NN, is stated in full on exactly one page, and is referenced elsewhere by link. Identifiers are never reused or renumbered; gaps are permitted. Failure mode: divergent copies of a rule; an implementer cannot tell which version is normative. Proven
GEN-04 — evidence tags Every rule carries Proven (argument with named premises), Assumed (justification plus the consequence of failure), or Open (the evidence that would close it). Every number carries derived, sourced, or unmeasured. Failure mode: assumptions are read as guarantees; unmeasured numbers are read as benchmarks. Proven
GEN-05 — hashing and domain separation Every protocol hash is keccak256 over a single canonical abi.encode of typed fields, never abi.encodePacked and never a bare concatenation, with a right-padded ASCII domain tag and the chain id as the first two fields. Every signed message carries its own domain tag; a signature is invalid outside its domain. Failure mode: cross-domain replay; second-preimage style ambiguity between concatenated fields. Proven (reused from prior Etna C8-R02/R03 and verified as a sound convention)
GEN-06 — time, and the ban on L1 lookahead All timing parameters are expressed in seconds or L1 block numbers. Consensus rounds, epoch numbers and validator-set versions are logical identifiers. No normative rule may depend on Ethereum proposer lookahead, on a fixed L1 slot duration, or on reading a wall clock during validation; wall clocks may only pace local signing and timeout actions. Failure mode: the protocol breaks when Ethereum changes its slot timing, or an implementer invents a timing rule the specification never fixed (R12). Proven
GEN-07 — controlled vocabulary Status labels are fixed by STATUS-01–STATUS-11; other terms are fixed by the glossary in 09. A document may not introduce a synonym for a status label or redefine a glossary term locally. Failure mode: the same word means different guarantees in different deliverables. Proven
GEN-08 — evidence categories for feasibility claims Every feasibility claim is classified as exactly one of: (1) compatibility in principle, (2) evidence from an existing implementation or published benchmark — with version, hardware, workload and methodology — (3) an analytical estimate, or (4) an unmeasured implementation question. Categories are never merged, and category (2) claims must state whether the measurement transfers to this workload. Failure mode: a benchmark for a different workload is presented as evidence for this one. Proven
GEN-09 — honest naming of the architecture The design is described as a PoS-sequenced validity rollup. The term based rollup must not be used for it, and no deliverable may imply that Ethereum proposers determine L2 transaction order. Failure mode: users infer an L1-sequencing guarantee the design does not provide (D4). Proven

Status labels

Each label below is normative: it fixes the evidence a participant must hold, the guarantee a user may rely on, the conditions that can revoke it, and who can observe it.

STATUS-01 — proposed A block body received from a leader. Evidence: the object itself. Guarantee: none — it may be invalid. Revocable: discarded freely. Visible to: whoever received it. Treating a received proposal as progress is the origin of most "we saw it first" disputes.Proven
STATUS-02 — locally execution-valid This node executed the block against a known parent state and every consensus and validity rule passed. Guarantee: this node will not build on an invalid block. Revocable: yes, if the parent is displaced. Visible to: this node only. Local validity is not agreement and not availability (STATUS-09).Proven
STATUS-03 — voted A validator signed a vote for this block at a given (height, round). Guarantee: the vote irreversibly constrains that validator's future votes through the lock rule (CONS-04). Revocable: never — a vote is a signed commitment; signing a conflicting vote is slashable equivocation (CONS-11). Votes are the only irreversible act a validator performs; everything else can be retried.Proven
STATUS-04 — PoS-certified / provisional A published commit certificate over (chain id, epoch, height, block hash) carrying signed voting power s that satisfies the predicate 3 * s > 2 * W (checked arithmetic, with W the epoch's total voting power; the L2 client, the zkVM guest and the L1 contract evaluate it identically, and a strict/non-strict mismatch would split chain acceptance from L1 acceptance), plus the receiver's own data-availability check for the block body. The certificate and its evidence are unchanged in every mode; the guarantee is that the block is certified by more than two thirds of the epoch's voting power under the published consensus rules, and that — above the last L1-accepted checkpoint — it MAY BE REPLACED by a completed stall resolution (GOV-04) if no batch extending the current checkpoint has been accepted for T_STALL_GOV; the exact trigger, timelock and effect are owned by REC-01 and GOV-04, which leave the latest L1-accepted checkpoint exactly as it is and discard everything above it wholesale: the action has no choice about which transactions inside that range survive, so a stall resolution cannot be used to pick winners. That is a statement about the discard, not a promise about the outcome: the discarded transactions are not replayed automatically and their inclusion is not guaranteed, so every party whose transaction was discarded must resubmit it, and the design does not promise that the same set of transactions will end up included; history at or below that checkpoint is never replaced. This closes review round 4 finding R4-MB-05: the wholesale discard is what the stall resolution enforces, and the resubmission consequence is stated instead of a non-selective outcome guarantee. For history at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it (STATUS-07). No timeout, rotation, admission rule, published upgrade or operator action may replace history at any height; only a completed stall resolution may, and only above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint. This status is never the first status a user may rely on as irreversible: STATUS-06 and above are. No artifact may call a confirmation at this status "final", "settled" or "irreversible", and every interface that shows a confirmation above the last L1-accepted checkpoint must disclose that it can be replaced (STATUS-11). Failure mode: the certificate is the same evidence in every mode, so a reader assumes the guarantee is the same. Above the checkpoint it is not: the design accepts a governance-gated replacement of that history — the timelock buys the exit window, but the trigger-to-queue wait is not protocol-bounded — and a user who treats the confirmation as settled can lose the outcome it describes. At or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint the same label is backed by the rule that no path — the stall resolution included — may change that history (REC-01), and at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it (STATUS-07). Proven (conditional on INV-01's premises) (D-15: the certificate and its evidence are unchanged, the guarantee is not; the replacement is the timelocked, resume-only stall resolution of GOV-04, and clearing a stall depends on governance liveness)
STATUS-05 — proof-ready A completed ZK proof bound to this batch's data commitment exists off-chain. Guarantee: the batch can be landed. Revocable: a proof of a different batch is not a substitute; a better proof of the same data commitment may replace it. Separating "a proof exists" from "the batch is settled" prevents premature withdrawal.Proven
STATUS-06 — accepted on L1 The Inbox accepted (data, proof) in one transaction and advanced its checkpoint (L1-01). Guarantee: the batch's data is public on Ethereum and the transition is L1-committed. Revocable: only by an L1 reorganisation below finality. This is the first status a user may rely on as irreversible: no L2 rule — including the stall resolution of GOV-04 — can revoke an accepted batch, and the only revocation is an L1 reorganisation below finality. It is the status that unlocks bridge withdrawals and message authentication. (D-15: STATUS-04 is provisional above the checkpoint; STATUS-06 and above are the first irreversible statuses, and the stall resolution touches nothing at or below an accepted batch)Proven
STATUS-07 — Ethereum-finalized The accepting L1 transaction is in a finalized Ethereum block, or the L1 fact the claim depends on is at the configured Ethereum finality depth (SYS-02). L2 state may only depend on L1 facts that can no longer be reorganised; otherwise a PoS-finalized L2 block can become unprovable. Proven
STATUS-08 — withdrawal-eligible Status STATUS-06 or STATUS-07, a withdrawal root at a height not below the signal's height — an epoch-boundary checkpoint carrying an aggregation proof that attests at least K_PROOF_BACKENDS distinct registered backend families (L1-13) — the message-authentication path, the delay required by MSG-03 measured from the root's l1BlockNumber, and the absence of an unexpired withdrawal veto; an ordinary checkpoint that is not a withdrawal root MUST NOT anchor a value release, and a veto delays but never cancels an already-proven withdrawal (MSG-04). Withdrawal availability is strictly later than L1 acceptance, and under D-13 the root costs one verification of an aggregated proof rather than k verifications; the two user-visible delays (up to one epoch plus the slowest inner proof, and up to T_VETO) are disclosed rather than hidden. (D-13: settlement and root attestation share the one-verification aggregation path; MSG-04's veto is a bounded delay, not a pause lever.) Proven
STATUS-09 — local receipt is not availability A node that holds a block may not treat its own possession as evidence that the data is available to others, and may not report a status above STATUS-03 to another party on the basis of local possession alone. Failure mode: users infer retrievability from a peer's claim; the certified-but-unavailable class of failures becomes invisible until a halt. Proven
STATUS-10 — an elapsed timeout is never evidence of absence No rule may conclude that a certificate, proof, or block does not exist because a timeout elapsed. Timeouts trigger local actions (view change, retry, halt), never an assertion about what exists elsewhere, and never an invalidation (HALT-01). Failure mode: a withheld-then-revealed certificate would invalidate a decision taken on the strength of a timeout. Proven
STATUS-11 — label disclosure obligation Any interface, document or learning page that shows a progress indicator must use these labels and must state which status is being shown. A "confirmation" shown to a user before STATUS-06 must be accompanied by the statement that the batch's data and proof are not yet on Ethereum. In addition, any interface that shows a confirmation above the last L1-accepted checkpoint must say that it can be replaced: the statement must name the sanctioned stall resolution (GOV-04), must not present the confirmation as final, settled or irreversible, and must not promise that a stalled chain resumes without governance (the assumption A-GOV-2); a status indicator that shows only "confirmed" without the replacement disclosure violates this rule. The protocol MUST expose, as public facts an interface can read: (i) the height of the latest L1-accepted checkpoint — the Inbox's current pointer lastLandedHeight and its record, not an Ethereum-final view (L1-06, L1-07); (ii) the resume point — lastLandedHeight + 1, derived from L1 state, with no stored retirement floor (GOV-04(c)); (iii) whether a stall-resolution entry is queued and when it was queued (govResumeState, govResumeQueuedAt, GOV-04(b)), so that the void-on-progress race — a batch accepted after queueing voids the pending entry — is observable; and (iv) whether the chain is currently halted for backpressure, which validators enforce and the consensus client exposes because no L1 contract can enforce the cap (HALT-03, LIVE-02); and (v) whether a withdrawal root exists for a height and, while a withdrawal veto is active, the timestamp at which it ends (L1-13, MSG-04), because an interface that shows a withdrawal must state the two additional delays of STATUS-08 and must not present a proven withdrawal as executable while an unexpired veto stands. (D-13: the root and its family count are public facts; MSG-04: the veto is public and self-expiring, so an interface can state exactly when it ends.) (D-13 and MSG-04 are also the reason STATUS-08 now names the root and the veto: the first irreversible status is unchanged, but what unlocks a release is not.) An interface MUST NOT present a confirmation above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint as final, and MUST be able to tell that a stall-resolution entry is queued, and MUST state both waits honestly — T_STALL_GOV is when an entry may be queued, not when one will be: otherwise the void-on-progress race is unobservable by rule and the replacement disclosure this rule requires cannot be made truthfully. This closes review round 4 findings R4-LIV-10 and STATUS-11: the boundary, the derived resume point, a queued entry with its queueing time and the backpressure halt are readable facts, so an interface can state replaceability, observe the void race and disclose the governance-liveness assumption (A-GOV-2). Failure mode: users treat a 2 s PoS confirmation as settlement (R14, D6 "user expectations"), now compounded by the fact that the confirmation can be discarded by the stall resolution, and by the fact that clearing a stall depends on governance liveness. Disclosure is the only protection the protocol gives a user who acts on a provisional outcome, because losses are not compensated (ECON-11). Proven (D-15: a confirmation above the last L1-accepted checkpoint must be disclosed as replaceable, and the governance-liveness assumption behind the replacement — A-GOV-2 — must be disclosed with it)

Verdict summary and its exact scope

Within scope: a specification-level design in which permissionless TAIKO-staked L2 validators certify blocks every 2 s; batches settle on Ethereum only when a ZK proof of consensus finality and execution lands and advances the checkpoint — the batch's data MAY have been published and recorded in an earlier L1 transaction under D-11, and a publication alone settles nothing — and that one proof object is an n-of-m aggregation the protocol verifies once (D-13); the shared SignalService/Bridge/vault addresses are preserved by in-place upgrade; no discretionary path can change history at any height and nothing at or below the latest L1-accepted checkpoint can be changed by recovery (at or below an Ethereum-finalized checkpoint even an L1 reorganisation cannot change it, STATUS-07); and above the latest L1-accepted checkpoint a confirmation is provisional, replaceable only by the permissionless, bonded and delayed recovery of REC-02. (user decision D-7: Mode B selected)

Outside scope: that the design is implemented, audited, benchmarked or safe to deploy; that proving throughput or 2 s cadence has been measured; that any attack is unprofitable; that ZK verification repairs failed consensus assumptions; that a PoS confirmation above the last L1-accepted checkpoint is irreversible (it is provisional); that the recovery of REC-02 is resistant to a cheaply induced settlement stall — that argument is not independently reviewed and no convergence is claimed — or that it compensates any loss it causes.
