## 2. Requests, pricing, bonds and refunds

### C3-R01. Two request shapes and the save

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `saveForcedInclusion(BlobReference)` is today's shape: exactly one blob, `kind = BLOB`, `dataHash` the versioned hash. `saveForcedInclusionCalldata(bytes manifest)` carries at most FI_CALLDATA_MAX bytes, `kind = CALLDATA`, `dataHash = keccak(manifest)`; the bytes live in L1 history forever and the request is eligible for FOCIL inclusion lists (C5). Both require `msg.value ≥ FI_BOND + FI_FEE × 4^(level + inProgress)` (C3-R02); `feeWei = msg.value − FI_BOND` is recorded as the fee, any excess being a bounty for the includer (today's refund call disappears). A new entry stores its requester (20 bytes), `feeWei` (`uint88`) and a kind byte (the kind in one bit, the status in two, the exponent `e` in five) in the one word it already writes for its version tag, and the unescalated fee it was priced at, `fiFeeWei = FI_FEE` at save time (`uint88`), beside `savedAt`, in place of the pending count the earlier text stored there (D18, B's note: refunds and credits use the fee stored per entry at save time, C3-R03), so recording the requester and the fee adds no storage; the bond is the constant FI_BOND, never stored per entry. `fiEntry(id)` normalises both layouts (§8); a legacy Shasta entry reads `dataHash = blobHashes[0]`, `savedAt = timestamp`, `feeWei = fiFeeWei = feeInGwei × 10^9`, `e = 0`, `kind = BLOB`, requester and bond zero. The queue is unbounded: a full ring would be a censorship lever. The fresh storage a new entry writes (about 98k to 196k state gas under EIP-8037) is a second anti-stuffing floor. (Page §1; the calldata shape adopted from candidate B, PR #22188; round 2, S8; round 6, R6H-1; round 6 fix pass.) **Adversary schedule:** an overpayment is a bounty, not a refund; a requester whose address reverts cannot block a landing, since every payout is pull (C3-R03); a legacy entry has no requester and so no refund.

### C3-R02. The price: quadratic in the backlog, escalated per stall, decaying per week

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The price is fixed at save time:

```text
FI_FEE     = FI_BASE_FEE × (FI_FEE_THRESHOLD + pending) / FI_FEE_THRESHOLD
required   = FI_BOND + FI_FEE × 4^(level + inProgress)                       (SKIP_ESCALATION = 4)
inProgress = 1 iff block.timestamp − fiClearTip ≥ LAND_WINDOW and the head entry is due, else 0
level      = max(0, runCount − ⌊(block.timestamp − runLastAt) / ESCALATION_DECAY⌋)
```

`runCount` and `runLastAt` are the escalation run (`FiStall`, §8): each stall is counted once, at the landing that burns its first void (C3-R09), which sets `runCount = level + 1` and `runLastAt` to its block time, so the level rises by one per stall and falls by one per ESCALATION_DECAY since the last stall. The kind byte keeps `e = level + inProgress`; `e ≤ 19` fits every payable fee, since `FI_FEE × 4^20` exceeds `2^88` wei and the ETH supply, so a save at `e ≥ 20` cannot be paid. `getForcedInclusionFee()` returns `required`. (Page §1; round 3, H1: without escalation a poison stream cost 0.02 ETH per day to re-arm; round 6, R6H-1: the price counts the stall in progress and the run decays rather than resets.) **Adversary schedule:** an attacker posts entries before any stall is visible; but an entry that survives a stall's void set and can seed the next was saved after `cut − FI_DELAY = clearTip + 14,100 s`, past `clearTip + LAND_WINDOW`, while the stall was in progress or after it was counted, so with stalls less than ESCALATION_DECAY apart the k-th seed pays at least `4^(k−1)` times the floor whatever was pre-posted; closer spacing buys nothing, a week's spacing returns each to the floor (§11); splitting a stall's voids over landings neither lowers nor inflates the count; nor can a requester buy the `inProgress` surcharge at a moment when the clocks disagree to its advantage, since the wall clock can only run ahead of the anchored clock, so the surcharge is charged early at worst and never avoided, and an honest requester charged early is refunded on consumption with content (C3-R03) (J's review of #22200 (2026-10-01), J-3).

**[proven: the two clocks of `inProgress`; the lag bound is derived]** `block.timestamp` is the L1 wall clock of the saving block and `fiClearTip` is an anchored L1 time, the timestamp of a past L1 block, so `block.timestamp ≥ fiClearTip` always holds (an L1 block's timestamp is at most the current block's). The comparison therefore over-approximates a stall by at most the anchoring lag, the age of the anchored time against the wall clock, which is bounded by ANCHOR_MAX_AGE plus the landing deadline: `ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX = 1,800 + 3,600 = 5,400 s` **(derived; the page states no number for this lag; LAND_WINDOW_MAX is C2 §12's constant, ANCHOR_MAX_AGE is C7's)**. The only error is early, never late: no requester can make the wall clock run behind the anchored clock, so the surcharge can be charged early but never avoided, and an honest requester charged early is refunded on consumption with content, as every surcharge is (C3-R03). The residual, that an honest requester fronts the surcharge up to the lag earlier than the anchored stall, is recorded in §12 (J's review of #22200 (2026-10-01), J-3).

### C3-R03. Bond, surcharge, refunds per entry and the two pull paths

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For every entry except a stall's first void, the escalation is a refundable deposit. Settlement is per entry id and irreversible (D18, B-C3-03): the two status bits of the entry's kind byte record it, `00` meaning unsettled; the consuming landing or forced batch settles an entry only if its status is `00`, writing a nonzero status (a warm modify of a word already read, no state growth), and no rule ever writes a status back toward `00` or from `11` to anything. Outcomes, with `s_i = fiFeeWei_i × (4^e − 1)` the surcharge from the fee stored at save time (C3-R01; D18, B's note):

| Outcome | Status bits | Includer's credit (ETH pull ledger, `withdrawFiFees`) | Requester's per-entry pull (`withdrawFiRefund(id)`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsettled: pending, or not yet consumed by any landing | `00` | nothing yet | nothing |
| Consumed with content, or voided by expiry | `01` | `feeWei − s_i` (the unescalated fee plus any overpayment, undiluted) | `FI_BOND + s_i` |
| Voided by a stall, not the stall's first void | `10` | nothing | `FI_BOND + feeWei` |
| The stall's first void (the first entry the stall's landing marks void, C3-R09 step 5) | `11`, written by the burn | nothing | nothing: bond and whole fee, surcharge included, burned |
| Claimed | `11`, written by a successful `withdrawFiRefund` | | paid once |
| Legacy Shasta entry (no requester, no bond) | as above | its fee as on expiry; burned if it is the first void | nothing |
| Entry requeued by C4's M2 (`feeWei = 0`) | as above | nothing | nothing; nothing burned |

The status moves only along `00 → 01`, `00 → 10`, `00 → 11` (burn), `01 → 11` and `10 → 11` (claim). `withdrawFiRefund(id)` requires status `01` or `10`, a new-layout entry and `msg.sender == requester`, pays and sets `11`; a status other than `00` is written only by a settlement, so an entry never consumed can never be claimed, and a burned entry (`11`) can be neither claimed nor burned again. The earlier condition `id < head` is dropped: a CONFLICT rewind (C2-R14) can move the head below a settled entry, whose refund stays claimable since its deposit was settled once. `withdrawFiFees(to)` drains the includer's one ledger slot; nothing from a voided entry reaches an includer, so a self-including attacker recovers only its own refunds. **Replay:** an entry consumed again after a CONFLICT head rewind (C2-R14), on the first restart or any later one, is re-executed (its block derives from the entry's bytes and the chain, C3-R07, so the derivation needs no status) but is not settled again: no credit, no refund, no burn, no status write. The sequencer of the replaying range earns no fee for an already settled entry; that lost fee is the accepted cost of the invariant (D18). (Page §1, §5, §8; round 6, R6H-1; round 6 fix pass: a proportional split gave the includer a `4^(−e)` share of an overpayment, and a per-requester ledger would have written 64 fresh slots, about 6.3 M state gas, per landing; D18 for settlement and the stored fee; errors `NotRefundable`, `NotRequester`.) **[proven: one settlement per deposit, on every restart]** A settlement requires status `00` and writes a nonzero status in the same step; the only other writer is `withdrawFiRefund`, which requires `01` or `10` and writes `11`; no rule writes `00`, and C2-R14's rewind moves the head and the clock, not the status bits. So each deposit is credited, made refundable or burned at most once, and paid out at most once, however many times its id is consumed. **Adversary schedule:** an attacker gets the surcharge back on every entry except a stall's first void, exactly the entry §11 prices, so the refund lowers no stall's cost; an honest request never pays the escalation but fronts it, about `0.001 × 4^level` ETH (about 4 ETH after six stalls), refunded minutes later; B's CONFLICT trace (deposit 0.051 ETH, consumed, both credits withdrawn, head rewound and the entry consumed again) credits and refunds nothing the second time (vector C3-T12); a DAO change of FI_BASE_FEE or FI_FEE_THRESHOLD between save and consumption changes no credit or refund, both reading `fiFeeWei_i`.

## 3. One clock: due-ness, the cursor, the stall clock and the void set

### C3-R04. Due-ness and expiry at the anchored L1 time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For an entry `i` and an L1 timestamp `T`:

```text
due(i, T)  := i < tail(T) ∧ savedAt_i + FI_DELAY ≤ T
void(i, T) := (kind_i = BLOB ∧ savedAt_i + FI_EXPIRY ≤ T) ∨ stalled(i, T)        (stalled: C3-R06)
```

`T` is always the timestamp of an L1 block whose hash is pinned: the parent block's anchor at preconfirmation time (C3-R08), the same anchored headers the guest hash-chains to the tip (C3-R10), the landing's verified anchor tip at the inbox (C3-R09), a forced batch's anchor (C3-R11). Never a landing's own timestamp. Entries are FIFO with non-decreasing save times, so the due entries form a prefix; under a stall every entry due at the cut is void, otherwise only expired blob entries are. Calldata entries never expire. No finality assumption is needed: every entry counted at an anchor sits in an ancestor of that anchor; a reorg that removes the entry removes the anchor, which already invalidates the block. (Page §2; round 1, F4 for the clock; the claim-36 lever of the current protocol, where due-ness at the landing's timestamp let anyone make a proven landing revert.) **Adversary schedule:** a third party saves an entry after a proof is made: its save time is later than every anchor of the range, so no block relied on it (C3-R09 step 4); an entry becoming due while blocks are in flight is a non-event for them.

### C3-R05. The derived cursor and the stall clock

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Every block has a cursor, derived and never stored: after a landing, `cursor = head`; for block `h` with parent `p`, `cursor(h) = cursor(p) + 1` if `kind(h) ∈ {FI, FORCED}`, else `cursor(p)`. Every block also has a run, the number of consecutive kind FI blocks ending at it: `run(h) = run(p) + 1` if `kind(h) = FI`, else `0`; after a landing `run = fiRun`, the value the inbox stored for `lastLanded` (C3-R09 step 6: the end block's run; a forced batch stores zero, C3-R11 (7); C4's M3 sets it to zero at `drainedAt`, the genesis child not being kind FI; a CONFLICT restart, C2-R14, sets it to MAX_FI_RUN, so the first block after a restart is a sequencer block with a fresh anchor, since the run of the restored `lastLanded` is not kept per checkpoint). The run is stored because the guest chains from `lastLanded`'s header and sees no earlier block, so without it a range could begin a fresh run behind a run the previous landing ended; C3-R08 reads it (D17). Beside them the stall clock `clearTip` is derived, starting at the stored `fiClearTip`. With `T_p` the parent's anchor time and `cut(p) = clearTip(p) + FI_SKIP`:

```text
clearTip(h) = T_p        if no entry at cursor(h) is due at T_p;
            = cut(p)     else if T_p ≥ cut(p) and no entry at cursor(h) is due at cut(p)   (the void set is used up);
            = clearTip(p) otherwise.
```

It is read at the parent's anchor, like V8, so every sequencer block V8 admits because nothing is due moves it, and what a range assumes about the first entry it does not consume, that it is not due (moving the clock) or that it is due (holding it), is exactly the pair of inequalities the inbox checks (C3-R09 step 4: `fiNotDueAt` and `fiDueAt`, D18). Inside a forced batch the recursion is not applied block by block: the batch has one cut, its parent's, and the clock after it is this recursion applied once at its last block (C3-R11 (4) and (7), D18). It is the latest parent-anchor time at which a block's cursor had no due entry, or the cut of a stall whose void set is used up; a block, hence a landing, that leaves a due entry of the current void set unconsumed does not move it; being a function of the chain, how ranges are split into landings never changes a block's content. The inbox stores it at each landing from the journal's `fiClearTipAfter`; at `drainedAt` it is set to the drain block's timestamp, so the queue is clear at the fork and the run starts at level zero (C4's M3; a clock left at zero would have made the first Etna block look four hours into a stall); after a CONFLICT resolution C2-R14 sets it to the restart time. Nodes read it from the last `ForcedInclusionsConsumed` event. (Page §2, §4, §10; issue #22147; round 6, R6H-1; C4's M3.) **Adversary schedule:** splitting ranges changes nothing, for the run as for the cursor, since the inbox stores the end block's run and the guest starts from it; a chain that keeps consuming its due entries keeps resetting the clock, so a live chain voids nothing unless its backlog never clears for FI_SKIP; a prover that asserts a nonexistent or not yet due entry to be due, to hold the clock and void an honest entry under a false cut later (B-C3-01), is refused at the inbox (C3-R09 step 4, vector C3-T10).

### C3-R06. Void by stall: the cut, the void set and what is burned

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For a block whose parent `p` has `T_p ≥ cut(p)`, `stalled(i, T_p)` holds for every entry due at `cut(p)`, at any cursor position and not only at the stored head. Such an entry derives to the empty FI block (C3-R07) without reading its payload and counts as consumed for L8 and for the cursor; the stall's first void loses its bond and fee, the others are refunded (C3-R03). The void set is fixed at the cut, not at `T_p`: an entry that becomes due after the cut is served with content, or, if it is itself unprovable, starts the next stall on the restarted clock. Up to MAX_FI_PER_LANDING are consumed per landing; a landing that leaves entries of the void set does not move `fiClearTip`, so the next landing keeps voiding: a pre-posted stream of `N` entries costs one FI_SKIP plus `⌈N / 64⌉` landings, not `N` stalls. The gate is the stall clock, never an entry's own age: an entry that waited hours in a backlog is never voided while some block catches up with the due prefix at least once every FI_SKIP, and a backlog that never clears for FI_SKIP, which only saturating stuffing produces (§11), is a stall too. The protocol cannot tell a poison entry from an honest entry that sat at the head through a four-hour outage and burns the bond and fee of either; honest entries due at a cut are voided, refunded and must re-post, §11's bound applying to the re-post. Expiry takes precedence over a stall (page §8; D18, B's note; §13 item 2 closed): an entry that is void by expiry at the evaluating block's `T_p` (C3-R04) takes the expiry outcome (status `01`, C3-R03) even if it is due at the cut; it is not marked in `fiStallMask` and so can be neither the stall's first void nor a refundable stall void; the stall's first void is the first entry the mask marks. (Page §2, §12; round 1, F4; round 2, F5; round 4, R4H-2 refuted on a premise a pre-posted unprovable stream removes; round 6, R6H-1 for voiding the whole due prefix at the cut.) **Adversary schedule:** any number of unprovable entries posted at any spacing before the trigger are voided together, so pre-posting buys no further stall; an entry posted to keep a stall going (saved after `cut − FI_DELAY`) is not voided by it, so a stuffer cannot hold a stall open with refunded entries: its later entries are served with content at their unescalated fees. The judge's trace of round 6 (two pre-posted entries due together, no landable range) is closed by C3-R08's waiver, this void set and C3-R09's cap, which J re-checked (D12; §13).

## 4. FI blocks and placement

### C3-R07. FI block content (F3)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** An FI block for entry `i` with parent `p`: `parentHash = p.hash`, `timestamp = p.timestamp + 1`, `parentBeaconBlockRoot` and `PH.anchorNumber` equal to the parent's (the anchor never advances in an FI block), `extraData.kind = FI (3)`, `coinbase` the parent's (includer-independent), `gasLimit = FI_GAS_LIMIT` with nothing reserved on top because no protocol transaction runs in it, transactions the decoded manifest's in order with invalid ones skipped, as today. An undecodable payload and a void entry each yield the empty FI block with the same header fields and no transactions; a void entry yields it without reading the payload. Term and view are the sequencer's (the seat follows from them) and the sequencer signs it like any block; it may sit inside a holder view or a sentinel view 255, never view 254 (C7's H3: FORCED iff view 254). ### C3-R12. Resumption, the forced ring and races

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A forced landing voids exactly what a replacement voids, and only when replacement is already permitted: the certified blocks above `lastLanded` whose term passed the deadline machinery without landing. The current holder, or a dead-mode sequencer, resumes on the forced head with a RESUME view change (S2-R15) and re-includes the voided transactions; the RESUME `l1Ref` is defined once in S2-R09 and checked by S3d against the forced ring (`forcedRecord(uint8)`: first height, L1 block number and time of each forced landing, FORCED_RING slots overwritten; round 5, R5S-3). Races: a late original, a REPLACE fork, a dead-mode landing and a forced batch all target `lastLanded`; whichever lands first wins, the loser invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape, else about 25k gas with no blob fee on the forced side (C2-R20). Nobody is penalized by losing such a race: round 4 removed the abandonment penalty and the resumer's waiver (R4H-5, R4-C3), because a certified view is landable by anyone from the holder's reserve and no rule reads the absence of a landing (I5, S3). (Page §7; limitation L6.) **Adversary schedule:** a forcer gains nothing but its own transaction's ordering; one reorg of the backlog per stall, bounded by the grace period (L6).

## 7. Fees, proving budget, expiry, FOCIL and the attester policy

### C3-R13. Fees are ETH, pull-only, to the includer

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Ordinary landing: the includer is the sequencer of the range holding the FI block (it surrendered a one-second slot; the lander is already paid per block in TAIKO, C2-R16). Forced landing: `rewardTo`. In both cases the includer receives the fee less the surcharge (C3-R03); the only refunds are the requester's, each pulled per entry. No split and no push. (Page §8; round 6 fix pass.) **Adversary schedule:** a payee that reverts blocks nothing; nothing reaches an includer from a voided entry.

### C3-R14. The proving budget

**[assumed: proposed rule]** FI blocks execute under the per-block zk-gas limit of C7's V10 and additionally under FI_ZK_GAS_LIMIT, a lower per-FI-block budget the guest checks, so the proving cost of an FI block is bounded by construction; a guest completeness bug inside that budget is the residual the stall rule covers. (Page §8.) **Adversary schedule:** proving grief costs the attacker one entry's price per FI block; an entry no guest can prove within the budget is a stall seed, priced by C3-R02.

### C3-R15. Blob expiry and retention

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Nodes and provers fetch the payload on the save event and retain it until consumed. Calldata entries never expire; only a stall voids them. A blob entry consumed at anchor `a` satisfies `savedAt + FI_EXPIRY > T(a)`, and a landing consuming it must occur within the landing horizon of the range's first term (C2: the smaller of ROLE_HORIZON, 34 h, and the EIP-2935 window, 27 h at 12-s slots and 4.55 h at 2-s slots; a range older than it is unlandable), so its proof is generated within `savedAt + FI_EXPIRY + 34 h`, under 9 days, inside retention under A-RETAIN. The page's `ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE = 5,700 s` (its "7 d + 95 min") is when the forced path opens, not a bound on the consuming landing: under C2-R09 the original landing stays valid until a replacement or forced batch lands, which only the horizon bounds (§13 item 5). An entry reaches FI_EXPIRY only if no landing of two or more blocks happened for seven days; then it derives to the empty FI block with the expiry outcome of C3-R03, which takes precedence over a stall for an expired entry. No owner call replaces today's queue reset. (Page §8; round 6, R6H-1.) **Adversary schedule:** after a week-long outage the head entry voids to an empty block and the requester re-posts, as calldata if it wants permanence (TH7 blob expiry).

### C3-R16. FOCIL legs (the C5 seam)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** After Hegotá the protected legs are calldata requests, the blob-free forced landing (about 3 KiB, about 1.2 M gas, inside FOCIL's remaining-gas rule), slashing evidence and fee withdrawals; not protected are blob-carrying requests and every ordinary landing. A user who fears L1-builder censorship uses the calldata shape, and then the whole escalation leg is inclusion-list eligible; FI_CALLDATA_MAX is half of FOCIL's inclusion-list cap. Before Hegotá the floor is T1 and FI_DELAY absorbs ordinary inclusion latency. (Page §8, §15; limitation L12.) **Adversary schedule:** L1 censorship of a blob-shape request beyond T1 is not defended (L12).

### C3-R17. The attester anchor-lag policy (recommended, not validity)

**[assumed: proposed policy]** While a node's own L1 view holds a due, unconsumed entry, it withholds attestation from a sequencer block whose anchor timestamp is more than FI_ANCHOR_LAG older than its own L1 head (in seconds, slot-independent; as 20 L1 blocks it fell below ANCHOR_MIN_AGE at 2-s slots, round 5 fix pass). An attester that withholds holds no attested block of the view, so after TIMEOUT it signs a timeout (S2-R07): the stale holder is taken over within seconds and pays MISS (S3), unless it first gets one block attested by part of the committee and then stops (S2-R07 (d)), keeping its view to term end without MISS while the entry waits one term; either way the next holder inherits the cursor. §11's bound does not rely on it. (Page §8; adoption is an open question of the page, §12.) **Adversary schedule:** a colluding quorum ignores the policy; a sequencer with a slow L1 node is timed out (false positive, §11).

## 11. Argument and adversary coverage

**[proven: the inclusion bound, conditional on T1, T3, A-PROVER, A-A1 and A-V]** Honest path: an entry saved at `s` is due at every L1 block with timestamp at least `s + 300`; the first such block is anchored by an L2 block at or after `s + 348`, the FI block follows one second later and is attested within the round trip (about `s + 6 min`), landed 2 to 3 minutes later. Compliant adversary (stale anchors within ANCHOR_MAX_AGE, deadline landings, a colluding quorum): the first term starting at or after `s + FI_DELAY + ANCHOR_MAX_AGE` has `T_floor ≥ s + FI_DELAY` for every landing of its blocks (C3-R09 step 2; at most one TERM of grid alignment and one TERM to its end, the 120 s of §10), so its landings must consume the entry and must land by its end plus `LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE`, else the forced path opens; with the one deferral the latest is `s + 6,720 s`, or the forced path plus proving and inclusion (about 4 min). The bound uses only the inbox count and A1, never certification timing, so a view that produces nothing for a term (S2-R07 (d)) or a timed-out stale holder (C3-R17) stays inside it, with one exception D22 item 5 introduces: the inbox count of a term whose chain is a single waived first block is `F = 0` (C3-R09 step 2), so such a term's landing need not consume the entry, and the bound then holds for the first term, at or after the one above, whose chain holds a block after its waived first block (its second block is forced FI while the entry is due, so its landing must consume it). It holds for every entry not due at a stall's cut; such an entry is voided and refunded, and the bound applies to its re-post. (Page §9, §12; round 3, C3; round 5, R5-C3.) The page's timeline in seconds after the save: `300` due; `348` anchored, `349` the FI block; about `530` landed on the honest path; `2,160` the first term whose landings must consume it begins; at most `6,120` it lands, `6,720` with the deferral, or the forced path by about `6,960`; absent pipeline, with the page's assumption that the last landing was at `−100` (100 s before the save), the batch is buildable at about `2,100` and lands at about `2,350`, the same 2,160 plus about 180 s of §10 measured from the last landing.

**[proven: the stall economics, conditional on A-FEES and C3-R02, C3-R06, C3-R09]** A stall voids entries only once no block has caught up with its due entries for FI_SKIP of anchored time; the void set is fixed at the cut, so entries posted to hold a stall open are served with content; a pre-posted stream of `N` unprovable entries ends after one FI_SKIP plus `⌈N/64⌉` landings, the void landing always satisfiable. The only entry that can seed the next stall was saved after the previous cut minus FI_DELAY, priced with that stall in progress or already counted, so with stalls less than ESCALATION_DECAY apart the k-th costs its poster at least `0.05 + 0.001 × 4^(k−1)` ETH whatever it pre-posts; such a run lasts about `1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH)` stalls of about four hours, and beyond it the rate is at most one stall at the floor price per ESCALATION_DECAY, until the DAO ships a guest fix, never a prerequisite for liveness. (Page §12, §16; round 6, R6H-1.)

**Accepted Medium (D12).** The residual of the stall rule, accepted by J's re-check as a Medium, is carried here with its numbers: a stall is a landing delay of about four hours (FI_SKIP, C3-R06) on every landing path, ordinary, replacement, forced and dead mode alike; it is repeatable at the floor price once per ESCALATION_DECAY (one stall a week at `0.05 + 0.001` ETH, C3-R02) and priced geometrically within a week (the k-th stall of a run at least `0.05 + 0.001 × 4^(k−1)` ETH, §10's stall seed cost row); honest entries due at a cut are voided, refunded (C3-R03) and must be re-posted, the inclusion bound applying to the re-post. Its disposition depends on the journal fields `fiStallMask`, `fiStallClock` and `fiNotDueAt`, and since D18 `fiDueAt`, being implemented exactly as C3-R09 and C3-R10 write them, and on settlement being per entry id (C3-R03); C6 carries the limitation under its own id (§13 item 3).

**[open: the residual of the TERM_END, FALLBACK and sentinel waiver (D22 item 5; §13 item 18)]** A holder that takes the TERM_END waiver, has its one NORMAL block certified and then stops, with at least `m − Q(m) + 1` attesters, colluders or honest, having attested it (11 at `m = 32` under `Q(32) = 22`) so that no TIMEOUT forms (S2-R07 (d)), or with a colluding quorum, ends its term with a landable one-block chain that consumes nothing; its landing is timely, so A1's replacement window and with it the forced path never open (C2-R08, C2-R09, C3-R11 (1)), and the next term's first block is waivable again (its TERM_END lock is that block, stamped before `termEnd − 1`). A due entry is therefore delayed by one term for each consecutive term held this way, with no bound on the count of such terms; the attacker forfeits each term's revenue after its first block. The sentinel case of the waiver (C3-R08, §13 item 18 (c)) opens the same residual in open-empty and no-committee terms, where no certificate or TIMEOUT is involved: a bonded sequencer lands a one-block sentinel chain under the waiver, timely, with `F = 0`, and the residual there is per landing, not per term: S1-R16's object changes with `lastLanded`, so the next chain's first block is waivable again, in the same term as in the next. A bonded sequencer that wins each landing race can land successive one-block sentinel chains with `F = 0` inside one open-empty term, each landing moving `lastLanded` and so invalidating a competing honest chain (a waived NORMAL first block followed by the FI block) built on the old `lastLanded`. Any bonded party may instead land a longer chain first, which C2's first-landing rule admits, but only by winning that landing race; an attacker that wins every race delays the entry throughout open-empty terms. The per-opening drain claims and the 40-FI ceiling are unaffected, since each extra waiver only lowers the FI count. A candidate closure within C3 is §13 item 18 (c)'s, for the arbiter. Before D22 the same holder had no valid first block while an entry was due (C7-8's halt), so production stopped and the forced path opened instead. The 6,720-s bound is therefore conditional on a term within it whose holder produces a second block, or on the forced path; closing the residual needs a rule outside C3's (a TIMEOUT that S2-R07 (d) cannot suppress, or a landing rule in C2 that refuses a range whose waived first block ends a term while an entry is due), for the arbiter.

**[assumed, argued here and not on the page, for J (§13 item 10): the compounded delay of an honest entry; to be promoted on J's verdict]** An honest entry re-posted after a cut is served within the inclusion bound unless a new seed, saved after `cut − FI_DELAY` and due before it, is at the head; each such seed is one priced stall, so an honest entry's total delay under an attacker with budget `B` is at most about `(1 + log4(B / 0.001 ETH)) × FI_SKIP` plus one inclusion bound within a run, and one FI_SKIP plus the bound per week beyond it.

**[proven: an FI becoming due never invalidates an in-flight block]** Due-ness is read at the parent's anchor (C3-R08); a block's parent anchor is fixed when the block is built. **[proven: a forced batch voids only what a replacement voids and only when a replacement is permitted]** C3-R11 (1) and (2) are C2-R08 and C2-R09's conditions. **[proven: a void entry never needs its blob]** C3-R06 and C3-R07 derive the empty block without the payload.
