## 2. Requests, pricing, bonds and refunds

### C3-R01. Two request shapes and the save

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `saveForcedInclusion(BlobReference)` is today's shape: exactly one blob, `kind = BLOB`, `dataHash` the versioned hash. `saveForcedInclusionCalldata(bytes manifest)` carries at most FI_CALLDATA_MAX bytes, `kind = CALLDATA`, `dataHash = keccak(manifest)`; the bytes live in L1 history forever and the request is eligible for FOCIL inclusion lists (C5). Both require `msg.value ≥ FI_BOND + FI_FEE × 4^(level + inProgress)` (C3-R02); `feeWei = msg.value − FI_BOND` is recorded as the fee, any excess being a bounty for the includer (today's refund call disappears). A new entry stores its requester (20 bytes), `feeWei` (`uint88`) and a kind byte (the kind in one bit, the status in two, the exponent `e` in five) in the one word it already writes for its version tag, and the pending count it was priced at (`uint24`) beside `savedAt`, so recording the requester adds no storage; the bond is the constant FI_BOND, never stored per entry. `fiEntry(id)` normalises both layouts (§8); a legacy Shasta entry reads `dataHash = blobHashes[0]`, `savedAt = timestamp`, `feeWei = feeInGwei × 10^9`, `kind = BLOB`, requester and bond zero. The queue is unbounded: a full ring would be a censorship lever. The fresh storage a new entry writes (about 98k to 196k state gas under EIP-8037) is a second anti-stuffing floor. (Page §1; the calldata shape adopted from candidate B, PR #22188; round 2, S8; round 6, R6H-1; round 6 fix pass.) **Adversary schedule:** an overpayment is a bounty, not a refund; a requester whose address reverts cannot block a landing, since every payout is pull (C3-R03); a legacy entry has no requester and so no refund.

### C3-R02. The price: quadratic in the backlog, escalated per stall, decaying per week

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The price is fixed at save time:

```text
FI_FEE     = FI_BASE_FEE × (FI_FEE_THRESHOLD + pending) / FI_FEE_THRESHOLD
required   = FI_BOND + FI_FEE × 4^(level + inProgress)                       (SKIP_ESCALATION = 4)
inProgress = 1 iff block.timestamp − fiClearTip ≥ LAND_WINDOW and the head entry is due, else 0
level      = max(0, runCount − ⌊(block.timestamp − runLastAt) / ESCALATION_DECAY⌋)
```

`runCount` and `runLastAt` are the escalation run (`FiStall`, §8): each stall is counted once, at the landing that burns its first void (C3-R09), which sets `runCount = level + 1` and `runLastAt` to its block time, so the level rises by one per stall and falls by one per ESCALATION_DECAY since the last stall. The kind byte keeps `e = level + inProgress`; `e ≤ 19` fits every payable fee, since `FI_FEE × 4^20` exceeds `2^88` wei and the ETH supply, so a save at `e ≥ 20` cannot be paid. `getForcedInclusionFee()` returns `required`. (Page §1; round 3, H1: without escalation a poison stream cost 0.02 ETH per day to re-arm; round 6, R6H-1: the price counts the stall in progress and the run decays rather than resets.) **Adversary schedule:** an attacker posts entries before any stall is visible; but an entry that survives a stall's void set and can seed the next was saved after `cut − FI_DELAY = clearTip + 14,100 s`, past `clearTip + LAND_WINDOW`, while the stall was in progress or after it was counted, so with stalls less than ESCALATION_DECAY apart the k-th seed pays at least `4^(k−1)` times the floor whatever was pre-posted; closer spacing buys nothing, a week's spacing returns each to the floor (§11); splitting a stall's voids over landings neither lowers nor inflates the count.

### C3-R03. Bond, surcharge, refunds per entry and the two pull paths

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For every entry except a stall's first void, the escalation is a refundable deposit. Outcomes, written by the consuming landing or forced batch into the two status bits of the entry's kind byte (a warm modify of a word already read, no state growth):

| Outcome | Status bits | Includer's credit (ETH pull ledger, `withdrawFiFees`) | Requester's per-entry pull (`withdrawFiRefund(id)`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumed with content, or voided by expiry | `01` | `feeWei − FI_FEE × (4^e − 1)` (the unescalated fee plus any overpayment, undiluted) | `FI_BOND + FI_FEE × (4^e − 1)` |
| Voided by a stall, not the stall's first void | `10` | nothing | `FI_BOND + feeWei` |
| The stall's first void (the entry at the stored head when the stall stopped every landing at it) | `00`, nothing written | nothing | nothing: bond and whole fee, surcharge included, burned |
| Claimed | `11` | | set by a successful `withdrawFiRefund` |
| Pending (id at or above the head) | `00` | | nothing |
| Legacy Shasta entry (no requester, no bond) | as above | its fee as on expiry; burned if it is the first void | nothing |
| Entry requeued by C4's M2 (`feeWei = 0`) | as above | nothing | nothing; nothing burned |

`withdrawFiRefund(id)` requires `id < head`, status `01` or `10`, a new-layout entry and `msg.sender == requester`, pays and sets `11`; a burn writes nothing, so an entry never consumed can never be claimed. `withdrawFiFees(to)` drains the includer's one ledger slot; nothing from a voided entry reaches an includer, so a self-including attacker recovers only its own refunds. `FI_FEE` is recomputed at consumption from the stored pending count. (Page §1, §5, §8; round 6, R6H-1; round 6 fix pass: a proportional split gave the includer a `4^(−e)` share of an overpayment, and a per-requester ledger would have written 64 fresh slots, about 6.3 M state gas, per landing; errors `NotRefundable`, `NotRequester`.) **Adversary schedule:** an attacker gets the surcharge back on every entry except a stall's first void, exactly the entry §11 prices, so the refund lowers no stall's cost; an honest request never pays the escalation but fronts it, about `0.001 × 4^level` ETH (about 4 ETH after six stalls), refunded minutes later.

## 3. One clock: due-ness, the cursor, the stall clock and the void set

### C3-R04. Due-ness and expiry at the anchored L1 time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For an entry `i` and an L1 timestamp `T`:

```text
due(i, T)  := i < tail(T) ∧ savedAt_i + FI_DELAY ≤ T
void(i, T) := (kind_i = BLOB ∧ savedAt_i + FI_EXPIRY ≤ T) ∨ stalled(i, T)        (stalled: C3-R06)
```

`T` is always the timestamp of an L1 block whose hash is pinned: the parent block's anchor at preconfirmation time (C3-R08), the same anchored headers the guest hash-chains to the tip (C3-R10), the landing's verified anchor tip at the inbox (C3-R09), a forced batch's anchor (C3-R11). Never a landing's own timestamp. Entries are FIFO with non-decreasing save times, so the due entries form a prefix; under a stall every entry due at the cut is void, otherwise only expired blob entries are. Calldata entries never expire. No finality assumption is needed: every entry counted at an anchor sits in an ancestor of that anchor; a reorg that removes the entry removes the anchor, which already invalidates the block. (Page §2; round 1, F4 for the clock; the claim-36 lever of the current protocol, where due-ness at the landing's timestamp let anyone make a proven landing revert.) **Adversary schedule:** a third party saves an entry after a proof is made: its save time is later than every anchor of the range, so no block relied on it (C3-R09 step 4); an entry becoming due while blocks are in flight is a non-event for them.

### C3-R05. The derived cursor and the stall clock

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Every block has a cursor, derived and never stored: after a landing, `cursor = head`; for block `h` with parent `p`, `cursor(h) = cursor(p) + 1` if `kind(h) ∈ {FI, FORCED}`, else `cursor(p)`. Beside it the stall clock `clearTip` is derived, starting at the stored `fiClearTip`. With `T_p` the parent's anchor time and `cut(p) = clearTip(p) + FI_SKIP`:

```text
clearTip(h) = T_p        if no entry at cursor(h) is due at T_p;
            = cut(p)     else if T_p ≥ cut(p) and no entry at cursor(h) is due at cut(p)   (the void set is used up);
            = clearTip(p) otherwise.
```

It is read at the parent's anchor, like V8, so every sequencer block V8 admits because nothing is due moves it, and what a range assumes about the first entry it does not consume is exactly the inequality the inbox checks (C3-R09 step 4). It is the latest parent-anchor time at which a block's cursor had no due entry, or the cut of a stall whose void set is used up; a block, hence a landing, that leaves a due entry of the current void set unconsumed does not move it; being a function of the chain, how ranges are split into landings never changes a block's content. The inbox stores it at each landing from the journal's `fiClearTipAfter`; at `drainedAt` it is set to the drain block's timestamp, so the queue is clear at the fork and the run starts at level zero (C4's M3; a clock left at zero would have made the first Etna block look four hours into a stall); after a CONFLICT resolution C2-R14 sets it to the restart time. Nodes read it from the last `ForcedInclusionsConsumed` event. (Page §2, §4, §10; issue #22147; round 6, R6H-1; C4's M3.) **Adversary schedule:** splitting ranges changes nothing; a chain that keeps consuming its due entries keeps resetting the clock, so a live chain voids nothing unless its backlog never clears for FI_SKIP.

### C3-R06. Void by stall: the cut, the void set and what is burned

**[assumed: proposed rule]** For a block whose parent `p` has `T_p ≥ cut(p)`, `stalled(i, T_p)` holds for every entry due at `cut(p)`, at any cursor position and not only at the stored head. Such an entry derives to the empty FI block (C3-R07) without reading its payload and counts as consumed for L8 and for the cursor; the stall's first void loses its bond and fee, the others are refunded (C3-R03). The void set is fixed at the cut, not at `T_p`: an entry that becomes due after the cut is served with content, or, if it is itself unprovable, starts the next stall on the restarted clock. Up to MAX_FI_PER_LANDING are consumed per landing; a landing that leaves entries of the void set does not move `fiClearTip`, so the next landing keeps voiding: a pre-posted stream of `N` entries costs one FI_SKIP plus `⌈N / 64⌉` landings, not `N` stalls. The gate is the stall clock, never an entry's own age: an entry that waited hours in a backlog is never voided while some block catches up with the due prefix at least once every FI_SKIP, and a backlog that never clears for FI_SKIP, which only saturating stuffing produces (§11), is a stall too. The protocol cannot tell a poison entry from an honest entry that sat at the head through a four-hour outage and burns the bond and fee of either; honest entries due at a cut are voided, refunded and must re-post, §11's bound applying to the re-post. For an expired entry the expiry outcome takes precedence (page §8; §13 item 2). (Page §2, §12; round 1, F4; round 2, F5; round 4, R4H-2 refuted on a premise a pre-posted unprovable stream removes; round 6, R6H-1 for voiding the whole due prefix at the cut.) **Adversary schedule:** any number of unprovable entries posted at any spacing before the trigger are voided together, so pre-posting buys no further stall; an entry posted to keep a stall going (saved after `cut − FI_DELAY`) is not voided by it, so a stuffer cannot hold a stall open with refunded entries: its later entries are served with content at their unescalated fees. The judge's trace of round 6 (two pre-posted entries due together, no landable range) is closed by C3-R08's waiver, this void set and C3-R09's cap, which J re-checked (D12; §13).

## 4. FI blocks and placement

### C3-R07. FI block content (F3)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** An FI block for entry `i` with parent `p`: `parentHash = p.hash`, `timestamp = p.timestamp + 1`, `parentBeaconBlockRoot` and `PH.anchorNumber` equal to the parent's (the anchor never advances in an FI block), `extraData.kind = FI (3)`, `coinbase` the parent's (includer-independent), `gasLimit = FI_GAS_LIMIT` with nothing reserved on top because no protocol transaction runs in it, transactions the decoded manifest's in order with invalid ones skipped, as today. An undecodable payload and a void entry each yield the empty FI block with the same header fields and no transactions; a void entry yields it without reading the payload. Term and view are the sequencer's (the seat follows from them) and the sequencer signs it like any block; it may sit inside a holder view or a sentinel view 255, never view 254 (C7's H3: FORCED iff view 254). Its canonical blob record is `headerCore ‖ seqSig`, with the view change if it opens a view, and no `txListBytes`: the transactions derive from the entry's payload witness, which hashes to `dataHash` (C3-R10), so the record is about 250 bytes and no FI block adds a blob to any landing (C2-R02). (Page §3; issue #22147; round 6 fix pass for the payload-free record.) **[proven: content is includer-independent given the parent, the term, the view and the opening object]** Every header field is the parent's, a constant or FI_GAS_LIMIT, except the three the sequencer's position fixes: `termId`, `view` and, when the block opens a view, `vcHash`; the transaction list is a function of the entry's bytes. So two honest builders in one `(term, view, opening object)` produce one block, and C2's blob encoding of it is a function of the certified chain.

### C3-R08. The placement rule V8 and the first-block waiver

**[assumed: proposed rule, stated here once; C7-R11 names the conjunct and its evaluation step and cites this rule for its content]** With `T_p = L1[anchor(p)].timestamp` and the cursor of C3-R05:

- **FI block (kind FI):** valid iff the entry at the parent's cursor is due at `T_p`, the anchor equals the parent's and the content is per C3-R07; its cursor is the parent's plus one.
- **Sequencer block (kind NORMAL):** valid iff no entry is due at the parent's cursor at `T_p`, or the MAX_FI_RUN = 2 blocks ending at `p` are both FI blocks. A sequencer block may advance the anchor freely; the obligation the new anchor creates binds from the next block.
- **Genesis child (kind GENESIS_CHILD):** the first Etna block is a sequencer block exempt from the obligation; its parent's anchor is the drain block recorded at `drainedAt` (C4's M3) and the obligation binds from its child.
- **First-block waiver:** the first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change, or of a sentinel dead-mode view, may be a sequencer block even if an entry is due; the obligation binds from its second block. For RESUME it lets the re-included transactions of the voided range take the state before any forced transaction saved after them; for REPLACE and dead mode it lets a fork from `lastLanded` advance its anchor past `fiClearTip + FI_SKIP` before the obligation binds, so the blocks after it void a poison prefix instead of carrying it. Keyed on the opening object, it is used at most once per opening, and openings are scarce and gated (a REPLACE needs A1 and Q lock votes, a RESUME follows a landed forced or replacement batch, a dead-mode opening object changes once per term after DEAD_TERMS without a landing), so an honest entry waits at most one second per opening. L8's count is unchanged, so the inclusion bound does not move.

Consequences: an entry becoming due while blocks are in flight binds the first block after the anchor advances past its due time; nothing is ever reorged by an FI; a backlog drains at two blocks in three; the obligation crosses term and takeover boundaries with the cursor, so an FI block may be the first or second block of a view or term, carrying that sequencer's term and view bytes and, if it opens the view, its view change (C3-R07). What C3 owns of the term budget: FI blocks are about 250-byte records, at most `2/3 × 60 = 40` a term; their exemption from the byte bound is C7's V10, their reward and exposure treatment S3-R13 and S3-R15. (Page §4; replaces the former V5(e); round 2, S3; round 4, R4H-6; round 6, R6H-1 for the REPLACE and dead-mode waiver; issue #22147.) **Adversary schedule:** a sequencer that skips a due entry produces a block no honest attester attests (S2-R04 requires V8) and the guest refuses (C3-R10); using the waiver twice needs two openings; a stuffer holds honest users to one block in three (L8).

## 5. The inbox check and the guest

### C3-R09. The inbox check at landing (C2's L8, normative here)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** In order, for a range of `n` blocks whose first segment has term `termId`:

1. `fiHeadBefore == head`; `fiHeadAfter ≤ tail`; consumed count `c = fiHeadAfter − fiHeadBefore ≤ MAX_FI_PER_LANDING`.
2. `T_floor = max(lastLanded.anchorTipTimestamp, termStart(termId) − ANCHOR_MAX_AGE − 1)`, except that a void-by-stall landing, one whose first FI block is evaluated at or after the cut so that the journal marks its first consumed entry void by stall, uses `min(that, fiClearTip + FI_SKIP)`, capping the floor at the cut. `D` = the number of entries due at `T_floor`, walking at most 64. Require `c ≥ min(D, ⌈2n/3⌉)`, else `FiCountTooLow`. Every consumed entry is due at the landing's anchor tip timestamp.
3. `fiConsumedHash = keccak(chainId ‖ head ‖ c ‖ (i, kind, dataHash, savedAt) for each consumed entry)` from storage, exported to the journal; it reads only entries, which never change once saved.
4. For the first entry the range does not consume, the journal exports `fiNotDueAt` (`X`): the highest parent-anchor time at which a block of the range relied on no entry being due at a cursor equal to `fiHeadAfter` (V8 admitting a sequencer block, or the stall clock moving), or the cut when the range claims a stall's void set used up with its cursor there, whichever is later, and zero if neither. Require the stored entry at `fiHeadAfter`, if any, to have `savedAt + FI_DELAY > X`, else `FiNotDue`; save times are non-decreasing, so no later entry is due at `X` either.
5. Effects. Fees of entries consumed with content or voided by expiry are credited, less each entry's surcharge, to the pull ledger of the range's sequencer, and each such entry's status written `01`. Entries the journal marks void by stall (`fiStallMask`, bit `j` for entry `fiHeadBefore + j`) are written `10`, except the first marked entry when the journal's `fiStallClock` (the `clearTip` under which they were voided, constant within a stall) differs from the stored `burnedFor`: its bond and whole fee are burned (the ETH leaves in this landing, to the destination S3-R20 names, by a push that cannot revert), nothing is written, `burnedFor` is set to that stall clock, and the stall is counted (`runCount = level + 1`, `runLastAt = block.timestamp`). A stall is thus burned and counted exactly once, however a lander splits its voids into landings. Legacy and requeued entries as in C3-R03.
6. `head = fiHeadAfter`; `fiClearTip = fiClearTipAfter`, the end block's `clearTip` from the journal, whose reliance on the entry at `fiHeadAfter` not being due is step 4's check; `lastLanded.anchorTipTimestamp` updated (C2-R06).

(Page §5; landing page L8; round 6, R6H-1 for the cap, the mask, the stall clock and `fiNotDueAt`; round 6 fix pass for the per-entry status.) **[proven: step 2 is exact and satisfiable, conditional on A-V]** Every block in the range has a timestamp at or after its term's start (V4) and V5 bounds a parent's anchor to within ANCHOR_MAX_AGE of the parent's timestamp; the first block's parent is `lastLanded`, whose anchor tip is the other arm of the max; anchors are monotone, so every parent anchor in the range is at least `T_floor`. Entries due at `T_floor` are due at every block of the range, and V8 forces at least `⌈2n/3⌉` FI blocks in any valid stretch that starts on an FI block. A stall-voided entry counts as consumed, so a range that voids a poison prefix meets the count exactly as one that includes it, and a range whose first block is waived meets it by ending after the FI blocks that follow. The cap at the cut keeps a void-by-stall landing satisfiable: without it, `T_floor` at the start of a REPLACE view's term can lie past the cut, so `D` would count an entry due after the cut, outside the void set and, if unprovable, includable by no range; with it `D` counts only the void set and the range [the waived first block, the empty FI blocks of the void set] has `c = D ≤ 64`. The cap applies only where the guest has proved the first FI block voids at or after the cut, and V8 still binds every block (step 4), so no landing can use it to skip an entry that was due. (The page words the parent bound as "at most one second less" than the child; §13 item 6.) **[proven: no third party can make a proven landing revert through the queue]** `T_floor` is storage plus the chain's own term id; the counted entries were saved at least FI_DELAY before it; the consumed hash reads entries that never change; the head moves only by a landing on this parent; fees and refunds are pull; the walk is bounded; and the first unconsumed entry is checked by an inequality that an entry saved after the proof always passes. (Round 6, R6H-1: the previous design put that entry's save time in the hash.) **Adversary schedule:** a landing that skips a due entry fails step 2, its blocks unlandable and replaceable with no penalty (TH7); an unprovable entry saved to fall due one second after the cut cannot block the void landing and becomes the next stall, priced with this one in progress (C3-R02).

### C3-R10. What the guest checks

**[assumed: proposed rule]** From the consumed-hash preimage and the anchored headers it already verifies (C2-R04), the guest checks C3-R07 and C3-R08 for every block of the range with the cursor starting at `fiHeadBefore` and the stall clock at `fiClearTipBefore`, both from inbox storage through the journal: each FI block's payload witness hashes to its `dataHash` (the KZG versioned hash for blobs, keccak for calldata; void entries need no witness); in a block whose parent's anchor has reached the parent's cut, every entry due at that cut is voided and marked in `fiStallMask`, the parent's `clearTip` exported as `fiStallClock`; each sequencer block satisfies V8 against the consumed entries' save times while the cursor is below `fiHeadAfter`; FI blocks execute under FI_ZK_GAS_LIMIT (C3-R14); and the journal's eight queue fields equal the recomputed values. Once the cursor reaches `fiHeadAfter` the guest takes as input whether the entry there is due: every "not due" it relies on enters `fiNotDueAt`, which the inbox checks against storage, while a "due" can only end the range there or hold the clock until the next block V8 admits. FI_SKIP is a `protocolParams()` constant (C2-R04), so a guest with another value yields no valid proof. (Page §5; round 6, R6H-1.) **Adversary schedule:** a sequencer that omits, reorders or alters an FI's transactions produces an end hash different from the certified one or a journal the inbox rejects: unlandable either way.

## 6. The forced batch, resumption and races

### C3-R11. The forced batch (F7)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A landing through the same `land` with sentinel view FORCED = 254, sequencer address zero, empty certificate, no view changes and no blobs. Armed (`forcedBatchArmed()`) when `block.timestamp > replaceableFrom()` and the head is due at the latest L1 block; back to serving at the next ordinary landing. ### C3-R12. Resumption, the forced ring and races

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A forced landing voids exactly what a replacement voids, and only when replacement is already permitted: the certified blocks above `lastLanded` whose term passed the deadline machinery without landing. The current holder, or a dead-mode sequencer, resumes on the forced head with a RESUME view change (S2-R15) and re-includes the voided transactions; the RESUME `l1Ref` is defined once in S2-R09 and checked by S3d against the forced ring (`forcedRecord(uint8)`: first height, L1 block number and time of each forced landing, FORCED_RING slots overwritten; round 5, R5S-3). Races: a late original, a REPLACE fork, a dead-mode landing and a forced batch all target `lastLanded`; whichever lands first wins, the loser invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape, else about 25k gas with no blob fee on the forced side (C2-R20). Nobody is penalized by losing such a race: round 4 removed the abandonment penalty and the resumer's waiver (R4H-5, R4-C3), because a certified view is landable by anyone from the holder's reserve and no rule reads the absence of a landing (I5, S3). (Page §7; limitation L6.) **Adversary schedule:** a forcer gains nothing but its own transaction's ordering; one reorg of the backlog per stall, bounded by the grace period (L6).

## 7. Fees, proving budget, expiry, FOCIL and the attester policy

### C3-R13. Fees are ETH, pull-only, to the includer

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Ordinary landing: the includer is the sequencer of the range holding the FI block (it surrendered a one-second slot; the lander is already paid per block in TAIKO, C2-R16). Forced landing: `rewardTo`. In both cases the includer receives the fee less the surcharge (C3-R03); the only refunds are the requester's, each pulled per entry. No split and no push. (Page §8; round 6 fix pass.) **Adversary schedule:** a payee that reverts blocks nothing; nothing reaches an includer from a voided entry.

### C3-R14. The proving budget

**[assumed: proposed rule]** FI blocks execute under the per-block zk-gas limit of C7's V10 and additionally under FI_ZK_GAS_LIMIT, a lower per-FI-block budget the guest checks, so the proving cost of an FI block is bounded by construction; a guest completeness bug inside that budget is the residual the stall rule covers. (Page §8.) **Adversary schedule:** proving grief costs the attacker one entry's price per FI block; an entry no guest can prove within the budget is a stall seed, priced by C3-R02.

### C3-R15. Blob expiry and retention

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Nodes and provers fetch the payload on the save event and retain it until consumed. Calldata entries never expire; only a stall voids them. A blob entry consumed at anchor `a` satisfies `savedAt + FI_EXPIRY > T(a)`, and a landing consuming it must occur within the landing horizon of the range's first term (C2: the smaller of ROLE_HORIZON, 34 h, and the EIP-2935 window, 27 h at 12-s slots and 4.55 h at 2-s slots; a range older than it is unlandable), so its proof is generated within `savedAt + FI_EXPIRY + 34 h`, under 9 days, inside retention under A-RETAIN. The page's `ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE = 5,700 s` (its "7 d + 95 min") is when the forced path opens, not a bound on the consuming landing: under C2-R09 the original landing stays valid until a replacement or forced batch lands, which only the horizon bounds (§13 item 5). An entry reaches FI_EXPIRY only if no landing of two or more blocks happened for seven days; then it derives to the empty FI block with the expiry outcome of C3-R03, which takes precedence over a stall for an expired entry. No owner call replaces today's queue reset. (Page §8; round 6, R6H-1.) **Adversary schedule:** after a week-long outage the head entry voids to an empty block and the requester re-posts, as calldata if it wants permanence (TH7 blob expiry).

### C3-R16. FOCIL legs (the C5 seam)

**[assumed: proposed rule]** After Hegotá the protected legs are calldata requests, the blob-free forced landing (about 3 KiB, about 1.2 M gas, inside FOCIL's remaining-gas rule), slashing evidence and fee withdrawals; not protected are blob-carrying requests and every ordinary landing. A user who fears L1-builder censorship uses the calldata shape, and then the whole escalation leg is inclusion-list eligible; FI_CALLDATA_MAX is half of FOCIL's inclusion-list cap. Before Hegotá the floor is T1 and FI_DELAY absorbs ordinary inclusion latency. (Page §8, §15; limitation L12.) **Adversary schedule:** L1 censorship of a blob-shape request beyond T1 is not defended (L12).

### C3-R17. The attester anchor-lag policy (recommended, not validity)

**[assumed: proposed policy]** While a node's own L1 view holds a due, unconsumed entry, it withholds attestation from a sequencer block whose anchor timestamp is more than FI_ANCHOR_LAG older than its own L1 head (in seconds, slot-independent; as 20 L1 blocks it fell below ANCHOR_MIN_AGE at 2-s slots, round 5 fix pass). An attester that withholds holds no attested block of the view, so after TIMEOUT it signs a timeout (S2-R07): the stale holder is taken over within seconds and pays MISS (S3), unless it first gets one block attested by part of the committee and then stops (S2-R07 (d)), keeping its view to term end without MISS while the entry waits one term; either way the next holder inherits the cursor. §11's bound does not rely on it. (Page §8; adoption is an open question of the page, §12.) **Adversary schedule:** a colluding quorum ignores the policy; a sequencer with a slow L1 node is timed out (false positive, §11).

## 11. Argument and adversary coverage

**[proven: the inclusion bound, conditional on T1, T3, A-PROVER, A-A1 and A-V]** Honest path: an entry saved at `s` is due at every L1 block with timestamp at least `s + 300`; the first such block is anchored by an L2 block at or after `s + 348`, the FI block follows one second later and is attested within the round trip (about `s + 6 min`), landed 2 to 3 minutes later. **[assumed: failure profile]** Requesters all offline: an empty queue; all malicious: stuffing and unprovable entries bounded and priced, undecodable manifests derive to empty blocks, no landing made to revert, the worst a requester loses its own bond and fee. Sequencers: holder offline, takeover with the cursor inherited; all offline or malicious past the predicate, forced batches serve the queue and dead mode ordinary traffic; they cannot skip a due entry (V8 at attesters, the count at the inbox, V8 in the guest). Attesters fewer than Q: forced batches carry no certificate; all malicious: skipping chains are unlandable, staleness bounded. Provers and landers all offline: inclusion is conditional on A-PROVER; blob entries expire after seven days, calldata never. L1 builders: FOCIL after Hegotá, else T1. DAO: never needed. (Page §13.)

**[assumed: false positives and false negatives, page §14]** An attester that cannot fetch a blob payload within FI_DELAY withholds, and 11 of 32 doing so blank the honest term without penalty (S2-R07); no false negative, due-ness being a function of pinned data. A colluding quorum defers entries saved after `T_floor − FI_DELAY` by at most one landing. A resumer's fresh chain can lose the parent race to a forced batch, and after a chain-wide proving outage a forced batch may void an honest holder's certified blocks that did include the FIs (no penalty, revenue lost); a deadline landing delays every entry by the full window, the floor rather than an escape. A blob entry saved just before a 7-day outage is voided. Honest entries due at a cut are voided and refunded; one at the head through a four-hour outage loses bond and fee; a poison poster recovers everything but each stall's first void. Every requester fronts the escalation while the level is above zero. A sequencer with a slow L1 node is timed out under C3-R17; a colluding quorum ignores the policy. Under saturating stuffing honest users get one block in three.
