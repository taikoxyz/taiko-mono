## 2. Time, the snapshot and invariant I1

### S1-R01. The term grid and its origin

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `termOf(τ) = ⌊(τ − T0) / TERM⌋`, `termStart(t) = T0 + TERM × t`, `termEnd(t) = termStart(t + 1)`; `cycleStart(c) = termStart(CYCLE_TERMS × c)`, the grid's constants are §11's. T0 is zero until the DAO's second upgrade-class call `armEtna(T0)` with `T0 ≥ block.timestamp + MIGRATION_LEAD` sets it once; the upgrade transaction itself calls `initEtna` without T0, so the DAO reads the domain count and, off-chain, the owner structure before fixing the grid (round 4, R4-C5; C4 owns the migration sequence and its reading of the launch condition, S1-R13). No slot, epoch, beacon root, lookahead or `blockhash` is read anywhere in this section (R5). **Adversary schedule:** a grid fixed before the registry exists would start in no-committee mode; the DAO arms it after reading the count. Source: sequencing page §1; interfaces page `EtnaConfig`, `armEtna`.

### S1-R02. Future-dated effect (Rule D), the snapshot and invariant I1

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A registry write at L1 time `τ` changes eligibility only for instants `T ≥ τ + DELAY`: DELAY_REG for entry, exit and key rotation, the only inputs that change `E_t`, a rank or a key read, and for the hard-floor suspension and `reactivate()`; DELAY_S for class A and class B suspensions and reserve lapses, which change no rank (round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2; round 6, R6S-2). Term `t` evaluates eligibility at the single instant `termStart(t)` and reads the version of the domain tree effective at `termStart(t)` (S1-R10). `S(t)` is the highest L1 block with timestamp at most `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`: a node replays registry events and the mix log up to `S(t)`; under a total L1 halt the schedule stays defined. **Invariant I1, owned by S1:** for every term inside ROLE_HORIZON, the holders, committee, eligibility, seed and domain count the inbox computes at landing time equal what nodes computed at `termStart(t)`, whatever registry writes became effective in between; dead mode (S1-R17) is the stated exception and only adds rights. **Adversary schedule:** a write placed just after `S(t)` to change term `t` at landing time; by Rule D it takes effect after `termEnd(t)`, so neither view includes it. Source: sequencing page §1, §2 and §3; round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass.

**[proven: node snapshot equals landing-time computation, conditional on A-T1]** A write in a block after `S(t)` has timestamp above `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, so its effect falls at or after `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK + DELAY_S = termStart(t) + 2 × TERM > termEnd(t)` for the shortest delay (DELAY_S = LOOKBACK + 2 × TERM, §11), and later for DELAY_REG. Any write whose effect falls after `termStart(t)` creates a later version (S1-R10) and copies, never changes, the nodes it shares with the version effective at `termStart(t)`, so the inbox reads at landing exactly the version a node read at `S(t)`. The flag reads of S1-R09 are dated at least DELAY_S after their write, so nodes and the inbox read the same flag for every term. Under A-T1 a reorg below LOOKBACK does not occur, which is what "every input to term `t` is at least 20 minutes old at term start" protects. Source: sequencing page §3 and §16, claims 1 and 2.

## 3. Seats and the entry capital

### S1-R03. The seat array, its bound and the cap

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Seats live in an append-only array of at most MAX_SEATS records (a `uint16` index, §11), in the upgraded Inbox proxy after its 43-slot gap, with the logic in a linked library so the landing path reads the registry without an external call and the existing TAIKO bond ledger is reused (R2: no new address; the syncer address stays the Inbox proxy). An owner holds at most CAP seats. Indices are contract-assigned: FREE recycled holes first (a seat whose `activeUntil + RECYCLE_GRACE ≤ now`), then appends at the scheduled length, so a same-block append by an incumbent never reverts an entrant's transaction (round 2). The array is dense: its length equals the number of seats ever claimed. A slot is written fresh once, when its seat is first claimed, and only overwritten afterwards. **Adversary schedule:** exclusion by filling the array costs the entry capital of S1-R04 for every seat (§11: 2.32 billion TAIKO, above A-SUPPLY); crowding an entrant's append is impossible because indices are assigned by the contract. Source: sequencing page §2 and §11; interfaces page `Seat`; parameters page MAX_SEATS, CAP, RECYCLE_GRACE.

### S1-R04. Registration and the entry capital

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `register(count)` requires a ledger balance of at least `1.05 × (seatCount + count) × B_SEAT + (seatCount + count) × LAND_RESERVE`, a registered sequencer key and attester key (S1-R05), and no pending exit. It assigns `count` indices (S1-R03), sets each seat's `activeFrom = τ + DELAY_REG`, writes one domain-tree version per seat (S1-R10) and emits `SeatClaimed` and `DomainWrite`. The three parts of the capital are S3's quantities and S1 names only their roles: seat stake `seatCount × B_SEAT` (the slashable floor), the 5 % buffer above it (where liveness debits land before seat stake), and the landing reserve `seatCount × LAND_RESERVE` (the seam to S3; its ledger properties are C2-R17's and S3's). No admission decision exists. **Adversary schedule:** a key already bound (refused, S1-R05); a registration timed to grind a seed (effective DELAY_REG later, after the cycle's last affecting instant, S1-R11); a mass registration (priced per seat, S1-R06). Source: sequencing page §2; roles page §1 Entry; round 4, R4H-3; round 6, R6H-3.

### S1-R05. Keys: proof of control, proof of possession, validity intervals

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `registerKeys(seqKey, seqKeySig, blsPubkey, pop)` binds to the calling owner a sequencer key (a secp256k1 address with no code, S3 rule S8) whose signature `seqKeySig` is over `keccak(DOMAIN_KEYREG ‖ chainId ‖ owner)`, a proof of control, and a BLS12-381 attester key with a proof of possession over `(pubkey, owner, chainId)`; a key already bound anywhere in the registry is refused (`BadProofOfPossession`, `BadKeyControlSignature`, `KeyHasCode`). Each key an owner ever binds is a `KeyRecord{keyHash, validFrom, validUntil}`: `validFrom = τ + DELAY_REG` of its binding, `validUntil` the instant the next key of that kind takes effect, open for the current key. `rotateKey(kind, newKey, pop)` appends a record valid from `τ + DELAY_REG` and closes the old key's interval at the same instant; for a sequencer key the `pop` is its DOMAIN_KEYREG signature; the old key stays liable until `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW`. Every read of an owner's key for term `t` (C7's V1, `isHolder`, the committee walk, `committeeRoot[t]`, `keyOwnerAt`) takes the key valid at `termStart(t)`, so a rotation effective after a term starts, up to ROLE_HORIZON before its landing, changes nothing for that term (round 6, R6S-4). **Adversary schedule:** a front-run of a victim's `registerKeys` fails, lacking the key's signature over the victim's address (round 6, judge's note); a rotation inside a landing window to change the pinned root (R6S-4); a BLS key without possession, a framing vector for S2's certificate-pair rule (slashing page §1). Source: interfaces page §1 `KeyRecord`, `registerKeys`, `rotateKey`; slashing page §1; roles page §1 Entry.

### S1-R06. DELAY_REG, MIN_TENURE and the eligibility-input rule

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Entry and exit are the only inputs to the rank domain, both owner-triggered writes dated `τ + DELAY_REG`. `requestExit` is accepted for a seat only once `τ + DELAY_REG ≥ activeFrom + MIN_TENURE`, so every seat is in the domain, drawn and bled for at least a week and every domain write takes effect exactly at `τ + DELAY_REG`, monotone in transaction order (round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass). Churn is priced, not capped: no rule limits writes per minute, each pays its own tree version (§11), and no incumbent can delay or crowd out an entrant (round 5 fix pass; the per-slot cap is §15's). Suspensions of every kind are per-owner flags read only after a draw (S1-R09); none changes `E_t` or a rank. **Adversary schedule:** register-and-exit churn to dilute others (sortition is over registered seats only, S1-R12, so it dilutes nobody; round 3, H5); a self-inflicted suspension after the seed is fixed (voids only the owner's own draws, S1-R09). Source: sequencing page §2 and §5; parameters page §1; round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2.

## 4. Exits

### S1-R07. Owner-requested exit and release

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `requestExit(seats)` takes any count of the caller's seats (round 5, R5-C4), each a domain write under S1-R06's tenure rule, sets each seat's `activeUntil = τ + DELAY_REG`, emits `ExitRequested` and `DomainWrite`, and keeps the seat in the domain until then. `finalizeExit(seats)` releases seat stake and reserve together once `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` has passed for the last seat and the last key's `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` has passed (roles page §1 Exit); while any seat is active or pending, withdrawal is limited to the excess over `1.05 × seatCount × B_SEAT + seatCount × LAND_RESERVE`, and the reserve leaves the ledger only through this path (round 4, R4H-3; the ledger rules are S3's). An expired seat becomes FREE after RECYCLE_GRACE (S1-R03). **Adversary schedule:** exit then equivocate (the stake is held through EVIDENCE_WINDOW, S3); exit to dodge a draw (effective two hours later, after the last instant a write affects the cycle, S1-R11). Source: sequencing page §2 and §10; interfaces page `requestExit`, `finalizeExit`.

### S1-R08. Permissionless exits: `exitSlashed` and `exitStale`

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Anyone may call `exitSlashed(owner, seats)` for an owner slashed under a class-A rule (S3), and `exitStale(owner, seats)` for an owner continuously suspended or lapsed (S1-R09) for longer than STALE_EXIT without reactivating (`NotStale` otherwise). Each is a domain write like `requestExit`: effective `τ + DELAY_REG`, subject to the same tenure rule, one version per seat, so it moves no rank after a seed is fixed. An owner that tops up and reactivates within the week is never exited; a class A owner cannot reactivate (S1-R09). **Adversary schedule:** seats of a slashed owner left in the domain as permanent void entries (round 5 fix pass); seats parked in an open-ended suspension (a class A slash, a hard-floor suspension, a class B balance left below the floor, a reserve lapse) that shrink every committee they are drawn into (S2-R02, limitation L16); the parked seats leave the domain a week plus DELAY_REG after they park, and parking sits those seats in no committee, lowering the parker's own chances (round 6, R6-C1). Source: sequencing page §5; interfaces page `exitSlashed`, `exitStale`; parameters page STALE_EXIT; limitation L16.

## 5. Suspension and reactivation flags

### S1-R09. The two-interval store, `eligible`, and what sets the flag

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Each operator record holds two suspension intervals `[s1, e1)`, `[s2, e2)`. `suspended(owner, T)` is true iff `T` lies in one of them; `eligible(i, T) := inDomain(i, T) ∧ ¬suspended(owner(i), T)` is what a drawn seat must satisfy to hold a view or fill a committee slot; the flag is read only after the draw and is never an input to the rank domain (round 5, R5S-2). The intervals hold, as flags whose debits and thresholds S3 owns:

- a **class B suspension** (the third structural strike within STRIKE_DECAY, S3-R05): `[τ + DELAY_S, τ + DELAY_S + SUSPEND(n))` for the n-th strike, SUSPEND(n) in §11; a class B debit that leaves the ledger below `seatCount × B_SEAT` instead opens an **open-ended** interval closed only by a top-up plus `reactivate()`, whose effect is dated `τ' + DELAY_REG`;
- a **class A slash** (S3-R05: the whole ledger): an open-ended interval from `τ + DELAY_S`, left only by exit (`exitSlashed`, S1-R08), never by `reactivate()` (§13 item 12);
- a **reserve lapse** (reserve below one term of exposure per seat, S3's line): from `τ + DELAY_S`, open until a top-up to the required level plus `reactivate()`, and never shorter than SUSPEND(3) (round 6, R6S-2), so the closing instant is `max(τ + DELAY_S + SUSPEND(3), τ' + DELAY_REG)`;
- the **hard floor** (ledger below 50 % of `seatCount × B_SEAT` after MISS debits, the only liveness-driven suspension): from `τ + DELAY_REG`, open until top-up plus `reactivate()` at `τ' + DELAY_REG`; DELAY_REG-dated because the crossing MISS is applied at a landing whose timing anyone, the owner included, can choose.

`reactivate()` requires the ledger at the registration requirement of S1-R04 and closes the open interval at `τ' + DELAY_REG`; `Suspended`, `ReserveLapsed` and `Reactivated` carry the interval bounds. A suspended owner's seats stay in the domain: drawn as a holder they are a void view, skipped without any timeout (S1-R12); drawn as a committee slot they are an empty slot (S1-R13). **Adversary schedule:** an owner that reports itself or launders its reserve after a seed is fixed voids only its own views and empties only its own slots; no other owner's rank moves (R5S-2, R6S-2); a third party timing the owner's crossing by landing its late terms: the same. Source: sequencing page §2, §7 and §10; interfaces page `Operator`, events; parameters page DELAY_S, SUSPEND(3); round 4, R4-C2; round 6, R6S-2.

**[open: the two-interval store never drops a live interval]** The pages assert this (sequencing page §7 and §16, claim 3; parameters page SUSPEND(3) row; round 4, R4-C2) with the sizing `SUSPEND(3) ≥ ROLE_HORIZON + DELAY_S` (§11) as the only support, and no page states what a third write does while two intervals are live. The sizing alone does not establish it: a class B suspension at `τ`, a reserve lapse at `τ + 100` and a hard-floor or class A interval at `τ + 200` are three live intervals of one owner, and a longer minimum length makes such overlap more likely, not less. Closure: the page or C8 names the rule for a third write, for instance that a write while an interval is open extends or merges that interval instead of adding one (§13 item 13); S1-V03 carries the three-write case.

**[proven: no suspension moves a rank]** `inDomain` reads `activeFrom` and `activeUntil` only; both are written by the DELAY_REG-dated entry and exit paths of S1-R04, S1-R07 and S1-R08 and by nothing else; the flag enters `eligible` after the draw. So an owner write of any kind after a seed is fixed changes which seats a committee draws only by exit, which is effective DELAY_REG later and after the cycle's last affecting instant (S1-R11). Source: sequencing page §2 and §16; round 5, R5S-2.

## 6. The persistent domain tree

### S1-R10. Versions tagged by effective time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The contract keeps the domain as a persistent (versioned) segment tree over seat ids whose leaves are `inDomain` bits and whose nodes hold counts. Every domain write (an entry or an exit; nothing else, since suspensions and lapses are flags) creates a new version by path copying, about 16 fresh nodes per seat, tagged with its effective time `E = τ + DELAY_REG` and logged as `DomainVersion{effectiveTime, root, count}` with a `DomainWrite(version, seat, effectiveTime, delta)` event. Effective times are monotone in transaction order (S1-R06), so the log is sorted with no reordering. `domainVersionAt(T)` is the latest version with `E ≤ T`, a binary search of the log; term `t` reads `domainVersionAt(termStart(t))`, `domainCount(t)` its count `E_t` and `seatAtRank(t, r)` a descent in it. A version is immutable once written, and every version with `E ≤ termStart(t)` exists from `termStart(t) − DELAY_REG` on. No mutation ring, correction set, lazy application or per-slot cap exists: a walk reads one version and its cost does not depend on how many writes happened since the term started. **Adversary schedule:** writes timed inside a term's landing window to change its computed holders (they create a later version; round 4, R4H-1); writes to inflate the gas of a stale pin (one version per walk; round 5 fix pass, the mutation ring of §15). Source: sequencing page §5 and §10; interfaces page `DomainVersion`, `domainVersionAt`, `domainCount`, `seatAtRank`.

**[assumed, unmeasured: the tree's gas]** A domain write costs about 16 fresh nodes plus execution gas, paid by the writer, for an entry and an exit alike (§11); state gas is metered outside the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap, so `register(8)` fits one call (round 6, R6S-4). A walk is a sequence of rank lookups, each a descent of `log₂(registry size)` node reads in one version; §11's figures are the round-6 simulation's (A-GAS), independent of later writes.

## 7. The cycle seed

### S1-R11. The accumulator, the seed and freshness

**[assumed: proposed rule]** **RND1.** `land`, every registry mutation and the public no-op `poke()` execute, at most once per L1 block, `seedAcc = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32 seedAcc, uint256 block.prevrandao))` and emit `Poked`, so nodes replay the mix log in block order. **RND2.** `seed(c) = keccak(DOMAIN ‖ chainId ‖ seedAcc_at(T_seed(c)) ‖ c)` with `T_seed(c) = cycleStart(c) − LOOKBACK`; the first mixing call at or after `T_seed(c)` fixes it on-chain (`SeedFixed`), and nodes compute the same value from the log without waiting. **RND3 (freshness).** `fresh(c)` iff a mix occurred after `freshFrom(c) = cycleStart(c) − 3,660 s`, the last instant at which a registry write can affect cycle `c` (§11). `seedOf(c)` returns the seed, `fresh` and `fixed`. **Adversary schedule:** grinding by registry writes (none when fresh; when not fresh, which needs no mix for 41 minutes, a total-censorship event, the residual lever is a few bits of seat placement); L1-proposer bias (the proposers of the last `m` blocks before `T_seed(c)` hold two bits each under A-RANDAO, so `m` colluding proposers choose among at most `4^m` seeds for one 60-term cycle, the bound of every RANDAO consumer). Rejected seed sources are §15's. Source: sequencing page §4 and §16, claim 5; interfaces page hashing convention, `poke`, `seedOf`, `seedAccumulator`.

**[proven: no grinding of any kind when the seed is fresh, conditional on A-KECCAK]** Every input that changes `E_t` or a rank is DELAY_REG-dated (S1-R06), the DELAY_REG inequality of §11 puts `freshFrom(c)` before `T_seed(c)`, and no suspension, reserve lapse or self-submitted slash changes a rank (S1-R09), so no owner write made after the seed could be known changes which seats any committee or view of cycle `c` draws. Source: sequencing page §4 and §16; round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2.

## 8. The term walk

### S1-R12. Ranks, the holder walk, views and void views

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `idx(t, k) = keccak(seed(c) ‖ 0x01 ‖ t ‖ k) mod E_t` (one fixed `abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 1, uint32 t, uint16 k)`, an exception to the tag-first order) is a rank among the domain seats at `termStart(t)` in seat-id order; the seat at rank `r` is the r-th such seat id, read from the version of S1-R10. **Walk.** For `k = 0 .. MAX_TRIES − 1`: skip ranks already visited; append the seat's owner to the holder list `H` if not yet present; stop at MAX_DISTINCT distinct owners or when every domain seat is visited. The walk does not read suspension, so it is a pure function of the domain at `termStart(t)`. **Views.** `holder(t, v) = H[v]` for `v < |H|`; beyond that, view `v` is the owner of the seat at rank `idx(t, v)`. A view whose owner is suspended or lapsed at `termStart(t)` is void and skipped without any timeout, exactly as a repeated rank beyond `|H|` is (round 5, R5S-2; the wording is transcribed, §13 item 4). At most V_MAX (S2) takeovers happen in one term (S2 owns the view change); after that the term idles to its end. The walk draws a holder for every view whenever `E_t ≥ 1`. `holderOf`, `holdersOf` (with `mode`), `sequencerAt` and `isHolder(t, v, who)` expose it; `isHolder` is what C2's L4 checks, in the domain version effective at `termStart(t)`, taking the sequencer key valid then (S1-R05). The seat is carried nowhere: `(termId, view)` and the holder list imply it (C1-R03, S2-R03). **Adversary schedule:** an owner that splits seats across addresses to raise its primary share (linear in seats, S1-R19); a drawn owner suspended after the seed (a void view, never a moved rank, S1-R09); phantom indices held to make terms open-empty (round 3, H5: the rank walk is over registered seats only). Source: sequencing page §5; interfaces page `holderOf`, `isHolder`, hashing convention.

### S1-R13. The committee walk, empty slots, `m` and the launch condition

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `committeeOf(t)` is the first K (S2) distinct domain seats of a second walk `keccak(seed ‖ 0x02 ‖ t ‖ m)` (one fixed `abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 2, uint32 t, uint16 m)`; the meaning of the `m` input is §13 item 3) over at most MAX_TRIES_COMMITTEE tries, skipping every seat of the term's primary holder (view 0, excluded by owner: the committee must not certify its own blocks) and the specific seats of the backups (excluded by seat only, so a backup's other seats may attest; a backup that takes over is then certified by a committee it may sit in, which S2's collusion count already includes since it counts the leader). Owner repeats among the rest are allowed, so the committee is stake-weighted, and it is drawn without replacement from the pool, so a cartel's share of a committee follows the hypergeometric law, not the binomial (round 3, H7). A drawn seat whose owner is suspended or lapsed at `termStart(t)` is an empty slot: it stays in the walk's output, `m` (the count of non-empty slots) shrinks by one, `Q(m)` scales as S2-R02 states, and no re-draw shifts the later slots (round 5, R5S-2). If fewer than K are found, the committee is the `m` seats found; a term with `m < K_MIN_CERT` (S2) is in no-committee mode (S1-R18). The committee size is therefore at most `min(K, E_t − seats(primary) − |H| + 1)` less any empty slot, about `K(1 − s)` in expectation when a share `s` of the pool is suspended or lapsed (S2-R02; round 6, R6-C1). Full committees need `E_t ≥ K + CAP + V_MAX` domain seats (44, §11) and no suspended seat among those drawn. The **redraw walk** of S2-R16 is `keccak(seed ‖ 0x03 ‖ t ‖ n ‖ m)` (`abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 3, uint32 t, uint8 n, uint16 m)`), its first K seats with the same exclusions, for the same leader, view and opening object; S2 owns its trigger, its proof and its effect. The published **launch condition** is at least 8 distinct owners and at least 72 seats in the domain at arm time, read by the DAO before `armEtna(T0)` and enforced by no contract (round 3, C4; `EtnaArmed` records the count read): a heuristic over a Sybil-able, revocable count, chosen so that a small cartel blanks few terms at launch (simulated on the slashing page §5, S3); if it fails, launch may begin in no-committee mode (round 4, R4-C5). It is liveness sizing, not an anti-monopoly bound (round 2, F1). **Adversary schedule:** a primary certifying its own blocks (excluded by owner); a committee pinned from calldata (S1-R14: the inbox walks it itself); at exactly 44 seats any two owners holding 12 seats sit with a blocking minority whenever at most one of their seats was drawn as a backup (the page's "about 40 % of terms", not reproduced, §13 item 6); parking before a seed to shrink committees (S1-R08, S2-R02). Source: sequencing page §8; Anchor page §5 and parameters page §1 (the launch condition), K_MIN_CERT; round 4 fix, I1; limitation L16.

### S1-R14. `recordAssignment`, the pin and the role horizon

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Committees are computed on-chain, never taken from calldata (round 4 fix, invariant I1). `recordAssignment(t)`, callable by anyone while `termStart(t) ≤ now ≤ termStart(t) + ROLE_HORIZON` (reverts before `termStart(t)`), walks the holders and the committee in the domain-tree version effective at `termStart(t)`, each try one rank lookup, and pins `assignmentDigest[t] = keccak(term, seed, E_t, holders)` and `committeeRoot[t] = keccak` of the K attester key hashes, each the drawn owner's attester key valid at `termStart(t)` (S1-R05), emitting `AssignmentRecorded` and `AssignmentPinned`. Within ROLE_HORIZON every landing also pins each term it settles (at most SETTLE_MAX, C2) and a landing of an unpinned term inside the horizon walks it in the same transaction, its gas added to the landing; a `CommitteeProof` supplied with a certificate, a view change or evidence is checked against the pinned root, so nobody can pin a committee of its own choosing. The pinner is paid PIN_REWARD from the term holder's reserve (the ramp, `landableFrom(t)`, PIN_MAX, the unpaid pin above the first unlanded term and the self-landing skip are C2-R16's; the funding is S3's), and no reward is paid when the pinner is the holder (sequencing page §8; parameters page PIN_MAX; a clause C2-R16 does not state, §16); a pin is never urgent, because the version it reads never changes. `ROLE_HORIZON = COMMITTEE_RING × TERM` (§11) is the role horizon: roles stay computable on-chain for about 34 h after a term's start, and seat recycling and suspension intervals are sized for it (§11). A term older than it that was never pinned is unlandable and therefore replaceable, the one time-based void; C2-R10 owns it with the landing horizon. The P2P envelope carries `assignmentDigest` (S2-R22), so a node with a differing digest knows its L1 view is stale. **Adversary schedule:** a pin before term start to fix a committee early (refused); a rotation between term start and the pin (the root takes the key valid at `termStart(t)`, R6S-4); nobody pinning under congestion (the first landing carries the walk at its own cost; PIN_MAX's coverage is C2 §12's). Source: sequencing page §8 and §10; interfaces page `recordAssignment`, `assignmentDigest`, `committeeOf`, events; round 5, R5H-2 and fix pass; round 6, R6H-2.

**[assumed, simulated: the walk's gas]** Worst case 576 rank lookups (64 holder tries and 512 committee tries), each a descent in one version; §11 carries the figures per registry size with their tags. Every pin fits one transaction under the 16.8 M EIP-7825 cap at every registry size; a 10 M-gas block (the size assumed at 2-s slots) carries the pin through 4,096 seats, and the 65,535-seat case needs a block gas limit above 10 M and sits above A-SUPPLY. Source: sequencing page §8; parameters page §1 and §3; round 5, R5H-2.

## 9. Open-empty terms, dead mode and no-committee mode

### S1-R15. Open-empty terms

**[assumed: proposed rule; the one definition]** A term is open-empty iff its domain is empty at `termStart(t)` (`E_t = 0`, so `|H| = 0`) or every view the walk draws for it (views 0 to V_MAX) is void, its owner suspended or lapsed at `termStart(t)`; without the second case such a term would idle to its end (round 6, R6H-2). In an open-empty term any address with a ledger bond of at least B_SEAT may sequence under sentinel view 255, and the first landing on L1 wins (`Holders.mode = OPEN_EMPTY`, `isOpenTerm`). **Adversary schedule:** opening a term with a drawn owner in good standing: impossible, since before `termStart(t)` an owner can void only its own views by a suspension or lapse dated at least DELAY_S earlier, so a term opens only when every drawn owner is suspended or lapsed (about `q^5` for a void seat share `q` spread over many owners, 0.4 % at `q = 1/3`, §11), and opening adds sequencing rights without removing any. Source: sequencing page §6 and §16; interfaces page `Holders`, `isOpenTerm`.

**[proven: open-empty is a function of L1 state at S(t)]** Both disjuncts are functions of the domain version and the flags at `termStart(t)`, every input DELAY_REG- or DELAY_S-dated, so nodes and the inbox agree (S1-R02); only the drawn owners' own suspensions or lapses make it true. Source: sequencing page §16, last claim.

### S1-R16. Sentinel chains and the first block after a reopening

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Every sentinel-view chain opens with the object `keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32("ETNA_DEAD"), bytes32 lastLanded.blockHash, uint32 t))`, which nodes and the guest accept as a fresh opening object after any landing (C7 V2 absorbed the former Anchor rule A5; S2-R01 names it; issue #22147) and which enters every equivocation key (S2-R17). The first block of a dead-mode view, like the first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change, may be a sequencer block although a forced-inclusion entry is due, once per opening object, so that a chain reopened from `lastLanded` can advance its anchor before the forced-inclusion obligation binds (C3's V8; round 6, R6H-1). **Adversary schedule:** two sentinel chains from one `lastLanded`: the same opening object, so their blocks share an equivocation key and C2's "first landing wins" arbitrates. Source: sequencing page §6.

### S1-R17. Dead mode

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The chain is in dead mode iff `termOf(now) − lastLandedTerm > DEAD_TERMS`, `lastLandedTerm` being C2-R06's record. In dead mode any address with a ledger bond of at least B_SEAT may sequence under sentinel view 255 from `lastLanded`, with no certificate, no attester and no registration delay, and the first landing wins; dead-mode landings are replacements and obey C2's A1 (C2-R09, C2-R11). Dead mode is the one rule of this section that is not a pure function of `S(t)`: it depends on the node's current view of `lastLandedTerm`, only ever adds authorized sequencers, and the landing contract arbitrates. A chain whose whole registry vanished is recovered by one honest bonded party in about 75 minutes plus proving, no DAO action. **Adversary schedule:** an opportunist voiding a slow but live chain: dead mode opens 4,440 s after the first unlanded term's end (§11), after the undeferred replace window (3,900 s) and 60 s before the once-deferred one (4,500 s), harmless because every dead-mode landing obeys A1 and none lands before `replaceableFrom` (round 6 restatement; the obligation C2 §16 placed on S1). Source: sequencing page §6, §11 and §14; parameters page §1 DEAD_TERMS.

**[proven: bounded disagreement, confined to recovery]** Two nodes with different views of `lastLandedTerm` differ on dead mode only while one lags L1; the right it grants is a landing right that L1 ordering settles, and no certified block is voided by the mode itself. Source: sequencing page §6.

### S1-R18. No-committee mode

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A term whose committee has fewer than K_MIN_CERT (S2) non-empty slots (S1-R13) is in no-committee mode (`Holders.mode = NO_COMMITTEE`): the holder is still chosen by sortition, no attestations, certificates or view changes exist (S2-R01), landings are accepted with an empty certificate and always with two ZK leaves (C2-R11; limitation L19), and users get the sequenced level only. Unlike dead mode, the landing right of a no-committee term is reserved to its sortition holder until the term's economic deadline `termEnd(t) + LAND_WINDOW` (C2-R07); only after that may any bonded address land from `lastLanded` under sentinel view 255 (round 2, S9). Between K_MIN_CERT and K non-empty slots the committee is smaller and the quorum scales (S2-R02). **Adversary schedule:** a parked majority beyond A-T11 forcing no-committee terms (L16, accepted); inside A-T11 `m < 8` has probability about `3·10⁻⁷` per term (S2 §6, reproduced there). Source: sequencing page §6 and §8; limitation L16, L19.

## 10. Anti-monopoly caps as rules

### S1-R19. The share bound, the cap and what is priced rather than bounded

**[assumed: proposed rule]** An owner with `w` of `A` domain seats in good standing is drawn primary with probability `w / A` (a suspended owner's draw is a void view and moves no other owner's rank); CAP bounds one address to `8 / A`; Sybil cost is linear in seats (the entry capital of S1-R04 per seat, the domain write's gas, and MIN_TENURE of exposure to draws and to MISS); exclusive control needs every domain seat in good standing and cannot be held against an honest entrant whose append is a plain L1 transaction live DELAY_REG later (A-LIQ). No consecutive-term cooldown and no weight halving exist (§15); binary suspension with strikes replaces both (S3). **Limitation L9 [assumed, proposed for C6's register]:** an entity's seat share is only priced, never bounded, because no owner-keyed rule can distinguish one entity's many owners from many entities; the price of primary share is linear in seats and unbounded at exclusion, but productive revenue is not linear: a cartel holding a blocking minority of a committee and of both its redraws can blank an honest primary's term, and above one third of the seats its revenue share is superlinear (L2, limitation L9; round 4, R4-C1); the collusion bound is A-T11, an assumption, not a rule. The revenue simulations, the price table and whether linear cost plus CAP plus per-incident slashing satisfies a super-linear-cost requirement are S3's (sequencing page §18 leaves the last open). **Adversary schedule:** TH9 monopolization: a share `X` of primary terms against `H` honest seats costs `H × X / (1 − X)` seats (64 seats for 50 % and 576 for 90 % against `H = 64`, §11); bounded and priced below one third, not defended above it. Source: sequencing page §5, §12 and §16, claims 6 and 7; limitation L9.

**[proven: the share bound]** Each try of the holder walk is a uniform rank over the domain under A-KECCAK, so the first try lands on one of an owner's `w` seats with probability `w / A`; the cap holds because `w ≤ 8` by S1-R03; exclusive control requires every rank to belong to the cartel, which an honest entrant breaks DELAY_REG after registering. Not proven: anything about revenue share (S3). Source: sequencing page §16.
