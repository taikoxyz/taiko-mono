## 2. Time, the snapshot and invariant I1

### S1-R01. The term grid and its origin

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `termOf(τ) = ⌊(τ − T0) / TERM⌋`, `termStart(t) = T0 + TERM × t`, `termEnd(t) = termStart(t + 1)`; `cycleStart(c) = termStart(CYCLE_TERMS × c)`, the grid's constants are §11's. T0 is zero until the DAO's second upgrade-class call `armEtna(T0)` with `T0 ≥ block.timestamp + MIGRATION_LEAD` sets it once; the upgrade transaction itself calls `initEtna` without T0, so the DAO reads the domain count and, off-chain, the owner structure before fixing the grid (round 4, R4-C5; C4 owns the migration sequence and its reading of the launch condition, S1-R13). No slot, epoch, beacon root, lookahead or `blockhash` is read anywhere in this section (R5). **Adversary schedule:** a grid fixed before the registry exists would start in no-committee mode; the DAO arms it after reading the count. Source: sequencing page §1; interfaces page `EtnaConfig`, `armEtna`.

### S1-R02. Future-dated effect (Rule D), the snapshot and invariant I1

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A registry write at L1 time `τ` changes eligibility only for instants `T ≥ τ + DELAY`: DELAY_REG for entry, exit and key rotation, the only inputs that change `E_t`, a rank or a key read, and for the hard-floor suspension and `reactivate()`; DELAY_S for class A and class B suspensions and reserve lapses, which change no rank (round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2; round 6, R6S-2). Term `t` evaluates eligibility at the single instant `termStart(t)` and reads the version of the domain tree effective at `termStart(t)` (S1-R10). `S(t)` is the highest L1 block with timestamp at most `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`: a node replays registry events and the mix log up to `S(t)`; under a total L1 halt the schedule stays defined. **Invariant I1, owned by S1:** for every term inside ROLE_HORIZON, the holders, committee, eligibility, seed and domain count the inbox computes at landing time equal what nodes computed at `termStart(t)`, whatever registry writes became effective in between. Two exceptions are stated, both read at the sentinel object's L1 reference (S1-R16) and both only adding rights: dead mode (S1-R17) and the post-deadline path of no-committee mode (S1-R18), each a function of `lastLandedTerm` and the timestamp at that reference. The committee role of a class-A slash is not an exception: under D37(c) S1-R13 reads `slashedAt` at the committee reference `S(t)`, as every other input of term `t` (the former second exception, which compared `slashedAt` with `termStart(t)` in a node's local view and in the inbox's view at landing, is withdrawn with its residual, §13 item 16). **Adversary schedule:** a write placed just after `S(t)` to change term `t` at landing time; by Rule D it takes effect after `termEnd(t)`, so neither view includes it. Source: sequencing page §1, §2 and §3; round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass.

**[proven: node snapshot equals landing-time computation, conditional on A-T1]** A write in a block after `S(t)` has timestamp above `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, so its effect falls at or after `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK + DELAY_S = termStart(t) + 2 × TERM > termEnd(t)` for the shortest delay (DELAY_S = LOOKBACK + 2 × TERM, §11), and later for DELAY_REG. Any write whose effect falls after `termStart(t)` creates a later version (S1-R10) and copies, never changes, the nodes it shares with the version effective at `termStart(t)`, so the inbox reads at landing exactly the version a node read at `S(t)`. The flag reads of S1-R09 are dated at least DELAY_S after their write, so nodes and the inbox read the same flag for every term. The `slashedAt` read of S1-R13's empty-slot predicate is covered as well: the predicate is `slashedAt ≠ 0 ∧ slashedAt ≤ termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, true exactly when the slash's block lies in the prefix ending at `S(t)`, `slashedAt` being the timestamp of that block (S3-R05's record as S1 reads it; the obligation on S3 is in §16); `slashedAt` is written once per owner (S3-R08's `AlreadySlashed`) and never reset, a property of S3's storage (S3-R05's record and S3-R08; the obligation on S3 is in §16), while D37(a) guarantees only that no fresh collateral at that address meets it (S1-R04, S1-R05), so the inbox evaluating it from any later L1 state, at a pin or at a landing, obtains the value a node obtained at `S(t)`. Under A-T1 a reorg below LOOKBACK does not occur, which is what "every input to term `t` is at least 20 minutes old at term start" protects. Source: sequencing page §3 and §16, claims 1 and 2.

## 3. Seats and the entry capital

### S1-R03. The seat array, its bound and the cap

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Seats live in an append-only array of at most MAX_SEATS records (a `uint16` index, §11), in the upgraded Inbox proxy after its 43-slot gap, with the logic in a linked library so the landing path reads the registry without an external call and the existing TAIKO bond ledger is reused (R2: no new address; the syncer address stays the Inbox proxy). An owner holds at most CAP seats. Indices are contract-assigned: FREE recycled holes first, then appends at the scheduled length, so a same-block append by an incumbent never reverts an entrant's transaction (round 2). A slot becomes FREE only when the exit of the seat it held has been finalized under S1-R07 (its stake released), never before: an expired seat not yet finalized keeps its index and its record, so no entrant is assigned it and the old claim is never ambiguous (a seat exited by `exitSlashed` or `exitStale` may be finalized by anyone, S1-R07, so a slashed or abandoned owner's silence does not keep its slots out of recycling) (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-03; D19: RECYCLE_GRACE is removed from this rule and from §11). The array is dense: its length equals the number of seats ever claimed. A slot is written fresh once, when its seat is first claimed, and only overwritten afterwards, each overwrite at a registration that finds it FREE. **Adversary schedule:** exclusion by filling the array costs the entry capital of S1-R04 for every seat (§11: 2.32 billion TAIKO, above A-SUPPLY); crowding an entrant's append is impossible because indices are assigned by the contract; holding slots FREE-but-unreleased to starve entrants of holes is pointless, since appends are unbounded below MAX_SEATS and a hole is only an index. Source: sequencing page §2 and §11; interfaces page `Seat`; parameters page MAX_SEATS, CAP; D19.

### S1-R04. Registration and the entry capital

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `register(count)` requires, as two amounts (S3-R04; D20), a nonreserve balance (S1-R09's comparator) of at least `1.05 × (seatCount + count) × B_SEAT` and a reserve of at least `(seatCount + count) × LAND_RESERVE`, `seatCount` the owner's held seats (§1, so a seat exited but not finalized still counts), a registered sequencer key and attester key (S1-R05), no pending exit, and an owner with no class-A `slashedAt` (S3-R05): `register` by an owner whose `slashedAt` is set reverts `OwnerSlashed`, whatever its seats, ledger or keys, before and after its terminal exit (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); D37(a)). Entry stays permissionless: a new address is a new owner with no `slashedAt`, its own ledger and fresh keys (S1-R05), and the slashed owner's records, key records and evidence attribution stay as they are. It assigns `count` indices (S1-R03), sets each seat's `activeFrom = τ + DELAY_REG`, writes one domain-tree version per seat (S1-R10) and emits `SeatClaimed` and `DomainWrite`. The three parts of the capital are S3's quantities and S1 names only their roles: seat stake `seatCount × B_SEAT` (the slashable floor), the 5 % buffer above it (where liveness debits land before seat stake), and the landing reserve `seatCount × LAND_RESERVE` (the seam to S3; its ledger properties are C2-R17's and S3's). No admission decision exists. **Adversary schedule:** a key already bound (refused, S1-R05); a class-A slashed owner that exits, finalizes, deposits fresh capital and registers unused keys at the same address, which would leave its new seats as empty committee slots under the persistent `slashedAt` and its new keys outside class-A accountability through `AlreadySlashed` (B's W12 verdict, the joint S1/S3 lifecycle finding: refused, `OwnerSlashed`; the same capital at a new address is an ordinary entrant, accountable from its first seat); a registration timed to grind a seed (effective DELAY_REG later, after the cycle's last affecting instant, S1-R11); a mass registration (priced per seat, S1-R06). Source: sequencing page §2; roles page §1 Entry; round 4, R4H-3; round 6, R6H-3.

### S1-R05. Keys: proof of control, proof of possession, validity intervals

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `registerKeys(seqKey, seqKeySig, blsPubkey, pop)` binds to the calling owner a sequencer key (a secp256k1 address with no code, S3 rule S8) whose signature `seqKeySig` is over `keccak(DOMAIN_KEYREG ‖ chainId ‖ owner)`, a proof of control, and a BLS12-381 attester key with a proof of possession over `(pubkey, owner, chainId)`; a key already bound anywhere in the registry is refused (`BadProofOfPossession`, `BadKeyControlSignature`, `KeyHasCode`), and `registerKeys` by an owner with a class-A `slashedAt` reverts `OwnerSlashed` (S1-R04; D37(a)). Each key an owner ever binds is a `KeyRecord{keyHash, validFrom, validUntil}`: `validFrom = τ + DELAY_REG` of its binding, `validUntil` the instant the next key of that kind takes effect, open for the current key until one of the two closing transitions below. `rotateKey(kind, newKey, pop)` appends a record valid from `τ + DELAY_REG` and closes the old key's interval at the same instant; for a sequencer key the `pop` is its DOMAIN_KEYREG signature; a key is **pending** between its binding and its `validFrom`. **Two live intervals per kind (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); D37(d)):** a record is live while its `validUntil` is open or later than `now`; a `rotateKey` of a kind that already has two live records (the current key, closed at its successor's future `validFrom`, and that pending successor) reverts `KeyRotationPending`, so at most one rotation per kind is pending and the next is accepted from the pending key's `validFrom` on, when the closed record stops being live. The transition is total: every rotation write either appends the second live record or reverts, and no record is merged, shortened or dropped by a rotation. **Retirement (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-02; D19):** the second closing transition is the owner's terminal exit (§1, S1-R07): at that exit's `activeUntil`, both current keys get `validUntil := activeUntil`, and a pending key of either kind (bound, `validFrom` not yet reached) is dropped, its record closed at the same instant with `validFrom = validUntil` so that it was never valid; a partial exit touches no key record. Because `rotateKey` and `requestExit` are both dated `τ + DELAY_REG` and effective times are monotone in transaction order (S1-R06), a rotation requested before the exit request takes effect at or before `activeUntil` and is the current key closed there (valid on `[its validFrom, activeUntil)`, empty when the two requests share a block time); only a rotation requested after the exit request is still pending at `activeUntil` and is dropped. A closed key stays liable until `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW`; key records persist after retirement, so `keyOwnerAt` resolves a retired key for every term it was valid in and evidence against those terms remains verifiable. A key hash that appears in any record, closed or not, is bound and is refused at `registerKeys` and `rotateKey`, so an owner that returns after a terminal exit binds fresh keys; only an owner with no class-A `slashedAt` returns (D37(a)). Every read of an owner's key for term `t` (C7's V1, `isHolder`, the committee walk, `committeeRoot[t]`, `keyOwnerAt`) takes the key valid at `termStart(t)`, so a rotation effective after a term starts, up to ROLE_HORIZON before its landing, changes nothing for that term (round 6, R6S-4). **Adversary schedule:** a third rotation of one kind while one is pending, whose effect on the pending record no rule stated (refused, `KeyRotationPending`, D37(d)); a slashed owner rebinding fresh keys at its own address (`OwnerSlashed`, D37(a)); a front-run of a victim's `registerKeys` fails, lacking the key's signature over the victim's address (round 6, judge's note); a rotation inside a landing window to change the pinned root (R6S-4); a BLS key without possession, a framing vector for S2's certificate-pair rule (slashing page §1); a rotation requested before the exit request (current at `activeUntil` and closed there, liable for the terms it was valid in); a rotation requested after the exit request to leave a key that never answers for the exited seats' duties (pending at `activeUntil` and dropped, so no duty was ever dated under it); evidence of a duty of an exited seat against a retired key (resolved through the persistent record, dated by its term, inside `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW`). Source: interfaces page §1 `KeyRecord`, `registerKeys`, `rotateKey`; slashing page §1; roles page §1 Entry; D19.

### S1-R06. DELAY_REG, MIN_TENURE and the eligibility-input rule

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Entry and exit are the only inputs to the rank domain, both owner-triggered writes dated `τ + DELAY_REG`. `requestExit` is accepted for a seat only once `τ + DELAY_REG ≥ activeFrom + MIN_TENURE`, so every seat is in the domain, drawn and bled for at least a week and every domain write takes effect exactly at `τ + DELAY_REG`, monotone in transaction order (round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass). Churn is priced, not capped: no rule limits writes per minute, each pays its own tree version (§11), and no incumbent can delay or crowd out an entrant (round 5 fix pass; the per-slot cap is §15's). Suspensions of every kind are per-owner flags read only after a draw (S1-R09); none changes `E_t` or a rank. **Adversary schedule:** register-and-exit churn to dilute others (sortition is over registered seats only, S1-R12, so it dilutes nobody; round 3, H5); a self-inflicted suspension after the seed is fixed (voids only the owner's own draws, S1-R09). Source: sequencing page §2 and §5; parameters page §1; round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2.

## 4. Exits

### S1-R07. Owner-requested exit and release

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `requestExit(seats)` takes any count of the caller's seats (round 5, R5-C4), each a domain write under S1-R06's tenure rule, sets each seat's `activeUntil = τ + DELAY_REG`, emits `ExitRequested` and `DomainWrite`, and keeps the seat in the domain until then. Seat exit and key retirement are separate (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-02; D19). An exit is classified at its `activeUntil` (§1): terminal iff no seat of the owner is active or pending at that instant. A seat pending when the exit is requested is active by then, because `register` refuses an owner with a pending exit (S1-R04), so every pending seat was registered earlier and its `activeFrom` precedes the exit's `activeUntil`; two exits sharing one `activeUntil` (two `requestExit` calls in one block) that leave nothing active are both terminal and run S1-R05's retirement once. **Partial exit:** when some seat of the owner is active or pending at `activeUntil`, the keys stay current for the remaining seats and no key record changes; `finalizeExit(seats)` releases the exiting seats, in the one sense of the held-seat withdrawal rule below (their share of the locked floor drops; nothing is transferred), once `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` has passed for every seat named in the call. Evidence against a duty of an exited seat is bounded by that window because every duty is dated by its term (S1-R05, S3's windows), so the open `validUntil` of a current key never delays a partial release. **Terminal exit:** when no seat of the owner is active or pending at `activeUntil`, that instant is the instant at which S1-R05's retirement transition runs (both current keys closed at `validUntil := activeUntil`, a pending rotation dropped); `finalizeExit` of that seat releases it at `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` (the floor drops to zero), the same instant as the keys' liability ends, and the owner is RETIRED (§12) with its key records kept. The two paths are one function: `finalizeExit(seats)` requires every named seat to be EXITING or EXPIRED with its `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW ≤ now`, not yet finalized, and either the caller's or exited by `exitSlashed` or `exitStale` (S1-R08), which anyone may finalize, the release accruing to the seat's owner's ledger in every case (so a slashed or abandoned owner's slots do not stay unrecycled for want of its call); a seat already finalized is FREE with no owner, so a second call for it reverts (`NotExiting`); finalization clears the seat's owner and makes the slot FREE (S1-R03) in the same transaction as the release, and nothing else makes a slot FREE. **The held-seat withdrawal rule (stated once here; S3-R04 consumes it verbatim, §16; B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02), nonblocking clarification; D37).** Let `h` be the owner's held seats (§1: pending, active, EXITING or EXPIRED, that is, not yet finalized). (i) While `h ≥ 1`, a withdrawal takes only from the nonreserve balance (`balance − reserveGwei`, S3-R02), and at most `max(0, min(nonreserve − 1.05 × h × B_SEAT, balance − (1.05 × h × B_SEAT + h × LAND_RESERVE)))`: it leaves the nonreserve balance at or above the stake and buffer of the held seats and the ledger at or above the whole floor, so reserve held above `h × LAND_RESERVE` never stands in for nonreserve stake, which class-B and MISS debits alone can reach (S3-R02) and which S3-R02's "non-withdrawable while seats are active" and S3-R04's "limited to the nonreserve balance above `1.05 × floor`" protect. (ii) `finalizeExit` of `count` seats transfers and credits nothing: it sets `h := h − count` and then caps the reserve, `reserveGwei := min(reserveGwei, h × LAND_RESERVE)`, the excess becoming nonreserve; that is the whole release, a drop of the locked floor by `count × (1.05 × B_SEAT + LAND_RESERVE)` on the two pockets as they stand after every reward, MISS and slash debit. (iii) No part of either pocket is allocated to a seat: a debit taken before finalization stays with the owner's pockets, so a finalization frees at most what the pockets hold above the new floor and nothing when they are below it (the shortfall stays with the remaining seats under S1-R09's flags), and nothing is minted. (iv) At `h = 0`, after a terminal finalization, no floor remains and the whole remaining ledger is withdrawable, for a class A owner whatever the slash left. A seat's share of the floor leaves only at its `finalizeExit`, never at its `activeUntil`, and the reserve leaves the ledger only through (ii) (round 4, R4H-3; the recovery verifier's finding on the previous revision; the amounts and debits are S3's). The permissionless exits of S1-R08 are finalized by the same calls, terminal when they take the last seat. **Adversary schedule:** exit then equivocate (the stake is held through EVIDENCE_WINDOW: the exiting seats stay held, so a withdrawal at `activeUntil` cannot reach their stake, partial or terminal, S1-V09); exit to dodge a draw (effective two hours later, after the last instant a write affects the cycle, S1-R11); a partial exit used to retire keys the remaining seats still answer with (impossible: a partial exit retires nothing); a terminal exit to escape a pending key's duties (it had none, S1-R05); finalizing twice, or an entrant claiming a slot before its release (refused by the FREE-on-finalization rule); a one-seat owner whose reserve a maximum reward debit left at 7,239 TAIKO claiming LAND_RESERVE = 14,400 at finalization (B's W12 example: it withdraws what the pockets hold, (iv); nothing is minted and no withdrawal fails for want of the difference); a one-seat owner that moves stake into the reserve (S3-R02's schedule), holding 21,000 of nonreserve and 30,000 of reserve, withdrawing against the reserve surplus: the floor is 35,400 and the balance 51,000, and a cap on the ledger alone would allow 15,600, all from the nonreserve, leaving 5,400, below the buffer line and the 10,000 hard floor with no flag set, since only debits set flags (S1-R09, S3-R04), and class-B and MISS debits could then collect at most 5,400; under (i) the nonreserve term is `21,000 − 21,000 = 0`, so nothing is withdrawable and the surplus 15,600 of reserve leaves only through (ii) (the arbiter's verification of this revision; S1-V09). ### S1-R08. Permissionless exits: `exitSlashed` and `exitStale`

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Anyone may call `exitSlashed(owner, seats)` for an owner slashed under a class-A rule (S3), and `exitStale(owner, seats)` for an owner continuously suspended or lapsed (S1-R09) for longer than STALE_EXIT without reactivating (`NotStale` otherwise). ## 5. Suspension and reactivation flags

### S1-R09. The two-interval store, `eligible`, and what sets the flag

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Each operator record holds two suspension intervals `[s1, e1)`, `[s2, e2)`. `suspended(owner, T)` is true iff `T` lies in one of them; `eligible(i, T) := inDomain(i, T) ∧ ¬suspended(owner(i), T)` is what a drawn seat must satisfy to hold a view or fill a committee slot; the flag is read only after the draw and is never an input to the rank domain (round 5, R5S-2). Every floor comparison that sets a flag below is made on S3's nonreserve comparator, the ledger `balance` less `reserveGwei` (D20; B's W8 verdict on #22202), never on the reserve-inclusive balance, and MISS never debits the reserve (S3); S1 consumes the comparator and defines no second one. The intervals hold, as flags whose debits and thresholds S3 owns:

- a **class B suspension** (the third structural strike within STRIKE_DECAY, S3-R05): `[τ + DELAY_S, τ + DELAY_S + SUSPEND(n))` for the n-th strike, SUSPEND(n) in §11; a class B debit that leaves the nonreserve balance below `seatCount × B_SEAT` instead opens an **open-ended** interval closed only by a top-up plus `reactivate()`, whose effect is dated `τ' + DELAY_REG`;

- a **reserve lapse** (reserve below one term of exposure per seat, S3's line): from `τ + DELAY_S`, open until a top-up to the required level plus `reactivate()`, and never shorter than SUSPEND(3) (round 6, R6S-2), so the closing instant is `max(τ + DELAY_S + SUSPEND(3), τ' + DELAY_REG)`;

## 6. The persistent domain tree

### S1-R10. Versions tagged by effective time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The contract keeps the domain as a persistent (versioned) segment tree over seat ids whose leaves are `inDomain` bits and whose nodes hold counts. Every domain write (an entry or an exit; nothing else, since suspensions and lapses are flags) creates a new version by path copying, about 16 fresh nodes per seat, tagged with its effective time `E = τ + DELAY_REG` and logged as `DomainVersion{effectiveTime, root, count}` with a `DomainWrite(version, seat, effectiveTime, delta)` event. Effective times are monotone in transaction order (S1-R06), so the log is sorted with no reordering. `domainVersionAt(T)` is the latest version with `E ≤ T`, a binary search of the log; term `t` reads `domainVersionAt(termStart(t))`, `domainCount(t)` its count `E_t` and `seatAtRank(t, r)` a descent in it. A version is immutable once written, and every version with `E ≤ termStart(t)` exists from `termStart(t) − DELAY_REG` on. No mutation ring, correction set, lazy application or per-slot cap exists: a walk reads one version and its cost does not depend on how many writes happened since the term started. **Adversary schedule:** writes timed inside a term's landing window to change its computed holders (they create a later version; round 4, R4H-1); writes to inflate the gas of a stale pin (one version per walk; round 5 fix pass, the mutation ring of §15). Source: sequencing page §5 and §10; interfaces page `DomainVersion`, `domainVersionAt`, `domainCount`, `seatAtRank`.

**[assumed, unmeasured: the tree's gas]** A domain write costs about 16 fresh nodes plus execution gas, paid by the writer, for an entry and an exit alike (§11); state gas is metered outside the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap, so `register(8)` fits one call (round 6, R6S-4). A walk is a sequence of rank lookups, each a descent of `log₂(registry size)` node reads in one version; §11's figures are the round-6 simulation's (A-GAS), independent of later writes.

## 7. The cycle seed

### S1-R11. The accumulator, the seed and freshness

**[assumed: proposed rule]** **RND1.** `land`, every registry mutation and the public no-op `poke()` execute, at most once per L1 block, `seedAcc = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32 seedAcc, uint256 block.prevrandao))` and emit `Poked`, so nodes replay the mix log in block order. **RND2.** `seed(c) = keccak(DOMAIN ‖ chainId ‖ seedAcc_at(T_seed(c)) ‖ c)` with `T_seed(c) = cycleStart(c) − LOOKBACK`; the first mixing call in a block with timestamp at or after `T_seed(c)` is the fixing call: it reads `seedAcc` as stored when it begins, before its own block's mix, fixes the seed on-chain (`SeedFixed`, carrying `fresh`) and only then mixes. **Cutoff order (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), clarification; D19):** `seedAcc_at(T_seed(c))` is the accumulator after the last mix in the committed L1 prefix that precedes the fixing call in transaction order; since RND1 mixes at most once per block and the fixing call is the first mixing call at or after `T_seed(c)`, that is exactly the set of mixes in blocks with timestamp below `T_seed(c)`, and a mix in the fixing call's own block counts iff it precedes the fixing call, which none does. Nodes compute the same value from the log without waiting, as the accumulator after the last `Poked` in a block with timestamp below `T_seed(c)`. **RND3 (freshness) and its fallback.** `fresh(c)` iff the last mix counted by the cutoff above lies in a block with timestamp after `freshFrom(c) = cycleStart(c) − 3,660 s`, the last instant at which a registry write can affect cycle `c` (§11); "a mix after `freshFrom(c)`" means that and nothing else. `fresh(c)` is evaluated once, at the fixing, and stored with the seed; a mix after the fixing call, a `poke()` included, never relabels a stale seed and never changes the fixed seed. `seedOf(c)` returns the seed, `fresh` and `fixed`. **Fallback (J's review of #22201, 2026-10-01, Medium J-1, under the arbiter's decision):** the seed of cycle `c` is fixed at `T_seed(c)` from the accumulator as it stands, mixed after `freshFrom(c)` or not; no rule waits for a mix, no rewarded action guarantees one (a landing mixes, but nothing pays for a landing in order to mix), and a `poke()` is a voluntary call nobody is paid to make. When `fresh(c)` is false, the last mixer, the sender of the last mixing transaction at or before `freshFrom(c)`, held the only choice over `seed(c)`: one accumulator value per write it made (a registry write or a `poke()`, each a transaction it paid for), so it can bias at most cycle `c`'s selection (per cycle; a stall past `freshFrom(c + 1)` extends the same choice to `c + 1`, within the outage regime of S1-R17), choosing among as many candidate seeds as writes it paid for; when its last write is a registry write, that write may also edit the domain of cycle `c`'s last terms with the seed known (assumed, not proven here); and it cannot delay or prevent the fixing, which RND2 ties to `T_seed(c)` and to the first mixing call at or after it. Every seat has an incentive to `poke()` after `freshFrom(c)`, since an unfresh seed invites bias against it, and a `poke()` is a cheap call whose gas is unmeasured (§11; no page estimates it). An unfresh seed needs no landing, registry write or `poke()` between `freshFrom(c)` and `T_seed(c)`, 2,460 s (§11). That gap fits inside LAND_WINDOW_MAX (C2 §12), so it can occur on a slow but live chain. A chain with no landing for a whole cycle (CYCLE_TERMS × TERM = 3,600 s) is in the outage regime, within 15 terms of dead mode (DEAD_TERMS = 75, S1-R17). The unpriced dependency of freshness on an unpaid call is limitation LS1-1 (§13), placed on C6's accepted-limitations register (§16). **Adversary schedule:** grinding by registry writes (none when fresh; when not fresh, the fallback's bound: the last mixer's choice among its own paid writes, per cycle, and a domain edit of cycle `c`'s last terms if its last write was a registry write); L1-proposer bias (the proposers of the last `m` blocks before `T_seed(c)` hold two bits each under A-RANDAO, so `m` colluding proposers choose among at most `4^m` seeds for one 60-term cycle, the bound of every RANDAO consumer). Rejected seed sources are §15's. Source: sequencing page §4 and §16, claim 5; interfaces page hashing convention, `poke`, `seedOf`, `seedAccumulator`; J's review of #22201 (J-1) for the fallback; B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01) and D19 for the cutoff order.

**[proven: no grinding of any kind when the seed is fresh, conditional on A-KECCAK and on a mix after `freshFrom(c)`]** Every input that changes `E_t` or a rank is DELAY_REG-dated (S1-R06), the DELAY_REG inequality of §11 puts `freshFrom(c)` before `T_seed(c)`, and no suspension, reserve lapse or self-submitted slash changes a rank (S1-R09), so no owner write made after the seed could be known changes which seats any committee or view of cycle `c` draws. Without a mix after `freshFrom(c)` the claim does not hold and the fallback clause's bound applies: the last mixer's choice is bounded as S1-R11's fallback clause states (assumed, not proven here); when its last write is a registry write, that write may also edit the domain of cycle `c`'s last terms with the seed known. Source: sequencing page §4 and §16; round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2; J's review of #22201 (J-1).

## 8. The term walk

### S1-R12. Ranks, the holder walk, views and void views

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `idx(t, k) = keccak(seed(c) ‖ 0x01 ‖ t ‖ k) mod E_t` (one fixed `abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 1, uint32 t, uint16 k)`, an exception to the tag-first order) is a rank among the domain seats at `termStart(t)` in seat-id order; the seat at rank `r` is the r-th such seat id, read from the version of S1-R10. **Walk.** For `k = 0 .. The walk does not read suspension, so it is a pure function of the domain at `termStart(t)`. **Views.** `holder(t, v) = H[v]` for `v < |H|`; beyond that, view `v` is the owner of the seat at rank `idx(t, v)`. ### S1-R13. The committee walk, empty slots, `m` and the launch condition

**[assumed: proposed rule; the one definition of the committee walk, consumed by S2-R16 and its vector S2-V11]** The walk is stated once here, under B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-01, and D19, replacing the former `keccak(seed ‖ 0x02 ‖ t ‖ m)` whose repeated evaluation gave one hash, not a walk, whose initial selection read its own output size, and whose live `m` rerolled every rank when a post-seed lapse changed it. **Draws.** Draw `i` (`i = 0, 1, 2, ...`) computes `keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 kind, uint32 t, uint8 n, uint16 i))` with `kind = 2` for the initial walk and `kind = 3` for a redraw, `n` the redraw count (0 for the initial walk, S2-R16's count otherwise), `seed = seed(c)` of the term's cycle, and `i` the draw counter; one fixed `abi.encode` in that field order, an exception to the tag-first order. The residue of the hash modulo `E_t`, the count of the domain version effective at `termStart(t)`, which every term of cycle `c` has fixed before `T_seed(c)` because every write that affects a term of the cycle has `τ ≤ freshFrom(c) < T_seed(c)` (S1-R02, S1-R10, §11 freshFrom row), is a rank among the domain seats at `termStart(t)` in seat-id order, read from the version of S1-R10. **Exclusions.** Every seat of the term's primary holder (view 0, excluded by owner: the committee must not certify its own blocks) and the specific seats of the backups (views 1 to V_MAX, excluded by seat only, so a backup's other seats may attest; a backup that takes over is then certified by a committee it may sit in, which S2's collusion count already includes since it counts the leader). **Skip and stop.** A draw whose seat is already selected or excluded is skipped and selects nothing; the walk stops after K (S2) distinct selections or after WALK_MAX = 8 × K draws (§11), whichever comes first. **Committee.** `committeeOf(t)` is the selected seats in draw order. An empty slot stays in the walk's output at its rank in draw order. **The committee reference (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); D37(c)).** Every input of `committee(t)` (the domain version, the flags and `slashedAt`) is read at one fixed L1 reference, the committee reference `S(t)`, by nodes and by the inbox: a node reads it from its replay up to `S(t)` (S1-R02), the inbox at the pin from its persistent records, the domain version effective at `termStart(t)` (S1-R10), the intervals at `termStart(t)` (S1-R09) and `slashedAt` against the bound `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, and every later reader (a landing, a certificate or view-change check, evidence, a sentinel regime test of S1-R16) takes the marks and `m` from the pin (S1-R14). No reader uses its local L1 view at `termStart(t)` or the state at its landing for this predicate. **[proven: deterministic `m`, conditional on A-T1]** The three inputs are functions of the prefix ending at `S(t)` (S1-R02's proven paragraph for the version and the flags; for `slashedAt`, its one write per owner, never reset by S3's storage, S3-R05 and S3-R08, D37(a) adding only that no fresh collateral at that address meets it), and each later read evaluates the same function of persistent records; A-T1 (no reorganisation of a block older than LOOKBACK) keeps that prefix fixed from `termStart(t)` on, which is the reorg premise, the same as every other input of term `t`. A slash in a block after `S(t)` empties nothing in term `t`, nor in any later term starting less than LOOKBACK after the slash (at most LOOKBACK / TERM = 20 terms that D20's former dating emptied, §11); for those terms its keys stay in `m` and `Q(m)` while a zero ledger backs nothing, where D20 counted them in the term in progress only; S3-R10's subtraction from the published backing must cover every term starting in `(slashedAt, slashedAt + LOOKBACK)`, not only a term already started as S3-R10 now words it, and S2-R18's published backing likewise (obligations on S3 and S2, §16). The committee size is therefore at most `min(K, E_t − seats(primary) − |H| + 1)` less any empty slot, about `K(1 − s)` in expectation when a share `s` of the pool is suspended or lapsed (S2-R02; round 6, R6-C1). Full committees need `E_t ≥ K + CAP + V_MAX` domain seats (44, §11) and no suspended seat among those selected. **Redraw.** The redraw walk of S2-R16 at count `n ≥ 1` is the same algorithm with `kind = 3` and that `n`, the same exclusions and the same snapshot; its draw counter restarts at 0 and its `m` is its own post-selection count. The redraw committee at count `n` is a function of `(seed, t, n)` and the term's exclusions only: no view, leader or opening object (D16's and D21's logical `vcHash`) enters it, so every view of the term that reaches count `n` draws the same seats (the per-term reading of L9, S1-R19); S2 owns its trigger, its proof and its effect, and the obligation to cite this rule rather than restate a formula is placed on S2 (§16). **Gas.** WALK_MAX bounds the committee part of a pin and of each redraw verification to 256 rank lookups at K = 32, so a pin is at most 64 + 256 = 320 lookups in one version (S1-R14); the simulated figures of §11 priced 576 lookups and stay the cited bound, unmeasured. C8 encodes the algorithm (the draw loop, the skip, both stops, the empty-slot marking and the post-selection `m`) as the one reference implementation sketch, with S1-V01 and S1-V08 as its vectors (§16). The published **launch condition** is at least 8 distinct owners and at least 72 seats in the domain at arm time, read by the DAO before `armEtna(T0)` and enforced by no contract (round 3, C4; `EtnaArmed` records the count read): a heuristic over a Sybil-able, revocable count, chosen so that a small cartel blanks few terms at launch (simulated on the slashing page §5, S3); if it fails, launch may begin in no-committee mode (round 4, R4-C5). It is liveness sizing, not an anti-monopoly bound (round 2, F1). **Adversary schedule:** a primary certifying its own blocks (excluded by owner); a committee pinned from calldata (S1-R14: the inbox walks it itself); at exactly 44 seats any two owners holding 12 seats sit with a blocking minority whenever at most one of their seats was drawn as a backup (the page's "about 40 % of terms", not reproduced, §13 item 6); parking before a seed to shrink committees (S1-R08, S2-R02); a lapse or self-report after the seed to reroll a committee against another owner (impossible: `m` enters no hash, every rank is fixed by `seed`, `t`, `n` and `i`, and the lapse empties only the lapsed owner's slots, S1-V08); a draw sequence engineered to exhaust WALK_MAX (the seed is fixed before any owner write of the cycle can take effect, S1-R11, and the failure probability of §11 is a property of the snapshot alone). Source: sequencing page §8; Anchor page §5 and parameters page §1 (the launch condition), K_MIN_CERT; round 4 fix, I1; limitation L16; D19; B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02) and D37(c) for the committee reference.

**[proven: the walk selects K seats within WALK_MAX draws except with probability below 3 × 10⁻¹¹ per walk at 72 or more domain seats, conditional on A-KECCAK]** With `E_t ≥ 72` and at most `CAP + V_MAX = 12` excluded seats, at least `E_t − 12 ≥ 60` seats are selectable; while fewer than K = 32 are selected, at least `60 − 31 = 29` of them remain unselected, so each draw selects with probability at least `(E_t − 43) / E_t`, which increases in `E_t` and equals `29 / 72` at `E_t = 72` (the excluded count does not grow with `E_t`). The number of selections in 256 draws therefore dominates a binomial with 256 trials and success probability `29 / 72`, mean `μ = 103.1`; the walk fails only if that count is below 32, and the Chernoff lower-tail bound `exp(−μ δ² / 2)` with `δ = 1 − 32 / 103.1 = 0.690` gives `exp(−24.5) = 2.2 × 10⁻¹¹ < 3 × 10⁻¹¹`. The expected draw count at 72 seats is `72 × (H_60 − H_28) ≈ 54.2`, so an ordinary walk ends after about a fifth of WALK_MAX. Source: D19; the arithmetic is this page's.

**[proven: the walk's shortfall between 44 and 72 seats, conditional on A-KECCAK]** With the worst case of 12 excluded seats, the number of selections after `j` draws is a Markov chain that moves from `s` to `s + 1` with probability `(E_t − 12 − s) / E_t` and stays otherwise; iterating it exactly for 256 draws gives the probability of fewer than K = 32 selections as about 8.6 % at `E_t = 44`, 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ at 46, 2.1 × 10⁻⁷ at 48, 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 50 and 1.9 × 10⁻¹³ at 52 (fewer excluded seats only lower these). The expected draw count at 44 seats is `44 × H_32 ≈ 178.6`, so at the full-committee minimum WALK_MAX alone shortens about one committee in twelve; a short walk is a smaller committee under S2-R02's scaling and S1-R14's variable-length root. Source: the recovery verifier's computation on this revision, reproduced by this page's dynamic program.

### S1-R14. `recordAssignment`, the pin and the role horizon

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Committees are computed on-chain, never taken from calldata (round 4 fix, invariant I1). `recordAssignment(t)`, callable by anyone while `termStart(t) ≤ now ≤ termStart(t) + ROLE_HORIZON` (reverts before `termStart(t)`), walks the holders and the committee in the domain-tree version effective at `termStart(t)`, each try one rank lookup, and pins `assignmentDigest[t] = keccak(term, seed, E_t, holders)` and `committeeRoot[t]`, a length-prefixed commitment to the selected seats of S1-R13 in draw order: the selection count `c ≤ K` (fewer than K when the walk stops at WALK_MAX), the post-selection count `m`, and for each position `j < c` the attester key hash of that seat's owner valid at `termStart(t)` (S1-R05) with a mark saying whether the position is an empty slot (S1-R13's predicate); empty slots keep their positions and their key hashes, marked, and no padding to K exists (the byte layout is C8's, §16), emitting `AssignmentRecorded` and `AssignmentPinned`. The marks and `m` are read at the committee reference `S(t)` (S1-R13; D37(c)), which the pin names by its bound `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, a function of `t` and of no pinner's choice, so a pin made at any instant of the horizon, by `recordAssignment` or by a landing, writes the same marks and `m`, and these are what nodes computed at `termStart(t)`. Within ROLE_HORIZON every landing also pins each term it settles (at most SETTLE_MAX, C2) and a landing of an unpinned term inside the horizon walks it in the same transaction, its gas added to the landing; a `CommitteeProof` supplied with a certificate, a view change or evidence is checked against the pinned root, so nobody can pin a committee of its own choosing. The pinner is paid PIN_REWARD from the term holder's reserve (the ramp, `landableFrom(t)`, PIN_MAX, the unpaid pin above the first unlanded term and the self-landing skip are C2-R16's; the funding is S3's), and no reward is paid when the pinner is the holder (sequencing page §8; parameters page PIN_MAX; a clause C2-R16 does not state, §16); a pin is never urgent, because the version it reads never changes. `ROLE_HORIZON = COMMITTEE_RING × TERM` (§11) is the role horizon: roles stay computable on-chain for about 34 h after a term's start, and suspension intervals are sized for it (§11); a slot is recycled only at finalization, at least EVIDENCE_WINDOW after `activeUntil` (S1-R03, S1-R07), which is above ROLE_HORIZON + DELAY_S (§11), so no recycled seat changes a decided term. A term older than it that was never pinned is unlandable and therefore replaceable, the one time-based void; C2-R10 owns it with the landing horizon. The P2P envelope carries `assignmentDigest` (S2-R22), so a node with a differing digest knows its L1 view is stale. **Adversary schedule:** a pin before term start to fix a committee early (refused); a rotation between term start and the pin (the root takes the key valid at `termStart(t)`, R6S-4); nobody pinning under congestion (the first landing carries the walk at its own cost; PIN_MAX's coverage is C2 §12's). Source: sequencing page §8 and §10; interfaces page `recordAssignment`, `assignmentDigest`, `committeeOf`, events; round 5, R5H-2 and fix pass; round 6, R6H-2.

**[assumed, simulated: the walk's gas]** Worst case 320 rank lookups (64 holder tries and WALK_MAX = 256 committee draws, S1-R13; D19), each a descent in one version; the simulated figures were produced for the former 576-lookup worst case and are kept in §11 as the cited bound, unmeasured, since no simulation of the 320-lookup walk exists. Every pin fits one transaction under the 16.8 M EIP-7825 cap at every registry size; a 10 M-gas block (the size assumed at 2-s slots) carries the pin through 4,096 seats, and the 65,535-seat case needs a block gas limit above 10 M and sits above A-SUPPLY. Source: sequencing page §8; parameters page §1 and §3; round 5, R5H-2.

## 9. Open-empty terms, dead mode and no-committee mode

### S1-R15. Open-empty terms

**[assumed: proposed rule; the one definition]** A term is open-empty iff its domain is empty at `termStart(t)` (`E_t = 0`, so `|H| = 0`) or every view the walk draws for it (views 0 to V_MAX) is void, its owner suspended or lapsed at `termStart(t)`; without the second case such a term would idle to its end (round 6, R6H-2). In an open-empty term any address with a ledger bond of at least B_SEAT, read at the L1 reference of its sentinel object (S1-R16; D37(b)), may sequence under sentinel view 255, and the first landing on L1 wins (`Holders.mode = OPEN_EMPTY`, `isOpenTerm`); whether a class-A slashed owner that deposits again is such an address is §13 item 17. **Adversary schedule:** opening a term with a drawn owner in good standing: impossible, since before `termStart(t)` an owner can void only its own views by a suspension or lapse dated at least DELAY_S earlier, so a term opens only when every drawn owner is suspended or lapsed (about `q^5` for a void seat share `q` spread over many owners, 0.4 % at `q = 1/3`, §11), and opening adds sequencing rights without removing any. Source: sequencing page §6 and §16; interfaces page `Holders`, `isOpenTerm`.

**[proven: open-empty is a function of L1 state at S(t)]** Both disjuncts are functions of the domain version and the flags at `termStart(t)`, every input DELAY_REG- or DELAY_S-dated, so nodes and the inbox agree (S1-R02); only the drawn owners' own suspensions or lapses make it true. Source: sequencing page §16, last claim.

### S1-R16. Sentinel chains and the first block after a reopening

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Every sentinel-view chain opens with the object

`keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32("ETNA_DEAD"), bytes32 lastLandedHash, uint32 t, uint48 l1RefNumber, bytes32 l1RefHash))`

one fixed `abi.encode` of five static words in that order (an exception to the tag-first order, §1), where `lastLandedHash` is the block hash of the `lastLanded` the chain extends, `t` the term of its first block, and `(l1RefNumber, l1RefHash)` the object's **L1 reference** (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); C7 §11 item 20; D22 item 4; D37(b)). The width of `l1RefNumber` follows C8's `Replace` struct and does not change the preimage, since `abi.encode` pads every static field to one word. The object's fields travel with the first block under it, as a view change travels with the first block of a view (S2-R01); its hash is that block's `PH.vcHash`, which the sequencer signature covers (S2-R03), so the reference is signed by the chain's one signer (D22, C7-R04) and every later block under the object carries the same `vcHash`. Nodes and the guest accept it as a fresh opening object after any landing (C7 V2 absorbed the former Anchor rule A5; S2-R01 cites it; issue #22147), and it enters every equivocation key (S2-R17).

**The reference.** It is valid iff (1) `l1RefHash` is the hash of block `l1RefNumber` in the verifier's canonical L1 view and that block's timestamp is at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE (C7) before the first block's `PH.timestamp` (S2-R09's staleness rule; A-L1VIEW); (2) that timestamp is at or after the bound `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, so `S(t)` lies in the reference's prefix (the reference is `S(t)` itself only when `S(t)`'s timestamp equals the bound, and a later block otherwise); (3) `l1RefNumber` is at or above the anchor and reference floor (C4-R07's `activationL1Number`, or C4-R12's `restartL1Number` after a restart).

**What is read at it.** Every sentinel test is read in the L1 state at the reference, never at a node's L1 head or clock: the **regime** (dead mode as S1-R17 states it, from `lastLandedTerm` and the reference's timestamp; open-empty and the no-committee holder path, functions of the committee reference `S(t)` (S1-R13, S1-R15, S1-R18), which (2) places in the reference's prefix; S1-R18's post-deadline path, from the first unlanded term `t′ = lastLandedTerm + 1` recorded at the reference, its mode a function of `S(t′)`, which precedes `S(t)`, and its deadline `termEnd(t′) + LAND_WINDOW` compared with the reference's timestamp); the **parent**, the first block's parent being the `lastLanded` recorded at the reference, whose hash must equal `lastLandedHash`; and the **registry**, the signer's ledger bond of at least B_SEAT (S1-R15, S1-R17, and S1-R18's post-deadline path) or, on S1-R18's holder path, the sortition holder of `t`. **At landing** the inbox checks `l1RefHash` against `blockhash` or the EIP-2935 window (C5-R02; a reference outside the window is unlandable), and the range must extend the current `lastLanded` (C2-R05); a dead-mode or post-deadline range is a replacement and lands only at `block.timestamp > replaceableFrom` (A1, C2-R08; S1-R17, S1-R18). The reference's number, hash and timestamp are journal outputs: the guest reads the timestamp from the header whose hash is `l1RefHash`, as it does for C2-R04's `l1AnchorTipTimestamp`, so the inbox parses no L1 header and C5-R07's "never an inbox upgrade" stands (the journal fields are C2's and the fourth reader is C5-R02's to list, §16). **[proven: one reading of the regime and the parent, conditional on A-L1VIEW]** A landing between the reference and the sentinel landing changes `lastLanded.blockHash`, so the object then names a superseded parent and the range cannot land; a CONFLICT restart, which may restore an earlier `lastLanded`, raises the floor to `restartL1Number` (C4-R12), so a reference taken before it is refused by (3). Hence a sentinel range that lands extends the `lastLanded`, and has the `lastLandedTerm` (so the `t′` of S1-R18), recorded at its reference, and the inbox's dead-mode and deadline tests, made with the reference's timestamp, are the ones nodes made. The bond C2-R05's L4 reads at landing is C2's; if it reads the current ledger, a signer whose own bond fell below B_SEAT after its reference cannot land its chain, a loss to that signer alone (§16).

The first block of a dead-mode view, like the first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change, may be a sequencer block although a forced-inclusion entry is due, once per opening object, so that a chain reopened from `lastLanded` can advance its anchor before the forced-inclusion obligation binds (C3's V8; round 6, R6H-1). This waiver sentence is unchanged in this revision: whether a renewable waiver lets a signer censor forced inclusions is C3's, pending D33.

**Adversary schedule:** two sentinel chains from one `lastLanded` under one reference: the same opening object, so their blocks share an equivocation key and C2's "first landing wins" arbitrates; under two references: two opening objects, as an original tail and its REPLACE fork are two (S2-R17), nothing under either is certified (no certificate exists under sentinel 255), and the first landing wins; a reference chosen before a landing, to claim dead mode or a parent the chain has since passed: the object names a superseded `lastLanded` and the range is unlandable, and how fork choice ranks such a chain against the landed chain is S2-R17's and C7's (§16); a reference chosen to read a stale registry (a bond since withdrawn): bounded below by (2), and a term's committee facts are the pin's at `S(t)` whatever the reference (S1-R13); a reference newer than ANCHOR_MIN_AGE, to read an L1 state not every node holds: invalid by (1). Source: sequencing page §6; D22 item 4; C7 §11 item 20; D37(b).

### S1-R17. Dead mode

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The chain is in dead mode at an L1 block `R` iff `termOf(timestamp(R)) − lastLandedTerm > DEAD_TERMS`, `lastLandedTerm` being C2-R06's record in the state at `R`; a sentinel chain claims dead mode at its object's L1 reference (S1-R16), and nodes, the guest and the inbox test it there and nowhere else (D22 item 4; D37(b)). In dead mode any address with a ledger bond of at least B_SEAT, read at that reference, may sequence under sentinel view 255 from `lastLanded`, with no certificate, no attester and no registration delay, and the first landing wins; dead-mode landings are replacements and obey C2's A1 (C2-R09, C2-R11). Dead mode and S1-R18's post-deadline path are the two rules of this section that are not functions of `S(t)`: each depends on `lastLandedTerm` and the timestamp at the sentinel object's reference, a fixed signed L1 block every verifier reads alike (S1-R16), only ever adds authorized sequencers, whose landings are replacements under A1, and the landing contract arbitrates. S1-R18's mode boundary itself is not such a rule: under D37(c) it is a function of the committee reference `S(t)` (S1-R13). A chain whose whole registry vanished is recovered by one honest bonded party in about 75 minutes plus proving, no DAO action. **Adversary schedule:** an opportunist voiding a slow but live chain: dead mode opens 4,440 s after the first unlanded term's end (§11), measured at the reference's timestamp, so the first dead-mode block follows at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE later, at 4,488 s or after; after the undeferred replace window (3,900 s) and before the once-deferred one (4,500 s), harmless because every dead-mode landing obeys A1 and none lands before `replaceableFrom` (round 6 restatement; the obligation C2 §16 placed on S1). Source: sequencing page §6, §11 and §14; parameters page §1 DEAD_TERMS.

**[proven: bounded disagreement, confined to recovery]** Two nodes with different views of `lastLandedTerm` differ on dead mode only while one lags L1; the right it grants is a landing right that L1 ordering settles, and no certified block is voided by the mode itself. Source: sequencing page §6.

### S1-R18. No-committee mode

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A term whose committee has fewer than K_MIN_CERT (S2) non-empty slots (S1-R13) is in no-committee mode (`Holders.mode = NO_COMMITTEE`): the holder is still chosen by sortition, no attestations, certificates or view changes exist (S2-R01), landings are accepted with an empty certificate and always with two ZK leaves (C2-R11; limitation L19), and users get the sequenced level only. Unlike dead mode, the landing right of a no-committee term is reserved to its sortition holder until the term's economic deadline `termEnd(t) + LAND_WINDOW` (C2-R07); only after that may any bonded address land from `lastLanded` under sentinel view 255 (round 2, S9), as a replacement under A1 (below). Between K_MIN_CERT and K non-empty slots the committee is smaller and the quorum scales (S2-R02). The boundary is a function of the committee reference `S(t)`: the non-empty count reads S1-R13's predicate there, `slashedAt` included, so nodes and the inbox agree on the mode for every term under A-T1 (S1-R13's proven paragraph; D37(c)), and a class-A slash after `S(t)` cannot flip the mode of term `t`. **The two sentinel paths (S1-R16; D37(b)).** A sentinel chain's object names `t`, the term of its first block, and its blocks carry the term containing their `PH.timestamp` (C7-R06), so a block of term `t` has `PH.timestamp < termEnd(t)` and its reference, at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE older, is stamped before `termEnd(t) − 48`, never at `termEnd(t) + LAND_WINDOW`; the deadline is therefore tested on an earlier term than `t`. (a) **Holder path:** `t` is in no-committee mode at `S(t)` and the first block's signer is the sortition holder of `t`, a function of `S(t)`; no deadline is tested. (b) **Post-deadline path:** the term tested is `t′ = lastLandedTerm + 1`, the first unlanded term, `lastLandedTerm` read at the object's reference as for dead mode (S1-R17); the path is open iff `t′` is in no-committee mode at `S(t′)` and the reference's timestamp is at least `termEnd(t′) + LAND_WINDOW`, never a clock; the signer is any address with a ledger bond of at least B_SEAT read at the reference, and the chain's blocks carry the term `t` containing their `PH.timestamp`, so `PH.timestamp ≥ termEnd(t′) + LAND_WINDOW + 48` and `t > t′`. `t′` is no field of the object: the object binds it through `lastLandedHash` and the reference. Path (b) opens nothing when the first unlanded term has a committee: that term's holders and C2's replacement rules govern it. **Path-(b) landings are replacements and obey C2's A1 (C2-R08, C2-R09, C2-R11), as dead-mode landings do (S1-R17).** The reference test above makes the chain valid for nodes and the guest; the inbox then accepts its range only at `block.timestamp > replaceableFrom`, a test on inbox storage and the block timestamp at landing. C2-R08 computes `replaceableFrom` from the same first unlanded term `t′`, so it is at least `termEnd(t′) + LAND_WINDOW + REPLACE_GRACE` (2,100 s with C2 §12's 1,800 and 300 s, quoted), later under chained late landings, and an announcement defers it once by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE. Until then the backlog above `lastLanded`, whatever the modes of its terms, stays landable and wins if it lands first (C2-R09), after which the path-(b) object names a superseded parent and its range is unlandable (S1-R16). Path (b) therefore voids a certified backlog of later committee terms only where C2's replacement path would, under C2-R08's schedule ("a landable backlog is never reorged while someone bonded is landing it"). The alternative, requiring every term from `t′` to `t` to be in no-committee mode, is not taken: it makes the path's validity depend on the modes of terms the chain does not sequence, and after `replaceableFrom` it protects nothing C2's REPLACE path does not already leave replaceable. Choosing `t′ = lastLandedTerm + 1`, the definition §11 already uses for dead mode, with path-(b) landings under A1, is this revision's proposal for the arbiter's confirmation with D37 (the arbiter's verification found the former single-term wording unsatisfiable, and the recovery verifier found the satisfiable path outside A1). **Adversary schedule:** a parked majority beyond A-T11 forcing no-committee terms (L16, accepted); a bonded non-holder claiming term `t`'s deadline with a block inside `t` (impossible: the reference precedes `termEnd(t) − 48`, and path (b) tests `t′ < t`); a post-deadline chain while the first unlanded term had a committee (path (b) refuses it); a bonded non-holder landing path (b) at once over a later committee term's certified backlog (`t′` no-committee and silent; `t′ + 1` with a committee and certified blocks on `lastLanded`, landable until `termEnd(t′ + 1) + LAND_WINDOW = termEnd(t′) + 1,860`; a reference at `termEnd(t′) + 1,800` and a first block at `termEnd(t′) + 1,848`): its landing is refused until `replaceableFrom`, at least `termEnd(t′) + 2,100` and later after an announcement, and a landing of the backlog in between makes the chain unlandable (A1; S1-V14); inside A-T11 `m < 8` has probability about `3·10⁻⁷` per term (S2 §6, reproduced there). Source: sequencing page §6 and §8; limitation L16, L19.

## 10. Anti-monopoly caps as rules

### S1-R19. The share bound, the cap and what is priced rather than bounded

**[assumed: proposed rule]** An owner with `w` of `A` domain seats in good standing is drawn primary with probability `w / A` (a suspended owner's draw is a void view and moves no other owner's rank); CAP bounds one address to `8 / A`; Sybil cost is linear in seats (the entry capital of S1-R04 per seat, the domain write's gas, and MIN_TENURE of exposure to draws and to MISS); exclusive control needs every domain seat in good standing and cannot be held against an honest entrant whose append is a plain L1 transaction live DELAY_REG later (A-LIQ). No consecutive-term cooldown and no weight halving exist (§15); binary suspension with strikes replaces both (S3). **Limitation L9 [assumed, proposed for C6's register]:** an entity's seat share is only priced, never bounded, because no owner-keyed rule can distinguish one entity's many owners from many entities; the price of primary share is linear in seats and unbounded at exclusion, but productive revenue is not linear: a cartel holding a blocking minority of a committee and of both its redraws can blank an honest primary's term, and above one third of the seats its revenue share is superlinear (L2, limitation L9; round 4, R4-C1); the collusion bound is A-T11, an assumption, not a rule. The revenue simulations, the price table and whether linear cost plus CAP plus per-incident slashing satisfies a super-linear-cost requirement are S3's (sequencing page §18 leaves the last open). **Adversary schedule:** TH9 monopolization: a share `X` of primary terms against `H` honest seats costs `H × X / (1 − X)` seats (64 seats for 50 % and 576 for 90 % against `H = 64`, §11); bounded and priced below one third, not defended above it. Source: sequencing page §5, §12 and §16, claims 6 and 7; limitation L9.

**[proven: the share bound]** Each try of the holder walk is a uniform rank over the domain under A-KECCAK, so the first try lands on one of an owner's `w` seats with probability `w / A`; the cap holds because `w ≤ 8` by S1-R03; exclusive control requires every rank to belong to the cartel, which an honest entrant breaks DELAY_REG after registering. Not proven: anything about revenue share (S3). Source: sequencing page §16.
