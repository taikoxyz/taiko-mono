## 1. Scope, vocabulary and claim convention

**[assumed: section boundary]** S1 owns the term grid and its origin, the seat and operator records, registration with its key proofs and key validity intervals, the entry and exit delays, the exits of every kind, the suspension and reactivation flags as flags (not the debits that set them), the persistent domain tree and its versions, the cycle seed and its freshness, the holder and committee walks with their exclusions, the snapshot rule and invariant I1, the assignment pin, the open-empty, dead-mode and no-committee regimes as regimes, the anti-monopoly caps as rules, and the seat bound. S2 owns `Q(m)`, certificates, timeouts, view changes and the redraw's trigger (S1 states only the redraw walk); S3 every TAIKO amount, the lapse line, MISS, strikes, the classes and the revenue simulations; S4 the role arguments; C2 landing, `replaceableFrom`, A1, the landing horizon and PIN_REWARD's ramp; C3 forced inclusion and V8; C4 the migration and the launch reading; C7 validity; C8 interfaces and storage; C6 the registers. None of their text is repeated here.

**[assumed: notation]** **assumed** heads a proposed rule or a premise; **proven** means an argument on this page, not machine verification; **open** means unresolved, with the closure condition named. An identifier `S1-Rnn` has exactly one normative definition; §11 is the only definition of S1's numbers. `‖` denotes the fields of one canonical `abi.encode` under C8's hashing convention (interfaces page, adopted from PR #22188); the four fixed exceptions to its tag-first order are the accumulator (S1-R11), the holder walk (S1-R12), the committee and redraw walk (one layout, S1-R13) and the sentinel opening object (S1-R16). "Round n, id" cites candidate A's red-team iterations; "round 5 fix pass" its verified revision after round 5.

| Term | Definition [assumed: vocabulary] |
|---|---|
| Seat | One unit of bond B_SEAT (S3) and one record `Seat{owner, activeFrom, activeUntil}` at a fixed index of an append-only array; the index never moves. |
| Owner | The address holding seats, the slashable identity (S3), whose operator record binds its keys; it may be a contract. |
| Domain at `T`, `E_t` | The seats with `activeFrom ≤ T < activeUntil` (`inDomain(i, T)`); `E_t` is their count at `termStart(t)`. Suspension is not a domain input. |
| Rank | The position of a seat among the domain seats in seat-id order; the sortition reads ranks, never seat ids. |
| Version | One immutable state of the persistent domain tree, tagged with the effective time of the write that created it. |
| `S(t)` | The highest L1 block with timestamp at most `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`: the state every input of term `t` is read from. |

## 2. Time, the snapshot and invariant I1

### S1-R01. The term grid and its origin

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `termOf(τ) = ⌊(τ − T0) / TERM⌋`, `termStart(t) = T0 + TERM × t`, `termEnd(t) = termStart(t + 1)`; `cycleStart(c) = termStart(CYCLE_TERMS × c)`, the grid's constants are §11's. T0 is zero until the DAO's second upgrade-class call `armEtna(T0)` with `T0 ≥ block.timestamp + MIGRATION_LEAD` sets it once; the upgrade transaction itself calls `initEtna` without T0, so the DAO reads the domain count and, off-chain, the owner structure before fixing the grid (round 4, R4-C5; C4 owns the migration sequence and its reading of the launch condition, S1-R13). No slot, epoch, beacon root, lookahead or `blockhash` is read anywhere in this section (R5). **Adversary schedule:** a grid fixed before the registry exists would start in no-committee mode; the DAO arms it after reading the count. Source: sequencing page §1; interfaces page `EtnaConfig`, `armEtna`.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** The grid functions `termOf`, `termStart`, `termEnd` and `cycleStart` have no failure path: they are definitional arithmetic on T0 and §11's constants, and no S1 call refuses because of them. The two refusals the rule implies for `armEtna` itself, a second call (the rule's "sets it once") and a `T0` below `block.timestamp + MIGRATION_LEAD` (the rule's requirement), belong to C4-R03, the owning rule of the migration sequence; C4-R14's catalogue declares `AlreadyArmed` among its errors, and which name each condition raises is C4's to assign, not S1's. Vector: S1-V18.

### S1-R02. Future-dated effect (Rule D), the snapshot and invariant I1

**[assumed: proposed rule]** A registry write at L1 time `τ` changes eligibility only for instants `T ≥ τ + DELAY`: DELAY_REG for entry, exit and key rotation, the only inputs that change `E_t`, a rank or a key read, and for the hard-floor suspension and `reactivate()`; DELAY_S for class A and class B suspensions and reserve lapses, which change no rank (round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2; round 6, R6S-2). This dating defers to S1-R09's permitted prospective merge gap, its one exception (an exception to this dating only, not to I1 below): a merge under S1-R09's rule (iii) may suspend an owner over a gap shorter than 480 s that starts at or after the write plus LOOKBACK, earlier than the write plus DELAY_REG but never at an instant a node has already evaluated (S1-R09's proof (c1)). Term `t` evaluates eligibility at the single instant `termStart(t)` and reads the version of the domain tree effective at `termStart(t)` (S1-R10). `S(t)` is the highest L1 block with timestamp at most `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`: a node replays registry events and the mix log up to `S(t)`; under a total L1 halt the schedule stays defined. **Invariant I1, owned by S1:** for every term inside ROLE_HORIZON, the holders, committee, eligibility, seed and domain count the inbox computes at landing time equal what nodes computed at `termStart(t)`, whatever registry writes became effective in between. Two exceptions are stated, both read at the sentinel object's L1 reference (S1-R16) and both only adding rights: dead mode (S1-R17) and the post-deadline path of no-committee mode (S1-R18), each a function of `lastLandedTerm` and the timestamp at that reference. A merge of S1-R09's suspension store is no exception: under D45(1)'s minimum record length it never changes the answer at an instant a node has already evaluated (S1-R09's proven paragraph, (c2) impossible; §13 item 19, closed). The committee role of a class-A slash is not an exception: under D38(c) S1-R13 reads `slashedAt` at the committee reference `S(t)`, as every other input of term `t` (the former second exception, which compared `slashedAt` with `termStart(t)` in a node's local view and in the inbox's view at landing, is withdrawn with its residual, §13 item 16). **Adversary schedule:** a write placed just after `S(t)` to change term `t` at landing time; by Rule D it takes effect after `termEnd(t)`, so neither view includes it. Source: sequencing page §1, §2 and §3; round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** No failure path: definitional. Rule D dates the effect of writes whose acceptance and refusal are their own rules' (S1-R04 to S1-R09); no write is refused for its dating, and `S(t)` stays defined under a total L1 halt (the rule's own sentence). I1 is an invariant, not a run-time check: no contract compares the inbox's computation with a node's, and a disagreement between them would be a defect of an implementation, which S1-V03, S1-V04 and S1-V12 test, not a verdict this rule issues.

**[proven: node snapshot equals landing-time computation, conditional on A-T1]** A write in a block after `S(t)` has timestamp above `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, so its effect falls at or after `termStart(t) − LOOKBACK + DELAY_S = termStart(t) + 2 × TERM > termEnd(t)` for the shortest delay (DELAY_S = LOOKBACK + 2 × TERM, §11), and later for DELAY_REG. Any write whose effect falls after `termStart(t)` creates a later version (S1-R10) and copies, never changes, the nodes it shares with the version effective at `termStart(t)`, so the inbox reads at landing exactly the version a node read at `S(t)`. The flag reads of S1-R09 are dated at least DELAY_S after their write, and a merge of S1-R09's store changes no answer at an instant before the write's time plus LOOKBACK, so nodes and the inbox read the same flag for every term (S1-R09's proven paragraph, whose case (c2) D45(1)'s minimum record length makes impossible). The `slashedAt` read of S1-R13's empty-slot predicate is covered as well: the predicate is `slashedAt ≠ 0 ∧ slashedAt ≤ termStart(t) − LOOKBACK`, true exactly when the slash's block lies in the prefix ending at `S(t)`, `slashedAt` being the timestamp of that block (S3-R05's record as S1 reads it; the obligation on S3 is in §16); `slashedAt` is written once per owner (S3-R08's `AlreadySlashed`) and never reset, a property of S3's storage (S3-R05's record and S3-R08; the obligation on S3 is in §16), while D38(a) guarantees only that no fresh collateral at that address meets it (S1-R04, S1-R05), so the inbox evaluating it from any later L1 state, at a pin or at a landing, obtains the value a node obtained at `S(t)`. Under A-T1 a reorg below LOOKBACK does not occur, which is what "every input to term `t` is at least 20 minutes old at term start" protects. Source: sequencing page §3 and §16, claims 1 and 2.

## 3. Seats and the entry capital

### S1-R03. The seat array, its bound and the cap

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Seats live in an append-only array of at most MAX_SEATS records (a `uint16` index, §11), in the upgraded Inbox proxy after its 43-slot gap, with the logic in a linked library so the landing path reads the registry without an external call and the existing TAIKO bond ledger is reused (R2: no new address; the syncer address stays the Inbox proxy). An owner holds at most CAP seats. Indices are contract-assigned: FREE recycled holes first, then appends at the scheduled length, so a same-block append by an incumbent never reverts an entrant's transaction (round 2). A slot becomes FREE only when the exit of the seat it held has been finalized under S1-R07 (its stake released), never before: an expired seat not yet finalized keeps its index and its record, so no entrant is assigned it and the old claim is never ambiguous (a seat exited by `exitSlashed` or `exitStale` may be finalized by anyone, S1-R07, so a slashed or abandoned owner's silence does not keep its slots out of recycling) (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-03; D19: RECYCLE_GRACE is removed from this rule and from §11). The array is dense: its length equals the number of seats ever claimed. A slot is written fresh once, when its seat is first claimed, and only overwritten afterwards, each overwrite at a registration that finds it FREE. **Adversary schedule:** exclusion by filling the array costs the entry capital of S1-R04 for every seat (§11: 2.32 billion TAIKO, above A-SUPPLY); crowding an entrant's append is impossible because indices are assigned by the contract; holding slots FREE-but-unreleased to starve entrants of holes is pointless, since appends are unbounded below MAX_SEATS and a hole is only an index. Source: sequencing page §2 and §11; interfaces page `Seat`; parameters page MAX_SEATS, CAP; D19.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** A slot that is not FREE is never assigned (an exited seat not yet finalized keeps its index and record); that is an assignment outcome, not a refusal: the registration takes the next FREE hole or appends (S1-V11). The two bounds imply a refusal the rule does not name: a `register(count)` that would leave the owner holding more than CAP seats (held seats in §1's sense) is refused as a whole, since the rule states that an owner holds at most CAP seats, S1-R19's proven paragraph relies on `w ≤ 8`, and S1-R04's `register(count)` assigns exactly `count` indices, so no partial assignment exists; and a `register(count)` that would need more indices than the FREE holes plus the appends left below MAX_SEATS is refused as a whole, since the index is a `uint16`. **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** The first reverts `OwnerCapExceeded` and the second `RegistryFull`, each for the whole call. Vector: S1-V20.

### S1-R04. Registration and the entry capital

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `register(count)` requires, as two amounts (S3-R04; D20), a nonreserve balance (S1-R09's comparator) of at least `1.05 × (seatCount + count) × B_SEAT` and a reserve of at least `(seatCount + count) × LAND_RESERVE` (funded by S3-R02's `fundReserve`; `deposit` credits the nonreserve balance only), `seatCount` the owner's held seats (§1, so a seat exited but not finalized still counts), a registered sequencer key and attester key (S1-R05), no pending exit, and an owner with no class-A `slashedAt` (S3-R05): `register` by an owner whose `slashedAt` is set reverts `OwnerSlashed`, whatever its seats, ledger or keys, before and after its terminal exit (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); D38(a)). Entry stays permissionless: a new address is a new owner with no `slashedAt`, its own ledger and fresh keys (S1-R05), and the slashed owner's records, key records and evidence attribution stay as they are. It assigns `count` indices (S1-R03), sets each seat's `activeFrom = τ + DELAY_REG`, writes one domain-tree version per seat (S1-R10) and emits `SeatClaimed` and `DomainWrite`. The three parts of the capital are S3's quantities and S1 names only their roles: seat stake `seatCount × B_SEAT` (the slashable floor), the 5 % buffer above it (where liveness debits land before seat stake), and the landing reserve `seatCount × LAND_RESERVE` (the seam to S3; its ledger properties are C2-R17's and S3's). No admission decision exists. **Adversary schedule:** a key already bound (refused, S1-R05); a class-A slashed owner that exits, finalizes, deposits fresh capital and registers unused keys at the same address, which would leave its new seats as empty committee slots under the persistent `slashedAt` and its new keys outside class-A accountability through `AlreadySlashed` (B's W12 verdict, the joint S1/S3 lifecycle finding: refused, `OwnerSlashed`; the same capital at a new address is an ordinary entrant, accountable from its first seat); a registration timed to grind a seed (effective DELAY_REG later, after the cycle's last affecting instant, S1-R11); a mass registration (priced per seat, S1-R06). Source: sequencing page §2; roles page §1 Entry; round 4, R4H-3; round 6, R6H-3.

### S1-R05. Keys: proof of control, proof of possession, validity intervals

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `registerKeys(seqKey, seqKeySig, blsPubkey, pop)` binds to the calling owner a sequencer key (a secp256k1 address with no code, S3 rule S8) whose signature `seqKeySig` is over `keccak(DOMAIN_KEYREG ‖ chainId ‖ owner)`, a proof of control, and a BLS12-381 attester key with a proof of possession over `(pubkey, owner, chainId)`; a key already bound anywhere in the registry is refused (`BadProofOfPossession`, `BadKeyControlSignature`, `KeyHasCode`), and `registerKeys` by an owner with a class-A `slashedAt` reverts `OwnerSlashed` (S1-R04; D38(a)). **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** `KeyHasCode` is the refusal of a sequencer key whose address has code, on any call that checks it; an address that gains non-EIP-7702 code after registration (for example through EIP-8298 `SETCODEFROM`) is not refused retroactively: its signature counts as absent wherever it is verified, under C5-R08's premise A-8151 (EIP-8151 ships in its A-tier form, `ecrecover` returning zero for such an address). The recovery primitive itself does not revert; the consuming check still rejects as its own rule states (a holder's required signature now absent fails C7-R05; a guest cannot establish C2-R04's assertion; an insufficient or invalid proof still reverts L11 `BadProof`), and no protocol-wide non-reversion guarantee is stated (B-W18-A06). Each key an owner ever binds is a `KeyRecord{keyHash, validFrom, validUntil}`: `validFrom = τ + DELAY_REG` of its binding, `validUntil` the instant the next key of that kind takes effect, open for the current key until one of the two closing transitions below. `rotateKey(kind, newKey, pop)` appends a record valid from `τ + DELAY_REG` and closes the old key's interval at the same instant; for a sequencer key the `pop` is its DOMAIN_KEYREG signature; a key is **pending** between its binding and its `validFrom`. **Two live intervals per kind (B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02); D38(d)):** a record is live while its `validUntil` is open or later than `now`; a `rotateKey` of a kind that already has two live records (the current key, closed at its successor's future `validFrom`, and that pending successor) reverts `KeyRotationPending`, so at most one rotation per kind is pending and the next is accepted from the pending key's `validFrom` on, when the closed record stops being live. The transition is total: every rotation write either appends the second live record or reverts, and no record is merged, shortened or dropped by a rotation. **Retirement (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-02; D19):** the second closing transition is the owner's terminal exit (§1, S1-R07): at that exit's `activeUntil`, both current keys get `validUntil := activeUntil`, and a pending key of either kind (bound, `validFrom` not yet reached) is dropped, its record closed at the same instant with `validFrom = validUntil` so that it was never valid; a partial exit touches no key record. Because `rotateKey` and `requestExit` are both dated `τ + DELAY_REG` and effective times are monotone in transaction order (S1-R06), a rotation requested before the exit request takes effect at or before `activeUntil` and is the current key closed there (valid on `[its validFrom, activeUntil)`, empty when the two requests share a block time); only a rotation requested after the exit request is still pending at `activeUntil` and is dropped. A closed key stays liable until `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW`; key records persist after retirement, so `keyOwnerAt` resolves a retired key for every term it was valid in and evidence against those terms remains verifiable. A key hash that appears in any record, closed or not, is bound and is refused at `registerKeys` and `rotateKey`, so an owner that returns after a terminal exit binds fresh keys; only an owner with no class-A `slashedAt` returns (D38(a)). Every read of an owner's key for term `t` (C7's V1, `isHolder`, the committee walk, `committeeRoot[t]`, `keyOwnerAt`) takes the key valid at `termStart(t)`, so a rotation effective after a term starts, up to ROLE_HORIZON before its landing, changes nothing for that term (round 6, R6S-4). **Adversary schedule:** a third rotation of one kind while one is pending, whose effect on the pending record no rule stated (refused, `KeyRotationPending`, D38(d)); a slashed owner rebinding fresh keys at its own address (`OwnerSlashed`, D38(a)); a front-run of a victim's `registerKeys` fails, lacking the key's signature over the victim's address (round 6, judge's note); a rotation inside a landing window to change the pinned root (R6S-4); a BLS key without possession, a framing vector for S2's certificate-pair rule (slashing page §1); a rotation requested before the exit request (current at `activeUntil` and closed there, liable for the terms it was valid in); a rotation requested after the exit request to leave a key that never answers for the exited seats' duties (pending at `activeUntil` and dropped, so no duty was ever dated under it); evidence of a duty of an exited seat against a retired key (resolved through the persistent record, dated by its term, inside `validUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW`). Source: interfaces page §1 `KeyRecord`, `registerKeys`, `rotateKey`; slashing page §1; roles page §1 Entry; D19.

### S1-R06. DELAY_REG, MIN_TENURE and the eligibility-input rule

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Entry and exit are the only inputs to the rank domain, both owner-triggered writes dated `τ + DELAY_REG`. `requestExit` is accepted for a seat only once `τ + DELAY_REG ≥ activeFrom + MIN_TENURE`, so every seat is in the domain, drawn and bled for at least a week and every domain write takes effect exactly at `τ + DELAY_REG`, monotone in transaction order (round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass). Churn is priced, not capped: no rule limits writes per minute, each pays its own tree version (§11), and no incumbent can delay or crowd out an entrant (round 5 fix pass; the per-slot cap is §15's). Suspensions of every kind are per-owner flags read only after a draw (S1-R09); none changes `E_t` or a rank. **Adversary schedule:** register-and-exit churn to dilute others (sortition is over registered seats only, S1-R12, so it dilutes nobody; round 3, H5); a self-inflicted suspension after the seed is fixed (voids only the owner's own draws, S1-R09). Source: sequencing page §2 and §5; parameters page §1; round 4, R4-C2; round 5, R5S-2.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** The tenure requirement is a refusal the rule states without a name: for a seat with `τ + DELAY_REG < activeFrom + MIN_TENURE` named in a `requestExit`, that seat's exit is not accepted; **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** the call reverts `TenureNotMet`, and a multi-seat `requestExit` is atomic: it reverts as a whole if any named seat fails tenure. Effective times need no failure path: they are `τ + DELAY_REG`, monotone because L1 block timestamps are. Vector: S1-V19.

## 4. Exits

### S1-R07. Owner-requested exit and release

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `requestExit(seats)` takes any count of the caller's seats (round 5, R5-C4), each a domain write under S1-R06's tenure rule, sets each seat's `activeUntil = τ + DELAY_REG`, emits `ExitRequested` and `DomainWrite`, and keeps the seat in the domain until then. Seat exit and key retirement are separate (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), B-S1-02; D19). An exit is classified at its `activeUntil` (§1): terminal iff no seat of the owner is active or pending at that instant. A seat pending when the exit is requested is active by then, because `register` refuses an owner with a pending exit (S1-R04), so every pending seat was registered earlier and its `activeFrom` precedes the exit's `activeUntil`; two exits sharing one `activeUntil` (two `requestExit` calls in one block) that leave nothing active are both terminal and run S1-R05's retirement once. **Partial exit:** when some seat of the owner is active or pending at `activeUntil`, the keys stay current for the remaining seats and no key record changes; `finalizeExit(seats)` releases the exiting seats, in the one sense of the held-seat withdrawal rule below (their share of the locked floor drops; nothing is transferred), once `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` has passed for every seat named in the call. Evidence against a duty of an exited seat is bounded by that window because every duty is dated by its term (S1-R05, S3's windows), so the open `validUntil` of a current key never delays a partial release. **Terminal exit:** when no seat of the owner is active or pending at `activeUntil`, that instant is the instant at which S1-R05's retirement transition runs (both current keys closed at `validUntil := activeUntil`, a pending rotation dropped); `finalizeExit` of that seat releases it at `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW` (the floor drops to zero), the same instant as the keys' liability ends, and the owner is RETIRED (§12) with its key records kept. The two paths are one function: `finalizeExit(seats)` requires every named seat to be EXITING or EXPIRED with its `activeUntil + EVIDENCE_WINDOW ≤ now`, not yet finalized, and either the caller's or exited by `exitSlashed` or `exitStale` (S1-R08), which anyone may finalize, the release accruing to the seat's owner's ledger in every case (so a slashed or abandoned owner's slots do not stay unrecycled for want of its call); a seat already finalized is FREE with no owner, so a second call for it reverts (`NotExiting`); finalization clears the seat's owner and makes the slot FREE (S1-R03) in the same transaction as the release, and nothing else makes a slot FREE. **The held-seat withdrawal rule (stated once here; S3-R04 consumes it verbatim, §16; B's W12 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-02), nonblocking clarification; D38).** Let `h` be the owner's held seats (§1: pending, active, EXITING or EXPIRED, that is, not yet finalized). (i) While `h ≥ 1`, a withdrawal takes only from the nonreserve balance (`balance − reserveGwei − sentinelGwei`, S3-R02, which is `balance − reserveGwei` for an owner that never arms), and at most `max(0, min(nonreserve − 1.05 × h × B_SEAT, balance − (1.05 × h × B_SEAT + h × LAND_RESERVE)))`: it leaves the nonreserve balance at or above the stake and buffer of the held seats and the ledger at or above the whole floor, so reserve held above `h × LAND_RESERVE` never stands in for nonreserve stake, which class-B and MISS debits alone can reach (S3-R02) and which S3-R02's "non-withdrawable while the owner holds any seat" and S3-R04's citation of this rule protect (revised 2026-10-04, ECON-4-R1: earlier "S3-R02's \"non-withdrawable while seats are active\" and S3-R04's \"limited to the nonreserve balance above `1.05 × floor`\"", a sentence S3-R04 no longer contains). (ii) `finalizeExit` of `count` seats transfers and credits nothing: it sets `h := h − count` and then caps the reserve, `reserveGwei := min(reserveGwei, h × LAND_RESERVE)`, the excess becoming nonreserve; that is the whole release, a drop of the locked floor by `count × (1.05 × B_SEAT + LAND_RESERVE)` on the two pockets as they stand after every reward, MISS and slash debit. (iii) No part of either pocket is allocated to a seat: a debit taken before finalization stays with the owner's pockets, so a finalization frees at most what the pockets hold above the new floor and nothing when they are below it (the shortfall stays with the remaining seats under S1-R09's flags), and nothing is minted. ## 6. The persistent domain tree

### S1-R10. Versions tagged by effective time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The contract keeps the domain as a persistent (versioned) segment tree over seat ids whose leaves are `inDomain` bits and whose nodes hold counts. Every domain write (an entry or an exit; nothing else, since suspensions and lapses are flags) creates a new version by path copying, about 16 fresh nodes per seat, tagged with its effective time `E = τ + DELAY_REG` and logged as `DomainVersion{effectiveTime, root, count}` with a `DomainWrite(version, seat, effectiveTime, delta)` event. Effective times are monotone in transaction order (S1-R06), so the log is sorted with no reordering. `domainVersionAt(T)` is the latest version with `E ≤ T`, a binary search of the log; term `t` reads `domainVersionAt(termStart(t))`, `domainCount(t)` its count `E_t` and `seatAtRank(t, r)` a descent in it. A version is immutable once written, and every version with `E ≤ termStart(t)` exists from `termStart(t) − DELAY_REG` on. No mutation ring, correction set, lazy application or per-slot cap exists: a walk reads one version and its cost does not depend on how many writes happened since the term started. **Adversary schedule:** writes timed inside a term's landing window to change its computed holders (they create a later version; round 4, R4H-1); writes to inflate the gas of a stale pin (one version per walk; round 5 fix pass, the mutation ring of §15). Source: sequencing page §5 and §10; interfaces page `DomainVersion`, `domainVersionAt`, `domainCount`, `seatAtRank`.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** No failure path for the domain writes' versions and for `domainVersionAt` and `domainCount`, which are definitional; the one refusal is an external `seatAtRank` out of range, below. A version is created only by a domain write whose acceptance or refusal is its own rule's (`register`, S1-R04; `requestExit`, S1-R06 and S1-R07; `exitSlashed` and `exitStale`, S1-R08), and `domainVersionAt`, `domainCount` and `seatAtRank` are reads; `domainCount(t) = 0` is a defined value, S1-R15's first open-empty case, not a failure. **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** `domainVersionAt(T)` for a `T` earlier than every logged effective time returns the empty version with count 0, with no revert; an external `seatAtRank(t, r)` with `r ≥ E_t` reverts `RankOutOfRange`.

**[assumed, unmeasured: the tree's gas]** A domain write costs about 16 fresh nodes plus execution gas, paid by the writer, for an entry and an exit alike (§11); state gas is metered outside the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap, so `register(8)` fits one call (round 6, R6S-4). A walk is a sequence of rank lookups, each a descent of `log₂(registry size)` node reads in one version; §11's figures are the round-6 simulation's (A-GAS), independent of later writes.

## 7. The cycle seed

### S1-R11. The accumulator, the seed and freshness

**[assumed: proposed rule]** **RND1.** `land`, every registry mutation and the public no-op `poke()` execute, at most once per L1 block, `seedAcc = keccak256(abi.encode(bytes32 seedAcc, uint256 block.prevrandao))` and emit `Poked`, so nodes replay the mix log in block order. **RND2.** `seed(c) = keccak(DOMAIN ‖ chainId ‖ seedAcc_at(T_seed(c)) ‖ c)` with `T_seed(c) = cycleStart(c) − LOOKBACK`; the first mixing call in a block with timestamp at or after `T_seed(c)` is the fixing call: it reads `seedAcc` as stored when it begins, before its own block's mix, fixes the seed on-chain (`SeedFixed`, carrying `fresh`) and only then mixes. **Cutoff order (B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01), clarification; D19):** `seedAcc_at(T_seed(c))` is the accumulator after the last mix in the committed L1 prefix that precedes the fixing call in transaction order; since RND1 mixes at most once per block and the fixing call is the first mixing call at or after `T_seed(c)`, that is exactly the set of mixes in blocks with timestamp below `T_seed(c)`, and a mix in the fixing call's own block counts iff it precedes the fixing call, which none does. Nodes compute the same value from the log without waiting, as the accumulator after the last `Poked` in a block with timestamp below `T_seed(c)`. **RND3 (freshness) and its fallback.** `fresh(c)` iff the last mix counted by the cutoff above lies in a block with timestamp after `freshFrom(c) = cycleStart(c) − 3,660 s`, the last instant at which a registry write can affect cycle `c` (§11); "a mix after `freshFrom(c)`" means that and nothing else. `fresh(c)` is evaluated once, at the fixing, and stored with the seed; a mix after the fixing call, a `poke()` included, never relabels a stale seed and never changes the fixed seed. `seedOf(c)` returns the seed, `fresh` and `fixed`. **Fallback (J's review of #22201, 2026-10-01, Medium J-1, under the arbiter's decision):** the seed of cycle `c` is fixed at `T_seed(c)` from the accumulator as it stands, mixed after `freshFrom(c)` or not; no rule waits for a mix, no rewarded action guarantees one (a landing mixes, but nothing pays for a landing in order to mix), and a `poke()` is a voluntary call nobody is paid to make. When `fresh(c)` is false, the last mixer, the sender of the last mixing transaction at or before `freshFrom(c)`, held the only choice over `seed(c)`: one accumulator value per write it made (a registry write or a `poke()`, each a transaction it paid for), so it can bias at most cycle `c`'s selection (per cycle; a stall past `freshFrom(c + 1)` extends the same choice to `c + 1`, within the outage regime of S1-R17), choosing among as many candidate seeds as writes it paid for; when its last write is a registry write, that write may also edit the domain of cycle `c`'s last terms with the seed known (assumed, not proven here); and it cannot delay or prevent the fixing, which RND2 ties to `T_seed(c)` and to the first mixing call at or after it. Every seat has an incentive to `poke()` after `freshFrom(c)`, since an unfresh seed invites bias against it, and a `poke()` is a cheap call whose gas is unmeasured (§11; default 75,000 gas derived in C6 MP-21). An unfresh seed needs no landing, registry write or `poke()` between `freshFrom(c)` and `T_seed(c)`, 2,460 s (§11). That gap fits inside LAND_WINDOW_MAX (C2 §12), so it can occur on a slow but live chain. A chain with no landing for a whole cycle (CYCLE_TERMS × TERM = 3,600 s) is in the outage regime, within 15 terms of dead mode (DEAD_TERMS = 75, S1-R17). The unpriced dependency of freshness on an unpaid call is limitation LS1-1 (§13), placed on C6's accepted-limitations register (§16). **Adversary schedule:** grinding by registry writes (none when fresh; when not fresh, the fallback's bound: the last mixer's choice among its own paid writes, per cycle, and a domain edit of cycle `c`'s last terms if its last write was a registry write); L1-proposer bias (the proposers of the last `m` blocks before `T_seed(c)` hold two bits each under A-RANDAO, so `m` colluding proposers choose among at most `4^m` seeds for one 60-term cycle, the bound of every RANDAO consumer). Rejected seed sources are §15's. Source: sequencing page §4 and §16, claim 5; interfaces page hashing convention, `poke`, `seedOf`, `seedAccumulator`; J's review of #22201 (J-1) for the fallback; B's W8 verdict on #22201 (2026-10-01) and D19 for the cutoff order.

**[assumed: failure behaviour, stated from the rule text without a new choice (D44 item 3; owner S1 by D71)]** No failure path on seed grounds: the accumulator, the fixing call and `seedOf` refuse nothing. The degraded outcome is defined, not a failure: with no mix after `freshFrom(c)` the seed is still fixed from the accumulator as it stands, `fresh(c)` false (the fallback clause, limitation LS1-1); before the fixing call `seedOf(c)` reports `fixed` false; a `poke()` after the fixing changes neither `seed(c)` nor `fresh(c)` (S1-V05). **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** A second `poke()` in the same L1 block returns without mixing and does not revert; for `land` and the registry writes the mix is a side effect of calls whose acceptance other rules govern.

**[proven: no grinding of any kind when the seed is fresh, conditional on A-KECCAK and on a mix after `freshFrom(c)`]** Every input that changes `E_t` or a rank is DELAY_REG-dated (S1-R06), the DELAY_REG inequality of §11 puts `freshFrom(c)` before `T_seed(c)`, and no suspension, reserve lapse or self-submitted slash changes a rank (S1-R09), so no owner write made after the seed could be known changes which seats any committee or view of cycle `c` draws. Without a mix after `freshFrom(c)` the claim does not hold and the fallback clause's bound applies: the last mixer's choice is bounded as S1-R11's fallback clause states (assumed, not proven here); when its last write is a registry write, that write may also edit the domain of cycle `c`'s last terms with the seed known. ## 8. The term walk

### S1-R12. Ranks, the holder walk, views and void views

**[assumed: proposed rule]** `idx(t, k) = keccak(seed(c) ‖ 0x01 ‖ t ‖ k) mod E_t` (one fixed `abi.encode(bytes32 seed, uint8 1, uint32 t, uint16 k)`, an exception to the tag-first order) is a rank among the domain seats at `termStart(t)` in seat-id order; the seat at rank `r` is the r-th such seat id, read from the version of S1-R10. **Walk.** For `k = 0 .. ### S1-R15. Open-empty terms

**[assumed: proposed rule; the one definition]** A term is open-empty iff its domain is empty at `termStart(t)` (`E_t = 0`, so `|H| = 0`) or every view the walk draws for it (views 0 to V_MAX) is void, its owner suspended or lapsed at `termStart(t)`; without the second case such a term would idle to its end (round 6, R6H-2). ## 10. Anti-monopoly caps as rules

### S1-R19. The share bound, the cap and what is priced rather than bounded

**[assumed: proposed rule]** An owner with `w` of `A` domain seats in good standing is drawn primary with probability `w / A` (a suspended owner's draw is a void view and moves no other owner's rank); CAP bounds one address to `8 / A`; Sybil cost is linear in seats (the entry capital of S1-R04 per seat, the domain write's gas, and MIN_TENURE of exposure to draws and to MISS); exclusive control needs every domain seat in good standing and cannot be held against an honest entrant whose append is a plain L1 transaction live DELAY_REG later (A-LIQ).
