## 3. What Etna needs from L1 today

### C5-R01. The required L1 set, and no consensus-layer dependency

**[assumed: proposed rule]** At launch Etna's correctness depends on these L1 facts and on no others: (a) blobs: EIP-4844 `BLOBHASH` (read by the inbox, C2-R05 L6) and the KZG commitments whose versioned hashes bind the proof to blob content; the point-evaluation precompile (0x0A, 50,000 gas, EIP-4844, as cited 2026-09-30, [S02 §4.6]) is listed by the page as required, and whether the inbox calls it is open (§14 item 12); (b) PeerDAS as deployed, EIP-7594, 21 blobs per block since BPO2 (mainnet epoch 419072, re-verified 2026-10-01 in the mainnet `BLOB_SCHEDULE`), the baseline of about 224 KiB/s (§8); (c) EIP-7825, a transaction gas cap of 16,777,216, a constraint a landing must satisfy (C5-R04); (d) `ecrecover` (with the caveat of C5-R08) and the EIP-2537 BLS12-381 precompiles for attestation and evidence verification; (e) `block.timestamp` for every deadline in seconds, `block.prevrandao` (EIP-4399, Final) for the seed snapshot (S1-R11) and L1 block hashes through `BLOCKHASH` and the EIP-2935 contract (C5-R02). None of these comes from Glamsterdam or Hegotá. There is **no consensus-layer dependency**: no proposer lookahead (EIP-7917), no slot-length constant, no builder or relay API, no FOCIL, no EXECUTE precompile; no rule of Etna reads an L1 slot number, epoch or beacon root. Fallback for (a) and (b): none is specified. R7 asks for explicit data availability in the landing and permits either blobs or calldata; blobs are Etna's design choice, made because calldata costs 64 gas per byte after EIP-7976 and C2's data binding is to versioned hashes, not because R7 excludes calldata (corrected after B's W12 verdict, comment 5945082389). A calldata path would be a design change in C2, not a violation of R7. **Adversary schedule:** a rule or parameter that smuggles in a slot, epoch or lookahead input; a data-availability path through calldata that bypasses C2's versioned-hash binding. (Page l1-dependencies, "Required" table and its last row; survey §0 and §4.6; parameters page §1 header, the R5 audit; no red-team round changed the table.)

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no failure path: definitional. The rule lists the L1 facts Etna's correctness rests on and adds no check of its own; a revert where one of them is read belongs to the reading rule (C2-R05 L6 for `BLOBHASH`, C5-R02 for block hashes, C5-R04 for the EIP-7825 cap). The text states that no fallback is specified for (a) and (b), so Etna does not handle the absence of a listed primitive; a fork-date slip is C5-R18 row 9. Vector: L02 (§11).

### C5-R02. L1 history reads: blockhash and EIP-2935 only, windows in seconds

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Etna reads an L1 block hash by block number only through `blockhash` (the last 256 blocks) or the EIP-2935 contract (the last 8,191 blocks): by the inbox's anchor-tip check (C2-R05 L7), by the hatch's anchor `A`, whose hash the inbox reads by `blockhash` or EIP-2935 (C3-R07 check 5), by the comparison of a REPLACE or RESUME `l1Ref` (S2) and by S3d's signed L1 reference. A read outside the window fails closed (C2's `AnchorTooOld`; S3d's evidence reverts). No rule reads L1's EIP-4788 contract, or any L1 beacon root, by slot timestamp or otherwise: under ePBS an Empty slot N leaves root(N-1) unwritten (an inference from the Gloas specification, **unverified as observed behaviour**, [S02 §4.7]), and the ring holds 8,191 slot roots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots. A window that must be served from EIP-2935 is stated in seconds and is less than `W_2935(s) = 8,191 × s` at the shortest supported slot time `s`, or is stated in block numbers. **[proven: arithmetic, conditional on at most one execution block per slot]** 8,191 consecutive blocks span at least 8,191 slots, so the EIP-2935 window never falls below `8,191 × s` seconds, and Skipped or Empty slots only lengthen it; the table is in §8. (Page l1-dependencies, "Slot-time robustness" rows for the 4788 window and EIP-2935 / BLOCKHASH and the "Optional" paragraph on beacon roots; anchor-free blocks, issue #22147; round 6 fix pass for `FI_ANCHOR_LAG`.) **Adversary schedule:** an anchor tip or evidence reference older than the window at a 2-s slot; a probe of L1's 4788 contract at an Empty slot.

### C5-R03. The L2 oracle windows do not move with L1 slot time

**[assumed: proposed rule]** L2's EIP-4788 ring is keyed by L2 timestamp and holds 8,191 seconds of entries; L2's EIP-2935 contract serves the last 8,191 L2 blocks, about 2.28 hours at one block per second (limitation L21). Both are EIP constants and neither is denominated in L1 slots, so a change of L1 slot time moves the L1 windows of C5-R02 and no L2 window. The reveal-by-timestamp window is the one C1 states: an entry for timestamp `t` is guaranteed readable by a block with timestamp `T` only if `T - t` is strictly under 8,191 s, because a jump of exactly 8,191 s overwrites it in the next block (C1 §6 P-RING, §7 ring bound; C1-R08 step 5); a pinned origin has no deadline (C1-R05). C5 adds nothing to those rules and defines no L2 parameter. **[proven: from the page constants]** 8,191 is the EIP-4788 `HISTORY_BUFFER_LENGTH` and the EIP-2935 `HISTORY_SERVE_WINDOW` (re-verified 2026-10-01, [E4788], [E2935]). **Adversary schedule:** a reveal that relies on a timestamp window counted in L1 slots; an L1 fork that shortens slots and is wrongly read as shortening L2's reveal window. (Page l1-dependencies, slot-time row "L1's EIP-4788 root window"; anchor-free blocks, issue #22147.)

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no failure path of its own: definitional, because C5 "adds nothing" to C1's rules. A reveal whose timestamp entry has left the ring fails as C1-R08 step 5 states (`EtnaOracleUnavailable`), and a pinned origin has no deadline (C1-R05); C1 owns both. Vector: L01.

### C5-R04. Per-block L1 limits shrink with slot time; every Etna capacity is per second

**[assumed: proposed rule]** No Etna rule counts L1 blocks per batch or blobs per batch as a fixed number. Capacity limits are per second and per landing; a landing is under the budget `LANDING_GAS_BUDGET` (C2 §12) so that it also fits the smallest block of §8 (**[open: C6 MP-05]** C2's "under 2.7 M worst" underprices REPLACE and RESUME view changes, and an uncapped landing of MAX_VIEWS such view changes exceeds the budget; MP-05 gives a type/count-aware accounting, in which 13.8 M is an illustrative subtotal, not a bound, and records a proposed disposition for C2 and C5 that keeps this budget: costed witness admission caps with range splitting and permissionless base-term pre-pinning, an irreducible-landing check over the longest VC chain through segmentless views and terms plus the next segment's end certificate (its fit open; before launch a VC-only advance of `lastVCHash`, landing acceptance of recorded VCs without re-verifying their witness, or a bound on the chain), and, if a single REPLACE or RESUME landing with its end certificate and permitted redraws does not fit at MAX_SEATS, a permissionless cached or chunked redraw verification before launch), and the 16,777,216 cap of EIP-7825 is a ceiling above that budget, not a target. At a 2-s slot a block carries 3 blobs, so a range may need several landings; C2's per-landing caps and the paid-landing proof (C2-R16) absorb that and are not restated. **[proven: arithmetic]** Under the 60 M schedule a 2-s block is 10 M gas, so a 10 M budget equals the whole block and the 16,777,216 cap does not fit it (168 percent); under the 200 M schedule the cap is 8.4, 16.8, 25.2 and 50.3 percent of a 12-, 6-, 4- and 2-s block. S1's committee pin fits a 10 M block through 4,096 seats (about 5.4 M gas, assumed) and only the 65,535-seat maximum (about 10.1 M) needs more (page, slot-time row "Gas per block"). (Page l1-dependencies, "Required" row on EIP-7825 and the slot-time rows for gas and blobs per block; survey §5; round 6, R6H-3 for the per-landing reward unit.) **Adversary schedule:** an L1 fork that halves the block gas limit and blob limit (EIP-7782) or scales them with slot time (EIP-8198) and leaves a landing that no longer fits.

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no Etna failure path of its own. A landing transaction whose gas exceeds 16,777,216 is not a valid L1 transaction under EIP-7825 (L1's behaviour, the constraint of C5-R01 (c)); the per-landing caps and their failure (C2-R01: an oversized landing "is not includable, never slashable") are C2's and are not restated. Whether every landing fits LANDING_GAS_BUDGET stays **[open: C6 MP-05]** as stated above. Vector: L03.

## 4. Environment changes Etna must survive

### C5-R05. ePBS, Empty slots and the canonical-at-N+1 premise behind ANCHOR_MIN_AGE

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Etna treats a landing as safely included on L1 only when a later block has been built on its block, which A-CANON makes canonical in practice (C2's LANDED_CONFIRM_DEPTH of one block and the level L1-safe, C2-R20) and treats an L1 block as the same block for every honest node only when it is at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old (C7-R09, A-L1VIEW). The premise that links the two is **A-CANON with `L_canon(s) = 1 to 1.5 slots`**: payload due at 50 percent of the slot (`PAYLOAD_DUE_BPS: 5000`), payload attestation at 75 percent (`PAYLOAD_ATTESTATION_DUE_BPS: 7500`), the next proposer building at the next slot boundary, giving +12 to +18 s at 12-s slots, +6 to +9, +4 to +6 and +2 to +3 s at 6-, 4- and 2-s slots (page, slot-time diagram; survey §4.1). ANCHOR_MIN_AGE is C7's number; the page's rationale for it is "at least 2.5 times ePBS canonical-at-N+1". **C5's reading of that rationale, [proven: arithmetic] and [assumed: reading]:** with the page's upper figure `L_canon(12 s) = 18 s`, `2.5 × 18 = 45 s ≤ 48 s`; the inequality `ANCHOR_MIN_AGE ≥ 2.5 × L_canon` tolerates `L_canon` up to `48 / 2.5 = 19.2 s`, a margin of 1.2 s at the top of the 12-s range (19.2 s is the tolerated `L_canon`, not the margin) (7.5 s needed at 2 s); read with the lower figure (12 s) the product is 30 s, the reading C7 item 11 found underived. Which the page intends is open (§14 item 8). An Empty slot leaves the transaction unexecuted; it delays inclusion by one slot and consumes deadline seconds, and cannot cause a replacement, which needs a landed proof (landing page §16 robustness row); in shape 2 the expiry frame makes an expired copy, and the trailing status `VERIFY` a stale copy, invalid and free (C5-R12). Under ePBS a landing is confirmed one slot later than today, a delay the one-second preconfirmations hide (R4); if ePBS slips, the design is more conservative than needed (C5-R18 row 5). **Adversary schedule:** a builder that wins the bid and withholds the payload (Empty slot); a reorganization of N after N+1 was seen (A-CANON: more than 20 percent of the stake suffices, what is necessary is assumed, §14 item 7); a longer `L_canon` than the table (C5-R18 row 4). (Page l1-dependencies, "Tolerate" ePBS row and the slot-time diagram; parameters page §2, row ANCHOR_MIN_AGE; limitation L11.)

### C5-R06. Gas repricing is tolerated; the hot path writes only overwritten slots

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The Glamsterdam repricings (EIP-8037 state gas, EIP-8038 access costs, EIP-7976 calldata floor, EIP-7778 refund accounting, EIP-2780 intrinsic decomposition) change what Etna's L1 calls cost, not whether they work. Rule: the landing hot path writes only overwritten ring slots; a fresh slot is written only by a registration, an exit, an offence record or an L1 checkpoint word, and is paid by the writer; **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]** exempt from "the hot path writes only overwritten slots" are ring-slot writes on the ring's first lap, a hatch's fresh `hatchTip[block.number]` slot (C3-R07) and a first credit to a new pull-ledger account (an includer's entry under C3-R03, a `rewardTo` with no prior entry under C2-R16), whose state gas the transaction that writes them pays (the lander, the hatch submitter or the saver); state gas lies outside the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap, so a registration of eight seats (about 13 M state gas) fits one call (page "Tolerate" gas row, round 6, R6S-4). The L1 constants are in §8; the consumers keep their own arithmetic (S1 for the domain-tree writes, C2-R15 for the two fresh checkpoint words, about 196k state gas). The values are **not settled**: EIP-8368 and EIP-8372 recalibrate state-gas pricing after 30 days of Glamsterdam mainnet data (cited 2026-09-30); if the repricings slip, every cost is only lower [S02 §4.8]; no scheduled EIP reprices precompiles (EIP-7904 is analysis only), so the pairing and KZG costs stand. **Adversary schedule:** a repricing that makes a registration or a checkpoint write dearer.

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no runtime failure path: a repricing changes cost, not whether a call works, and a fresh-slot write is paid by its writer. The rule constrains the implementation's write set, and a departure from it is found by L04's trace, not by a revert of any call. Other rules state fresh-slot writes on the landing path that the rule's list does not name: a hatch's `hatchTip[block.number]` (C3-R07, C2-R09), a first credit to an includer's pull ledger (C3-R03) and a first reward credit to a `rewardTo` with no ledger entry (C2-R16). Those writes, with first-lap ring writes, are the rule's stated exemption **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]**, not nonconformances. Vector: L04.

### C5-R07. The L1 header profile for the guest's parser and C1's reveal

**[assumed: proposed rule; answers the "joint C1/C2/C5 pin of the supported L1 fork" of C2 §16]** The supported L1 header profile is the live fork's header plus the fields scheduled forks append. The four positions that the guest and C1-R08 read (parent hash 0, state root 3, number 8, timestamp 11) are unchanged by every scheduled EIP: EIP-7928 adds `block_access_list_hash` and EIP-7843 adds `slotNumber` (uint64) to the Glamsterdam header (both `Review`, re-verified 2026-10-01), and EIP-7668 (Hegotá CFI, `Stagnant`) empties `logsBloom`, which changes a field's length and no position. C1's bounds (12 to 32 scalar fields, 1,536 bytes, C1 §6) are accepted for the supported forks if a header read from each supported network at implementation stays inside them; the live item count and RLP length are **unmeasured** (§14 item 15). The guest hashes anchored headers and parses parent hash, number and timestamp itself; on L2 only `revealCheckpoint` decodes one. A fork that moved one of the four positions needs, in the page's words, "a guest release and an L2 SignalService upgrade"; C5's reading is that the L2 SignalService needs an upgrade only if index 3 or 8 moves, because `revealCheckpoint` decodes only items 3 and 8, and never an inbox upgrade; and is **not defended** by the published parameters (TH25; bridge-migration page §8); none is scheduled. The durable form of this claim is a rule, not a forecast (J-1 of J's review of #22209): **a fork that inserts or moves a field at or before index 11 needs a new parser profile version, and the guest release and upgrade that profile requires, before Etna follows that fork**; the implementation's regression check reads a live header of each supported fork and compares it with C1's bounds and the four positions. Beacon-state layout changes (EIP-7688, EIP-8015) are irrelevant: no beacon proof is read (C5-R02). (Page l1-dependencies "Tolerate" header row; survey §2 and §3; round: anchor-free blocks, issue #22147.) **Adversary schedule:** a fork that inserts a field before index 11; a header larger than the parser cap, unrevealable by timestamp (a pin does not cure it).

### C5-R08. Signing keys under EIP-8151

**[assumed: proposed rule]** Every key that signs an attestation, a seal or evidence is a code-less account or a BLS key, because EIP-8151 makes `ecrecover` return zero for an address with non-EIP-7702 code (Proposed for Inclusion, Draft, A-tier; EIP-8298 `SETCODEFROM` interacts), cited 2026-09-30. The rule binds S1 (key registration) and S3 (S8, the 7702 residual); C5 states only the L1 dependency. **Assumed:** the EIP ships, if at all, in its A-tier form (A-8151). If it does not ship, nothing changes. (Page l1-dependencies, "Tolerate" and "Hazards" rows; survey §4.)

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** C5 states no revert. The consequence the rule names is EIP-8151's: `ecrecover` returns zero for a signer address with non-EIP-7702 code, so a signature by such a key never verifies as that address in the consumer's check; a key whose address has code at registration is refused, `KeyHasCode` (S1-R05); an address that gains non-7702 code after registration is not refused retroactively: its signature counts as absent when it is verified, with no revert of the enclosing call, and S1 exposes the condition as `KeyHasCode` on any call that checks it (S1-R05) **[assumed: arbiter answer, D72]**; S3's consumers are not in tree (S3 **open**). If EIP-8151 does not ship, nothing changes. Vector: L05 **[open: fixture after code]**.

### C5-R09. The blob market

**[assumed: proposed rule]** The blob fee of a landing tends to be at least `n × 8,192 × base_fee` in gas-equivalents at every slot time (EIP-7918, `Final`: reserve price `BLOB_BASE_COST = 2**13` times the base fee per blob, re-verified 2026-10-01 as `Final`; the EIP says the blob base fee "will settle at a level where a blob costs at least BLOB_BASE_COST execution gas" [E7918 L145], an equilibrium floor and not a per-block bound, **assumed** as a hard bound where C5-R15 uses it), so a part-empty blob wastes money and a lander fills blobs before posting (C2-R02's packing); the sparse blobpool (EIP-8070, eth/72, `Review`) lets a node drop a blob transaction, so a lander submits to provider nodes or builders; the lander reward must cover the reserve price at every slot time (C2-R16). No value of this rule is Etna's: the reward constants are C2's and S3's. **Adversary schedule:** a blob base fee at its reserve price during low demand that makes the break-even fee of C2-R16 optimistic; a blob transaction dropped by a sparse pool. (Page "Tolerate" blob-market row; survey §4.6.)

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no failure path: definitional. The rule states a market premise and the lander's response to it, and no value of it is Etna's; a blob transaction dropped by a sparse pool is resubmitted by the lander to provider nodes or builders, not refused by Etna, and whether the reward covers the reserve price is C2-R16's. Vector: L06.

## 5. What Etna takes from Glamsterdam and Hegotá, and what it does not

### C5-R10. FOCIL (EIP-7805): the floor under small legs, nothing under a blob-carrying landing

**[assumed: proposed rule; answers C3's obligation on C5]** Etna depends on FOCIL for nothing. Where it ships, it is an L1 inclusion floor for small, blob-free transactions: calldata forced-inclusion saves, the blob-free hatch, slashing evidence, registrations and withdrawals. C3's FOCIL-protected legs are calldata saves, the hatch (blob-free) and withdrawals; blob saves are not protected (L12); before Hegotá the floor is T1 (C3-R12). ### C5-R12. The two shapes, the direct land, and which transaction uses which

**[assumed: proposed rule; the one normative statement of the layouts, C2-R20 citing it]**

```text
Shape 1: public mempool, sender = gate G, nonce = nonce(G)        (four frames, three VERIFY and one SENDER)
  0 VERIFY  target EXPIRY_VERIFIER, flags 0, data = 8-byte expiry timestamp
  1 VERIFY  target G (= tx.sender), flags APPROVE_EXECUTION (0x2): G's pins, APPROVE(0x2)
  2 VERIFY  target P (payer, an EOA with empty code), flags APPROVE_PAYMENT (0x1):
            default code, signature index 1, APPROVE(0x1)
  3 SENDER  target INBOX, data = landFor(P, input)
  signatures: [0: ARBITRARY, empty; 1: SECP256K1, signer = P (20 bytes), empty msg]
Shape 2: direct to builder, sender = actor P, nonce = nonce(P)    (four frames, three VERIFY and one SENDER)
  0 VERIFY  target EXPIRY_VERIFIER, flags 0, data = 8-byte expiry timestamp
  1 VERIFY  target P (= tx.sender, an EOA with empty code), flags APPROVE_EXECUTION_AND_PAYMENT (0x3):

  2 SENDER  target INBOX, data = land(input)
  3 VERIFY  a stateless status verifier, flags 0, data empty:
            reverts unless FRAMEPARAM(0x05, frame 2) == 1 (the status of the frame before it)
  signatures: [0: SECP256K1, signer empty (resolves to P) or P, empty msg]

```

**[proven: from [E8141 L137-200, L321-332, L386-434, L660-673, L675-727] at commit `356a004`] Both layouts are valid frame transactions; the page's three-frame shape 2 is not (B-C5-01).** Processing starts with `payer = None` and `sender_approved = false` (L395-397); a `SENDER` frame with `sender_approved` false makes the transaction invalid (L408-409); after the last frame `payer` must be set, or the transaction is invalid (L434); only `APPROVE` sets either (L707-723). Shape 2 above adds frame 1. This unrolls any effects of `APPROVE`." (L425); so a stale copy whose frame 2 reverts fails frame 3, the whole transaction is invalid, P's nonce increment and `max_cost` collection are unrolled, and it costs nothing (whether a post-payment `VERIFY` stays legal is §14 item 16).

Shape 2 is the **primary** landing path; shape 1 is the censorship-resistant fallback that propagates through the public mempool; the direct land is the third path and the one that exists when no frame transaction does. Which transactions use which (interfaces page §6): `land` (ordinary, forced, replacement): shape 2, shape 1 as fallback; slashing evidence and `announceLanding`: shape 2's four-frame layout with that call in frame 2 (the approval frame is required for the same reason), a plain transaction as fallback; every other call (`recordViewChange`, `recordCertificate`, `recordAssignment`, `poke`, registry, `saveForcedInclusion*`, withdrawals): plain. A shape-1 transaction with a trailing status VERIFY and a plain shape-1 transaction are alternatives for the same `(G, n)`, so a builder-submitted variant and a public-pool variant compete for one nonce (page, "compatible on one seat"); that variant is shape 1 plus a frame 4 checking frame 3's status, its approvals are shape 1's and valid, but it has five frames and C5-R13 pin 1 ("exactly four frames") rejects it as written (pins open, §14 item 17). **What the consensus text gives [proven: from [E8141 L390-392, L408-409, L421, L423-425, L434, L655, L717, L723, L816, L1096, L1104, L1176] and the record §3, §4 claims 1 to 3 and 7]:** the nonce check is the first step of frame-transaction processing and an increment happens only inside a payment-scoped `APPROVE`, so of any set of transactions sharing `(G, n)` at most one is ever included and every other is invalid, charged nothing, its blob fee included, because the payer owes the blob fee only once payment is approved; a `VERIFY` frame that reverts or halts invalidates the whole transaction wherever it sits, so in shape 2 any revert of frame 2 (lost race, `NotNext`, bad proof, rotated image, ring buffer or out of gas) fails frame 3 and makes the transaction invalid and free, P's nonce is not consumed, pipelining and multi-segment submission are allowed, and the inbox needs no new trust root because `msg.sender` is P; a revert in a `SENDER` frame after payment approval is an included transaction that pays, and **never rolls back the nonce increment**. ### C5-R13. The load-bearing pins of the gate

**[assumed: proposed rule, as revised after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01 and the record's erratum]** Each pin, if missing, lets a third party forge `msg.sender == G` or burn the nonce at will. G's code enforces exactly these:

1. exactly four frames, with G as frame 1;
2. frame 3 is the only `SENDER` frame: target INBOX, flags 0, value 0, data the ABI encoding of `landFor(address _rewardTo, LandInput _in)` with the **selector pinned**, **word 0 (`_rewardTo`) equal to P**, and **68 ≤ length ≤ `LAND_CALLDATA_MAX`** bytes;
3. the gate reads only the fixed prefix of that data with `FRAMEDATALOAD`: the selector, word 0 and the offset word of the dynamic input, `4 + 32 + 32 = 68` bytes; the rest is covered by P's signature over the whole transaction (an empty-`msg` signature commits to `compute_sig_hash(tx)`, which covers the full frame list, [E8141 L1466]);
4. the payer, the signer and the named actor are the same address;
5. two signatures with fixed modes and no padding malleability (the `ARBITRARY` entry has length 0, because its raw bytes are elided from the signature hash, [E8141 L1446]);
6. a minimum tip (its value is not on the pages, §14 item 17);
7. no external calls, no `SLOAD`, no `TIMESTAMP` or `NUMBER` in G's bytecode, which is the condition for public-pool eligibility and for the invariant that the inbox sees `msg.sender == G` only if a frame transaction's `VERIFY` ran G's code.

`LAND_CALLDATA_MAX` is **assumed 8,192** bytes, sized at implementation from the proof, the certificate and the segment descriptors: the page's typical landing (one segment, one view change, two Groth16-wrapped leaves of about 320 bytes each) is about 4.5 KB of ABI data and the four-view worst case (eight redraw proofs and four boosts) about 8 KB. A bound set too small refuses only the gate path, never the direct land. The erratum: the record's 68-byte length pin belonged to the PDF's `proposeFor(address, bytes32)`, whose data lives in blobs; `landFor` carries its proof and certificate in calldata, so its data is dynamic and the length is a range [record erratum of 2026-10-01; parameters page §1]. The packed-input pins of the record's §8 (`deadline == 0`, `numForcedInclusions == 0`) belong to that call, and the page's revised pin list does not carry them; whether the record's pins on the modes and flags of frames 0 and 2 and its `numBlobs` guardrail are part of "fixed modes" is open (§14 item 17). Each pin **fails closed**: a transaction that no longer matches is invalid and free. (Page frame-transactions, "Load-bearing pins of the gate"; record §8.) **Adversary schedule:** a third party that appends a later `SENDER` frame under the execution approval (approval is transaction-scoped, so an unpinned frame count authorizes it); a different `_rewardTo` signed by another key; a padded `ARBITRARY` signature that changes the hash; a calldata tail beyond the bound; a 68-byte or otherwise malformed `landFor`, signed and paid by the attacker as its own P, that passes pins 2 and 3 and reverts in ABI decoding (G reads only the prefix and cannot rule it out), burning `n` at C5-R15's minimum attack price.

**[proven: a forged `msg.sender == G` cannot redirect a reward or land an invalid range, conditional on C2-R04 and C2-R05 applying identically to `land` and `landFor`]** The proof binds `rewardTo` in the journal for every landing kind and every inbox check depends on state and calldata, not on the caller (C2-R04, C2-R05); so what a compromised or forged gate gains is the power to burn a shape-1 nonce and nothing about funds or content. Why `landFor` exists as a separate, gated entry point at all, given that `land` takes `rewardTo` in calldata, is open (§14 item 5).

### C5-R14. The gate address, its lifecycle and the absence of a switch

### C5-R15. Shape 1 versus shape 2 versus the direct land: the cost of losing

**[assumed: proposed rule; the stale-nonce cost is stated as a cost]** Zero cost is a property of shape 2 and, in shape 1, only of a race against a competitor for the **same** `(G, n)` in the same block. The three paths on each loss:

| Event | Shape 2 | Shape 1 | Direct land |
|---|---|---|---|
| Another landing consumed the parent (stale copy) | invalid and free: frame 2 reverts at the inbox's first check, so frame 3 (the trailing VERIFY) reverts and frame 1's payment approval is unrolled ([E8141 L425]); P's nonce unconsumed | **still valid**: frame 3 reverts at the inbox's first check, **P pays `C_stale`, nonce `n` is burned for every shape-1 user** | reverts and pays D-FLOOR (§8) |
| Same `(G, n)` loser in the same block | not applicable (own nonce) | invalid and free | not applicable |
| Winner's own revert after approval (bad proof, rotated image, out of gas) | invalid and free | included, pays for the work done, **burns `n`** | reverts and pays |
| Builder dependence | the builder must accept the shape (A-BUILDER-2) | none: propagates through the public mempool, if a client ships its rules (C5-R17) | none |

The reason a stale copy in shape 1 is valid: the public-pool rules let G's validation prefix read no storage but G's own, so G cannot compare the input with `lastLanded` before payment; the copy fails only inside frame 3 (page "Public-mempool shape", after the #22188 review; record §3.4). **The cost, in gas and blob fee:** `C_stale(L, n_b) = gas_floor(L) × p_gas + n_b × 131,072 × p_blob`, with `p_gas` the L1 gas price, `p_blob` the blob gas price (whose product with the blob gas is at least `n_b × 8,192 × base_fee`, C5-R09) and `L` the length of frame 3's data. The page states the parts, "about 30k gas of frame execution plus the intrinsic cost, 21k plus its calldata, and the blob fee". **[proven: arithmetic on the EIP-8141 text re-fetched 2026-10-01, [E8141 L213-217, L453-515, L599]; conditional on that text and on EIP-7976 as written, unmeasured]** A frame transaction is charged `max(execution component, calldata floor)`; its intrinsic cost is `12,000 + 475 per frame` (not 21,000), and its floor is `12,000 + 475 × frames + signature gas + 64 × (bytes of all frame data and signature fields)`. For shape 1 (four frames, signature gas 100 + 2,800, data bytes `8 + L + 20 + 65`): `gas_floor(L) = 16,800 + 64 × (L + 93)`, which is 27,104 at the 68-byte prefix, **310,752 at the page's typical `L = 4,500`** and **547,040 at `LAND_CALLDATA_MAX`**; the execution component (the three prefix frames, about 14,600 gas by the record's example limits, plus about 30k for the early revert, plus calldata at 4 and 16 gas per byte) is the larger only below about `L = 850`, so for every real landing the floor binds and `C_stale` is about 0.31 M gas at a typical landing. The direct land pays the same floor on its own bytes, `(15,000 to 21,000) + 64 × L` (EIP-2780 decomposition and the base term, cited 2026-09-30), about 0.30 M gas at `L = 4,500`. A shape-2 transaction (four frames, one `SECP256K1` signature of 2,800 gas with an empty signer field, data bytes `8 + 0 + L + 0 + 65`) has `gas_floor_2(L) = 16,700 + 64 × (L + 73)`, 309,372 at `L = 4,500`; only a winner pays it, a loser being invalid. The pages' "about 25k gas plus a blob fee" for a loser without the gate (C2-R20, interfaces §6, roles page) and "about 30k" for a lost evidence race (S3) are consistent only with a transaction of little calldata, **not with a landing whose proof and certificate are calldata**; they are carried to C2 and S3 as obligations (§13) and recorded (§14 item 2). The nonce burn is a lever **against shape-1 users only**: an honest lander moves to shape 2 or the direct land, neither of which shares the nonce. **Two prices, not one.** The **typical stale call**, an honest shape-1 landing beaten by another, costs its sender `C_stale`, 310,752 gas plus the blob fee at `L = 4,500`. The **minimum nonce-burning attack** is cheaper: pins 2 and 3 of C5-R13 check only the 68-byte prefix and a length of at least 68 bytes, so an attacker acting as its own P (pin 4) sends a shape-1 transaction whose frame 3 is the pinned selector, `_rewardTo = P` and an offset word with no valid `LandInput` behind it; it passes every pin, reverts in the inbox's ABI decoding, and burns `n` with no landing and no blob, unless G's open `numBlobs` guardrail demands one (§14 item 17). **[proven: arithmetic, same conditions]** its charge is `max(execution component, 27,104)` gas, at least the 27,104-gas floor at 68 bytes; the execution component at 68 bytes (intrinsic 16,800, standard calldata of at most 2,576 gas for 161 bytes, the prefix frames and a decoding revert) is **unmeasured** (G03) and estimated at up to about 40,000 gas, so the attack costs about a tenth of a typical stale call, plus the minimum tip, per burned nonce. C6 L27 carries the typical figure; the minimum attack price is placed on C6 L27 (§13). After B's W12 verdict (comment 5945082389, nonblocking). ### C5-R17. What Etna must not rely on

**[assumed: proposed rule, the hazards of the page]** (a) Public-mempool rules for type `0x06` are not implemented in any shipped client (one-pending-per-sender, revalidation, `MAX_VERIFY_GAS = 100,000`; client status in §9); every mempool property is **unavailable until a client ships it**, and no rule or parameter of Etna assumes one. (b) An ePBS builder can pay its bid and reveal an invalid payload, so the primitive never guarantees that the L2 batch lands (record §6 scenario iii). (c) A client never derives the next landable range from `nonce(G)`: a reverted `SENDER` frame, an L1 reorganization or the direct path decouples them; `lastLanded` is the only source (C2-R05 L1). (d) Builder revert protection is an availability property, not a safety property; the consensus teeth are in the trailing VERIFY. (e) EIP-8151's `ecrecover` restriction (C5-R08). (Page frame-transactions, "Hazards"; record §8.) **Adversary schedule:** a rule that assumes the pool evicts a loser; a client that resubmits `n` after a reorganization; a builder that forces both transactions into one block (the payload is invalid, the slot is lost).

**Failure behaviour [assumed: stated from the rule text, no behaviour added]:** no failure path: definitional. Items (a) to (e) are prohibitions on what Etna's rules and clients may assume; the outcomes they name are behaviour stated elsewhere: under (b) an invalid ePBS payload loses the slot under the inherited execution rule (C5-R12), and under (c) the next landable range comes from `lastLanded` (C2-R05 L1). **[assumed: classification, not an L1 dependency; after J's observation and B's re-check on #22191, 2026-10-02]** (d) The first client implementation of the anchor-free fork (`taikoxyz/taiko-mono#22207`, draft, at client commit `1170344842dec57b1a4dcbc5a2d68a9f920a0895`, as read by B on 2026-10-02) rejects preconfirmation after Etna explicitly, pending changes to Catalyst, the preconfirmation sidecar. ## 14. Open items

**[assumed: historical discrepancy inventory; current dispositions below]** Items 1–22 preserve the dated source record. The W15 table separates accepted resolutions from remaining owner duties; old page wording does not introduce another operative rule.

1. **"Three frames."** The page states four frames for shape 1 (three `VERIFY`, one `SENDER`) and three for shape 2. The three-frame shape 2 sets neither `sender_approved` nor `payer` and is invalid on every input (B-C5-01); C5-R12 adds P's approval frame, so both shapes have four frames. **Closed in C5**; the page and C2-R20's paragraph keep the three-frame text until corrected (§13).
2. **The loser's cost.** The pages give "about 25k gas plus a blob fee" without the gate (C2-R20, interfaces §6, roles), "about 30k" for a lost evidence race (S3) and, for shape 1, "about 30k gas of frame execution plus the intrinsic cost, 21k plus its calldata". EIP-8141 sets intrinsic cost at 12,000 plus 475 per frame (the record's re-derivation gives about 18.8k) and charges `max(execution, calldata floor)` at 64 gas per byte, so a landing whose proof and certificate are calldata pays about 0.3 M gas on a loss. C5-R15 derives it; the pages' figures stand until C2 and S3 re-derive them.
3. **G's bytecode and gas** do not exist; "about 45 checks in 4,900 gas" and a size of 120 to 150 bytes are unverified [S03 §4 claim 10].
4. **LAND_CALLDATA_MAX.** 8,192 is assumed; the typical 4.5 KB is not itemized (two Groth16-wrapped leaves are 640 bytes) and the four-view worst case is "about 8 KB", leaving no stated headroom. The pin refuses only the gate path.
5. **Why `landFor` exists.** `land` takes `rewardTo` in calldata, bound in the journal; no check on the pages depends on the caller. If none does, a shape-1 `SENDER` frame could call `land` with `rewardTo = P` and the inbox would need no gate.
6. **The ring-buffer bound and the one-per-block revert.** The page keeps the ring-buffer bound "as the per-block spam bound" (C5-R16) and names "the inbox's one-per-block revert" in its Degradation fallback (C5-R18 row 1); C2-R05 has no one-per-block rule, so neither has a counterpart in C2's checks, and C5-R18 row 1 substitutes C2's early-revert checks for the page's words.
7. **A-INCL has four formulations** (§2) and two depth thresholds, LOOKBACK (1,200 s) and ANCHOR_MIN_AGE (48 s). L11 attributes a reorganization deeper than 48 s to "more than 20 percent of the stake", which the EIP states for consecutive controlled blocks; what stake four slots need is unstated, and EIP-7732 L456 gives a sufficient, not a necessary, condition. Closure: the EIP-7732 text or the Gloas specification states the stake a reorganization of ANCHOR_MIN_AGE needs.
8. **ANCHOR_MIN_AGE.** "At least 2.5 × canonical-at-N+1" gives 30 s (12 s) or 45 s (18 s) against 48 s; the page adds "24 slots at 2 s"; no procedure to raise it if `L_canon` grows.
9. **Blob capacity "about 224 KiB/s at every slot time"** is 224, 213.3, 224 and 192 KiB/s by rounding the blob limit; C2's "one eleventh" is about one ninth at 2 s.
10. **Retention at shorter slots** (C5-R18 row 10): A-RETAIN of 9 days, FI_EXPIRY of 7 days and the 18 days of C2 are not tied to one scale; EIP-7782 is silent, EIP-8198's rescaling is a `Draft` PFI.
11. **"Six times dearer"** (calldata fallback): 64 / 10 holds only for zero bytes; the survey gives 1.6 to 6.4.
12. **KZG precompile.** The page lists it as required; C2-R05 L6 reads `BLOBHASH` and the guest recomputes versioned hashes; whether the inbox calls 0x0A is unstated.
13. **The record's governance switch** (`_directProposeDisabled`) is superseded by the page.
14. **"Half of the inclusion-list cap"** for FI_CALLDATA_MAX is exact for the manifest, not for the transaction (4,373 bytes).
15. **Header profile.** The live item count and RLP length are in no source; no EIP orders the 7928 and 7843 fields; "appended" is the page's word.
16. **Block invalidity for type `0x06`** is inherited and not quoted in EIP-8141 [S03 §9 item 1]; whether post-payment `VERIFY` frames stay legal, and whether a consensus read cap or "fail-but-pay" is adopted, is open [S03 §9 items 2, 3].
17. **MIN_TIP** has a pin and no value [S03 §4 claim 35]. **Pins on frames 0 and 2** and the `numBlobs` guardrail are in record §8, not on the page's list; **the trailing-VERIFY variant of shape 1** has no pins, and with five frames it is rejected by pin 1 as written (C5-R12).
18. **Limits of the landing frame** (frame 3 of shape 1, frame 2 of shape 2). `limits.execution` and `limits.state` must cover the worst-case landing and first-lap writes; the pages give no value [S03 §7 item 18].
19. **Not re-fetchable on 2026-10-01:** the pull-request pages (state derived from the unchanged file). **Unverified as observed:** root(N-1) unwritten at an Empty slot.

21. **Survey baseline** (frames live before launch) versus the page ("optional"): the page governs.
