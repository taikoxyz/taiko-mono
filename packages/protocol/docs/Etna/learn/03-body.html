Commit to the block you executed

Prerequisites: confirmation labels and one authenticated origin.

Classification. These are proposed encoding rules. Proven means a specification fact or deduction assuming correct enforcement; Assumed names a premise; Open identifies missing validation.

The problem: “the same transactions” is ambiguous

Proven: changing transaction order, timestamp or the authenticated header origin can change execution. A commitment to an incomplete description leaves room for a different block. Etna therefore names exact public bytes, including the complete header and every ordinary signed transaction, and distinguishes a block commitment from a segment commitment.

The mechanism: execute first, hash afterward

Proven definitions: a block record B contains its complete canonical header and ordered raw signed transaction envelopes. A body groups one to 60 records using one exact, uncompressed RLP format. It also carries any mandatory-request records, including rejected input bytes; their queue semantics come in a later lesson. No private ordering option is left for the prover.

BodyV1 = RLP([1, blocks, mandatoryRequestRecords])
Block  = [headerRlpBytes, [signedTxBytes, ...]]
bodyHash = keccak256(exact complete BodyV1 bytes)

Assumed anchor-free rule: the signed transaction list may be empty. Standard EIP-4788/2935 calls are execution steps with their normal upstream gas and receipt semantics, not signed BodyV1 transactions or synthetic receipts. The header commits their resulting state. Fixed-k anchor signing disappears; no special sender, fee exemption or anchor gas reserve is added.

Proven rule: header and transaction entries are byte strings containing their inner encodings, not interchangeable nested lists. Reject nonminimal encodings, wrong shapes and trailing bytes. Reexecute against the known parent, recompute roots and header fields, and compare with the supplied header. Merely hashing a false header does not validate it.

Proven commitment definitions: different names protect different objects
NameWhat it binds
FR, block fragment rootThe execution domain, block number/hash, total record length and ordered chunk lengths/digests.
MR, segment manifest rootThe segment domain, parent and range, plus ordered rows containing each block's number, hash, record hash/length and FR.
bodyHashEvery body byte, including mandatory-request records outside the block list.
contextHashThe sealed execution description: parent, origin, results and data commitments. Introduced fully with staging next.

Proven: FR can be computed as soon as a block executes; it needs no future segment boundary, stage identifier or payment assignment. MR later binds the chosen complete segment. Feeding contextHash back into the current header or execution inputs would create a hash dependency cycle and is forbidden.

Worked example: two exact chunks

Assumed example: a complete valid B is 50,000 bytes. Proven arithmetic: split it at the fixed 32,768-byte boundary into lengths [32,768, 17,232]. FR commits both ordered Keccak digests and total length. A signed first chunk alone cannot establish complete-block validity. Swapping chunks or padding the last chunk fails the prescribed recomputation.

Proven bounds: this exceeds the 32,768-byte calldata body limit, so it requires blob mode, whose full-body limit is 122,880 bytes. A 32,768-byte record has one chunk, but its enclosing body is larger. The fixtures specify a 32,769-byte [32,768, 1] split and tiny RLP/hash examples; those toy bytes are not execution-valid blocks.

Defenses and limits

Proven under A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION: exact binding prevents silent substitution of transaction order, header origins or record bytes under the same commitment. It does not prove anyone can download the bytes, establish fair ordering, or make a soft block final. A commitment is not availability.

Assumed: collision-resistant Ethereum Keccak, correct parsers/executors, and available parent state. Open: independent clients and proving programs must reproduce all codec vectors and full execution cases; component hashes alone do not close that obligation.

Check your understanding

Can a segment MR stand in for the FR on an early block promise?

Proven: no. Their domains, inputs and meanings differ. Recompute the exact block FR.

Does a correct bodyHash prove the transactions executed correctly?

Proven: no. It identifies bytes; local execution and the eventual validity proof establish their execution.

Challenge this

Assumed: every execution-affecting input is public or authenticated by the parent context. Try hiding a system-call effect, changing a rejected input, or making the fee recipient depend on a later assignment. Identify which commitment and validation rule rejects the substitution.
