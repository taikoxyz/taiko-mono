Lesson 4: certificates and confirmation levels

Prerequisite: lesson 3. Design page: Preconfirmation & certificates §4.

Goal

Know what a certificate is, how fast it forms, and exactly what each confirmation level promises a user.

The idea

A block the sequencer signed is only its word. A block that 22 of 32 bonded attesters have executed and signed is a fact that costs at least 12 whole bonds to contradict. Attesters sign the header hash (not just the execution hash), so the signature also commits to which certificate the block carried; that detail is what later makes the "lock lie" offence provable. The sequencer includes the newest certificate it holds in the next block (lag at most one), so the chain itself records that its ancestors were published.

The rules

  • Committee. 32 seats per term, drawn by the seed, excluding the term's holders. Quorum Q = 22.
  • Attestation. A BLS signature over (chain, term, view, height, header hash). An attester signs a block only after V1 to V6 pass, only above its lock, one per height, and only if the block's timestamp is within 3 seconds of its own clock (a signing policy, not a validity rule).
  • Certificate C(h). Any 22 attestations aggregated into one 100-byte object with a 32-bit bitmap. Anyone can build it from gossip; the sequencer holds nothing the network does not.
  • Carried certificate. Block n includes C(n−1) if it has it, else C(n−2). A sequencer more than two blocks ahead of its newest certificate must hold.

Self-check

Why sign the header hash rather than the execution hash?

The header hash commits to the carried certificate. An attester that later signs a lock below a certificate it had verified can be slashed objectively (S3b); with execution hashes alone that lie would be unprovable.

Why is the quorum 22 and not 17?

Two certificates or view changes over one (term, view) then share at least 2·22 − 32 = 12 signers, each fully slashable. With a bare majority the overlap could be two keys, and reverting a locked block would cost two bonds.

What does "locked" cost to revert in TAIKO at the initial parameters?

At least 12 seats of 20,000 TAIKO, 240,000 TAIKO, plus the leader's bond for a certificate pair: the published credit bound wallets can use.

Challenge this

Ten percent of attesters are offline. Compute the probability that a term's committee cannot reach 22 signatures (binomial, 32 seats, 11 or more offline) and compare it with the design's 1.7·10−4. Then argue whether Q = 22 or Q = 24 is the better trade at 20 % offline.
