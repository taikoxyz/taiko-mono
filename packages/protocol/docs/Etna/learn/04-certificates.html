Lesson 4: certificates and confirmation levels

Prerequisite: lesson 3. Design page: Preconfirmation & certificates §1 and §4.

Goal

Know what a certificate is, how fast it forms, and exactly what each confirmation level promises a user, exceptions included.

The idea

A block the sequencer signed is only its word. A block that 22 of 32 bonded attesters have executed and signed is a fact that costs at least 12 whole attester bonds to contradict. Attesters sign the header hash (not just the execution hash), so the signature also commits to which certificate the block carried; that detail is what later makes the "lock lie" offence provable. The sequencer includes the newest certificate it holds in the next block (lag at most one), so the chain itself records that its ancestors were published.

The rules

  • Committee. Up to K = 32 seats per term, drawn by the sequencing page's walk from the seed, which excludes the primary holder by owner and the backups by seat only. The quorum scales with the actual size m: Q(m) = ⌊2m/3⌋ + 1, so 22 of 32; below K_MIN_CERT = 8 seats there is no committee at all (no-committee mode). The number of provably slashable double-signers behind a locked block, 2Q(m) − m, is published beside the confirmation level (12 at 32 seats, 6 at 16, 4 at 8). m counts the non-empty slots: a seat drawn while its owner is suspended or lapsed at the term's start is an empty slot (lesson 2), so with a share s of the domain suspended a committee averages about 32(1 − s) seats, and "12" holds only for a committee in which no suspended seat was drawn; with a third of the seats parked in a suspension, the most T11 allows a cartel, the slashable count averages about 8.4 rather than 12 and m falls below 8 in about 3·10−7 of terms (round 6, R6-C1). Parking cuts the parkers' own chance of a quorum too, and seats suspended or lapsed for more than STALE_EXIT = 7 days can be exited by anyone (exitStale).
  • Attestation. A BLS signature over (chain, term, view, opening object, redraw count, height, header hash); the redraw count is the number of committee redraws of the view (lesson 5), so a redrawn committee's messages never conflict with the first committee's. An attester signs a block only after V1 to V6, V8, V9 and V10 pass, only above its lock and on the lock's chain, only if it has signed no timeout for the view, one per (term, view, opening object, redraw, height), and only under the signing policy: the block's timestamp is at least receivedAt − STALE_MAX (3 s, measured when the node first received the block, so a block published on time stays attestable later, even by a redrawn committee, while one published late or privately never becomes attestable) and at most now + CLOCK_SKEW (1 s). Policy gates signing only, so clocks never desynchronise validity.
  • Certificate C(h). Any 22 attestations aggregated into one object of about 130 bytes with a 32-bit bitmap. Anyone can build it from gossip; the sequencer holds nothing the network does not. Two certificates over the same header are ordinary and never an offence. Anyone may record a certificate on L1 with recordCertificate; the inbox keeps the highest per view, which bounds the lock of any later committee's fallback for that view (round 6, R6S-3; lesson 5).
  • Carried certificate. Block n includes C(n−1) if it has it, else C(n−2) (CERT_LAG_MAX = 1). A sequencer more than two blocks ahead of its newest certificate must hold.

Self-check

Why sign the header hash rather than the execution hash?

The header hash commits to the carried certificate. An attester that later signs a lock below a certificate it had verified can be slashed objectively (S3b); with execution hashes alone that lie would be unprovable.

Why is the quorum 22 and not 17?

Two certificates or view changes over one (term, view) then share at least 2·22 − 32 = 12 signers, each fully slashable. With a bare majority the overlap could be two keys, and reverting a locked block would cost two bonds.

What does "locked" cost to revert in TAIKO at the initial parameters?

At a full committee, at least 12 attester keys plus the leader, all class A: 13 × B_SEAT = 13 × 20,000 = 260,000 TAIKO, about 39 ETH at the assumed rate. That is the published credit bound wallets can use, valid only within one redraw count, only while landing keeps up (a private-quorum stall is replaced from the last landed block after 35 to 65 minutes with nothing slashed) and only at a full committee: with m non-empty slots it is 2Q(m) − m attester seats plus the leader, and m falls with the suspended share of the domain (round 6, R6-C1). Clients show these exceptions beside the label.

Challenge this

Ten percent of attesters are offline. Compute the probability that a term's committee cannot reach 22 signatures (binomial, 32 seats, 11 or more offline) and compare it with the design's 1.7·10−4 (4.1·10−2 at 20 % offline). Then argue whether Q = 22 or Q = 24 is the better trade at 20 % offline, and what the size-scaled quorum does to both numbers when the registry has only 16 non-holder seats. Then redo the first computation with the committee redraw vote of lesson 5 (two redraws, each needing 17 of 32 votes from attesters that attested) and see how much of the offline risk it absorbs, remembering that an offline attester attested nothing and so cannot vote for a redraw.
