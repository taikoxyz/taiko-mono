The problem: a quorum's balance overstates the cost of an attack

Votes, owners and money differ

Assumed, S3-R01/R10. A committee position carries a key; its historically bound owner holds the ledger that a slash debits. Several positions can have one owner. Counting every signer's balance, or counting one owner repeatedly, overstates the available deterrent. Start from an evidence rule, determine the positions it implicates, then read distinct owners' collectible balances at an identified L1 block. Key attribution; S3-R10.

The mechanism: authenticate the offence, then debit once

Evidence precedes punishment

Assumed, selected S3a case. Anyone can submit two valid certificates with equal (t, v, vcHash, redraw, height) and different phHash. Membership proofs and aggregate signatures authenticate both. The owners represented in the intersecting bitmaps receive a class-A debit, once per owner; already-slashed keys do not create another collectible balance. Equal hashes are not guilt. S3-R08.

Assumed, payout rule. Class A takes the owner's ledger, including its reserve and sentinel pocket. The submitter receives min(10% × debit, 2,000 TAIKO); the remaining amount accrues for permissionless burn. An offender can submit its own evidence, so the deterrent excludes that share. Victims receive no automatic compensation. Invalid accusations fail verification and cost submission gas on the plain-transaction path; Frame Transactions have the separate conditional treatment in C5. S3-R02; S3-R03.

Open, evidence boundary: D83 accepts S2’s ancestry and aggregate evidence; D85 accepts the aggregate S3b interface and codec. S3a branch adoption and complete fixtures remain open. Do not substitute the old single-pair gas estimate for the new two-aggregate path. The earlier S6 rule authenticates a named ancestor preimage before comparing it; a substituted preimage is BadPreimage, not guilt. Silence or withheld data alone supplies no slashable proof. Accepted S2-R21. Assumed, D81 correction: S3d refuses evidence whose L1 reference is noncanonical at evidence time; the current catalogue supplies that narrower predicate, rather than an unconditional no-framing promise. Accepted S3-R08.

One strike is not one debit

Assumed, adopted rule: D79 (3) adopts at most one class-B strike per liable owner and offence term. The owner's sequencer key, attester key and multiple seats share that strike record. Monetary evidence remains keyed by its named object and lying key: another admitted offence can debit the owner even when that term adds no further strike. S3-R06; S3-R08 deduplication.

Proven arithmetic under those rules: a one-seat owner at the specified 21,000 TAIKO nonreserve registration balance, signing three distinct defective objects in one offence term, can lose 20,000 + 1,000 + 0 = 21,000 gross, with one strike. It does not lose three full seat bonds. The first debit already puts it below the floor. Under D88’s corrected class-B rule, a debit written at time τ opens that suspension at τ + DELAY_S (1,320 seconds, 22 minutes), with the minimum suspension duration and top-up/reactivation rules still applying. The MISS hard-floor path retains DELAY_REG. Challenger rebates further change net cost. These are S3-R06's example inputs and limits, not a cost floor or measured economics.

Signed residuals, not protection from every bug: the same client bug can produce one strike in each offence term. A buggy-client operator at registration-level balance can be floor-suspended by one defective object; a supplier collecting challenger shares from affected operators may profit. D79 (3) signs these false-positive risks. Coalescing reduces repeated strikes within a term; it changes neither the monetary debits nor the floor comparison, and it is separate from W22's class-A evidence repair accepted under D83, whose aggregate S3b consumer rows are accepted under D85 while S3a branch adoption and resource admission remain open.

D88: name the accused, then resolve the owner at the signed term

A later binding must not redirect the debit

Assumed, accepted under D88: slashSequencerEquivocation(a, b, accused) takes an explicit, untrusted accused address. Neither the PH body nor its signature carries that address. The S1 evidence row checks the two headers' common conflict fields and different PH hashes, refuses future or expired terms, and attempts both recoveries whatever the accused's code. On the recovery path both signatures must recover to the accused. Zero accused and any nonzero recovery to a different address are refused.

Assumed, attribution rule: resolve keyOwnerAt(accused, termStart(t)); if no binding was valid there, the accused is its own liable owner. Read that owner's slashedAt before fallback admission, then debit that owner's ledger, including its reserve and sentinel pocket. A current binding to another owner does not change this historical target. An earlier slash of that other owner does not satisfy this owner's replay check. The full check order governs errors; a replay after the target's class-A debit returns AlreadySlashed.

Assumed, conditional fallback: under A-8151, non-7702 code can make recovery return zero. With no nonzero mismatch, the fallback requires that code now and either a key binding valid at the signed term or an unbound address with its opt-in selfSignable bit set. This is a code-duty debit: it does not prove the accused signed either header. Its objective code duty and target must hold independently. Code-less or 7702-only addresses do not qualify for this fallback. Exact predicate; opt-in and constructor risk.

Worked case: K arms its own pocket with self-signing enabled and signs the conflicting sentinel headers while its binding to empty-ledger P is pending at the term start. Once that binding is active, K installs a 7702 delegation. The call still recovers K and debits K. With non-7702 code under A-8151, the qualified fallback instead debits K on its own code duty. A separate slashCoded(K) may debit P under S8's registered-key clause; it neither discharges nor blocks K's debit.

Can naming P as the accused redirect signatures that recover to K?

No. A nonzero recovery different from the accused is refused before owner resolution or fallback.

Does the no-recovery fallback establish that K equivocated?

No. It collects on the objective code duty under its admission checks. The recovery path authenticates the conflicting signatures.

D85: public aggregates can supply the lock-lie evidence

Do not require signatures that the public certificate does not reveal

Assumed, accepted evidence mechanism: a public certificate and closing contain aggregate signatures. An accuser cannot extract each member’s private individual signature from them. S3-R08’s aggregate form instead authenticates the complete certificate, class-H closing and committee proof in the same opening and redraw, checks the carried certificate and the lock-lie predicate, then computes their shared signer keys. The call attributes those keys to historical owners and debits each unslashed owner once. It is restricted to class H; a FALLBACK does not qualify for this aggregate form.

Proven conditionally, worked arithmetic: S3-V10 uses certificate C_A43 carrying C_A41 and a class-H closing at 40. The carried height 41 exceeds 40. Two quorums of 22 among 32 positions can share exactly 12 positions. With eight positions under one owner and four under another, the fixture has two class-A debits, not twelve independent balances. A closing at A42 on the carried certificate’s branch is the negative: endpoint height 43 alone is not a lock lie. These are logical fixtures, not executed evidence bytes.

Derived from assumed unit costs, unmeasured: S3’s aggregate budget row estimates about 677,600 gas for those twelve positions under two owners and about 1.18 million for twelve owners. Both exceed the assumed 600,000-gas evidence cap. The individual form’s old 340,000 estimate does not cover the aggregate call. Raising the cap versus splitting debits remains an owner decision after measurement; accepted interfaces establish no executable gas fit.

Assumed defence; open limits: forged membership or aggregates fail their checks, substituted committed preimages fail authentication, and a different redraw or non-class-H closing is inapplicable. Missing public evidence, S3a’s unadopted branch form and the unresolved resource budget remain gaps. The proposed generation test and its new signing domain do not extend the accepted evidence predicate.

Why can the accuser use the public aggregates without individual signatures?

The accepted aggregate form verifies both complete witnesses and their authenticated shared signer keys, then applies the carried-certificate predicate. It does not extract signatures.

Does accepting the interface show that twelve owners can be slashed within the evidence cap?

No. The unmeasured derived estimate already exceeds the cap; admission and complete fixtures remain open.

Challenge this: can an honest signing history satisfy the actual predicate, can a substituted committee or preimage survive authentication, and can the complete evidence fit its eventual admission rule? The answers depend on the owner specification and still-open fixtures and budgets.

Worked example: inventory, live deterrent and illustration

Three quantities, three meanings

Assumed fixture inputs, not a launch measurement: take S3-V09's 22 one-seat owners, each with 17,161 TAIKO at the same read block, and no earlier slash. Restrict the example to two conflicting equal-height certificates in the same 32-position committee and redraw. That evidence case implicates at least twelve positions by the set-intersection calculation. It does not validate the disputed general confirmation guarantee.

Proven conditionally, arithmetic from S3-R10 and V09: quoted economic inputs are assumptions; these are not fixed security floors.

Assumed, live reporting rule. In general, D(W,p) is the least sum of distinct owners' live net collectible amounts among feasible p-position subsets of witness W. A defined conservative fractional lower bound may be displayed instead. Publish the read block; later debits can reduce the amount, including to zero. The balance arithmetic does not establish that an arbitrary displacement supplies the required evidence.

Assumed, D73 scope. The historical D1 token figures are illustrations. An application's own H(X) describes its exposure to irreversible actions over a time interval; it is not a universal protocol parameter, admission gate or automatic bond-sizing rule. Check your understanding

Why is the 339,787.8 quorum inventory not the route deterrent?

The conflicting certificate can intersect only twelve of the twenty-two signers. The other ten may supply no slashable evidence.

Does four implicated positions always mean four collectible ledgers?

No. Several positions can belong to one owner. The debit and the live deterrent count that owner's ledger once.

Does an identified live read block guarantee the same payout later?

No. Later payouts, penalties or earlier slashes can reduce the actual ledger. The published amount is conditional on its read, and it is not compensation.
