The problem: a quorum's balance overstates the cost of an attack

Votes, owners and money differ

Assumed, S3-R01/R10. A committee position carries a key; its historically bound owner holds the ledger that a slash debits. Several positions can have one owner. Counting every signer's balance, or counting one owner repeatedly, overstates the available deterrent. Start from an evidence rule, determine the positions it implicates, then read distinct owners' collectible balances at an identified L1 block. Key attribution; S3-R10.

The mechanism: authenticate the offence, then debit once

Evidence precedes punishment

Assumed, selected S3a case. Anyone can submit two valid certificates with equal (t, v, vcHash, redraw, height) and different phHash. Membership proofs and aggregate signatures authenticate both. The owners represented in the intersecting bitmaps receive a class-A debit, once per owner; already-slashed keys do not create another collectible balance. Equal hashes are not guilt. S3-R08.

Assumed, payout rule. Class A takes the owner's ledger, including its reserve and sentinel pocket. The submitter receives min(10% × debit, 2,000 TAIKO); the remaining amount accrues for permissionless burn. An offender can submit its own evidence, so the deterrent excludes that share. Victims receive no automatic compensation. Invalid accusations fail verification and cost submission gas on the plain-transaction path; Frame Transactions have the separate conditional treatment in C5. S3-R02; S3-R03.

Open, evidence boundary: D83 accepts S2's ancestry and aggregate evidence; its complete S3/C8 adoption remains open. Do not substitute the old single-pair gas estimate for the new two-aggregate path. The earlier S6 rule authenticates a named ancestor preimage before comparing it; a substituted preimage is BadPreimage, not guilt. Silence or withheld data alone supplies no slashable proof. Accepted S2-R21. Assumed, D81 correction: S3d refuses evidence whose L1 reference is noncanonical at evidence time; the current catalogue supplies that narrower predicate, rather than an unconditional no-framing promise. Accepted S3-R08.

One strike is not one debit

Assumed, adopted rule: D79 (3) adopts at most one class-B strike per liable owner and offence term. The owner's sequencer key, attester key and multiple seats share that strike record. Monetary evidence remains keyed by its named object and lying key: another admitted offence can debit the owner even when that term adds no further strike. S3-R06; S3-R08 deduplication.

Proven arithmetic under those rules: a one-seat owner at the specified 21,000 TAIKO nonreserve registration balance, signing three distinct defective objects in one offence term, can lose 20,000 + 1,000 + 0 = 21,000 gross, with one strike. It does not lose three full seat bonds. The first debit already puts it below the floor, starting the delayed floor-suspension path; challenger rebates further change net cost. These are S3-R06's example inputs and limits, not a cost floor or measured economics.

Signed residuals, not protection from every bug: the same client bug can produce one strike in each offence term. A buggy-client operator at registration-level balance can be floor-suspended by one defective object; a supplier collecting challenger shares from affected operators may profit. D79 (3) signs these false-positive risks. Coalescing reduces repeated strikes within a term; it changes neither the monetary debits nor the floor comparison, and it is separate from W22's class-A evidence repair accepted under D83, whose consumer integration remains open.

Worked example: inventory, live deterrent and illustration

Three quantities, three meanings

Assumed fixture inputs, not a launch measurement: take S3-V09's 22 one-seat owners, each with 17,161 TAIKO at the same read block, and no earlier slash. Restrict the example to two conflicting equal-height certificates in the same 32-position committee and redraw. That evidence case implicates at least twelve positions by the set-intersection calculation. It does not validate the disputed general confirmation guarantee.

Proven conditionally, arithmetic from S3-R10 and V09: quoted economic inputs are assumptions; these are not fixed security floors.

Assumed, live reporting rule. In general, D(W,p) is the least sum of distinct owners' live net collectible amounts among feasible p-position subsets of witness W. A defined conservative fractional lower bound may be displayed instead. Publish the read block; later debits can reduce the amount, including to zero. The balance arithmetic does not establish that an arbitrary displacement supplies the required evidence.

Assumed, D73 scope. The historical D1 token figures are illustrations. An application's own H(X) describes its exposure to irreversible actions over a time interval; it is not a universal protocol parameter, admission gate or automatic bond-sizing rule. Check your understanding

Why is the 339,787.8 quorum inventory not the route deterrent?

The conflicting certificate can intersect only twelve of the twenty-two signers. The other ten may supply no slashable evidence.

Does four implicated positions always mean four collectible ledgers?

No. Several positions can belong to one owner. The debit and the live deterrent count that owner's ledger once.

Does an identified live read block guarantee the same payout later?

No. Later payouts, penalties or earlier slashes can reduce the actual ledger. The published amount is conditional on its read, and it is not compensation.
