Lesson 13: final assembly

Prerequisites: every lesson. Design pages: Liveness & safety, Limitations.

Goal

Build the protocol from its blocks in your head, run six scenarios through it, reproduce the two arguments that hold it together, and know what the design admits it cannot do.

Assembly

  1. Time (lesson 1) gives every block a term and every term an L1 snapshot.
  2. Seats (lesson 2) turn the snapshot, a persistent domain tree and a seed into holders, backups and a committee.
  3. Blocks (lesson 3) are signed, carry their L1 anchor hash in the header, and are locally checkable.
  4. Certificates (lesson 4) turn a block into a bonded fact within a second and a locked fact within two.
  5. View changes (lesson 5) answer silence, close every term, seal batches, and make withheld blocks worthless.
  6. Landing (lesson 6) carries a certified prefix and a proof to L1 and writes the checkpoint.
  7. Economics and replacement (lesson 7) keep landings coming without a prover role and never void by clock inside the landing horizon; single-proof landings are provisional.
  8. Slashing (lesson 8) makes every harmful deviation impossible, worthless or provable.
  9. Anti-monopoly (lesson 9) prices share and makes exclusion impossible.
  10. Forced inclusion (lesson 10) bounds censorship against everyone and voids an unprovable entry by a four-hour stall.
  11. Blocks without an anchor, bridge, migration (lesson 11) record each block's L1 anchor hash on L2, bind it to a canonical L1 block at landing, and deliver checkpoints by a permissionless reveal.
  12. L1 environment (lesson 12) is tolerated at every slot time and used only optionally.

The five invariants

The rules derive from these (arguments page §1). I1 sortition is a pure function of state at S(t): the holders and committee the inbox computes at landing equal what nodes computed at termStart(t), whatever registry writes became effective in between. I2 a drawn term's lander reward exists at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself: the reserve of 14,400 TAIKO per seat holds two terms of the 7,161-TAIKO exposure, is drained only by rewards to other owners, is refilled first by every reward credit, lapses the owner below seats × 7,161, and the ramps run from landableFrom(t) = max(termEnd(t), lastLanded.at) (round 6, R6S-2, R6H-2, R6H-3; exceptions in L20). I3 no minority takes a view from a producing leader: a view changes hands only when the leader is provably silent to the whole committee or the term ends. I4 nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement or forced batch after the deadline machinery, 2Q(m) − m slashings, or a DAO resolution of a proof conflict after a provisional single-proof landing (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01), at any lock and against any committee: S3b exempts no lock (round 5, R5S-1), and a later committee's FALLBACK is gated by L1 time, first record, a certificate at its lock (R5S-5) and refused below a recorded certificate of committee(t) (round 6, R6S-3). I5 no accusation object exists, and no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing.

Confirmation levels, with what each label is not

Sequenced (0.3 s): revertible without slashing. Attested (0.7 to 1.0 s): held and executed by Q bonded attesters; landable; revertible only by a view change formed without seeing the certificate, or an L1 reorg. Locked (1.7 to 2.0 s): revertible only with 2Q − K = 12 provably slashable attesters, by a landed replacement or forced batch after the deadline machinery, by an L1 reorg, or by a DAO resolution of a proof conflict after a provisional landing. Every client shows the exceptions beside the label (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): the bonds are a deterrent, not compensation; "12" holds at a full committee within one redraw count, and m is published (6 at 16, 4 at 8); across a redraw, ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers plus Q of the redrawn committee revert it with only keys in both slashable; a private-quorum stall (L3) ends in a landed replacement after 35 to 65 minutes with nothing slashable; the fallback race residual (L5); a no-committee term (m < 8) carries no "locked" label. Landed (minutes): two leaves, no unfinalized provisional record on the chain; L1 reorg only. Landed (provisional): one leaf in degraded mode, or any landing behind an unfinalized provisional record; checkpoint after a second-leaf proof of the same journal or 24 h; revertible by the DAO's conflict resolution. Final: L1-finalized.

The two arguments, in your words

Safety: why is no false state root ever finalized?

A landing writes a checkpoint only after two distinct ZK systems accept a journal whose parent root comes from storage, whose blob hashes come from blobhash, whose anchor tip is checked against L1 history, and whose end hash is bound by a 22-of-32 certificate verified on L1. A false root needs two unsound systems, or one plus a colluding quorum after two hours of stalled landing and no contradicting proof from the other leaf within 24 h; that window is delay, not evidence, so degraded-mode safety rests on T11 (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Reverting a locked block needs 2Q(m) − m provably slashable attesters, 12 at a full committee.

Liveness: why does the chain keep producing and landing blocks?

Silence is answered in 5 s by a view change, a dead term by the next term, a dead committee by a later committee's fallback from termEnd + 12 s (only with a certificate at its lock, never below a certificate of the term's own committee recorded on L1, round 6, R6S-3), dead provers by a reward ramp that pays anyone with the data, an unprovable forced-inclusion entry by a four-hour stall that voids every entry due at its cut (round 6, R6H-1), and a vanished registry by dead mode after 75 terms. No rung needs the DAO; the one halt that does, a proof conflict over a provisional landing, is a safety event, not a liveness rung.

What the design admits

The limitations page lists L1 to L22 plus L-OP and L-PF, among them the unslashable private-quorum stall (L3), the fallback race (L5), the landing horizon as the one time-based void (L14), the stall price (L16), the migration pause and the Shasta-tail fee leak (L17), certificate-free regimes needing two leaves (L19), the reserve's owner-triggered drains (L20), reveal-only checkpoints (L22), no emergency brake (L-OP) and provisional finality with a DAO-resolved conflict (L-PF). Status: six red-team rounds; round 6 found one High (R6H-1) and was revised, and its revision, two fix passes, the anchor-free L2 blocks, the adoptions from the Astra candidate (PR #22188) and the revision after that candidate's review of this design are all applied; readiness requires rounds 7 and 8 both to be clean, so the design is not ready; rounds 7 and 8 run against the converged specification of PR #22191, not this site's pages alone.

Cross-module quiz

1. Which single object is both the takeover mechanism and the batch seal, and why is that one object rather than two?

The view change. A separate sequencer-signed seal would be a second withholdable object; the committee's lock already says where the batch ends.

2. Name every rule that reads a node's wall clock, and its consequence when the clock is wrong.

The attester's timeout-signing condition (5 s from the view's opening object, restarted on each certificate, only while it holds no attested block still uncertified), the TERM_END clock fallback at termEnd + 2 s (the happy path signs on the certificate of the block stamped termEnd − 1, clock-free), the staleness policy (STALE_MAX = 3 s at receipt, CLOCK_SKEW = 1 s at signing) and the recordCertificate duty at termEnd + 2.5 s. A wrong clock signs late or early; validity and the switch decision are unaffected.

3. In what ways can a certified block be reverted, and at what cost?

A view change formed without seeing its certificate (a gossip-delay violation, cost 0, at most an attested block); a landed replacement or forced batch after the deadline machinery (a recorded REPLACE VC voids nothing until the replacement lands, whichever lands first wins, no penalty); an L1 reorg (outside T1); and, after a provisional single-proof landing, a DAO resolution of a proof conflict (L-PF).

4. Why is the forced-inclusion clock the anchored L1 time and not the landing time?

A proof is computed minutes before landing; an entry becoming due in between would let any third party make the proven landing revert. Anchored time is fixed when the block is built and hash-pinned. An entry saved in between cannot either: the journal names only fiNotDueAt, the latest anchor at which the range relied on none being due, checked by inequality against the stored save time, which a save made after the proof always passes (round 6, R6H-1).

5. Which contract addresses change at activation?

None: both SignalServices, the Bridge and the Vaults keep their addresses, so R2 holds. Bytecode: the L1 SignalService, the Bridge, the Vaults and the quota manager get compatible implementations that remove pause and the owner-only setters, storage unchanged (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01); the L2 SignalService adds revealCheckpoint and pinCurrentOrigin and closes the syncer path by the current-root guard (one namespaced pin mapping, VERSION unchanged); the Anchor becomes the treasury plus the guard (slots 0 to 257 keep their types); the Inbox proxy gets the Etna implementation; EIP-2935 and EIP-4788 are deployed on L2 at their canonical addresses (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147).

6. What does the DAO control after activation, and what happens if it never acts again?

Upgrades, image-id rotation with an overlap, constants by upgrade, the Anchor's legacy balance, naming the sound leaf after a proof conflict, and two residual DAO-only entry points as slow as an upgrade (DefaultResolver.registerAddress, the owner branch of BridgedERC20 mint and burn; L-OP). If it never acts: image windows stay open-ended, single-proof mode and replacement keep certified landing moving unless every ZK system is broken, a provisional landing becomes final by the 24-h rule, parameters freeze, and an unprovable forced-inclusion entry halts landing only until a four-hour stall voids it, over T days at most about T / 7 + 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls per budget (round 6, R6H-1). Only a proof conflict and a one-leaf outage in a certificate-free regime (L19) wait for it.

7. Give the three bins of the accountability argument with one example each.

Impossible (landing uncertified data; since the compatible upgrades, pausing bridging by any address but the DAO), worthless (withholding), slashable (equivocation). No rule accepts an unsigned claim.

8. Which limitation would you attack first as a red-teamer, and why?

Any answer with a trace is right; the iteration reports record what the red team actually found.
