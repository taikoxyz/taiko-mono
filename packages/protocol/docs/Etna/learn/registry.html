The problem: joining now must not rewrite yesterday's rights

Admission without an operator

Assumed, design motivation. Replacing a whitelist requires more than a public registration button. Nodes checking a block before its L1 landing need the same answer about who was eligible at that time. A later registration, exit or key rotation must not silently change that answer. S1 separates the time a write reaches L1 from the time it changes a role. S1-R02.

Assumed, vocabulary. An owner is the address whose keys and bonded seats are recorded; it may be a contract. A seat is a fixed registry index with an owner and an active interval. A term is a period on the protocol's time grid. A seat in that period's domain is in the registered pool; that alone is not a promise of a sequencing turn or user confirmation. S1 vocabulary; holder selection boundary.

The mechanism: public entry, future effect, retained liability

Follow the seat through its lifecycle

Assumed, S1-R04/R05. An entrant proves control of its sequencer key and possession of its attester key, funds the required nonreserve stake/buffer and landing reserve, and calls register(count). The contract checks the cap, key bindings, capital, absence of a pending exit and absence of the owner's class-A slash record. It assigns seat indices. No operator approves the address. A class-A-slash record remains attached to that owner; a fresh address enters as a new owner with its own capital and fresh keys. Registration; key checks.

Assumed, S1-R06/R07. An exit request must satisfy the minimum tenure. It takes effect after the registration delay; the seat still counts as held after it leaves the domain. Only finalization after the evidence window releases that seat's share of the withdrawal floor and allows index reuse. Finalization creates no payout entitlement beyond the remaining ledger. A partial exit leaves the remaining seats' keys alone; a terminal exit closes the keys' validity intervals while retaining historical attribution. Tenure; exit and release; duration register.

Assumed, S1-R08/R09. Specified cleanup paths do not depend on the affected owner returning: anyone may exit a class-A-slashed owner or a sufficiently stale suspended/lapsed owner, and finalize those exits under the same rules. Suspension is a separate eligibility flag; it does not itself erase the seat from the domain or reshuffle other owners' ranks. Permissionless cleanup; eligibility flags.

Why later writes do not change a past term

Read the version effective at the term's start

Assumed, S1-R02/R10. Entry, exit and key-rotation writes have future effective dates. The domain tree keeps immutable versions tagged by those dates. For a term, a node uses its prescribed older L1 reference and the version effective at the term's start; the inbox later computes from the same historical inputs. A subsequent write creates a later version instead of rewriting the earlier one. Snapshot rule; persistent versions.

Proven conditionally. Under S1's A-T1 reorg premise and its complete flag rules, the node and later landing agree on that term's role inputs. This claim is not “every write has the same delay”: S1-R09 has separate flag dates and a bounded prospective suspension-merge exception. D45's minimum-record rule prevents that merge from changing an instant a node has already evaluated. Snapshot argument; flag-store argument; named premises. We leave committee voting and user-confirmation guarantees to their own lessons.

Keep ordinary seat entry separate from optional sentinel bonding

An armed pocket is a different condition

Assumed, D45/D48 as applied in S1. Registering an ordinary seat does not require arming a sentinel pocket. The special bonded-signer paths read the liable owner's armed pocket at their L1 reference, not merely money at its signing key's address; a pending disarm counts as unavailable for new admission. That pocket has separate retention and class-A-debit rules. The no-committee designated holder path instead keeps holder plus non-exclusion admission and ordinary live collectible backing; it does not require that pocket. These conditions cannot be swapped. Bonded-signer definition; separate pocket; D48 holder-path scope.

Open. This distinction does not complete the recovery or slashing specification. D52 preserves C2/C5/C6 reference-premise obligations, C7 integration, the hatch synchronization and the joint S2/S3 evidence gate. The holder's live backing can be depleted; do not advertise the bonded-path floor for every registered holder. D52 merge limits; S1-R18's explicit bound.

Attack this building block

What entry and exit do—and do not—protect

Check your understanding

Does a successful registration immediately let the new seat participate?

Assumed rule: no. Registration creates a pending seat; its role interval starts at its future activeFrom. Relevant keys also need to be valid at the term's start. Registration; key intervals.

The seat has reached activeUntil. Is its index free, and did the owner receive a transfer?

Assumed rule: neither follows. It is expired but held until the evidence wait and finalization. Finalization makes the index free and lowers the ledger floor; it transfers and mints nothing. A partial exit leaves current keys in place for remaining seats. S1-R07.

Can an unarmed no-committee holder be described as having the bonded-signer pocket's floor?

Assumed scope: no. D48 keeps that holder path open without arming. Its backing is ordinary live collectible collateral, which may be depleted; the separate bonded-signer condition cannot be inferred from having a seat. S1-R18.

Challenge this

  • Assumed A-T1: can an L1 reorg invalidate the historical reference a node used? Identify the exact premise before claiming the old answer survives.
  • Proven conditionally, historical attribution: can a partial exit, key rotation or later registration make an earlier duty point to a different owner, or reuse an index before finalization?
  • Open composition: can a downstream rule silently replace the liable owner's pocket with a signing key's balance, or present a per-address cap as an entity cap? Check the source predicates and their remaining integration obligations.
