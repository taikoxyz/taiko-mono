The problem: correct parts can compose incorrectly

Proven lesson from the review: an exact proof statement is insufficient if its inner byte decoder is undefined. A safe queue rule is insufficient if the initial head never chooses its first snapshot. A nonce lock is insufficient if a reverted action can consume it without advancing the head. Composition needs explicit boundaries, not just individually plausible components.

The assembled mechanism

Assumed rules: nodes execute complete candidate blocks against authenticated parents and origins. Bonded signers accept public-fragment deadlines. A sealed candidate's complete data and context are staged without reserving anything. Any producer may authorize an applicable statement; any funded caller may atomically submit its proof, republish its data and pay the current rent. The verified successor processes the frozen forced prefix and publishes its L2 checkpoint to the existing L1 SignalService. Separately, every L2 block records its authenticated L1 execution hash through standard EIP-4788; ordinary pin/reveal transactions persist L1 checkpoints in the existing L2 SignalService when needed.

Proven safety sketch under A-LEGACY/A-CRYPTO/A-IMPLEMENTATION/A-L1/A-UPGRADE: H0 authenticates the legacy boundary. Inductively, each accepted successor verifies the complete execution/DA relation against the unique current parent. Only that accepted output can publish a new rollup checkpoint on L1. In the other direction, exact header-origin equality authenticates 4788 hashes; pin/reveal binds a full L1 header to one of those hashes before persisting its checkpoint. Explicit first-block guards close the legacy writer paths. Existing Bridge proofs and replay rules consume the retained authenticated state. Neither a soft signature, availability payment, auction price nor DAO assertion substitutes for the execution proof.

Proven conditional liveness sketch: no incumbent service or job reserves the parent. A new capable, funded prover with reconstructible state can stage and prove a fresh child. Rent eventually reaches zero if no one advances; refreshing an expired candidate does not reset that price. If somebody advances first, canonical progress occurred and the immutable FIFO snapshot constrains its required work. Eventual affordable inclusion, available data and feasible proving remain premises. Forced pinning selects its processing block's origin; a later pinned reveal has no ring expiry, under the 1,536-byte header and 2,048-byte queue limits. Caller funding, a recoverable header and separately feasible bridge proofs remain necessary. Zero capable provers, lost history or unbounded L1 censorship defeat those premises.

Worked example: one healthy segment

Assumed example: an accepted parent starts the rent clock at time 10,000. A usable authenticated origin is available, and the publisher issues blocks through 10,060. It stages at 10,060 and finishes proving by 10,240. Each bonded fragment is published by its own issuedAt+240 deadline. The segment matures at 10,420. A caller landing then pays q(420)=0.026666666666666667 ETH plus ordinary L1 costs.

Proven consequences: a new forced enqueue at 10,419 does not change this proof's parent-frozen workload. If the assigned worker stopped, another prover can submit without waiting for its job timeout. Another mature candidate can still win and invalidate this soft suffix. After successful acceptance, users still wait for Ethereum finality; no fixed L1 slot count defines that finality.

Can the combined design sustain a useful workload?

Proven coupled-budget examples: a maximum 60-block, 122,880-byte segment every 60 seconds yields at most one accepted block per second and 2,048 encoded body bytes per second before headers and other overhead. That schedule pays approximately 67.2 ETH/day in rent before proofs, gas and both DA publications. One segment every 900 seconds pays zero rent but processes at most one block per 15 seconds, so a continuous one-second soft branch eventually hits the 900-block speculative horizon.

Proven additional burdens under the stated scenarios: bonded publication of 10,000 data bytes per second at the modeled 64-gas-per-byte floor and 20 gwei contributes 1,105.92 ETH/day in byte-only calldata cost. With 1,000 predecessors and a conditional 660-second maximum accepted-head interval, the conservative forced-processing bound is 46 hours 29 minutes. None of these examples is an observed deployment result, and no uniform short delay applies to arbitrary paid backlog.

Open production verdict: the combined operating profile has no demonstrated affordable point meeting an agreed Taiko workload and service target. Humans must choose acceptable workload, delay and soft-confirmation semantics; engineers must then demonstrate the complete resource and funding budget. If the chosen workload cannot fit or be funded, revising parameters or mechanisms and reviewing the consequences is required. Historical specification convergence does not close this issue.

What these arguments do not establish

Proven scope: soft finality, separate beneficial owners, unlimited MEV insurance and a uniform short delay through arbitrary paid backlog are not provided. Assumed: a funded competitor ready before rent reaches zero is necessary for the positive capture-cost bound. Open: proving throughput, actual maximum gas, service economics, live storage compatibility, pin/reveal gas and first-block system-call/migration rehearsal require evidence. Historical clean reviews do not perform those experiments or approve changed rules.

Check your understanding

Every incumbent prover disappears. Which promise keeps the chain live?

Proven conditionally: open entry permits a new funded capable prover to act; no promise creates proving hardware or missing state. Without any such entrant, the chain halts safely.

Does a missed fragment deadline authorize a replacement state root?

Proven: no. Evidence settles a bounded service reserve. Canonical roots still require complete valid execution proofs and data.

A cartel wins every zero-rent head. Does this falsify the capture theorem or sustain one-second settlement?

Proven: it does not falsify the conditional theorem if no funded competitor was ready and includable before 900 seconds. But at exactly one 60-block segment per 900 seconds, canonical processing averages at most one block per 15 seconds. This exposes both the competition premise and the unresolved cost/capacity tradeoff; neither can be advertised away.

Challenge the complete protocol

Choose one invariant: authenticated roots, conservation of one asset, immutable forced work, historical default settlement or open replacement. Construct a starting state and an action sequence that violates it while satisfying every named premise. If your trace instead removes a premise, identify the user-visible failure and decide whether accepting that risk is justified. A severity label never waives a failed hard requirement.
