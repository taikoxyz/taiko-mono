Lesson 12: the L1 environment

Prerequisite: lesson 6. Design pages: L1 dependencies, Frame transactions.

Goal

Know which upcoming L1 changes matter, which do not, and how to check any Etna rule against a change of slot time.

The idea

Nothing in Glamsterdam or Hegotá is a hard dependency. Etna needs only what is live today: blobs with blobhash and the KZG precompile, the EIP-7825 transaction gas cap (224) as a constraint, ecrecover and the EIP-2537 BLS precompiles, block.timestamp, prevrandao, and blockhash with EIP-2935. It must tolerate ePBS (payloads canonical one slot later, Empty slots), state-gas repricing (EIP-8037, 97,920 gas per fresh slot), new header fields and shorter slots. It may use Frame Transactions for zero-cost races and FOCIL for calldata legs (registrations, evidence, calldata forced-inclusion requests); FOCIL never covers a blob-carrying landing.

The slot-time test

Every Etna constant is in seconds, TAIKO, wei, gas or a count. To check a rule at a new slot time, ask: does it count L1 blocks? Only two do, and both are L1-given: blockhash (256 blocks) and EIP-2935 (8,191 blocks). The attester's anchor-lag policy was 20 blocks until the round-5 fix pass found that at 2-second slots 20 blocks is 40 s, below the 48-s minimum anchor age, so honest blocks would be refused; it is now FI_ANCHOR_LAG = 288 s. Windows in seconds cover more blocks as slots shrink, so finality margins grow; windows in blocks shrink in wall time, so the design keeps its worst-case anchor age at an on-time landing (1,800 + 60 + 3,600 + 300 = 5,760 s, about 96 minutes) under the 2935 window at 2-second slots (16,382 s, 4.55 hours; the landing horizon is the smaller of that window and the 34-hour role horizon). Registry writes cost one domain-tree version per seat, about 16 fresh nodes or 1.6 M state gas, and state gas lies outside the per-transaction cap, so a register(8) fits in one call (round 6, R6S-4). Blob capacity per second is roughly constant, so batch sizes are per second; gas per block shrinks, so a landing stays under 10 M gas. Blobs per L1 block shrink too, and a landing is one transaction in one L1 block, so at 2-second slots a landing carries at most LANDING_BYTES = 3 × 130,044 = 390,132 bytes, and the per-landing reward is counted in units of LANDING_UNIT = 254,000 bytes, a constant at every slot time (round 6, R6H-3): landing k of a term is paid only if k ≤ ⌈bytesLanded / LANDING_UNIT⌉. Why that is enough: V9 holds every block's record to one blob (130,035 bytes), so a greedily packed landing that is not its term's last carries more than 390,132 − 130,044 = 260,088 bytes of payload even at 2-second slots, at most about 12,500 bytes of a term are not counted in bytesLanded, and bytesLanded after k greedy landings exceeds (k − 1) × 254,000 (landing page §5). A term at the V10 cap of 1,270,000 bytes (five units, about 9.8 blobs) therefore needs at most five landings at 2-second slots and two six-blob landings at 12-second slots, each paid, so the break-even fee is ≈ 100 gwei per landing at every slot time. R_BLOB (1 TAIKO per blob) is paid for at most 15 blobs per term, the five paid landings at three blobs each, so the 7,161-TAIKO per-term exposure bounds every landing pattern (round 6 fix pass).

Frame Transactions in two shapes

  • Direct to builder (shape 2): the lander's own nonce with an expiry frame in front and a status-VERIFY frame behind. Any revert makes the whole transaction invalid: no gas, no blob fee, no nonce consumed. This is the primary landing path and needs no builder trust for safety.
  • Public mempool (shape 1): a shared gate contract G whose account nonce is the lock; of all transactions sharing (G, n) in one block at most one can be included and the same-block losers are invalid and free. It is the only censorship-resistant race that propagates through the public mempool. Its pins: exactly four frames with G as frame 1; frame 3 the only SENDER frame, targeting the inbox's landFor(address _rewardTo, LandInput _in) with the selector pinned, word 0 equal to the payer P and a data length between 68 and LAND_CALLDATA_MAX bytes (8,192 assumed), the gate reading only that fixed prefix and P's signature covering the rest (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01: the research record's 68-byte pin came from a proposeFor(address, bytes32) whose data lived in blobs; a landing carries its proof in calldata). The gate is deployed only once the EIP is Final, because the expiry address, the expiry data and the opcode numbers are still moving; the inbox's gate address is an immutable, zero until then (a DAO upgrade changes it), and the direct land(...) from any EOA stays callable behind no switch.
  • A stale landing (its parent consumed by another landing) reverts in both shapes: shape 2's trailing VERIFY makes the revert invalid and free; a shape-1 copy is still valid, fails at the inbox's first check (parent hash and firstHeight, before blob or proof verification, about 30k gas of frame execution plus the intrinsic cost and the blob fee), burns nonce n and costs P that much, because the public-pool rules let G's prefix read no storage but its own, so G cannot compare the input with lastLanded before payment (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Shape 1 is zero-cost only against other shape-1 competitors for the same nonce.
  • Without EIP-8141: plain transactions with a deadline field through revert-protected builders; a losing lander pays its execution gas plus the burned blob fee, a stale landing reverts with a deadline error, and nothing in the inbox interface changes. Two changes stay in both regimes because they remove other-triggerable reverts: forced inclusions consumed by due time, and pull-based fees and refunds (withdrawFiRefund(id) per entry).

Self-check

Why does Etna never read L1's EIP-4788 beacon roots, although its L2 blocks use EIP-4788?

On L1, reading a root "at time T" means probing by slot timestamp (R5), the record has gaps at ePBS Empty slots, and its window is 8,191 slots, 4.55 hours at 2-second slots. The prevrandao accumulator gives the seed the same entropy without any of that. L2's use is different (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147): each L2 header's parentBeaconBlockRoot carries the hash of the anchored L1 execution block, and L2's own EIP-4788 contract records it keyed by L2 timestamp, so its 8,191-s reveal window is the same at every L1 slot time and none of those objections applies; on L2 that contract holds L1 execution block hashes, not beacon roots.

What is the one property Frame Transactions do not give?

That the L2 slot is used. They fix who pays for a lost race, never whether a landing happens; an ePBS builder can still pay its bid and reveal an invalid payload, and clients never derive the next landable range from nonce(G), only from lastLanded.

At 2-second slots, why does the inbox use the EIP-2935 path for almost every landing?

blockhash then covers only 512 s, 8.5 minutes, less than the happy-path landing latency after a 48-second anchor age plus proving; the 2935 path covers 16,382 s.

A shape-1 landing loses because a shape-2 landing by someone else consumed its parent in the previous block. What does its payer owe?

The early revert: the transaction is valid, frame 3 reverts at the inbox's first check (about 30k gas of frame execution) and P pays that plus the intrinsic cost and the blob fee, and nonce n is burned. Had the competitor been another shape-1 transaction for the same (G, n) in the same block, the loser would have been invalid and free. The design calls shape 1 a zero-cost race only against other shape-1 competitors (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).

Why is the per-landing reward unit 254,000 bytes, less than the 390,132 bytes of the three blobs one L1 block carries at 2-second slots?

The lander chooses its range boundaries and the sequencer its block sizes. At 2-second slots no landing carries more than three blobs, and a landing never splits a block's record, so a greedy landing can leave up to one record's worth of space unused. V9 holds every record to one blob (130,035 bytes), so a greedily packed landing that is not its term's last still carries more than 390,132 − 130,044 = 260,088 bytes of payload, at most about 12,500 bytes of a term go uncounted (framing, FI-block records of about 250 bytes and the view changes they open), and bytesLanded after k greedy landings exceeds (k − 1) × 254,000; counted in that unit, the reward pays each of the at most five landings a term at the 1,270,000-byte cap needs, however its blocks pack, and pays one-block landings at most once per term. Two earlier units failed against a schedule: one of six blobs paid one of the five three-blob landings a full term needed at 2-second slots, putting the break-even fee near 30 to 40 gwei instead of 100; one of three blobs, with records of up to three blobs, let a sequencer size its blocks so that each landing carried little more than one blob of the term, which then needed more landings than the reward paid (round 6, R6H-3).

Challenge this

Pick any parameter on the parameters page and try to break it at 2-second slots with ePBS Empty slots. The design claims every one survives; find the weakest. Then try to turn the stale shape-1 cost into a griefing lever: can a third party make an honest public-mempool lander pay early reverts repeatedly?
