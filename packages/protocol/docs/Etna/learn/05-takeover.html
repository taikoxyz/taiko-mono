Lesson 5: silence, takeover, and the withholding defence

Prerequisite: lesson 4. Design page: Preconfirmation & certificates §5 to §7.

Goal

Explain how two honest nodes reach the same decision about who sequences after a silence, and why an operator who builds privately gains nothing.

The idea

Non-receipt is unprovable: nobody can prove a block was not sent. So Etna never punishes silence. Instead, silence is answered by a signed object, the view change, that 22 attesters produce after 5 seconds in which no certificate appeared while they held no block of the view that they had attested and that is still uncertified. It names the highest certified block (the lock) and hands the term to the next view. The view change is also the seal of the closed view's batch: the sequencer never signs a seal, so there is nothing for it to withhold. Blocks the committee never received have no certificate, cannot be built upon (V3), cannot be landed (lesson 6), and are worthless within one timeout.

The rules

  1. Timeout. An attester signs the timeout TO(t, v) (no lock in it) when, for TIMEOUT = 5 s by its own clock, no new certificate of the view has appeared and it holds no block of the view that it attested and that is still uncertified (kind TIMEOUT), or when the term has ended (kind TERM_END). The second condition is about the attester's own attestations, not about which messages arrive: gossip drops a re-sent header, and a signed header costs nothing to emit, so arrival cannot tell a holding leader from a dead one. An attester attests only a block that passes every validity rule and its signing policy. Which leaders that covers: a leader held because a minority abstains produced two real blocks, the honest attesters attested them and they stay uncertified, so no honest attester times it out; a dead leader leaves nothing attested and uncertified, so every honest attester times it out; a leader that gossips headers without payloads, invalid blocks or future timestamps gets nothing attested and is timed out at 5 s like a dead one unless a blocking minority of the committee colludes with it, and it cannot later put different content under lock + 1, because two headers at one height are equivocation (S1). Round 3's trigger ("no certificate for 5 s") let an abstaining minority hand a live primary's term to the minority's backup; round 4 (R4H-4) changed it to "any signed header above the lock", which let a holder keep a view for a whole term by gossiping bodiless headers (round 5, R5H-1 and R5-C1); round 5's arrival-based repair was itself defeated by gossip deduplication, and the fix pass adopted the rule above, for which the certificate page is normative. Consequence: an abstaining minority of 11 or more can only make a term blank (no certificates; the leader keeps its view; nothing of it lands; nobody is penalized), never take the view or bleed the leader (invariant I3). The lock travels in a separate lock vote, which a key may only ever raise. The clock decides only whether to sign.
  2. Committee redraw. An attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for 5 s may sign REDRAW(t, v, vcHash, n) with n = 1 or 2. With ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 such signatures (17 of 32) the committee of the view is redrawn from the seed with a different domain byte, for the same leader, view and opening object; attestations, lock votes, certificates and view changes of the redrawn committee carry n. A REDRAW has only one form per (t, v, vcHash, n), so it needs no slashing rule. It raises the blanking threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 in the first draw and makes a cartel hold a blocking minority in every redraw (round 4, R4-C1). The price of the vote: L1 cannot check that the voters saw no certificate, so 17 colluders can redraw a committee that has already locked a block, and if 22 of the redrawn committee then lock below it, only the keys sitting in both committees are slashable; the lock-lie rule S3b spans redraw counts within one opening object, and the 12-slashings bound of lesson 4 holds within one draw.
  3. View change. Anyone holding 22 timeouts proposes VC(t, v, lock*) with the highest certificate it holds; attesters cast lock votes iff they hold that certificate's chain, re-voting upward if a higher lock appears, and never other lock for the view. The decision to switch is the existence of the VC, a signed object, so every node holding it switches identically.
  4. Next view. The next holder's first block carries the VC and builds on the lock. At term end the next term's holder does the same. If a whole committee is silent, the next term's committee signs a fallback VC after 12 s (and the one after it 10 s later, and so on); L1 records and lands a fallback only from that time, only while the term's own committee has recorded no VC for the view, and only with a certificate at the fallback's lock, and the own committee's VC at a higher lock supersedes a recorded fallback until a landing has consumed it (round 5, R5S-5: without those gates a colluding next committee could void an honest term's locked blocks with one unslashable record).
  5. Recording. Anyone can put a VC on L1 with recordViewChange, and the next leader and every signer must do so promptly, because the gates on a later committee's FALLBACK rely on the term's own view change being on L1 first (round 5 fix pass); the first recorded per (term, view, closes) wins and later landings must match it. A REPLACE or RESUME VC is accepted only if its lock equals lastLanded and the replaceable window has opened at record time; it is dead, ignored by that rule, once lastLanded moves above its lock (so it never blocks the original view's own closing VC); and a record voids nothing by itself: only a landed replacement or forced batch voids certified blocks (lesson 7; round 4, R4H-5).

Self-check

Two honest nodes have clocks 3 seconds apart. Can they disagree on who the holder is?

No. Each decides by the view changes it holds. One may sign its timeout later than the other, but neither switches until a VC exists, and a VC is the same object for both.

Why is the absence penalty burned rather than paid to the backup that takes over?

Paying the backup would create an incentive for a backup allied with 22 attesters to sign a timeout against a live primary. Burning removes the beneficiary.

What does a false view change cost the victim?

One view and 50 TAIKO, debited from the 5 % buffer above the floor and then from seat stake, never from the landing reserve, and nothing else: a timeout never changes eligibility, because red-team round 1 showed that a timeout must never drive suspension. Only the 50 % hard floor ends eligibility, after about 220 bled views from a full buffer (200 from the floor). Since round 4 a false timeout VC needs 22 attesters that all lie about receiving the leader's attestable blocks; an abstaining minority can blank a term but cannot produce one. The design accepts the bleed because it is nuisance-level and no signature pair can prove silence either way.

Challenge this

A colluding committee refuses to attest for a whole term and records its own TERM_END view change one second before the next committee's fallback would have formed. Work out what the honest fallback chain loses (the design says up to about a minute) and whether any rule could distinguish this from an honest slow committee.
