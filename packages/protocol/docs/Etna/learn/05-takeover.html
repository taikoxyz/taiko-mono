Lesson 5: silence, takeover, and the withholding defence

Prerequisite: lesson 4. Design page: Preconfirmation & certificates §1, §5 to §7.

Goal

Explain how two honest nodes reach the same decision about who sequences after a silence or at a term's end, and why an operator who builds privately gains nothing.

The idea

Non-receipt is unprovable: nobody can prove a block was not sent. So Etna never punishes silence. Instead, silence is answered by a signed object, the view change, that 22 attesters produce after 5 seconds without a certificate while holding no block of the view that they attested and that is still uncertified. It names the highest certified block (the lock) and hands the term to the next view. The view change is also the seal of the closed view's batch: the sequencer never signs a seal, so there is nothing for it to withhold. Blocks the committee never received have no certificate, cannot be built upon (V3), cannot be landed (lesson 6), and are worthless within one timeout.

The rules

  1. Timeout. An attester signs TO(t, v) of kind TIMEOUT (no lock in it; only while v < V_MAX = 4) when, for TIMEOUT = 5 s by its own clock, no new certificate of the view has appeared and it holds no block of the view, under the current redraw count, that it attested and that is still uncertified. The 5 s are counted from the moment the attester first holds the view's opening object (for view 0, the previous term's closing view change; for a later view, the view change that opened it; for a redrawn committee, the redraw) and restart on each new certificate (round 6, R6H-4), so a leader that publishes nothing is timed out 5 s after its view opened, and nobody can time out a view before holding the object that opened it. The second condition is about the attester's own attestations, not about arrival: gossip drops a re-sent header and a signed header costs nothing to emit, so arrival cannot tell a holding leader from a dead one. An attester attests only a block that passes every validity rule (V1 to V6, V8, V9, V10) and its signing policy, so "attested" already excludes a bodiless header, an unavailable payload, a forged anchor, a skipped forced inclusion, an oversized block and a future or stale timestamp. What that covers (certificate page §1): (a) a leader held because a minority abstains produced real blocks that the honest attesters attested and that stay uncertified, so nobody honest times it out and the term is blank, never transferred; (b) a dead leader leaves nothing attested and uncertified, so every honest attester times it out at 5 s and it pays MISS; (c) a leader gossiping bodiless, invalid or future-stamped headers gets nothing attested and is timed out at 5 s like a dead one, and cannot later put other content under lock + 1 (two headers at one height are S1); (d) in a real partition, or when a block was attested by only part of the committee and the leader then stops, the attesters that attested do not time out, fewer than 22 sign, and the view runs to its end without certificates and without MISS; with c colluding members withholding timeouts the split needs only 11 − c honest attesters to have attested, so at c ≥ 11 a colluding leader keeps a view producing nothing: one term unserved and one term's delay of a due forced inclusion (the 112-minute bound holds). Round 3's trigger, "no certificate for 5 s", let eleven abstainers hand a live primary's term to a cartel backup (round 3, C1 and S5); round 4 fixed the signal (R4H-4), round 5 bounded it (R5H-1, R5-C1), and the round-5 verification moved it from message arrival to the attester's own attestations. Consequence: an abstaining minority of 11 or more can only make a term blank (no certificates; the leader keeps its view; nothing of it lands; nobody is penalized) and, by withholding its term-end lock votes as well, delay the handoff out of that committee by 12 to 22 s, until a later committee's fallback (round 6, R6-C2); it can never take the view or bleed the leader (invariant I3). The lock travels in a separate lock vote, which a key may only ever raise. The clock decides only whether to sign.
  2. Term end and the pipelined handoff (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). An attester signs kind TERM_END, and then its lock vote, at the earlier of (a) the moment it holds a certificate, under the same opening object, of a block whose timestamp is termEnd − 1, the term's last possible block under V4, and that block extends its lock; or (b) termEnd + END_GRACE = 2 s by its clock, whether or not any block of the view ever arrived. (a) is clock-free and never a lock lie, since every header the key attested carried a certificate at or below its lock; a certificate of such a block on another branch fires nothing and the key waits for (b). Leader policy: issue the block stamped termEnd − 1 even when empty. Happy path: that block is published at termEnd − 1, certified by about termEnd − 0.3 s, the view change forms at about termEnd, and the next term's first block, stamped termEnd, carries it about 0.5 s later: the 1-s grid has no empty slot at the handoff. Clock fallback: when that block is missing or uncertified, TERM_END is signed at termEnd + 2 s, the view change forms at about + 2.5 s and the next term's first block comes at about + 3 s, so at most END_GRACE + 2 = 4 grid slots are empty, once per term; a leader that withholds its last block gains nothing but those four slots, since (b) runs on the attesters' clocks.
  3. Committee redraw. An attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for 5 s may sign REDRAW(t, v, vcHash, n) with n = 1 or 2 (REDRAW_MAX = 2). With ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 such signatures (17 of 32) the committee of the view is redrawn from the seed with a different domain byte, for the same leader, view and opening object; its members attest the view's valid blocks they received on time (the receipt-time staleness bound of lesson 4), and every message of the redrawn committee carries n. A REDRAW has one form per (t, v, vcHash, n), so it needs no slashing rule, changes no holder and applies no penalty. It raises the blanking threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 in the first draw and makes a cartel hold a blocking minority in every redraw (round 4, R4-C1). The price: L1 cannot check that the voters saw no certificate, so 17 colluders can redraw a committee that has already locked a block, and if 22 of the redrawn committee then lock below it, only the keys in both committees are slashable; S3b spans redraw counts within one opening object, and the 12-slashings bound of lesson 4 holds within one redraw count.
  4. View change. Anyone holding 22 timeouts of one (t, v, opening object, kind) proposes VC(t, v, lock*) with the highest certificate it holds, attaching it; an attester casts a lock vote iff it holds that certificate's chain and lock* is at or above its own lock, re-voting upward if a higher lock appears (lock votes are monotone) and never for another lock at one height, so with 22 honest attesters online the VC forms at the honest maximum in one extra round trip, about 0.4 s (round 3, S5). The decision to switch is the existence of the VC, a signed object, so every node holding it switches identically; a node lacking it learns it from the next view's first block (V2).
  5. Next view and the fallback. The next holder's first block carries the VC and builds on the lock; after V_MAX view changes the term idles to its end. If a whole committee is silent, the committee of a later term t' may sign a FALLBACK VC for any view of term t once termEnd + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK × (t' − t) has passed (12 s for the next committee, 22 s for the one after), only while it holds no VC of the term's own committee for that view, sees none recorded, and never below a recorded certificate of that committee. L1 applies the same gates (round 5, R5S-5; round 6, R6S-3): (i) recordViewChange and the landing accept a fallback only from that L1 time and only while no live VC of the term's own committee is recorded; (ii) a landing that consumes a fallback must present a certificate at its lock, so a fallback never lands uncertified blocks; (iii) the own committee's VC at a higher lock supersedes a recorded fallback until a landing has consumed it; (iv) anyone may record a certificate of the term's own committee with recordCertificate (about 165k gas for a pinned term, assumed), L1 refuses a fallback whose lock is below the highest certificate recorded for the view, and a fallback recorded before a higher certificate is dead, so a later committee can close the view at the certified lock. Gate (iv) needs no quorum of the term's own committee, which a blocking minority could deny, and calls nothing a lie: the later committee's keys attested nothing in the view. What remains (limitation L5) is a fallback consumed by a landing in the same L1 block as, or before, the record: it needs a colluding quorum of the later committee, a next leader that builds on the fallback lock and L1 ordering against the record, and it orphans every block above the fallback lock until that landing, minutes of blocks.
  6. Recording. Anyone can put a VC on L1 with recordViewChange; the next leader records every VC of the term's own committee that it carries, and every signer records one within TO_REBROADCAST = 2 s of its formation if no record exists yet, because the fallback gates rest on the own committee's records reaching L1 first (round 5 fix pass); the first recorded per (term, view, closes) wins and later landings must match it. When no TERM_END VC has formed by termEnd + 2.5 s, the view's highest certificate is recorded with recordCertificate, staggered so that one record normally suffices: the leader of the term's last view and the member at the lowest set bit of the certificate's bitmap send it at once, every other member only after one L1 block without a record (round 6, R6S-3). A blocking minority that withholds its term-end votes thus costs the honest members one or two records per withheld term (limitation L2), and the record still precedes any landing that could consume a fallback, which needs blocks built on its lock certified and proven. On the happy path the TERM_END VC forms at about termEnd and this costs nothing. A REPLACE or RESUME VC is recorded only if its lock equals lastLanded and the replaceable window has opened, and is dead once lastLanded passes its lock; a record voids nothing by itself: only a landed replacement or forced batch voids certified blocks (lesson 7; round 4, R4H-5).

Self-check

Two honest nodes have clocks 3 seconds apart. Can they disagree on who the holder is?

No. Each decides by the view changes it holds. One may sign its timeout or its clock-fallback TERM_END later than the other, but neither switches until a VC exists, and a VC is the same object for both. On the happy path the TERM_END signal does not read the clock at all: it fires on a certificate.

Why is the absence penalty burned rather than paid to the backup that takes over?

Paying the backup would create an incentive for a backup allied with 22 attesters to sign a timeout against a live primary. Burning removes the beneficiary.

What does a false view change cost the victim?

One view and MISS_PENALTY = 50 TAIKO, burned, debited from the 5 % buffer above the floor (1,000 TAIKO per seat, 20 bleeds) and then from seat stake, never from the landing reserve, and nothing else: a timeout never changes eligibility, because red-team round 1 showed that a timeout must never drive suspension. Only the 50 % hard floor ends eligibility, after 220 bleeds of one seat from buffer to floor. Since the round-5 fix pass a false timeout VC needs 22 attesters holding nothing they attested of the leader's blocks; an abstaining minority can blank a term but cannot produce one. The bleed is accepted because it is nuisance-level and no signature pair can prove silence either way.

Challenge this

A colluding committee refuses to attest for a whole term and records its own TERM_END view change one second before the next committee's fallback would have formed. Work out what the honest fallback chain loses (the design says up to about a minute; limitation L5 (a)) and whether any rule could distinguish this from an honest slow committee. Then take the other direction: with a blocking minority of the honest committee and a quorum of the next one, try to get a fallback below the honest lock consumed by a landing before the honest certificate record reaches L1, and name every resource you need (the design says: L1 ordering against a record sent at termEnd + 2.5 s, or one L1 block later if you also hold both first senders; limitation L5 (b)).
