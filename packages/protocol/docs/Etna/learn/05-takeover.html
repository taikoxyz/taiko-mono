Lesson 5: silence, takeover, and the withholding defence

Prerequisite: lesson 4. Design page: Preconfirmation & certificates §5 to §7.

Goal

Explain how two honest nodes reach the same decision about who sequences after a silence, and why an operator who builds privately gains nothing.

The idea

Non-receipt is unprovable: nobody can prove a block was not sent. So Etna never punishes silence. Instead, silence is answered by a signed object, the view change, that 22 attesters produce after 5 seconds without a new certificate. It names the highest certified block (the lock) and hands the term to the next view. The view change is also the seal of the closed view's batch: the sequencer never signs a seal, so there is nothing for it to withhold. Blocks the committee never received have no certificate, cannot be built upon (V3), cannot be landed (lesson 6), and are worthless within one timeout.

The rules

  1. Timeout. An attester signs the timeout TO(t, v) (no lock in it) when its own clock has seen no new certificate for 5 s and it has attested nothing of the view in that window (kind TIMEOUT; round 3 showed that without the second condition an abstaining minority could make the honest majority hand a live primary's term to the minority's backup), or when the term has ended (kind TERM_END). The lock travels in a separate lock vote, which a key may only ever raise. The clock decides only whether to sign.
  2. View change. Anyone holding 22 timeouts proposes VC(t, v, lock*) with the highest certificate it holds; attesters cast lock votes iff they hold that certificate's chain, re-voting upward if a higher lock appears, and never other lock for the view. The decision to switch is the existence of the VC, a signed object, so every node holding it switches identically.
  3. Next view. The next holder's first block carries the VC and builds on the lock. At term end the next term's holder does the same. If a whole committee is silent, the next term's committee signs a fallback VC after 12 s.
  4. Recording. Anyone can put a VC on L1 with recordViewChange; the first recorded per (term, view) wins and later landings must match it.

Self-check

Two honest nodes have clocks 3 seconds apart. Can they disagree on who the holder is?

No. Each decides by the view changes it holds. One may sign its timeout later than the other, but neither switches until a VC exists, and a VC is the same object for both.

Why is the absence penalty burned rather than paid to the backup that takes over?

Paying the backup would create an incentive for a backup allied with 22 attesters to sign a timeout against a live primary. Burning removes the beneficiary.

What does a false view change cost the victim?

One view and 50 TAIKO, and nothing else: a timeout never changes eligibility, because red-team round 1 showed that eleven abstaining seats could otherwise suspend honest operators. Only the 50 % hard floor ends eligibility, after about 200 bled views. The design accepts the bleed because it is nuisance-level and no signature pair can prove silence either way.

Challenge this

A colluding committee refuses to attest for a whole term and records its own TERM_END view change one second before the next committee's fallback would have formed. Work out what the honest fallback chain loses (the design says up to about a minute) and whether any rule could distinguish this from an honest slow committee.
