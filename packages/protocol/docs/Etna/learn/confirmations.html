What a certificate can promise

Signatures can prove participation. A safety claim needs the right conflicting evidence too.

The problem: a valid block is still waiting for L1

Separate a block, its votes and its promise

Assumed, S2-R03–R06. A leader signs the complete preconfirmation envelope's phHash. Attesters execute the received payload and check its actual signed inputs before signing. A certificate gathers a quorum of those attestations. A local lock remembers the highest held certificate and its chain, or the higher locally observed landed height. A certificate is evidence of signatures; it does not itself publish a custody checkpoint. Read the signed identity and the local lock.

Assumed, vocabulary. An opening identifies the view-change generation in which headers are signed; redraw distinguishes committee selections for that opening. These fields matter because the equal-height equivocation key includes both. A closing vote states the chain tip on which a view hands control onward. S2-R01; S2-R09.

The mechanism: follow one piece of evidence at a time

Certificates come before settlement

Proven conditionally, arithmetic only. With m = 32, Q = floor(2m/3) + 1 = 22. Two sets of 22 positions drawn from those same 32 overlap in at least 22 + 22 − 32 = 12. To turn this overlap into a slash, the two signed objects must also satisfy an evidence predicate. Two conflicting certificates at different heights are not automatically the equal-height S3a pair. S2-R02; S2-R21.

Worked adversary schedule: interleave two branches

Counting an intersection is not enough

Assumed, finding preconditions: one malicious leader and one committee position, m = 32 and Q = 22, one opening and redraw, valid execution and selectively delivered certificates. Proven conditionally, reported counterexample: the round-1 review describes interleaved certified heights. A concrete logical schedule starts with public C40: A41 carries C40; B42 extends the conflicting B41 and also carries C40; A43 extends A42 and carries C_A41. The leader plus one position completes certificates privately, revealing C_A41 so honest keys certify A43, then revealing terminal C_B42 so keys move their R06 locks to the higher conflicting certificate and close at B42. No key signed two headers at the same height; its attestation at A43 carries a certificate at 41, below the closing height 42, so the old height-only S3b comparison finds no lie. The closing displaces the locked A branch within its own opening without a slashable committee member. The leader's signed equivocation remains slashable. Heights 40–43 identify the logical schedule, not protocol parameters or executed fixture bytes.

Open, repair and exact fixture: this is a logical trace, not an executed test. The count in the promised argument is twelve; the reported schedule produces zero slashable committee members, so publishing the former as a general guarantee is unsound at D78. W22 proposes a protocol repair, to be attacked by J. This lesson does not adopt it. If the result instead widens the disclosed risk, D73 does not sign that wider risk automatically.

A merged sentence can still fail the next attack

Fully backed: once every committee closing on its chain above lastLanded is recorded on L1 and every FALLBACK on it is final, at an L1 block ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old. That is about 60 to 75 s after a view change at 12-s slots, and about 96 to 108 s after term end when a FALLBACK is on the chain. Check your understanding

Two 22-position quorums overlap in twelve. Does every fork slash twelve positions?

No. The signed objects must meet the same evidence rule, including its key and height requirements. The round-1 interleaving attack breaks the broader inference.

Does a certificate authorize bridge withdrawals?

No. Landing proof status, checkpoint publication and L1 finality are separate from the preconfirmation signatures.

Does D73's acceptance of G5 accept the new High?

No. Acceptance is scoped to the described residual. W22's repair needs review; a wider residual requires its own user decision.
