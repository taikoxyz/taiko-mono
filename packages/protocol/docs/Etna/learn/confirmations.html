What a certificate can promise

Signatures can prove participation. A safety claim needs the right conflicting evidence too.

The problem: a valid block is still waiting for L1

Separate a block, its votes and its promise

Assumed, accepted S2-R03–R06 under D83. A leader signs the complete preconfirmation envelope's phHash. Attesters execute the received payload and check its actual signed inputs before signing. A certificate gathers a quorum of those attestations. A local certificate lock retains an exact height and header identity. It advances only along that authenticated branch; a higher certificate on another branch is conflicting evidence, not a replacement lock. A separate persisted attested tip protects the signing history. Authorized landed recovery has its own reset rule. A certificate is evidence of signatures; it does not itself publish a custody checkpoint. Read the signed identity and the local lock.

Assumed, vocabulary. An opening identifies the view-change generation in which headers are signed; redraw distinguishes committee selections for that opening. These fields matter because the equal-height equivocation key includes both. A closing vote states the chain tip on which a view hands control onward. S2-R01; S2-R09.

The mechanism: follow one piece of evidence at a time

Certificates come before settlement

Proven conditionally, arithmetic only. With m = 32, Q = floor(2m/3) + 1 = 22. Two sets of 22 positions drawn from those same 32 overlap in at least 22 + 22 − 32 = 12. To turn this overlap into a slash, the two signed objects must also satisfy an evidence predicate. Two conflicting certificates at different heights are not automatically the equal-height S3a pair. S2-R02; S2-R21.

Historical D78 attack: interleave two branches

Counting an intersection is not enough

Assumed, finding preconditions: one malicious leader and one committee position, m = 32 and Q = 22, one opening and redraw, valid execution and selectively delivered certificates. Proven conditionally, reported counterexample: the round-1 review describes interleaved certified heights. A concrete logical schedule starts with public C40: A41 carries C40; B42 extends the conflicting B41 and also carries C40; A43 extends A42 and carries C_A41. The leader plus one position completes certificates privately, revealing C_A41 so honest keys certify A43, then revealing terminal C_B42 so keys move their R06 locks to the higher conflicting certificate and close at B42. No key signed two headers at the same height; its attestation at A43 carries a certificate at 41, below the closing height 42, so the old height-only S3b comparison finds no lie. The closing displaces the locked A branch within its own opening without a slashable committee member. The leader's signed equivocation remains slashable. Heights 40–43 identify the logical schedule, not protocol parameters or executed fixture bytes.

Assumed, disposition; open, exact fixture: this is the logical counterexample against D78, not an executed test of the repaired rules. D83 accepts the W22 mechanism below; it repairs this route instead of adding a user-accepted residual. Consumer adoption and executable evidence remain separate obligations.

Read the historical publication with its scope attached

A merged sentence can still fail the next attack

Assumed, historical D78 publication; open, complete implementation: the complete S2-R18 D1 text below is reproduced exactly, including its exceptions. D83 accepts the repair needed for the inside-opening argument; D85 accepts aggregate S3b and its C8 rows, while S3a branch-form adoption, other consumer integration, bounded executable evidence and fixtures are still required. Read the quotation as the specified conditional product, not as a deployed assurance or a new user acceptance.

"Locked, about 2 s (A-T9, unmeasured): a certified block carrying C(n). Displacing it by a conflicting certificate or attestation inside its own opening (S3a, S3b) makes 2Q(m_b) − m_b positions of its certifying committee provably slashable, less positions of owners already slashed: 12 at m = 32 (185,338.8 TAIKO only as the conditional launch illustration in §6; the label displays live owner-deduplicated backing at its read block). Until fully backed, nothing is slashable for: a second closing of any unlanded ancestor view, including across a redraw; a FALLBACK race; or the leader plus one committee position landing a privately held certificate’s continuation before the closing is recorded. Fully backed: once every committee closing on its chain above lastLanded is recorded on L1 and every FALLBACK on it is final, at an L1 block ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old. That is about 60 to 75 s after a view change at 12-s slots, and about 96 to 108 s after term end when a FALLBACK is on the chain. The backing is then R-C's minimum less already-slashed positions: 7 at m = 32 (108,114.3 TAIKO only as the conditional launch illustration, never a fixed floor) with an unlanded closing, else 12. Not covered: the redraw route with Q of a fresh draw; a landed replacement or forced batch after C2's deadline machinery; an L1 reorg deeper than ANCHOR_MIN_AGE; the DAO path; the landing horizon. A locked block can be lost with no slashable evidence when the leader plus one committee position wins R20’s private-continuation race; the resulting pipeline stall is repeatable and lasts until eligible certificate-free recovery, with no unconditional completion bound. The same coalition can strand a locked FALLBACK generation by completing a head-compatible H closing before F finality; this requires no private-continuation landing and has the same conditional certificate-free recovery exits (R20, G5-F)."

Assumed, D73/D78 scope. The G5 and G5-F disclosures are accepted residuals. G5 uses a privately held certificate's continuation; G5-F kills a provisional FALLBACK generation before finality without an attacker landing. Either can strand locks until conditional recovery. D83 separately repairs the interleaved-branch mechanism; the G5 acceptance did not make that attack harmless. Timings in the quote are unmeasured assumptions or conditional illustrations, not promised latency bounds. R20 and its schedules.

Open recovery composition: D83 accepts later-committee FALLBACK routing and local RESUME catch-up. The long empty-prefix L1 admission problem remains open in S2-V43: local progress does not show that the required landing fits. Do not turn the conditional recovery description into a completed liveness proof.

D83 repair: follow the branch and authenticate the conflicting votes

Assumed, accepted mechanism: S2-R06 retains the exact certified branch. A key locked on A41 refuses a closing at B42 when B42 is on another branch, despite its greater height. Both class-H and FALLBACK class-F votes use R09's ancestry check; equal height requires equal header identity. An uncertified attested tip does not force a closing above that tip.

Assumed, accepted evidence rule: R21 authenticates the locking endpoint certificate and an H closing in the same opening and redraw, then identifies their shared signer keys. It compares the carried certificate with the closing lock. The full public aggregates suffice; no private individual signature must be extracted. Different-redraw witnesses do not meet this aggregate form's scope; re-signing the same closing after a redraw alone proves no offence.

Proven conditionally, worked example: V44 has twelve malicious positions shared by two quorums, plus ten distinct honest positions on each side: both quorums have 22 of the same 32 positions. Certificate C_A43 carries C_A41; the H closing selects 40. The carried height 41 exceeds 40, so the public aggregate intersection establishes twelve implicated positions. Positions are not necessarily distinct keys or owners; owner deduplication and live backing still apply. A closing at A42 does not displace A41 and is not a lock lie just because the endpoint certificate is at 43.

Proven conditionally, bounded ancestry: under C7's ordinary term window and one-second minimum timestamp increase, each branch from the same opening lock at 40 has ordinary heights within 41 through 100 when TERM = 60. A descent from B100 down through B42 has 59 bodies and compares its last parent with A41. Landing/view caps do not add timestamps to that opening. This explains MAX_BRANCH_LINKS = TERM−1; it does not prove calldata or gas fit. All heights are a logical example.

Open, consumer integration: D83 accepts these S2 mechanisms and records the spacing objection refuted. D85 merges the aggregate S3b interface and codec, described next in the evidence lesson. The S3a branch form, exact fixtures and the evidence budget still need completion before publishing the fully integrated guarantee.

D86: whose committee verifies an inherited FALLBACK lock?

Assumed, accepted rule. The committee signing a FALLBACK closing and the committee that certified its inherited lock can differ. Verify the lock certificate using its own signed C.t and authenticated redraw history, and bind its exact height, phHash and signed context to the lock header. The closing still needs its own valid witness. S2-R09; R14(ii).

Proven conditionally, V46's logical example. C50 was certified by committee 100 at redraw 1. It becomes the lock of an empty term-101 view whose committee stays dark. Committee 102 at redraw 0 supplies the FALLBACK. Verify C50 against committee 100/redraw 1, and the closing against committee 102/redraw 0; committee 101 need not certify C50 again. These term and height numbers identify the S2-V46 example, not new parameters or an executed test.

Check the exception carefully. If actual transaction-pre-state lastLanded is (49, P49), walking an in-memory path to (50, P50) does not make the certificate optional. Only the existing exact match to actual pre-state (50, P50) supplies that exception. Equal height with a different phHash fails.

D89: which state does a recovery vote name?

Assumed, accepted historical read. For signed reference block N, A1 = timestamp(N) > replaceableFrom(start(N)). Authenticate N's header timestamp and recover the parent state with the implementation and parameter profile in force at N's start, or authenticate a replay yielding the same inputs. Read before every transaction that changes those inputs, including announcements, recovery records, landings and upgrades. N's RPC post-state and the current timestamp cannot substitute. S2-R09 owns this definition.

Illustrative values: timestamp(N) = 1,000; values are sample timestamps, not protocol parameters. Both cases follow S2-V47, with the other signing gates satisfied.

Grace increases900 → 1,100True: 1,000 is greater than 900. A later value does not turn that reference into a lie.

Two snapshots, two routes. REPLACE requires historical A1. RESUME may instead match its signed lock pair to authenticated hatchTip[N] at the end of N. Thus V47's RESUME can pass with A1 false when a qualifying landing leaves the matching tip; later landings in N update that tip. Do not move this read to the start of N.

Before signing. N must be canonical in the key's view, sufficiently old, strictly above activationL1Number, and satisfy the existing restart/reference floors. The activation block has no start-state Etna A1 value. If the node cannot authenticate a passing route, it waits or refuses; missing historical state or profile is neither a made-up value nor evidence of guilt. Reference age and R09's input rules remain separate checks.

A vote is not a live permission. Historical A1 does not say that the voted lock's own fresh grace has elapsed. Recording and landing still obey their live gates, including the separate RESUME/hatch rules. A reference to restart block K reads pre-restart A1; it does not bypass the restarted chain's live checks. In the increasing-grace example, a vote can pass historical A1 while the current record gate still waits. S2-R15; why restarts preserve the old read.

Pending CS-R3-01 repair: wait before retiring a signing scope

A young recovery can disappear from L1

Assumed, finding against accepted D90; open, proposed repair: A's round-three review found that merely observing a recovery retired the old signing scope before the reference-age rule applied. The following proposal is under A/J review, not an accepted confirmation guarantee. Its single rule is S2-R15; the repair record gives the attack and alternatives.

Assumed, worked schedule: a key holds certificate A120 in X. Recovery N lands B110; the node sees N at canonical-head age 12 seconds. If it immediately retires X and N then disappears in a shallow reorg, X may remain canonical while its signing state is barred. The proposal keeps the observation pending until N is canonical and reaches the existing 48-second ANCHOR_MIN_AGE. These heights and 12 seconds are illustrative, not measurements or new constants.

Proven conditionally: under the stated canonical-view, aged-block stability and durable-history assumptions, a shallow reorg cannot leave retirement from an unconsumed recovery. The same gate covers RESUME timeouts, which otherwise also bar old signing. Ordered, once-only history processing prevents replay; exact ancestry keeps a newer compatible certificate without reactivating an old retired scope. Open: exact replay fixtures, cost, complete history availability, C4 successor handling and long-prefix recovery. A reorg of an already aged processed block is outside the assumption; the proposal stops signing and preserves evidence. It supplies no automatic unretirement or unconditional recovery time.

Challenge this pending block

Can a stale notification, a younger RPC snapshot, a premature RESUME timeout or a same-block C4 restart make a client forget a prior signing promise? Can every signer authenticate and replay the needed mature history within its operating budget?

Check your understanding

Two 22-position quorums overlap in twelve. Does every fork slash twelve positions?

No. The signed objects must meet the same evidence rule, including its scope and authentication requirements. The D78 attack broke that inference; D83 supplies branch/evidence rules whose remaining consumers must also implement them.

Can N's RPC post-state authorize a recovery vote and an immediate landing?

It supplies neither permission by itself. A1 uses N's authenticated timestamp and start-state value; RESUME may instead use a matching authenticated end-of-N hatch tip. Even a valid historical vote leaves live record and landing gates in force. Missing inputs mean wait or refuse.

Which committee verifies V46's inherited C50, and does that complete recovery?

C50 uses its own committee 100/redraw 1; committee 102/redraw 0 authenticates the FALLBACK closing. An in-memory cursor cannot supply the actual pre-state lastLanded exception. Correct certificate verification still leaves the separate long-prefix admission, fixture and integration obligations open.
