Lesson 1: time without slots

Prerequisite: lesson 0. Design page: Sequencing rights §1 to §3 (constants: Parameters §1).

Goal

Understand the four time rules everything else rests on: terms, cycles, the snapshot, and future-dated effect. After this lesson you can answer "which L1 state decides who sequences at time t, and why do all nodes agree?"

The idea

Ethereum's slots and epochs are consensus-layer concepts that change length across forks and are invisible to contracts except through the beacon-root precompile. Etna uses only two clocks a contract can read: block.timestamp (seconds) and block.number (L1 blocks). It cuts wall time into terms of 60 seconds on a grid whose origin T0 the DAO sets once with armEtna(T0), at least MIGRATION_LEAD (7 days) ahead, after reading the registry (round 4, R4-C5); the grid's phase relative to L1 slots is irrelevant. A term's holder is computed from L1 state that is already old enough to be final, so it never changes under a reorg, and every registry change is dated far enough into the future that the "old" state and the "current" state give the same answer. The sequencing page calls this invariant I1: for every term inside the 34-hour role horizon, the holders and committee the inbox computes at landing time equal what nodes computed at the term's start, whatever registry writes became effective in between.

The rules

  1. Term. termOf(τ) = (τ − T0) / 60; termStart(t) = T0 + 60t. Every quantity in the design is a second count, an L1 block timestamp or a count; nothing is denominated in slots or epochs.
  2. Cycle. CYCLE_TERMS = 60 terms (one hour). One randomness seed is fixed per cycle, from the accumulator as it stood at cycleStart(c) − LOOKBACK (lesson 2).
  3. Snapshot S(t). The highest L1 block with timestamp ≤ termStart(t) − LOOKBACK, LOOKBACK = 1,200 s. At term start the inputs are 20 minutes old, beyond L1 finality at 12-second slots (768 s) and by a wider margin at shorter slots. A node replays registry events and the mix log up to S(t); no archive state is needed, and under a total L1 halt the schedule stays defined.
  4. Rule D (future-dated effect). A registry write executed at L1 time τ changes eligibility only for evaluation instants T ≥ τ + DELAY, with every DELAY ≥ LOOKBACK + 2·TERM = 1,320 s. A term evaluates eligibility at the single instant termStart(t). Two delays exist: DELAY_REG = 7,200 s for entry and exit, the only inputs that change E_t (the domain count: seats registered and inside [activeFrom, activeUntil) at termStart(t), suspended ones included) or a seat's rank; DELAY_S = 1,320 s for suspensions from class A or B evidence and, since round 6, for a reserve lapse, the suspension of an owner whose landing reserve fell below one term of exposure per seat, seats × 7,161 TAIKO (R6S-2; lesson 7). The hard-floor suspension that liveness bleed can reach and the return through reactivate() are DELAY_REG-dated but act through the same flag. A suspension of any kind changes no rank: the suspended owner's seats stay in the sortition domain, and a seat drawn while suspended is simply a void view (as a holder) or an empty committee slot. That is why the short delay is safe even though the owner can bring a suspension about itself (it can be its own challenger, and it can drain its own reserve): voiding its own draws or emptying its own committee slots after the seed is fixed never adds a cartel member to any committee and never moves another owner's rank, so a self-inflicted suspension costs a seat, the ledger or its draws and moves nobody else's draw (round 4, R4-C2, restated in round 5, R5S-2: every input that changes E_t or a rank is DELAY_REG-dated; a suspension changes neither).

Why it works

Take a write in a block after S(t). Its timestamp τ exceeds termStart(t) − LOOKBACK, so τ + DELAY ≥ τ + LOOKBACK + 2·TERM > termStart(t) + TERM. It cannot touch term t. A node replaying events up to S(t) and the inbox at landing therefore evaluate term t on the same inputs, provided the inbox can still answer "which seats were in the domain at termStart(t)" after later writes took effect. It can, because it keeps the domain as a persistent (versioned) tree over seat ids: each entry or exit adds a version tagged with its effective time τ + DELAY_REG and leaves older versions untouched, and term t reads the latest version whose effective time is at or before termStart(t). Effective times are monotone in transaction order (an exit is accepted only once τ + DELAY_REG ≥ activeFrom + MIN_TENURE, lesson 2), so the version log is sorted and the lookup is a binary search; every version a term needs exists two hours before the term starts (lesson 2; round 4, R4H-1, and the round-5 fix pass). No proofs are needed. This is the design's claim 2 on the sequencing page, tagged proven. Roles stay computable on-chain for ROLE_HORIZON = 34 h after a term's start; a term older than that whose assignment was never pinned is unlandable, the one time-based void in the design (limitations page).

Self-check

A registrant sends register at 10:00:00. Its seat is eligible from what time, and which is the first term that can draw it?

DELAY_REG is 7,200 s, so eligibility starts at 12:00:00; the first term whose start is at or after 12:00:00 can draw it. The snapshot of that term, 20 minutes before its start, already contains the write, so nodes and the inbox agree.

L1 reorgs 5 blocks deep at 12-second slots. Does any term's holder change?

No. Every input to a term is at least 1,200 s (100 blocks) old at its start. Only a reorg deeper than 20 minutes could change an assignment, which is outside the assumption T1.

Why is a suspension delayed by 1,320 s rather than applied immediately, and why may it be shorter than the 7,200 s of a registration?

An immediate suspension would change whether a term's drawn holder may serve, for a term nodes have already computed from S(t). The 1,320-s delay, LOOKBACK + 2·TERM, is the smallest that keeps the snapshot and the live state identical for every term. It may be shorter than DELAY_REG because a suspension changes no rank: E_t and every seat's rank are the same with or without it, so the seed of a cycle cannot be ground through it, whereas a registration or exit moves ranks and must be dated past the next cycle's seed instant (round 5, R5S-2).

Challenge this

Rule D makes a class A or B suspension or a reserve lapse bite 22 minutes late (a hard-floor suspension 2 h late), and a single MISS debit never adds a strike or triggers a floor check. Find a scenario where a misbehaving holder exploits those 22 minutes, and cost it. Then check lesson 5: a holder that gets nothing attested is timed out within 5 seconds regardless of eligibility, so what exactly does the delay let an attacker keep?
