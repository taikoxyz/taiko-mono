The problem: a proof can keep losing its parent

Make bypassing the request stop progress

A censor can keep landing ordinary ranges while ignoring a queued request. Each new landed parent can stale a permissionless prover's hatch. A timer alone does not stop that race.

Assumed, C3-R04/R06. Requests enter a first-in, first-out queue. Once the current request's window opens, every advancing landing must execute it. The requirement applies to the landing; no individual block must carry a request. A sequencer may also serve requests voluntarily before their windows open. Schedule and gate; voluntary request blocks.

Proven conditionally: while that window is open, moving the landed parent must serve the current request. Ignoring it can no longer stale its hatch by advancing the chain. C3's parent-stability argument.

The mechanism: a window, a gate and a hatch

Execution and consumption differ

Assumed. The schedule walks the expired prefix to find the current request. Its opening depends on its save time and the queue's stored schedule; later entries wait their turn. During its open window it must execute for a landing to advance. From its close, a landing must consume it, but may void it without execution. Time passing alone does not consume anything.

A hatch is a permissionless, blob-free landing from the last landed block, with the required ZK proofs and no certificate. It executes between one and HATCH_K requests, with HATCH_K = 32 assumed. HATCH_BOND = 0 removes a hatch admission bond; proving costs and request deposits remain. Open-window or replacement-deadline eligibility and hatch-spacing checks still apply. C3-R07's complete checks.

Why the hatch needs no holder or committee

The queue gate supplies its permission

Assumed · C2 L4. A hatch has one view-254 segment with a zero sequencer, no view changes, no certificate and no blobs. This branch reads no holder, committee pin, committee walk or ROLE_HORIZON for its term; it writes no committee pin. A view-254 segment mixed with an ordinary segment, or carrying a nonzero sequencer, fails the hatch shape. L4's view-254 branch.

Permission is still checked inside L8: an open request window, or T > replaceableFrom together with T ≥ hatchAt + HATCH_RESUME_GRACE; the balance check and non-maximal-hatch wait also apply. The proof still requires ZK_K accepting leaves. Passing L4 alone does not authorize a hatch. C3-R07 checks 1–4; evaluation order.

Limits: what the mechanism does not promise

A consumed request is not a successful transaction

  • Invalid or over-budget transactions are skipped; an undecodable payload produces no transactions. An includer can pre-execute a non-idempotent transaction. Content rules; success limitation.
  • Queue stuffing is priced, not bounded. The price depends on unmeasured full-hatch latency. Poisons can halt landings, and capped walks need an actor to clear them. Censorship-bound limits.
  • Voiding burns the request deposit and bounty; a later permitted replay does not restore that money. Window closure is distinct from BLOB/LEGACY payload expiry. Settlement; payload rules.

Check your understanding

The hatch term has never been pinned. Must a lander first draw its committee?

No. A hatch-shaped view-254 landing reads no committee at L4. It still needs C3's permission, queue and spacing checks and the required proofs. E21(v).

The current window is open. Can a censor keep landing ranges that ignore it?

No. The landing gate requires execution of the current request. No individual block has a request-carrying duty, but the complete landing must satisfy the gate. C3-R04.

The window closes. Has the request executed or received a refund?

No. Closing makes it voidable; a consuming action must still occur. Voiding burns its deposit and bounty, while execution has a separate refund path. C3-R03.
