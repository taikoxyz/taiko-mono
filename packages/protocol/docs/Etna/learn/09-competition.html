Price early capture without naming a winner

Prerequisites: mature staged data, open settlement and the distinction between bonds and payments.

The problem: open entry does not price repeated capture

Proven distinction: zero exclusive leases prevent an operator from owning a protocol veto. They do not stop one well-funded builder from winning every ordinary ordering race. Optional service bonds also miss a producer that makes no bonded promises. Per-address caps fail when one actor uses many addresses.

The mechanism: one declining price per parent

Proven rule: every accepted head starts a public ETH price at 0.05 ETH. It falls linearly to zero after 900 seconds. Any applicable proof-and-data transaction may pay the current price and win in L1 order. There is no reserved winner, future lease or reveal phase. Every accepted successor resets the next price, regardless of who produced or paid for it; registration or staging alone cannot reset it.

a = seconds since the current head was accepted
q(a) = ceil(50,000,000,000,000,000 × max(900 − a, 0) /900) wei
successful payment: 100% q → nonwithdrawable ETH sink
excess call value → actual caller's pull credit

Proven accounting: the bootstrap head uses activation time. The rent clock sits outside the execution commitment, so waiting changes price without changing the proof, while freshness still applies. Neither coinbase nor proof beneficiary recovers rent. This is an ETH admission cost; the earlier TAIKO bond is a different, potentially refundable liability. Failed admission cannot update the clock or collect rent.

Worked example: make the competition assumption explicit

Assumed A-COMPETITION(660): for each contested parent, an honest entrant forms an applicable candidate within 60 seconds, stages within another 120, and finishes retrieval/proving while the stage ages 360 seconds. It is ready by parent age 540. It can fund rent and gas, and L1 includes it within another 120 seconds unless somebody already consumed the parent. Its origin, stage and mandatory-work snapshot remain valid throughout. This is an economic and inclusion assumption, not merely a fast-prover benchmark.

Proven conditional cost: to capture every such parent, an incumbent must win by age 660. Each win therefore sinks at least q(660)=0.013333333333333334 ETH; 100 wins sink at least 1.3333333333333334 ETH. Splitting addresses changes neither clock nor cost. Capture is unprofitable when its aggregate incremental benefit, after ordinary costs but before rent, is below that rent sum. Unlimited MEV does not satisfy this bound. At the specified 100% coinbase share, fees from the producer’s own L2 transactions are internal transfers, not irreversible costs or external revenue. Count all accounts controlled by the same party together.

Proven rapid-capture example: each additional accepted child at the same L1 timestamp pays 0.05 ETH. Three such advances sink 0.15 ETH. Even empty-discretionary candidates need valid proofs, mature data and required FIFO processing; an invalid transaction cannot reset the price.

Defenses and limits

Proven conditional recovery: if nobody advances the head for 900 seconds, rent reaches zero for everyone. An old origin or stage may now be expired. A willing prover must construct, stage and prove a fresh candidate; the price stays zero while that parent remains current. There is no offline auction winner to replace or DAO timer to reset. Assumed: proving and L1 gas remain affordable.

Open: an incumbent without a funded competitor can wait for zero and keep every win. High MEV or builder censorship can defeat the economic premises. Honest winners pay too: a hypothetical 60-second acceptance cadence costs approximately 67.2 ETH/day in rent before other expenses. The 0.05-ETH parameter needs empirical calibration. Eventual zero rent does not guarantee sustained one-second service, diversity or fair outcomes.

Check your understanding

Can a new address receive a fresh zero-price clock?

Proven: no. Price belongs to the canonical parent, not to an address.

Does reaching zero make an expired proof applicable again?

Proven: no. The independent origin and stage freshness checks still apply.

Which premise fails if the only competitor cannot afford ETH?

Proven: A-COMPETITION includes funding and willingness. The quantified repeated-capture bound cannot be claimed from hardware availability alone.

Challenge this

Assumed: a real entrant is ready, funded and includable before zero, with incumbent gains below the stated threshold. Try private order flow, a larger MEV opportunity or gas-price shocks. Which assumption breaks, and does raising rent harm the honest entrant too?
