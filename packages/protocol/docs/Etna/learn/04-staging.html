Publish before competing

Prerequisites: provisional validity, authenticated origins and exact body commitments.

Classification. This is a proposed mechanism. Proven marks definitions or conditional deductions; Assumed names premises; Open identifies unresolved evidence. None is a deployment claim.

The problem: a private head start

Proven attack: if a producer can reveal its blocks for the first time when its proof wins L1, competitors cannot prepare from those bytes beforehand. A signature or data hash does not remove that private advantage. Nor can an on-chain contract observe whether every peer received a P2P broadcast.

Proven design choice: Etna makes every canonical candidate publish its actual data and complete execution context on L1 first. This is staging: a public candidate receipt, not a reservation or accepted block.

The mechanism: an objective public-data interval

Proven rule: once Inbox is ACTIVE, anyone can stage. In calldata mode the call carries the complete bounded body and checks its hash. In blob mode it carries a real current-transaction blob and binds its versioned hash; final proof verification establishes the blob/body relation. The public descriptor binds the exact parent, origin, execution results and commitments introduced in lesson 3. A detached promise of future bytes does not qualify.

Proven rule: the chosen immutable receipt must be at least 360 seconds old and no more than 900 seconds old at acceptance. The final action publishes the identical data again with its proof. The origin independently must remain at most 900 seconds old. A new salt or repeated publication cannot make an expired origin fresh.

Proven limits: the registry does not execute the body or certify its claimed output root. A malformed execution or stale parent can still have a staging receipt. Repeating the same stage identifier preserves its original timestamp; copying a valid publication grants no exclusive reward or ownership of its data. No stage writes a SignalService checkpoint.

Worked example: use the wait productively

Assumed example: origin time is 1000; a segment seals and stages at 1060. An entrant notices and retrieves it within 60 seconds, then produces its required proofs within 180 seconds. Its proof is ready by 1300, 120 seconds before age eligibility at 1420.

Proven arithmetic: the stage alone lasts through 1960, but the origin expires after 1900, so the usable overlap ends earlier. Assumed: a funded entrant can obtain inclusion while the action remains valid. The 120-second margin does not promise inclusion before eligibility or victory over another mature candidate.

Defenses and limits

Proven under A-L1, A-DA, A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION: first disclosure in the canonical acceptance action cannot satisfy the minimum stage age. Private computation before staging, mature competing branches and paid publication spam remain possible. Public availability is not proof that every peer downloaded the data.

Assumed: staged blobs remain retrievable throughout the usable horizon plus the retrieval budget; historical canonical reconstruction has an archive source. Open: launch retention, retrieval performance and duplicated publication costs require validation. An L1 reorg also removes receipts on the orphaned history and requires recalculating maturity.

Check your understanding

Does the earliest stage own the next canonical segment?

Proven: no. It creates no ordering right. Several eligible candidates may extend the same parent.

Does a 400-second-old receipt make a false claimed state root acceptable?

Proven: no. Correct execution and all other acceptance checks remain mandatory.

Can restaging the same old-origin body reset origin age?

Proven: no. Refreshing that origin changes the execution context and requires corresponding reconstruction and proof work.

Challenge this

Assumed: a funded entrant can retrieve the complete chosen candidate. Try hiding one execution input outside the staged descriptor/body, or supplying an unavailable blob hash. Which rule rejects it, and which availability assumption fails?
