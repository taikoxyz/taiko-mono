The problem: a correct block can lose

Proven/source: today's Taiko preconfirmations precede L1 proposal and proof transactions. A later landed proposal can replace an incompatible provisional branch. An application's successful local execution therefore does not mean Ethereum has selected that history. See the baseline summary, sections 1 and 7.

Proven distinction: Etna separates three questions. Does this candidate execute correctly? Did L1 accept this candidate's history? Has Ethereum finalized that acceptance? Answering the first does not answer the other two.

The mechanism: name the guarantee

Proven definitions in the proposed specification
LabelWhat a node establishes
VALID_IN_VIEWThe complete candidate correctly extends its known parent under its named L1 view, after authentication and execution checks. Still provisional.
AcceptedA successful L1 action supplied the required data and validity proof and extended the unique Inbox head. Still subject to an L1 reorg.
FinalEthereum finalized the L1 block containing that acceptance.

Proven rule: missing parent state, bytes or authenticated L1 context means UNKNOWN. Demonstrably wrong input means INVALID. A signature or transport acknowledgment alone establishes neither execution validity nor acceptance. Nodes follow the accepted chain while exposing any provisional service branch separately.

Proposed clock rule: a candidate more than two seconds ahead of a node's local clock is also temporarily UNKNOWN, with reason LOCAL_CLOCK_AHEAD. The node checks it again when its clock catches up. A clock difference alone is never proof of invalid execution or a punishable offence. This separates “I cannot admit it yet” from “the block is wrong.”

Proposed client report: a wallet should show the exact block and branch, whether it has checked execution and received the data, and whether it has verified L1 acceptance or finality. It should also show how old its L1 observation is. A valid but displaced block can be VALID_IN_VIEW and ORPHANED at the same time: it executed correctly but belongs to the losing history. Missing hashes stay unknown rather than becoming zero-valued placeholders. A signed service promise may have a limited penalty; it does not insure Alice's payment.

Worked example: Alice's payment

Assumed example: at time 1000.2 seconds, Alice submits a payment to a healthy publisher. Its next candidate appears at 1001.0; propagation and checking take another 0.5 seconds. Alice sees a soft result after 1.3 seconds. This illustrates the proposed one-second issuance interval, not a guaranteed measurement.

Proven consequence: candidate A can correctly spend Alice's nonce 7 while candidate B correctly spends the same nonce differently from the same parent using another transaction Alice also signed. If B's eligible data-and-proof action wins L1 order, A's incompatible suffix is discarded. A node must notify Alice and reevaluate her balance and nonce. A valid execution proof for A does not reserve its parent or force A to win.

Assumed second observation: another node receives A's timestamp-1001 candidate while its own clock still reads 998. Its allowance ends at 1000, so it reports UNKNOWN / LOCAL_CLOCK_AHEAD. When its clock reaches 999, that policy permits admission, subject to all the ordinary checks. No bond can be slashed just for this clock discrepancy. If B later wins, Alice's report identifies the conflicting L1 acceptance and says A is orphaned. Etna guarantees zero reimbursement for that ordinary soft-branch loss, even if A's publisher had bonded service duties.

What this block defends against

Proven: precise labels prevent a signature, execution result or RPC acknowledgment from being presented as final settlement. They do not stop a competing valid branch, make provisional trades reversible outside the chain, or protect against broken Ethereum finality.

Open product decision: applications must decide whether a revocable execution preview is useful for their actions. This design supplies neither a unique network-wide soft chain nor guaranteed reimbursement if that branch loses. Requiring either would need additional protocol work, not a stronger label.

Assumed: A-L1 supplies Ethereum ordering/finality; A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION supply correct verification; A-NET and A-EXEC support the advertised soft latency. Open: execution benchmarks must establish whether the proposed resource limits sustain that latency. No readiness claim follows from the diagram.

Check your understanding

Two nodes execute the same complete candidate. Must they disagree if only one likes its publisher?

Proven: with the same authenticated inputs and correct rules, they agree on execution validity. Service preference and canonical selection are separate questions.

The parent is missing, or the candidate is ahead of my clock. Is it invalid?

Proven: neither fact alone proves incorrect execution. Report UNKNOWN with the missing-parent or LOCAL_CLOCK_AHEAD reason, then recheck when the parent is recovered or the clock permits admission. A demonstrated deterministic rule violation remains INVALID.

Does a one-second cadence promise an irreversible payment in one second?

Proven: no. It describes candidate issuance. Acceptance and Ethereum finality are separate stages.

Challenge this

Assumed: clients preserve these labels through wallets, exchanges and applications. Try to find an interface that turns UNKNOWN into success, or treats a soft balance as final. What external action becomes unrecoverable if that branch loses?
