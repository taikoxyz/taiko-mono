Lesson 6: landing with a proof

Prerequisite: lesson 4. Design page: Propose-with-proof §1 to §4 and §8; constants on the parameters page.

Goal

Trace one landing from a certified block to a checkpoint in the L1 SignalService, and say which checks are the contract's and which are the proof's.

The idea

Today propose and prove are separate; Etna has one action, land, that carries blobs and a proof and finalizes immediately: the checkpoint is written in the same transaction, with two exceptions taught in lesson 7: a one-leaf landing in single-proof mode is provisional, and a two-leaf landing that follows an unfinalized provisional record writes none and is subsumed by the checkpoint written when that record is finalized (a landing accepted with one ZK leaf in single-proof mode is provisional and gets its checkpoint later; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Two design choices make it permissionless. First, the blob bytes are a pure function of the certified blocks (signed fields, signatures and the exact compressed transaction bytes, packed in a fixed layout), so two landers produce bit-identical blobs and any prover can prove for any lander's address. Second, the inbox verifies the end certificate itself, so the landed range is exactly a certified range: what users saw attested is what lands. There is no sequencer seal: the batch commitment is derived from the certified chain, so there is no seal message to withhold (§1).

The encoding (§2)

payload = 0x03 ‖ u32(numBlocks) ‖ Σ (u32(len(rec)) ‖ rec). A sequencer block's record is headerCore ‖ seqSig ‖ [viewChangeBytes if it opens a view] ‖ txListBytes, the last being the sequencer's exact zlib stream whose keccak is the signed txListHash, copied and never re-compressed. headerCore is the non-derivable part of the header: timestamp, coinbase, gasLimit, anchor number and hash, carried certificate, term, view, kind, vcHash and txListHash (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). A forced-inclusion block's record stops before txListBytes: its transactions are derived from the entry's own blob or calldata, so the record is about 250 bytes and no FI block adds a blob to any landing (round 6 fix pass). Packing is today's coder, 130,044 usable bytes per blob. Rule V9 bounds every record to 130,035 bytes, so any single certified block is landable alone at every slot time; rule V10 bounds a term's sequencer records to TERM_BYTES_MAX = 5 × LANDING_UNIT = 1,270,000 bytes, about 9.8 blobs, which sets the client throughput target of about 20 KiB/s (round 6, R6H-3). A misencoded landing yields an end hash different from the certificate and is unlandable: self-harm only.

The check list (§3; cheapest first, proof last)

  1. Sequence (L1): the range starts at lastLanded.height + 1; at most 256 blocks; several landings may sit in one L1 block, there is no one-per-block rule.
  2. Deadline (L2, only without Frame Transactions): the lander's own expiry, so a stale copy reverts early.
  3. Parent (L3): the parent hash equals lastLanded; the parent state root is read from storage, never from calldata.
  4. Segments, views and rights (L4): the range covers up to four consecutive views (MAX_VIEWS = 4); the view-change chain runs from the last landed vcHash through every boundary, each VC equal to the first live one recorded on L1 for its (term, view, closes) and carrying its redraw count (0, 1 or 2). Each segment's sequencer must be the holder by rank-based sortition over the version of the persistent domain tree effective at termStart(t), so what the inbox computes equals what nodes computed whatever the registry did since (round 4, R4H-1; round 5 fix pass); a suspended or lapsed seat drawn is a void view (round 5, R5S-2). Sentinel view 255 covers open-empty terms, dead mode (any bonded address) and no-committee terms, where the sortition holder keeps the right until its economic deadline and any bonded address may land after it (round 2, S9). The term's committee is the inbox's own on-chain walk, pinned by recordAssignment(t) or by the first landing that settles the term; the first pinner is paid PIN_REWARD, a ramp from 0 at landableFrom(t) to PIN_MAX = 400 TAIKO (round 5, R5H-2; round 6, R6H-2).
  5. End certificate (L5): popcount at least Q(m) = ⌊2m/3⌋ + 1 (22 of 32) and one aggregate signature over the end header hash, verified against the committee at the certificate's redraw count. Intermediate segments need no certificate of their own: each is ended by a view change whose lock certificate the signers verified and are S3b-accountable for. A segment closed by a FALLBACK view change of a later committee is the exception: the landing presents a certificate of the term's own committee at the fallback's lock, or the lock is lastLanded, so a fallback never lands uncertified blocks (round 5, R5S-5), and the fallback's lock must not be below the highest certificate of that committee recorded on L1 for the view with recordCertificate (round 6, R6S-3; lesson 5). Verified on L1, not in the guest.
  6. Blobs (L6): blobhash(i) matches each of at most 6 listed hashes and the next is zero; R_BLOB = 1 TAIKO per blob is paid for at most 15 blobs per term, counted in landing order (round 6 fix pass).
  7. Anchor tip (L7): the newest anchored L1 block is verified by blockhash (256 blocks) or the EIP-2935 contract (8,191 blocks; 4.55 h at 2-s slots); older is refused.
  8. Forced inclusion (L8): the consumed count meets the floor (lesson 10), at most 64 entries per landing; entries voided by a four-hour stall count as consumed, and for a landing that voids them the floor time is capped at the stall's cut, so it asks only for the void set (round 6, R6H-1); the stored entry at the new head, if any, must satisfy savedAt + FI_DELAY > fiNotDueAt; each consumed entry's fee less its escalation surcharge is credited to the includer's ETH pull ledger, and its status (consumed with content, voided and refundable, or burned) is written into two bits of the entry's own word, from which its requester pulls bond and refund with withdrawFiRefund(id), so no slot per requester is written (round 6 fix pass).
  9. Replacement (L9): only when the landing claims to replace; lesson 7.
  10. Effects (L10): lastLanded (height, hashes, roots, term, view, the L1 time at, the anchor tip); the term records, with each settled term's bytesLanded and paid-landing count and its landableFrom, fixed by the first landing that reaches it (round 6, R6H-2); the committee root of every term the landing settles (at most 4 walks); the forced-inclusion head, the stall clock fiClearTip and the escalation run; lander, attester and pin rewards; the prevrandao mix; and saveCheckpoint(endHeight, endBlockHash, endStateRoot) into the L1 SignalService, exactly as today, written at once for a two-leaf landing unless an unfinalized provisional record precedes it on its chain (lesson 7).
  11. Proof (L11): the verifier accepts the journal from two distinct ZK leaves (RISC0 and SP1, a third targeted; TEE never counts); a revert rolls everything back. Checks run cheapest first so a race loser exits within about 25k gas; with the trailing-VERIFY frame shape the loser is invalid and free, while a stale copy sent through the public-mempool gate whose parent has moved is still valid and pays that early revert (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).

Gas: about 1.5 M typical and under 2.7 M worst case for a pinned term (two ZK leaves about 600k, one aggregate certificate about 165k, two fresh checkpoint words about 196k; assumed until measured). A landing that pins an unpinned term adds the committee walk, about 1 M gas at 128 seats and 5.4 M at 4,096, so at large registries a lander pins first with recordAssignment(t) in its own transaction.

Contract versus proof (§4)

Outside the proof (inbox)Inside the proof (guest)
sequence, rights, certificate, blob hashes, anchor tip, queue state, deadlinesblob decoding, every header's signature and fields, execution to exactly each block hash, the hash chain from the parent to the end, every anchored L1 header chained to the tip, timestamps, the forced-inclusion blocks and their placement

The journal binds: chain id and the published-constants hash; parent hash and root; first and end heights, end hash, root and header hash; the blob-hash root; the segments as resolved by L4; the first block's vcHash; the anchor tip's number, hash and timestamp; the forced-inclusion head and stall clock before and after, the consumed-entries hash, the mask and clock of entries voided by a stall, and fiNotDueAt, the latest parent-anchor time at which a block of the range relied on no entry being due at the new head (round 6, R6H-1); and rewardTo, so proofs are per lander (round 2, S6). The consumed-entries hash covers only entries the range consumed, which no later save can change, and the inbox checks the entry at the new head, if one exists, only by inequality: its stored save time plus FI_DELAY must exceed fiNotDueAt, and the stall's cut if the range claims the stall's void set is used up. The schedule this covers: anyone can save an entry between proving and landing, but its save time is later than every anchor of the range, so it passes the inequality and no third party can make a proven landing revert by saving an entry. The verifier keeps two image sets per leaf with a two-hour overlap (zero when a DAO upgrade is flagged emergency) so a rotation never orphans an in-flight proof.

Self-check

Why is the certificate verified on L1 rather than inside the ZK proof?

So the withholding defence never rests on ZK soundness. If a proof system were unsound, an attacker could at most finalize a false root for a block that was genuinely certified, never land an uncertified one.

Why can any lander use a proof it found in the mempool?

It cannot, since round 2: the journal binds rewardTo, so a proof is valid only for the lander it was made for (a copied proof with a swapped reward address fails). The blobs are still deterministic, so a prover can prove for any lander's address on request; the trailing-VERIFY shape makes a losing landing free.

How does R2 hold?

The Inbox proxy address is unchanged, so it is still the L1 SignalService's authorized syncer, and the call is byte-identical to today's (§8). Addresses and storage of both SignalServices, the Bridge and the Vaults are unchanged, so R2 holds. Implementations: the L1 SignalService, the Bridge, the Vaults and the quota manager are upgraded in place before T0 to remove their operational powers, pause and unpause, changeBridgedToken and every owner setter, with every storage layout, message format, client-used selector and address kept (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01; working assumption A5 revised); the cost is that no brake faster than a DAO upgrade exists (limitation L-OP). The L2 SignalService implementation is upgraded in place likewise and adds revealCheckpoint and pinCurrentOrigin, closing the L2 syncer path from the first Etna block (one namespaced pin mapping, VERSION unchanged); the Anchor implementation is upgraded in place to the treasury plus the current-root guard (slots 0 to 257 keep their types; nothing is appended) (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147; lesson 11).

Challenge this

The inbox reads the parent state root from storage. Construct a scenario in which a lander tries to land a range on the wrong parent and show which check stops it and whether the lander pays anything in each of the three sending shapes: direct to a builder with a trailing VERIFY, through the public-mempool gate, and as a plain type-3 transaction without Frame Transactions.
