Etna learning course · lesson 8

Make the publication deadline absolute

Prerequisites: FR fragment commitments and separately reserved service promises.

Course mapPrevious: roles and liabilityNext: competition and rent
Design converged in rounds 07–08. The final judge confirms no new Critical/High in those rounds, the pre-activation intake correction, Medium dispositions and conditional R1–R7 passes. These lessons describe proposed rules; implementation, migration and launch evidence remain open.
Classification. Proven marks consequences of the proposed rules; Assumed names prerequisites; Open marks unresolved measurements. Missing gossip is not itself an on-chain proof of malicious intent.

The problem: answering eventually can hide withholding

Proven attack: a signer can withhold a block, wait until somebody challenges, then reveal just before a new response timeout. That may satisfy an ordinary serving promise while leaving competitors without data during the useful proving window. Repeatedly allowing a fresh timeout rewards late disclosure.

The mechanism: a deadline in every bonded fragment

Proven rule: every version-2 DutyClaim already promises actual publication by issuedAt +240 seconds. Publish complete fragment bytes in L1 calldata with the exact ordered lengths and digests that reconstruct its FR from lesson 3. The registry validates the manifest, selected index, digest and length, then records the immutable first publication time. Anyone with the data can publish; a bare commitment or blob-stage receipt does not substitute.

Proven evidence: after the deadline, anyone presents the original authenticated signature. The registry looks up the matching receipt itself. Absence, mismatching metadata or a first publication after the deadline proves default while evidence remains admissible. No previous challenge is necessary. The position loses 10 TAIKO once: 1 to the reporter, 9 to the sink. Fabricated signatures and duplicate payout attempts fail. Changing issuedAt creates a conflicting promise, not an extension.

Proven funding rule: a challenge may open strictly before publishBy, once per position, while its reserve is live and no matching publication exists. It escrows Q=2 ×4,000,000 ×(opening basefee +2 gwei) wei. The response deadline is min(opening +600, publishBy). The first targeted timely publication earns that challenge's Q; other timely satisfied challenges refund. Standalone publication earns no subsidy. Default refunds Q and applies the once-only slash.

Worked example: useful late bytes still miss the promise

Assumed example: Alice signs at 1,010, so publication is due at 1,250. Bob challenges at 1,050 with a 20-gwei basefee. Proven arithmetic: Q is 0.176 ETH and the deadline is min(1,650,1,250)=1,250. Exact publication at 1,249 satisfies the promise and can earn Q. First publication at 1,251 helps retrieval but cannot cure default. If Bob never challenged, Alice's signature and late receipt still permit reporting before evidence expires.

Proven cost under the surveyed 64-gas/nonzero-byte floor: a maximum fragment of 32,768 nonzero bytes alone contributes 32,768 ×64 ×20 gwei =0.04194304 ETH at a 20-gwei gas price. The full call also carries a manifest and envelope. This is real ETH expenditure, separate from refundable TAIKO collateral. Open: that cost, publication throughput and customer willingness need validation; no automatic reward funds every promise.

Defenses and limits

Proven: late disclosure cannot restart the clock or erase its historical miss. With an authentic signature and timely report, detection of this defined default is deterministic. It establishes a broken promise, not intent. A signer can publish on time after privately computing or selectively gossiping; detection of that earlier behavior can still be zero.

Assumed: a signer funds publication early enough for bounded L1 inclusion and data remains recoverable through archive access. Censorship or congestion can cause an honest signer to miss its deadline: worst-case false accusation of malicious intent is 100%. Hidden signatures first disclosed after evidence expiry escape slashing. Neither a receipt nor a penalty makes a provisional branch final; continue treating missing blocks as UNKNOWN.

Check your understanding

A challenge arrives one second before publishBy. Does it grant 600 more seconds?

Proven: no. Only one second remains; the original signer already promised publication by that absolute deadline.

Does publishing a matching blob discharge this fragment duty?

Proven: no. This duty requires its specific complete calldata publication and FR preimage.

Must a reporter prove that Alice deliberately withheld?

Proven: no. The claim is objective service default. Intent and universal P2P delivery are not established.

Challenge this

Assumed: enough data holders can afford timely publication. Try a sudden gas-price increase, withheld manifest or evidence disclosed after expiry. Which cases create a slashable default, and which escape this bounded promise?
