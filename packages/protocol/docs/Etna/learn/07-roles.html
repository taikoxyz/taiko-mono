Promise a service, reserve its liability

Prerequisites: exact fragments, public staging and proof-bearing settlement.

The problem: promises without permission

Proven distinction: removing a whitelist lets anyone enter, but does not make an unreliable service reliable. Conversely, making a registered operator indispensable gives an offline operator a way to halt the chain. Etna separates optional, punishable service promises from the open action that accepts a valid proof and data.

The mechanism: one reserve per promise

Proven rules: a publisher signs provisional blocks; a custodian promises public data. Either can enter by depositing TAIKO and opening a bucket: 60 positions reserving 10 TAIKO each, or 600 TAIKO total. A position is an obligation identifier, never an Ethereum slot. The immutable record names its owner, signing key, revision and branch context. Clients check the reserve in their authenticated L1 view before calling a signature bonded.

Proven limits: each position loses at most its reserved 10 TAIKO, once across offenses. Conflicting promises cannot create unlimited compensation. Stop making new promises at any time; withdraw unreserved funds immediately. Existing liability survives stopping, key rotation and new registrations. Issuance lasts 60 seconds; evidence remains admissible for 3,600 seconds afterward. Unslashed reserves become releasable strictly after that evidence deadline, following settlement of pending challenges.

Proven boundary: raw proof producers and transaction relayers need no service membership. Taking every bucket or job grants no canonical veto. The publisher may earn an accepted block's ETH coinbase fees; custody has no guaranteed issuance subsidy. Specific publication duties and response payments come next. Open: customers must value those optional services enough to fund them.

Worked example: leaving does not erase liability

Assumed example: Alice deposits 650 TAIKO and opens one bucket at timestamp 1,000. Proven arithmetic: 600 is reserved and 50 remains free. Claims may declare issuance times in [1,000,1,060); evidence closes at 4,660. Rotating her key at 1,020 does not change the original key or deadline.

Proven accounting: one objective default consumes one 10-TAIKO reserve: 1 TAIKO goes to its reporter and 9 becomes permanently nonwithdrawable. If all other positions survive, Alice can recover 590 reserved TAIKO strictly after 4,660, plus her original free 50. The original 650 equals 640 owner funds, 1 reporter reward and 9 in the sink. The DAO has no ordinary sweep of that sink.

Proven under the stated operating schedule: issuing one fragment promise per second from just-in-time buckets reaches a peak of 37,200 reserved TAIKO; four promises per second reaches 147,000. Separate custodian promises and sealed-publication promises add their own reserves. The capital is potentially refundable, but continued operation also needs real ETH for publication and landing. Open: the liquidity stress table makes explicit why a solvent reserve does not demonstrate an affordable service.

Proof jobs buy work, not the head

Proven rules: a sponsor escrows a chosen TAIKO bounty for an exact staged task. A voluntary worker reserves 1,000 TAIKO. Its deadline is max(acceptance +300, stage publication +480) seconds; origin and stage must stay fresh through deadline +120. Another prover remains free to win. Timely exact completion pays the bounty; a different timely winner returns bounty to sponsor and bond to worker. Absent timely revision retirement, if nobody consumed the parent by the deadline, default slashes the worker and returns the bounty. A later head change cannot erase that miss.

Proven boundary with lesson 5: proof offers and landing-intent messages do not create these jobs or alter their deadlines. A worker cannot treat another peer's advertised ETA as cancellation evidence. The contract's authenticated historical acceptance or retirement outcome decides settlement.

Assumed: the worker checks feasibility and funds proving, ETH gas and admission costs before accepting. A TAIKO bounty does not automatically supply ETH. Proven defense: accepting every job cannot reserve every canonical head. Open limit: if every capable prover stops, the chain waits; no bond manufactures a proof.

Check your understanding

Can Alice escape old claims by registering a new key?

Proven: no. Old buckets retain their original key and reserved liability through the evidence window.

Does a losing proof worker automatically lose 1,000 TAIKO?

Proven: no. A different winner consuming its parent by the deadline triggers objective cancellation.

What if every publisher leaves but a new prover arrives?

Proven: that prover may still satisfy open canonical admission. Assumed: it has data, proving capacity and funding; service registration is unnecessary.

Challenge this

Assumed: observers can obtain signatures and report before expiry. Try hiding evidence until after release or buying many identities. Open: bounded collateral does not insure unlimited MEV or establish independent ownership. Identify exactly which promise remains provable.
