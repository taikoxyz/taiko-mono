Lesson 10: forced inclusion

Prerequisites: lessons 3 and 6. Design page: Forced inclusion; constants on the parameters page; the stall residual is in limitation L16 on the limitations page.

Goal

Follow a forced-inclusion request from its L1 save to its L2 block under three sequencer behaviours, and name the bound in each case.

The idea

Three questions define the block: when is a request due, where must its block go, and who includes it when sequencers refuse. Etna answers: due at the L1 time the block's parent anchors (never at a landing's own time, so no third party can make a proven landing revert); at the first block after the anchor passes the due time, one request per block, at most two forced blocks in a row; and, past the replacement predicate, anyone, by landing a batch of exactly the due entries with no rights, no certificate and no blobs. A fourth question is what happens when no ZK guest can prove an entry's content: then one clock rule, the stall, voids every entry due at its cut, four hours after the queue was last clear, so a guest bug never halts landing for longer than that per stall.

The rules

  • Request (§1). One blob (today's shape) or up to 4 KiB of calldata, FOCIL-eligible after Hegotá; a checkpoint pin or reveal fits the calldata shape, so a user can force a durable L2 checkpoint (lesson 11). The payment is FI_BOND = 0.05 ETH plus a fee of FI_BASE_FEE × (50 + pending) / 50 × 4level + inProgress, set at save time: the bond is refunded when the entry is consumed, the escalated part is a deposit refunded too, and the unescalated fee pays the includer. Every new entry stores its requester and the exponent it paid in its own word; a queue never fills. Pending Shasta entries keep their legacy layout with no requester and no bond, and an entry consumed by a Shasta proposal abandoned at the migration is requeued at the fork with fee zero, its proposer having been paid already (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).
  • Due (§2). savedAt + 300 s ≤ T where T is an anchored L1 timestamp. A blob entry still unconsumed after 7 days expires: it derives to the empty FI block, the fee less the surcharge goes to the includer and bond and surcharge back to the requester; calldata entries never expire. Any due entry can also be voided by a stall (below).
  • Placement V8 (§4). A block whose parent's anchor makes an entry due must be that entry's block (cursor + 1), unless the previous two blocks were already forced blocks; the first Etna block is exempt, and so is the first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change or of a dead-mode view, once per opening object (round 6, R6H-1 extended the RESUME exception to the other two openings), and the obligation binds from their child. The waiver lets a fork from lastLanded advance its anchor to a stall's cut before the obligation binds (below); an honest entry waits at most one block per opening, and openings are scarce and gated. Forced blocks inherit the parent's anchor and coinbase and are marked kind FI in extraData; the cursor is never stored: every node and the guest derive it from the queue head at the last landing plus one per FI or forced-batch block (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147), and the stall clock alongside it (round 6, R6H-1). Their records carry no payload, the entry's own blob or calldata being the witness, so they are about 250 bytes, add no blob to a landing and earn no lander reward of their own (round 6 fix pass); they are exempt from the per-term byte budget V10, which counts sequencer blocks only, so a backlog never exhausts a term or times out its leader (round 4, R4H-6). Attester policy, not validity: while an entry is due, attesters withhold from a block anchored more than FI_ANCHOR_LAG = 288 s behind their L1 head, so a stale holder is timed out within seconds (§8).
  • Inbox count (§5). At landing, the consumed count must be at least the number due at a floor time every block in the range must have seen (the first term's start minus the 30-minute anchor age, or the last landed anchor if later), capped at two-thirds of the range and at 64 per landing: a lazy lander cannot dilute the obligation, and nothing a third party does moves the count. The landing writes each consumed entry's status into two bits of the entry's own word (consumed with content, voided and refundable, burned); the includer's fee less the surcharge goes to its pull ledger, and the requester pulls bond and refund per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id), so no slot per requester is written (round 6 fix pass).
  • Forced batch (§6). Once replaceableFrom has passed (with the one ten-minute deferral per range that a bonded landing announcement or an accepted REPLACE or RESUME record, whichever comes first, adds; round 5, R5-C3) and the head entry is due at the lander-chosen anchor: exactly the due entries, view 254, no certificate, two ZK systems always, fees less surcharges to rewardTo, which the forced journal binds. Its clock update has three cases: the anchor time if nothing beyond the consumed entries is due there, the cut if the batch used up a stall's void set, else unchanged (round 6 fix pass). The ordinary pipeline resumes on top with a RESUME view change whose first block re-includes the voided transactions before serving any further due entry. A forced batch on a chain that holds an unfinalized provisional landing inherits its status (lesson 7).
  • Stall (round 6, R6H-1; §2). The stall clock fiClearTip is the latest parent-anchor time at which a block's cursor had no due entry (the due prefix consumed or voided), or the cut of a stall whose void set is used up, derived per block like the cursor and stored at each landing: a block whose cursor has no due entry at its parent's anchor moves it to that parent anchor, a block that uses up a stall's void set restarts it at the stall's cut, and any other block leaves it, so how ranges are split into landings never changes it; at the fork it is the drain block's timestamp (lesson 11). The stall's cut is fiClearTip + FI_SKIP (4 h). Once a block's parent anchor reaches the cut, every entry due at the cut is void, at any queue position: it derives to the empty FI block and counts as consumed, up to 64 per landing, the next landings voiding on until the void set is used up, when the clock restarts at the cut. An entry that became due after the cut is served with content, or, if unprovable too, starts the next stall on the restarted clock. A fork from lastLanded reaches the cut through the first-block waiver, and its count takes its floor time no later than the cut, so it asks only for the void set. A forced batch is not a dependable way out: it must serve with content every entry that became due after the cut, a window an attacker can shrink to one L1 block or none. The first entry a stall voids, the stored head and the presumed poison, loses its 0.05 ETH bond and its fee, burned; every other voided entry is refunded in full, bond and fee, to its requester; the protocol cannot tell a poison entry from an honest one that sat at the head through a four-hour outage, and burns either. A legacy Shasta entry has no requester and no bond, so a stall that voids it refunds nothing: its fee is burned if it is the first void and otherwise goes to the includer, and a requeued entry's fee is zero. Price: the escalation level rises by one at each stall, counted once at the landing that burns its first void however many landings void it, and falls by one per ESCALATION_DECAY = 7 days since the last stall; while a stall is in progress at save time (the clock at least 30 minutes old with the head due) the exponent is one higher. The surcharge, exactly FI_FEE × (4e − 1) from the stored exponent e, comes back to the requester once the entry is consumed with content, and the includer keeps the fee less that surcharge, so whatever a requester overpaid is the includer's bounty, undiluted (round 6 fix pass); an entry a stall voids behind the first gets its whole fee back. So no honest entry pays the escalation unless it is a stall's first void; it must still front the surcharge at save time, about 4 ETH after six stalls, and that deposit falls only fourfold per week. The schedule this covers: an attacker can pre-post any number of unprovable entries at the unescalated price, but every one due at the cut is voided with the first, so a stream of N entries costs one stall plus ⌈N/64⌉ landings, not N stalls; an entry that survives to seed the next stall was saved during this one, after fiClearTip + 14,100 s, so it pays the next stall's price. With stalls less than a week apart the k-th therefore costs its poster at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4k−1 ETH: six back to back (a day) about 1.7 ETH, twelve (two days) about 5,600 ETH, and a daily drip gains nothing, since each stall restarts the decay clock; such a run buys about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls of about four hours each, and no DAO action is needed to end any of them. Stalls a week or more apart return to the floor price, so the long-run rate is one cheap stall a week, about 0.05 ETH and four hours of halted landing each (limitation L16).

Self-check

An entry becomes due while ten blocks are in flight. Which of them is invalidated?

None. Due-ness is evaluated at each block's parent's anchor, fixed when the parent was built. The obligation binds the first block after the anchor advances past the due time.

Why does the forced batch carry no blobs, and why does that matter for FOCIL?

Its data is already on L1 (the requests themselves). A blob-free 3 KiB transaction of about 1.2 M gas is eligible for inclusion lists, so the escalation leg gets L1-level censorship resistance; blob-carrying transactions do not.

What does stuffing the queue with 2,400 entries cost and buy?

About 60 ETH in quadratic fees plus about 196k state gas per entry, buying about an hour of delay for later entries while ordinary users keep one block in three. Kept up without a gap for four hours (about 9,600 entries, at least about 50 ETH), the same stuffing becomes a stall (round 6, R6H-1): every entry due at the cut is voided, refunded except the first; the stuffer's later entries are served with content and pay their fees; and every entry saved since the stall became visible fronted four times the floor, refunded once consumed with content, so the escalation lands on a stall's first void, not on stuffing as such.

An attacker posts ten entries that no guest can prove, all in one L1 block. How long can landing stall, and what does it cost?

Once they are due, no range that consumes them can be proven, and every landing path must consume them, so nothing lands until the stall clock is four hours old. Then the first block of a REPLACE, RESUME or dead-mode view, which may skip the obligation once, advances the anchor to the cut, and the blocks after it void all ten entries in one landing, whose count is taken at the cut and so asks for those ten only; a forced batch anchored there does the same only if no unprovable entry falls due just after the cut. The attacker loses the first entry's bond and fee, about 0.051 ETH, and gets the other nine back, bond and fee, one withdrawFiRefund call each; a second stall needs an entry saved during the first, priced four times higher, so with stalls less than a week apart the k-th costs at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4k−1 ETH, (round 6, R6H-1).

Challenge this

The inbox count uses a floor time derived from the range's term start minus 30 minutes. Try to construct a certified range that is valid at every node but fails the count, or one that passes the count while skipping a due entry. The design claims both are impossible (claim 2 on the page). Then attack the stall rule: make a stall void an honest entry without posting any entry a guest cannot prove, or seed a second stall within a week of the first without paying the escalated floor, and price each attempt (the design says the first needs stuffing at the drain rate for four hours and the second is impossible, because a surviving seed was saved during the stall). Finally, decide whether one four-hour stall a week for about 0.05 ETH, the schedule the escalation lets through, is acceptable.
