Lesson 10: forced inclusion

Prerequisites: lessons 3 and 6. Design page: Forced inclusion.

Goal

Follow a forced-inclusion request from its L1 save to its L2 block under three sequencer behaviours, and name the bound in each case.

The idea

Three questions define the block: when is a request due, where must its block go, and who includes it when sequencers refuse. Etna answers: due at the L1 time the block's parent anchors (never at a landing's own time, so no third party can make a proven landing revert); at the first block after the anchor passes the due time, one request per block, at most two forced blocks in a row; and, when the pipeline is past its replacement predicate, anyone, by landing a batch of exactly the due entries with no rights, no certificate and no blobs.

The rules

  • Request. One blob (today's shape) or up to 4 KiB of calldata (FOCIL-eligible after Hegotá). The whole payment is the fee; a queue never fills.
  • Due. savedAt + 300 s ≤ T where T is an anchored L1 timestamp. Blob entries void after 7 days (they derive to an empty block; calldata entries never void).
  • Placement V8. A block whose parent's anchor makes an entry due must be that entry's block (cursor + 1), unless the previous two blocks were already forced blocks. Forced blocks inherit the parent's anchor and coinbase. They are exempt from the per-term byte budget V10, which counts the sequencer's own blocks only (they are already bounded by one blob each and by the per-landing cap), so a blob-shaped backlog never exhausts a term and a leader is never timed out for lack of budget (round 4, R4H-6).
  • Inbox count. At landing, the consumed count must be at least the number due at a floor time every block in the range must have seen, capped at two-thirds of the range: a lazy lander cannot dilute the obligation, and nothing a third party does moves the count.
  • Forced batch. Once replaceableFrom has passed (including any deferral by a bonded landing announcement) and the head entry is due at the latest L1 block: exactly the due entries, view 254, no certificate, two ZK systems always, fees to the lander, the ordinary pipeline resumes on top with a RESUME view change whose first block re-includes the voided transactions before serving any further due entry. The entry at fiQueue.head that no guest can prove is skipped (a landing skips at most that one entry; the cursor never moves during the check) only once no landing of any kind has anchored L1 time within four hours (the gate is the last landing's anchor time, never the entry's age); its 0.05 ETH bond and fee are burned and the fee floor quadruples per skip in a day, so a stream of poison entries is bounded by the poster's budget, not by a DAO action (round 3).

Self-check

An entry becomes due while ten blocks are in flight. Which of them is invalidated?

None. Due-ness is evaluated at each block's parent's anchor, fixed when the parent was built. The obligation binds the first block after the anchor advances past the due time.

Why does the forced batch carry no blobs, and why does that matter for FOCIL?

Its data is already on L1 (the requests themselves). A blob-free 3 KiB transaction is eligible for inclusion lists, so the escalation leg gets L1-level censorship resistance; blob-carrying transactions do not.

What does stuffing the queue with 2,400 entries cost and buy?

About 60 ETH in quadratic fees plus L1 gas, buying about an hour of delay for later entries while ordinary users keep one block in three.

Challenge this

The inbox count uses a floor time derived from the range's term start minus 30 minutes. Try to construct a certified range that is valid at every node but fails the count, or one that passes the count while skipping a due entry. The design claims both are impossible (claim 2 on the page).
