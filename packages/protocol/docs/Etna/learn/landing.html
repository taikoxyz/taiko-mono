Etna · Accepted C2 specification

Landing data with its proof

The L1 action advances the rollup only when its data and required proof arrive together. Publishing a checkpoint usable for custody proofs has a separate gate.

Accepted specification: D88 merges the landing corrections taught here; C2 at af9c8dc owns the checks and effects. D86 and D88 align the inherited FALLBACK certificate rule. Earlier source pins below remain historical support for unchanged rules. Read the certificate lesson for the separate confirmation argument.

Assumed labels a selected rule or premise; proven conditionally an argument under those premises; open an unresolved dependency. Rule G and its new signed domain remain unadopted; RPT remains research, and S2-V43's long empty-prefix admission remains open. Acceptance supplies no clean-round, deployment, measured-performance or D1/D44 readiness claim.

Why put data and proof in the same action?

Execution can circulate while proving continues

Problem. A separate data proposal leaves an L1 commitment waiting for its proof. A required sequencer seal introduces another object its holder could withhold.

Assumed · C2-R01. Any address may submit land with a bounded range extending the stored landed head. Ordinary range data is attached as blobs and the proof travels in the same transaction. There is no separate proposal or sequencer seal. The lander needs no registration or bond.

A range need not line up with an L1 block interval. Adjacent landings can be accepted in the same L1 block. Range-size caps still apply; decoupled cadence does not mean unbounded work.

Assumed, unmeasured defaults under D69. At MP-06’s honest policy of landing each term once, the target is at most 180 seconds from the range’s last block to L1 inclusion. An earlier block waits up to one term more: 60 + 180 = 240 seconds from that block. These are conditional proving, aggregation and inclusion defaults, not observed performance, adversarial liveness bounds or the preconfirmation interval. A cost-driven split would require the policy and targets to be checked again. MP-06: policy, derivation and unrun procedure.

Open capacity question. A range with few blocks may still require a long chain of view-change witnesses from empty terms. MP-05 records this unresolved landing-cost bound. Splitting data alone does not prove that the remaining indivisible witness chain fits. D69 assigns that design work to its owners; no admission rule is added by this lesson.

Limit. Ordinary landing uses blobs; C2 defines no calldata data-availability fallback. Forced-request payload derivation belongs to C3, still an integration dependency.

Bind the proof to the bytes, parent and recipient

A proof cannot be retargeted

Mechanism. A journal is the proof's public statement. It binds the stored parent, attached blob hashes, execution result, L1 anchor information, forced-queue transition and reward recipient. A leaf is a ZK proof system accepting that statement.

Assumed · C2-R03–R05. Normal mode requires ZK_K = 2 distinct ZK leaves to accept the same journal. Only ZK leaves count toward ZK_K; a TEE does not. The guest proves execution, derivation and its specified header checks; the Inbox separately checks sequence, rights, the end certificate, attached blobs, canonical anchor tip and queue conditions.

Proven conditionally: under the named proof-soundness, independence and authenticated-history premises, the journal binds execution to the supplied data and stored parent. Changing the recipient changes that journal. Bob may submit Alice's unchanged proof and blobs, but Bob pays transaction costs while Alice remains the recipient. C2-R04 binding argument; C2-R02 proof reuse.

Assumed · atomicity. C2 runs L1–L9 checks, then L10's state effects, then L11's proof verification. The checkpoint or provisional-record write follows L11 and uses its returned zkLeaves_, the accepting ZK leaf ids. Presented proof tags do not decide the result. Any revert rolls the transaction's writes back. C2-R05 L10/L11; C2-R06 atomicity. Different valid witnesses can change blob bytes; each proof stays bound to its original bytes.

Cross empty views and authenticate an inherited lock

Count the view being closed; verify the block being locked

Assumed · C2-R05 L4. Each closed term VC.t may contribute at most V_MAX + 1 TIMEOUT, TERM_END or FALLBACK closings of holder views. REPLACE and RESUME add no allowance to that count. A TIMEOUT requires VC.v < V_MAX; other holder closings require VC.v ≤ V_MAX. TERM_END/FALLBACK closings of views 254 and 255 are outside this holder-view cap; TIMEOUT cannot close either. A hatch itself carries no view changes. Owning L4 rule.

Assumed · C2 L5 and S2-R09/R14. An empty view may inherit a certified block from an earlier term. Verify that lock certificate under its own signed term, view, opening and redraw history, with its exact height/PH pair and authenticated PH context. The FALLBACK's witness is verified separately. Every FALLBACK is checked, even if its closed view has no blocks. Only equality with the actual transaction-pre-state lastLanded pair dispenses with the lock certificate; advancing a local cursor cannot create that exception. FALLBACK finality and the other landing gates still apply. C2 L5; S2-R09; S2-R14.

Logical example, S2-V46: a term-100 certificate at redraw 1 can authenticate the block inherited by empty term 101, while committee 102 at redraw 0 signs its FALLBACK. Committee 101 need not recertify it. A substituted root, redraw history or same-height sibling PH fails authentication. Exact signatures and fixtures remain open.

Limit. This per-term count does not bound the number of empty terms in an indivisible landing prefix. S2-R15 / S2-V43's admission blocker remains open. The hatch has its own rights branch.

Advance execution without publishing custody

Provisional ancestry holds the checkpoint back

Problem. An unavailable proof backend can stop the normal threshold. Immediately publishing a checkpoint from the remaining backend would make its failure directly relevant to bridge custody.

Assumed · C2-R12–R15. After the degradation timer expires, a landing with a nonempty end certificate may use kRequired = 1. Exactly one distinct accepting leaf creates a provisional record with that leaf's id and writes no checkpoint. Two accepting leaves take the checkpoint path even in single-proof mode, subject to older provisional ancestry. The landed head and rewards advance in either case. Single-proof mode; provisional records; checkpoint gate.

Logical example, E16(v): with kRequired = 1 and no older provisional record, a bundle tagged leaves 0 and 1 is still provisional if only leaf 0 accepts. If both accept, the landing writes a checkpoint. Proof.leafId is a routing tag; the return value is the evidence of acceptance. Its exact byte encoding remains open.

A later normal-threshold landing also writes no checkpoint while an unfinalized provisional record precedes it. Proving “state S leads to S′” does not establish that starting state S was correct.

Limit. The provisional end supplies no new L1 checkpoint for withdrawal proofs. Even a published checkpoint is not payment by itself: the Bridge still requires its authenticated proof or retained-cache path. Custody interface obligation.

Time adds a recovery opportunity, not evidence

Critical limit, scoped by D81. Time supplies no new evidence of correctness. A false execution root in this mode needs a faulty accepting leaf, a colluding certifying quorum and no usable honest counterproof during the window. A false forced-inclusion disposition does not need that colluding quorum. One faulty leaf can land a truly executed, honestly certified range while falsely claiming that requests were included or voided. Status bits and money settle at landing. A timely sound counterproof prevents finalization, but does not restore that money. Accepted C2-R14; D79 (4) places this precise residual under signed L-PF. The delay is neither proof of correctness nor a refund mechanism.

A landing opportunity is not full recovery throughput

Proven arithmetic; signed limitation: C2-R12 at D81 bounds one landing by four occupied terms and at most 4 × 60 = 240 blocks. Occupied terms need not be consecutive, so this is not a 240-second elapsed-time bound. At full one-second production, one single-proof landing per DEGRADE_AFTER = 7,200 seconds carries at most 240 / 7,200 = 1/30 of production; bytes or additional segments can lower it. The excess backlog is replaceable and can lose its landing opportunity when data or the L1 reference window is lost. D79 (1) signs L-SPT as an R1 exception during proof-system failure. A faster single-proof design remains research, not a merged fix.

Assumed, separate signed sentinel limit: S1-R16 gives one sentinel landing a prefix ceiling of four occupied terms, at most 240 blocks. Once that prefix changes the landed parent, the remainder of the same object's chain cannot land. A successor needs a fresh object and a qualifying regime; dead mode may still qualify after a small catch-up, but continuation is not guaranteed. D79 (2) signs LS1-2 while keeping continuation as research. Those signed limitations are separate from the historical W22 confirmation/evidence review. A counterproof must contradict the same inputs

Agree on the question before comparing answers

Problem. Changing the blobs or recipient changes the statement. Conversely, allowing conflict only in selected end-state fields can miss a real contradiction elsewhere.

Assumed · C2-R04 / R14. C2 classifies every journal field as a fixed input or derived output. A conflict proof from another accepting leaf must keep every fixed input equal, differ in a derived output, and pass the specified immutable-queue checks. Every derived output may differ. The forced-inclusion fields are fixed inputs except for the derived fiBlocksMatch result. Current conflict predicate.

Assumed, D81 correction: the guest now binds the complete replay metadata to fiReplayHash before either branch. If the authenticated request metadata and actual blocks disagree, the sound guest can return fiBlocksMatch = false; that result is a counterproof, never an admissible landing. Substituting metadata while retaining the recorded hash fails the binding check rather than manufacturing a conflict. C3-R06; E12's positive and substituted-metadata cases.

Proven conditionally: if the sound guest admits at most one journal for those fixed inputs, two accepted journals with different derived outputs convict at least one leaf. The immutable-queue checks bind the same request claim; changing a fixed input is a different question and fails the conflict predicate. C2-R14's argument.

Assumed · D88 leaf check. Confirmation and conflict both call verify(..., 1) and require its zkLeaves_ to identify an accepting ZK leaf other than the record's leaf. A garbage proof tagged as that other leaf can do neither. C2-R14; E12(viii).

Assumed effect. CONFLICT stops every landing path, forced batches included. Earlier provisional records may finalize only within the permitted boundary. Choosing the sound leaf and restarting requires a DAO upgrade.

Residual risk. A sound guest may reject the recorded range entirely, leaving no alternative journal to prove. A colluding quorum may withhold a private carried-certificate preimage needed by the counterprover. Automatic counterproof availability is not guaranteed. These cases remain within the disclosed dishonest-quorum risk. C2-R14 residuals.

Proven conditionally: the restart's custody argument depends on the rolled-back suffix never publishing a checkpoint. It cannot undo completed L1 transfers or cached authorizations. Recovery below published custody is forbidden in the current design. Unpublished-suffix argument; C2-R21 upgrade obligation.

Races, retention and finality still have limits

Inclusion, checkpoint publication and finality are distinct

Assumed · C2-R09 / R20. Competing landings target the stored head. The first accepted landing wins; a stale copy fails sequence checks. The frame lesson explains when failure invalidates the transaction and when the loser still pays. C2 imports that construction.

A replacement record alone does not void the original. Inside the landing horizon, replacement takes effect when the replacement lands. Stale role history or an anchor outside the L1 history window can make the original unlandable beyond that horizon.

Assumed · C2-R18. The serving duty is RETAIN_SECONDS = 7,200 seconds after each block’s timestamp, shorter than the possible landing horizon. It is not an L1-accountable publication guarantee. Later recovery depends on someone retaining the bytes; archival availability is an explicit premise.

Assumed · C2-R20. “Landed” describes L1 inclusion, qualified as provisional where needed. “L1-safe” uses LANDED_CONFIRM_DEPTH of one L1 descendant; “L1-final” reports beacon-client finality. Neither label bypasses provisional checkpoint rules. An L1 reorg reverts Inbox state with L1.

DAO-dependent exceptions. Certificate-free landings cannot use single-proof mode: their safety lacks the attester-executed end certificate. A required backend outage in that regime can need a verifier upgrade. CONFLICT also waits for a DAO upgrade. C2 does not claim unconditional DAO-independent liveness in these cases.

Check your understanding

Two leaf tags are presented in single-proof mode. Does that publish a checkpoint?

Only two distinct accepting ZK leaves, as returned by verify, can take that path; older provisional ancestry still holds publication. One accepting leaf creates a provisional record. E16(v).

Can L1 finality or a successor's two-leaf proof publish a provisional root?

Neither bypasses C2's gate. L1 finality secures the transaction's place in Ethereum; a successor proof establishes a transition from its supplied parent. Publication still waits for the relevant provisional records to finalize in order, and the Bridge still checks its proof or retained-cache path. C2-R13; C2-R15.

Bob copies Alice's proof and changes rewardTo to Bob. Who gets paid?

The changed journal has no valid copied proof, so the submission fails. If Bob instead submits Alice's unchanged statement and bytes successfully, Alice remains the recipient and Bob bears submission costs. C2-R02.

Challenge this

  • Proof independence: can a shared guest bug defeat supposedly independent leaves? C2 puts common-mode risk outside its independence premise.
  • Counterproof availability: does the sound guest admit a journal for the recorded inputs, and can a counterprover obtain every private witness?
  • Custody boundary: can any path publish above an unresolved provisional record? Safe restart depends on excluding every such path.
  • Progress: who retains bytes and funding after an outage, and who acts in CONFLICT?
