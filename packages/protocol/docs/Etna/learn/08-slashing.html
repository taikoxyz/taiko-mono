Lesson 8: slashing and bonds

Prerequisites: lessons 5 and 7. Design page: Slashing & economics.

Goal

List every slashable offence with its evidence, and explain why nobody can be slashed by accusation.

The idea

A rule that accepts a claim needs a counter-claim game, a challenger bond and a judge. Etna's rules accept only the accused key's own signatures over conflicting messages, a signed object naming an invalid preimage, a signed L1 reference that is false, or a code-size fact; no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing (invariant I5; round 4). The absence bleed is not a slashing rule: it is applied by a TIMEOUT view change carried in a landing. A challenger controls none of these inputs, so false accusations are impossible rather than merely unprofitable, and challengers need no bond. The offender can always report itself, so the deterrent of a rule is the debit minus the capped 10 % challenger share.

Deliberately not offences: silence (a colluding committee could manufacture it), a missed term with no takeover and no landing (fault not attributable), an invalid block that never became state.

Sizing the bond

  1. Reverting a locked block needs 12 attester keys plus the leader, all class A: at least 13 × B. With B = 20,000 TAIKO that is 260,000 TAIKO, about 39 ETH at the assumed rate: the published credit bound for "locked".
  2. One uncertified tip block (S1): B minus the challenger cap must exceed what users act on within one second.
  3. Liveness: B ≥ 100 terms of revenue; the absence penalty (MISS_PENALTY = 50 TAIKO) applies only to a leader silent to the whole committee, so an honest online leader is never bled; a fresh owner's 5 % buffer (1,000 TAIKO per seat) absorbs 20 timeouts before seat stake is touched; the landing reserve (8,400 TAIKO per seat) covers two terms of worst-case reward exposure per seat.
  4. Sybil invariance: class A takes the whole ledger and the floor is seats × B, so splitting seats across owners changes nothing.

Self-check

An attester's key is stolen. What is the worst the thief can do?

Equivocate and self-report: the victim loses its whole ledger, the thief nets at most 2,000 TAIKO (the challenger cap).

Why does EIP-8151 matter here and how is it handled?

It restricts ecrecover for accounts with code, so a sequencer could attach code to make its own equivocation unprovable. The evidence function has two branches: if the key has code, slash for that fact (S8); else recover the signatures. Attesters use BLS and never touch ecrecover.

What is the accounting invariant?

The inbox's TAIKO balance equals the sum of ledger balances plus pending burn plus announcement bonds not yet refunded or forfeited; landing reserves are ledger entries that reward debits alone can lower; every credit is a same-transaction debit of another entry; nothing is minted.

Challenge this

Try to construct evidence that slashes an honest party under any rule in the table. For each attempt, name the input you would need and who controls it. The design's claim is that you will always need the victim's own signature or a quorum object plus an L1 fact.
