Lesson 9: anti-monopoly

Prerequisite: lesson 8. Design page: Slashing & economics §5 (committee rules: Sequencing rights §8; limitation L2 on the limitations page).

Goal

Compute what a given share of primary terms costs, see from simulation how much productive revenue that share actually buys once blank terms are counted, explain why 100 % is unreachable, and state precisely what cannot be prevented.

The idea

Sortition is uniform over domain seats, those registered and inside [activeFrom, activeUntil) at the term's start, suspended ones included (a suspended or lapsed owner's draw is a void view or an empty committee slot and moves no other owner's rank; round 5, R5S-2), so an entity with k of n domain seats is drawn primary in exactly k/n of terms, history-free, however it splits its seats across addresses, and holds k·K/n committee slots in expectation. Against H honest seats, a primary share X costs k = X·H / (1 − X) seats (with H = 64: 32 seats for one third, 64 for one half, 576 for 90 %, 1.28 M TAIKO of seat stake at one half); the marginal cost B·H / (1 − X)² grows without bound. That is the price of primary share only, and the design does not claim it bounds revenue. What a cartel earns also depends on how many honest-primary terms it can blank by abstaining in their committees (lesson 5), and round 4 (R4-C1) republished that from simulation (20,000 terms per row, script redraw_sim.py, not committed):

Reading: below about 25 % of the seats a cartel gains nothing (the redraws take its blank rate to near zero and its revenue equals its seat share); at the T11 boundary (one third) it gains about 5 points of revenue against a dispersed honest set and about 11 points against the eight 8-seat honest owners the launch condition produces, whatever its own seat structure (the three 8×8 rows agree within a point; round 5, R5-C2), because excluding an 8-seat honest primary by owner (and up to four backup seats) leaves a pool of about 84 to 88 seats in which the 32 cartel seats are about 36 to 38 %; with fewer, larger honest owners the boundary gain is larger still (six 8-seat honest owners against 24 single seats: about 15 points, 48 %). A single-seat entrant facing such a cartel is excluded only by its own seat, so the cartel stays at 33 to 35 % of the pool, about 16 % of the entrant's primary terms are blanked with two redraws and it keeps about 84 % of its fair revenue; above one third the gain is superlinear and the design does not defend it (limitation L2). The last column counts certified terms, which is exact when the fees of a delayed second carry over to later blocks, so that delay costs users latency rather than the honest holders revenue (round 6, R6-C2). A blocking minority can also withhold its TERM_END lock votes, and then the next leader cannot start until a later committee's FALLBACK, 12 to 22 s after the term's end; if the revenue of those seconds is lost instead, the one-third cartel's share against the eight 8-seat owners is 50.8 % by seconds rather than 44.0 % by terms (56.9 % rather than 48.9 % against six of them at 72 seats), and the extra 7 to 8 points need the term-end votes withheld after certified terms too, else they are 2.4 to 3.5. Each withheld term also costs the honest members about 165k gas per record to record the view's highest certificate on L1 for a term already pinned, plus the committee walk (about 1 M gas at 128 seats) when it is not, the usual case at termEnd + 2.5 s, normally one or two records per term because the duty is staggered (lesson 5; round 6, R6S-3 and the fix pass; limitation L2). Exclusion is impossible while one honest seat is live: a registration is a plain L1 transaction with future-dated effect, no rule limits how many registry writes take effect per minute (the round-5 fix pass removed a per-slot cap that let an incumbent delay an entrant), and the seat array (65,535 entries) costs 1.31 billion TAIKO of seat stake, 2.3 billion with buffer and reserve, more than the token supply to fill.

The impossibility result

No owner-keyed rule (consecutive-term cooldown, progressive fee burn, per-owner decay) costs a Sybil-capable adversary more than the per-seat price, because the registry cannot distinguish m owners of one entity from m entities. Such rules tax only honest large operators. The design therefore prices share and does not try to bound it (limitation L9); the collusion bound is T11 in its single form (fewer than one third of the seats malicious; fewer than m − Q(m) + 1 abstainers in a committee and its redraws; fewer than Q colluders), an assumption, not a rule. Performance decay is the absence bleed and the hard floor only: 50 TAIKO per timed-out view, suspension when the ledger falls to half the floor; strikes and the suspension ladder are reserved for structural offences, which need the offender's own signed lie.

What a cartel can still do

With m − Q(m) + 1 = 11 of a committee's 32 slots it can refuse to attest an honest primary's blocks: no certificate forms, and no timeout forms either, because an attester signs TIMEOUT only when, for 5 s counted from the moment it first held the view's opening object, no new certificate appeared and it holds no block of the view that it attested and that is still uncertified; the honest attesters attested the leader's two blocks, which the abstainers keep uncertified, so they never sign (round 4, R4H-4; round 5, R5H-1, tied the rule to the attester's own attestations so that a holder gossiping empty headers no longer keeps a view; round 6, R6H-4, fixed the window's origin). The term is blank: the leader keeps its view at the sequenced level, holds after two uncertified blocks, nothing of it lands, and nobody is penalized. Attesters that attested but saw no certificate can vote to redraw the committee for the same leader (⌊m/2⌋ + 1 = 17 of 32, up to REDRAW_MAX = 2 times; the redrawn committee can attest the held blocks because the staleness bound is measured when a node received a block, not when it signs), so the cartel needs a blocking minority in the first draw and in each redraw; that raises the first-draw threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 and is what turns the 57 % blank rate at one third of the seats into 22 % in the table above. A cartel majority of 17 can also redraw a committee that has already certified, since the redraw precondition is not verifiable on L1; what that buys is a re-roll toward Q in a fresh committee (limitation L16). The blanking probability is hypergeometric (the committee is drawn without replacement from the pool left after excluding the primary by owner and the backups by seat), so it depends on share and on the owner structure, barely on registry size: a cartel of 8 single-seat owners among 72 seats blanks 0 % of terms, which is what the launch condition (8 owners, 72 seats, a heuristic no rule enforces) aims at. The absence bleed is unreachable by a minority: a timeout view change needs Q = 22 signers none of which holds an attested block of the view that is still uncertified, so only a leader that is in fact not serving pays it, and eligibility is never removed by a timeout. Nor can a cartel move a draw by suspending its own owners with self-submitted evidence: a suspended seat keeps its rank and is a void view or an empty slot when drawn, so no owner write changes a committee after the cycle's seed is fixed (round 5, R5S-2). Parking its seats in an open-ended suspension before a seed is fixed does shrink every committee they are drawn into, and with it the number of slashable keys behind a lock, but parked seats sit in no committee, which lowers the cartel's own chance of a quorum or a blocking minority; with a third of the seats parked, the most T11 allows, a committee falls below 8 slots in about 3·10−7 of terms and the slashable count averages about 8.4 instead of 12, and the seats of an owner suspended or lapsed for more than STALE_EXIT = 7 days can be exited by anyone with exitStale (round 6, R6-C1). The entrant's seat is never removed, and forced inclusion bounds censorship inside the cartel's own terms.

Self-check

Why is a cap of 8 seats per address not an anti-monopoly measure?

It bounds an address, not an entity; entities split across addresses at no cost. Its real purpose is bounding one address to 8/A of the draws, so that no single address dominates the views of a term, and bounding the on-chain loops.

A term auction would let the richest party buy every term. Why is that worse than sortition?

An auction prices monopoly instead of bounding it, needs an L1 transaction per bidder per term that dies exactly when L1 is congested, and still needs a random fallback for an absent winner (sequencing page §5, alternatives compared).

Challenge this

Propose a rule that taxes consecutive control by one entity without being bypassable by address splitting. The design claims none exists without an identity-bearing primitive (sequencing page, open question 3); either find one or say which primitive (and its trust assumption) you would add.
