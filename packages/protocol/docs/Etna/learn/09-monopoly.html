Lesson 9: anti-monopoly

Prerequisite: lesson 8. Design page: Slashing & economics §5.

Goal

Compute what a given share of sequencing costs, explain why 100 % is unreachable, and state precisely what cannot be prevented.

The idea

Sortition is uniform over eligible seats, so an entity with k of n seats is primary in exactly k/n of terms, however it splits its seats across addresses. Against H honest seats, share X costs k = X·H / (1 − X) seats; the marginal cost B·H / (1 − X)² grows without bound. Exclusion is impossible while one honest seat is live because a registration is a plain L1 transaction with future-dated effect and the seat array (65,535 entries) costs more than the token supply to fill.

The impossibility result

No owner-keyed rule (consecutive-term cooldown, progressive fee burn, per-owner decay) costs a Sybil-capable adversary more than the per-seat price, because the registry cannot distinguish m owners of one entity from m entities. Such rules tax only honest large operators. The design therefore prices share and does not try to bound it; the collusion tables in lesson 4 are stated as an economic assumption (no entity above about 20 % of seats).

What a cartel can still do

With 11 or more of a committee's 32 slots it can refuse to attest an entrant's blocks; the honest 21 then time out honestly and the entrant loses 50 TAIKO for that view and nothing else. The probability of 11 or more slots at cartel seat share c is 4 %, 36 % and 79 % at c = 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4. Bleeding a one-seat entrant to the 50 % hard floor takes 200 of its own drawn views with the cartel present: about 139 days at c = 0.2, about a week at c = 0.33, five days at c = 0.4, at 40 seats; the entrant may top up and the cartel forgoes its attester income throughout. Round 1 removed the strike ladder from timeouts for exactly this reason. The entrant's seat is never removed, and forced inclusion bounds censorship inside the cartel's own terms.

Self-check

Why is a cap of 8 seats per address not an anti-monopoly measure?

It bounds an address, not an entity; entities split across addresses at no cost. Its real purpose is bounding on-chain loops and one address's share of backup views.

A term auction would let the richest party buy every term. Why is that worse than sortition?

An auction prices monopoly instead of bounding it, needs an L1 transaction per bidder per term that dies under congestion, and still needs a random fallback for an absent winner.

Challenge this

Propose a rule that taxes consecutive control by one entity without being bypassable by address splitting. The design claims none exists without an identity-bearing primitive; either find one or say which primitive (and its trust assumption) you would add.
