Lesson 9: anti-monopoly

Prerequisite: lesson 8. Design page: Slashing & economics §5.

Goal

Compute what a given share of primary terms costs, see from simulation how much productive revenue that share actually buys once blank terms are counted, explain why 100 % is unreachable, and state precisely what cannot be prevented.

The idea

Sortition is uniform over eligible seats, so an entity with k of n seats is primary in exactly k/n of terms, however it splits its seats across addresses. Against H honest seats, a primary share X costs k = X·H / (1 − X) seats (32 seats against 64 honest for one third, 64 for one half, 576 for 90 %); the marginal cost B·H / (1 − X)² grows without bound. That is the price of primary share only. What a cartel earns also depends on how many honest-primary terms it can blank by abstaining in their committees (lesson 5), and round 4 (R4-C1) republished that from simulation (20,000 terms per row, script redraw_sim.py):

Reading: below about 25 % of the seats a cartel gains nothing; at the T11 boundary (one third) it gains about 5 points of revenue against a dispersed honest set and about 11 points against the eight 8-seat honest owners the launch condition produces, whatever its own seat structure (the three 8×8 rows agree within a point; round 5, R5-C2), because excluding an 8-seat honest primary by owner (and up to four backup seats) leaves a pool of about 84 to 88 seats in which the 32 cartel seats are about 36 to 38 %; a single-seat entrant facing such a cartel is excluded only by its own seat, so the cartel stays at 33 to 35 % of the pool, and about 16 % of its primary terms are blanked with two redraws and it keeps about 84 % of its fair revenue; above one third the gain is superlinear and the design does not defend it (limitation L2). Exclusion is impossible while one honest seat is live because a registration is a plain L1 transaction with future-dated effect and the seat array (65,535 entries) costs more than the token supply to fill.

The impossibility result

No owner-keyed rule (consecutive-term cooldown, progressive fee burn, per-owner decay) costs a Sybil-capable adversary more than the per-seat price, because the registry cannot distinguish m owners of one entity from m entities. Such rules tax only honest large operators. The design therefore prices share and does not try to bound it; the collusion bound is T11 in its single form (fewer than one third of the seats malicious; fewer than m − Q(m) + 1 abstainers in a committee and its redraws; fewer than Q colluders), an assumption, not a rule.

What a cartel can still do

With m − Q(m) + 1 = 11 of a committee's 32 slots it can refuse to attest an honest primary's blocks: no certificate forms, and no timeout forms either, because an attester that attested the leader's two blocks, which the abstainers keep uncertified, never signs a timeout (round 4, R4H-4; round 3's trigger had let a third of the seats capture up to 87 % of terms; round 5, R5H-1, tied the rule to the attester's own attestations so that a holder gossiping empty headers no longer keeps a view). The term is blank: the leader keeps its view, nothing of it lands, and nobody is penalized. Attesters that attested but saw no certificate can vote to redraw the committee for the same leader (17 of 32, up to twice; the redrawn committee attests the held block the vote names, with the staleness bound waived), so the cartel needs a blocking minority in the first draw and in each redraw; that raises the first-draw threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 and is what turns the 57 % blank rate at one third of the seats into 22 % in the table above. The blanking probability is hypergeometric (the committee is drawn without replacement), so it depends on share and barely on registry size: a single 8-seat owner among 72 seats blanks 0 % of terms, which is what the launch condition (8 owners, 72 seats, a heuristic no rule enforces) aims at. The absence bleed is unreachable by a minority: a timeout view change needs 22 signers none of which holds an attested block of the view that is still uncertified, so only a leader that is in fact not serving pays it. Nor can a cartel move a draw by suspending its own owners with self-submitted evidence: a suspended seat keeps its rank and is a void view or an empty slot when drawn, so no owner write changes a committee after the cycle's seed is fixed (round 5, R5S-2). The entrant's seat is never removed, and forced inclusion bounds censorship inside the cartel's own terms.

Self-check

Why is a cap of 8 seats per address not an anti-monopoly measure?

It bounds an address, not an entity; entities split across addresses at no cost. Its real purpose is bounding on-chain loops and one address's share of backup views.

A term auction would let the richest party buy every term. Why is that worse than sortition?

An auction prices monopoly instead of bounding it, needs an L1 transaction per bidder per term that dies under congestion, and still needs a random fallback for an absent winner.

Challenge this

Propose a rule that taxes consecutive control by one entity without being bypassable by address splitting. The design claims none exists without an identity-bearing primitive; either find one or say which primitive (and its trust assumption) you would add.
