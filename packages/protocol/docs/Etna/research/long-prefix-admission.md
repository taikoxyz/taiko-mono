## 1. Purpose and status

This is a research note. It changes no rule. It answers the open W22 blocker (IT:12, IT:49–59; S2:206–208, S2:681; S2-R15, S2-R22, S2-V40/V42/V43). Owners: A owns C2, C3, C5, C6's measurement plan, C7, C8, S1, S3, S4; B owns S2, C1, C4. Every change to S2 or C4 below is a proposal to B.

**Selected design: RPT ("recovery-prefix tree with tip carry")**, section 5. It is design A from the candidate round, with that round's breaks repaired, revised after A's round-1 to round-4 reviews (section 9). It adds one new mechanism, the tip carry (5.4). The tip carry removes A's main remaining break, prefix growth across hatch and dead-mode landings, without moving `lastVCHash` (E) except at an actual landing.

**Scope narrowed in round 1 [assumed].** The tip carry and RESUME prefix nodes apply only after landings that C3-R09 can resume from and that are certificate-free (hatch-254, dead-mode view 255). Open-empty and no-committee sentinel landings keep today's behaviour, so RPT does not bound a prefix that such landings interrupt (section 7). Round 2 narrowed further: the carry re-checks a recovery root before consuming it (5.4), a RESUME node no longer excludes a tail RESUME (5.3 check 5), and MISS can repeat after a restart as it does today (5.6). Round 3 changed four things. A RESUME node is admitted on L1 only through C3-R09's `hatchTip` disjunct and at or above the restart floor, so a RESUME is never re-landable after a C2-R14 restart (5.3 check 6, 5.5). The landing-time `l1Ref` window re-check is dropped, which also removes round 2's slot-3 wedge (5.3, section 7 (f)). Settlement jobs evaluate ROLE_HORIZON at the consuming transaction's own time, and a hatch carry's term records wait for the next landing that reaches those terms (5.6). Finally, sentinel and hatch landings can neither name an anchor nor carry a segment or VC after a sentinel segment (5.3, 5.5). Round 4 changed six things. The V_MAX bound is read from each closing's signed `(t, v)`, so no per-term counter is stored, carried or restored, which removes round 3's restart wedge (5.3 check 3). After a sentinel segment, further sentinel segments of the same object are allowed (5.3; redefined by mode in round 5). A dead-mode carry writes no term record that the dead-mode landing already wrote, and defers the rest (5.6; its `recFrom` premise was corrected in round 5). A landing in the same L1 block as a hatch or dead-mode landing keeps the head resumable when it consumes no recovery VC, so C3's T25 stays landable; S2-V34's RESUME after a replacement is named as made unlandable (5.2, 5.5). `settlePrefix` is named as a fresh-slot writer in the C5-R06 amendment, and two citations and two byte counts are corrected (5.7, 9). Round 5 changed five things. A settlement job no longer trusts that a sentinel landing settles every term it passes (C2-R06 writes at most SETTLE_MAX records): `recFrom` is replaced by a write-if-absent rule (a job skips a term whose record already exists, C2:269's `landableFrom` telling a record for t from a ring alias) and a stored split term that picks which landing's pre-write `lastLanded.at` a term record uses; this also closes the hatch-carry-then-sentinel double write (5.6). The same-block rule covers holder landings only and is off in a restart block, and the RESET branch is chosen per L1 block from its last trigger, not from the last `headClass` (5.2, 5.5). Tail REPLACEs obey the restart floor (5.3). The pair check reads `prefixMeta[E]` when E is a node id, so V_MAX is proven for carried and anchored cursors and assumed only for a VC-written E (5.3 check 3). `SentinelNotLast` is defined by sentinel mode and pre-state `lastLanded`, not by a recomputed object id (5.3).

## 2. Problem

[proven: textual] The facts that create the blocker:

1. **E moves only at a landing.** The cursor E (`lastVCHash`, C8:409) is written only by S2-R15's proposed writer at a successful landing (S2:216). C2-R06 does not write it yet; adoption is open at C2:586 and S2:681.
2. **Sentinel and hatch landings keep E** (S2:221–222).
3. **E is bound into every landing proof.** It enters the journal's `segmentsRoot` as `entryOpening` (S2:212–214), and the provisional record's `startDigest` stores it for rollback (C2:211). So any change to E invalidates every proof prepared against the old E.
4. **Every landing carries a block and re-verifies its whole VC chain.**
   - Every landing carries at least one block (C2:140).
   - It verifies its whole VC chain from E, re-checking each witness "even when the VC equals one recorded on L1" (C2:143, MP:160).
   - Trailing closings after the end block are rejected (S2:216).
5. **One landing holds only about 35 common VCs.**
   - Fixed cost is about 4.12M (MP:160).
   - That leaves about 5.88M of the 10M budget, about 35 VCs at about 165k each.

[proven: arithmetic, conditional on MP's unit costs] **Consequence for dead mode.**
- Dead mode needs a reference more than 75 terms after `lastLandedTerm` (S2:428).
- An empty transition closes one term per VC and skips none (S2:202–204).
- So the first certified restart after a dead-mode recovery carries at least about 76 closings. That is about 12.5M, plus RESUME (0.8–2.72M, MP:139) and about 4.12M fixed, which is more than 16,777,216.
- Certificate-free landings keep E, so the chain only grows.
- V43 (S2:567) is the 100-closing instance.

**Design obligation** (IT:59; S2:206–208):
- keep every eligible REPLACE fork rooted before each empty advance, or re-argue C2-R09/S2-R13 actual-landing arbitration;
- keep RESUME one-use for each actual recovery;
- tell certificate-free recovery apart from a replacement's surviving ordinary generation;
- stay inside R1–R7, with G5/G5-F as the only residuals.

**[proven: textual] Arbitration premise.**
- A record decides nothing. The first landing chooses the branch (C2:181; S2:130, S2:174, S2:198, S2:233).
- S2:138's safety argument needs every void to happen at an I4-listed landing.
- Anything that changes E, or anything a landing proof commits to, without moving `lastLanded` violates that premise.

## 3. The four rejected prototypes as schedules

Source: IT:52–57, withdrawn at S2:208. Each costs the attacker no slash.

**P1: long prefix, no bound.** [proven: conditional on MP's unit costs]
1. E = X, in term t0.
2. A recovery lands at L in term t0+76 or later (t0+100 in V43).
3. Honest parties hold RESUME(X) and 100 TERM_END closings.
4. Any landing must carry all 101 VCs plus one block, which is at least about 16.5M of VC gas. It cannot be included, and it cannot be split (C2:140, S2:216).

**P2: the same recovery reference used twice, after a proof-free advance.** [proven: conditional on the withdrawn cursor prototype]
1. A recovery lands at L with reference N.
2. A cursor call consumes RESUME(X→Y). `lastLanded` stays L.
3. Y41 and Y42 are certified, and C(Y42) locks Y41.
4. Q keys (honest keys locked above L refuse, S2:134) sign RESUME(Y→Z) at L with the same N, in a fresh scope with `prevLock` 0.
5. The locked Y tail is voided with no landing.

**P3: a first RESUME over a replacement's surviving generation.** [proven: conditional on the withdrawn guard prototype]
1. REPLACE(X→Y) lands at L.
2. A recovery session is initialised from Y.
3. C(Y_{L+2}) locks Y_{L+1}.
4. RESUME(Y→Z) at L passes source, phase and reference checks.
5. The fresh Y tail is voided, with no recovery landing after it.

**P4: proof-free priority over REPLACE.** [proven: conditional on the withdrawn cursor prototype]
1. Head L, E = X.
2. A head-compatible H closing X at L is recorded.
3. Honest parties hold REPLACE(X→R) with `entryOpening = X`.
4. One cursor call advances E to id(H).
5. REPLACE fails L4 (`closes ≠ E`) and fails its `segmentsRoot`, though no landing chose H.
6. Variant: the same race against REPLACE(Y) inside successor Y.

## 5. Selected design: RPT

### 5.1 Principle

[assumed: proposed rules] The empty prefix is stored as verified, append-only **nodes**. Nodes are bound to the head they were built on and to the restart epoch.
- Nothing except a landing moves E, `lastLanded`, `startDigest` or `replaceableFrom`. The C2-R08 deferral flag moves at a landing, at an announcement, and at a recovery record, as today; a REPLACE node is a recovery record (5.3 check 6) and spends it exactly as `recordViewChange` would; no other append touches it.
- A holder landing may name a live node as its **anchor**. It then consumes E→anchor at O(1) cost.
- A pure hatch or dead-mode landing (5.4) must consume the current **tip**, the deepest live node, when the tip passes the root re-checks an anchored landing would apply. That is the only new effect on E, and it happens at an actual landing.
- Penalties and term records along a consumed prefix go into a **settlement job** that is drained after the landing. So nothing is applied to a branch that no landing chose.

`headClass` values: 0 ORDINARY (a certified landing, including REPLACE- and RESUME-consuming and anchored landings); 1 RESUMABLE (a hatch-254 or dead-mode view-255 landing, the certificate-free classes that C3-R06 step 7 marks in `hatchTip`); 2 OTHER_SENTINEL (an open-empty or no-committee view-255 landing). A landing's segments are all of one class (5.5, "No mixed landings": one direction textual, the other enforced by a proposed L4 rule), so the class is the landing's and L1 decides it from the landing kind, with one exception:
- **Same-block rule (round 4, narrowed in round 5) [assumed: proposed C2-R06 rule].** It covers **holder landings only**: a landing whose first segment is a holder segment (so not a sentinel-255 or hatch-254 landing), that is not replacement-classified (5.3, `isReplace`) and consumes no RESUME (`isResume`), keeps `headClass′ = RESUMABLE` when its pre-state `headClass` is RESUMABLE, `hatchL1 == block.number` and `restartL1Number < block.number`. Every sentinel landing takes its kind's class, so an open-empty or no-committee landing after a hatch in the same block is OTHER_SENTINEL (N2, N15 variant). `hatchL1` sits in the `FiQueue` word, slot 256, C8, which L8 already reads [proven: textual, C8's `FiQueue` row]; `restartL1Number` (C4-R11's migration namespace) is read only when the first two conditions hold, one cold SLOAD of about 2.1k [assumed: not packed with a word the landing already reads]. [proven: conditional on C3:304 and on C2:228 leaving `hatchL1` untouched at a restart] The test is exact. Within block N, `hatchL1 = N` is set only by a hatch, replacement or dead-mode landing (C3:304), a replacement sets ORDINARY, and with `restartL1Number < N` no restart wrote `headClass` in N. So a RESUMABLE pre-state with `hatchL1 = N` means that the last `hatchL1`-setting landing in N was a hatch or dead-mode landing and every landing after it in N was a holder landing kept RESUMABLE by this rule (a sentinel landing would have set OTHER_SENTINEL, a replacement or RESUME landing ORDINARY). Without the restart condition, a restart in N after a hatch-class landing could restore a RESUMABLE head below it, and a holder landing on that head would inherit RESUMABLE from a hatch that is not on its chain (round 5, N17). The restart condition costs nothing reachable for window hatches (no window opens before `restartTimestamp + HATCH_DELAY`, C2:231) or dead mode (the restart sets `lastLandedTerm = firstPermittedTerm − 1`, C2:228, and dead mode needs a 75-term gap, S2:428); whether an A1 hatch can land in the restart block is [open: A/C3], and if it can, T25 in that one block is refused (disclosed, O12). This keeps C3's T25 landable (C3:444; 5.5, N15). It never moves ORDINARY or OTHER_SENTINEL to RESUMABLE, and a landing it covers is not a pure resumable landing, so it never carries (5.4).

Only a landing whose first segment is a holder segment may name an anchor (5.3), so no sentinel or hatch landing is anchored.

A node is **live** iff `prefixMeta[id].epoch == PrefixWord.epoch` and `prefixMeta[id].height == lastLanded.height`.

[proven: conditional on C2-R05 L1, C3-R07 check 4, and C2-R14 adopting the epoch bump (5.8)] The invariants:
- **I-P3 (a head is identified by (epoch, height)).** Within one epoch, every writer of `lastLanded` is a landing with at least one block (C2:140; a hatch has `1 ≤ m`, C3-R07 check 4), so `lastLanded.height` strictly increases. The only other writer is the C2-R14 restart, which restores a lower height (C2:227) and, under 5.8, bumps the epoch. So within one epoch a height names one `lastLanded` value. A pair repeats only across epochs, and the epoch never repeats.
  - This replaces round 0's "heights never repeat", which C2-R14 falsifies, and its "C4-R12 must never lower height", which C2-R14's own restore makes impossible.
  - It also repairs A's 64-bit pairTag grind: the tag is (epoch, height), which cannot be ground.
- **I-P1 (liveness and insert-once).** By I-P3, a live node was built on the current head `(L.height, L.phHash)`; no separate phHash comparison is needed at landing. `prefixAt` is insert-once per `ctxKey`; since `ctxKey` binds epoch, height and phHash, a stale entry from a dead epoch never occupies a live context. A `prefixMeta` entry may be overwritten only when it is dead.
- **I-P2 (chain).** A live node has three properties:
  - its lock is `(L.height, L.phHash)`;
  - its `closes` is E or a live node;
  - its witness was verified exactly as S2-R10 (i)–(viii) would verify it at landing, and every time-dependent check is either fixed once the append is included, monotone in time for a fixed (epoch, head), or re-checked at every landing that consumes it, anchored or carried (5.3, "Root re-checks"; 5.4).

**Append checks, in order, for each VC.** [assumed: proposed C2-R2x]
1. **Parent.** The parent is E or a live node, and `vc.closes == parent`.
2. **Lock.** `vc.lock == (L.height, L.phHash)`.
3. **V_MAX, read from the signed view (round 4) [assumed: proposed reading of S2:54, S2:160 and C2-R05 L4's "at most V_MAX per term"].** A term has views 0 to V_MAX (S2:54). A TIMEOUT closing `(t, v)` opens `(t, v + 1)`; TERM_END or FALLBACK closing `(t, v)` opens `(t + 1, 0)`; REPLACE or RESUME closing `(t, v)` reopens `(t, v)` (S2:160). So the bound is a check on each closing's own signed `(t, v)`, with no counter:
   - **Pair.** The VC's signed `(t, v)` equals the pair its parent opens: from the parent's `NodeMeta` (`openTerm`, `openView`) when the parent is a node. When the parent is E and `prefixMeta[E]` exists (`epoch != 0`), the check uses that meta too (round 5; one SLOAD, about 2.1k). E has meta after every carry (E′ = the tip) and after every anchored landing without a tail (E′ = the anchor), whether or not the node is still live: a meta entry is overwritten only by a re-append of the same id, and an id fixes the VC's `(t, v, kind)` and so its opened pair. When E was written from a tail VC, which has no meta, the pair is not stored and the check is today's L4 [assumed: unchanged from today; a closing that misstates E's opened pair needs Q signers of committee(t) closing a view that was not open, which honest keys refuse, S2-R11]. The same applies to the first VC of an unanchored tail (5.3, "V_MAX across the anchor").
   - **Bound.** `v ≤ V_MAX` for every kind, and `v < V_MAX` for TIMEOUT (its opened view must be a holder view). TERM_END and FALLBACK close the term and are not counted; REPLACE and RESUME reopen the same view and are bounded by "at most one recovery VC per path" (check 6), not by V_MAX.
   - [proven: by induction on the pair check, conditional on its base case: the pair E opens. The base is checked on L1 when E has meta (a carried or anchored E′) and is assumed (honest Q, S2-R11) when E is a tail-written VC id] Along one chain the TIMEOUTs of a term advance the view by one each from the term's opening view, which is at least 0, so at most V_MAX TIMEOUTs of term t are admitted; this is C2 L4's "at most V_MAX per term" for view-advancing closings. Q signers who misstate the opened pair of a tail-written E (for example a TIMEOUT `(t, 1)` closing an E that opened `(t, 4)`) restart the count; that is today's exposure, unchanged by RPT.
   - Round 3 stored a per-term closing count `PrefixWord.(eTerm, eViews)` that every writer of E had to write. It counted RESUME and TERM_END as views, which contradicts S2:54/S2:160: a tip opened at view V_MAX left no room for RESUME(E′) or TERM_END, and the certified path wedged. It also needed a conservative `eViews = V_MAX` after a C2-R14 restart, because `startDigest`'s preimage does not hold the count, which wedged every certified closing of the restored E. Both are gone: nothing is stored, carried or restored for V_MAX (N9b, N9c, N1b).
4. **Not in the future.** `openTerm ≤ termOf(block.timestamp) + 1`. This stops a tip from naming a future opening. [assumed: the +1 allows a TERM_END signed at the term boundary]
5. **Same-slot conflicts.** `prefixAt[ctx][slot]` is empty or equal; equal is an idempotent no-op. A live operational record in the same C8-R07 family must carry the same id, else `ViewChangeMismatch`. RESUME nodes use slot 3, which no record shares, so a RESUME node and a REPLACE record or node at one context do not exclude each other (5.3 check 6, 6.4 N6).

### 5.4 Tip carry at pure hatch and dead-mode landings

[assumed: proposed amendment to S2:221–222 and C2-R06, a proposal to B]

A landing is **pure resumable** iff it is a hatch-254 landing or a dead-mode view-255 sentinel landing. Every such landing consumes no VC, names no anchor and lands no ordinary segment: S2:221–222 gives sentinel and hatch landings no VC, the anchor rule (5.3) refuses an anchor on any sentinel or hatch landing, and 5.5 "No mixed landings" with `SentinelNotLast` (5.3) shows that a landing with a sentinel segment carries only sentinel segments of one mode, opened from its pre-state `lastLanded`. A landing kept RESUMABLE by the same-block rule (5.2) is not pure resumable. Only a pure resumable landing carries the tip:
- If `prefixTip` is live, the next two job ring entries are free (one stays reserved for an anchored landing, 5.3), and the tip passes the **carry re-checks** below, it sets `E′ = prefixTip` and opens a settlement job (flag HATCH_CARRY for a hatch; for a dead-mode landing `splitTerm` = its own post-write `lastLandedTerm`, 5.6). Nothing else is written for V_MAX: the tip's opened view is signed into the next closing (5.3 check 3, fixtures N9b, N9c).
- Otherwise it keeps E, as today. The lander has no choice in any branch.
- **Carry re-checks.** When the tip's flags include HAS_RESUME or HAS_REPLACE, the carry runs the same O(1) root re-checks as an anchored landing (5.3): for a RESUME root, no operational family-2 record with another id exists at the root context; for a REPLACE root, A1 as defence in depth. No window re-check (round 3, 5.3). If any check fails, the carry is skipped and E is kept. The tip does not fall back to another branch. [assumed: liveness narrowing, section 7; a per-root-kind tip that carries the best passing branch is a possible later refinement, O11] So the carry never writes an E′ that an anchored landing would refuse, and the settlement job never applies MISS along such a path.
- **A carried REPLACE path.** A tip with HAS_REPLACE that passes the re-checks is carried. The landing is still classified by its own kind: a hatch or dead-mode landing is already a replacement-class trigger for C3-R06 step 7 (`hatchL1`, `hatchTip`), for C2-R08 (bond forfeited) and for the RESET trigger (S2:200), so the carried REPLACE adds no new classification. `PrefixJobOpened` reports `pathHasReplace = true, carried = true`, and `Landed.headClass = RESUMABLE`. Observers select the RESET branch from `headClass` (5.5): reset keys enter RESET and sign only RESUME closing E′, which here lies inside the REPLACE generation; they do not enter ATTESTING. The job applies MISS along the carried path like any other job.
- Cost: about 6.3k of reads, about 5k of root re-checks, one overwritten E write and one job (5.7).
- The hatch or sentinel journal must not commit E′. [assumed; C3/C7 to confirm, since S2:222 leaves the hatch's PH vcHash bytes open] A proof prepared before the tip moved therefore stays valid.

Open-empty and no-committee sentinel landings, holder landings and REPLACE landings never carry.

Why this is safe, for a pure resumable landing:
1. **[proven: conditional on C2-R09 (C2:181, "why descendants are void") and S2:200] It voids nothing the landing does not already void.**
   - The landing's first block is a hatch or sentinel block at height L+1 on parent L. Every unlanded certified block above L conflicts with it at L+1 or descends from one that does, so this I4-listed landing voids all of them.
   - The landing lands no certified block and names no opening (S2:221–222), so no landed block's opening disagrees with E′: E′ names an empty branch with lock L, and the landed blocks belong to no branch.
   - The carried nodes are empty closings with lock L, including a carried RESUME or REPLACE root, which opens a generation that has landed no block. Consuming them lands no certified block and voids nothing further.
   - The head moves above L, which kills every REPLACE/RESUME at L (C2-R09). So "preserve an eligible REPLACE fork rooted before each empty advance" holds: no REPLACE at L is eligible after this landing.
   - The settlement job applies MISS along E→E′, the branch this landing consumed by rule, not an unchosen one. A carry job's term records wait until a landing has reached every carried term, and the job skips any term whose record a landing already wrote (5.6, round 5).
2. **[proven: by construction] No branch is chosen without a landing.** E′ is written only inside `land`/`landFor` and the hatch.
3. **[assumed: S1 committee sampling and T11] The adversary gains little by shaping the tip.**
   - A deeper branch needs a quorum of each later committee.
   - Every branch is empty and lies below the head that the landing replaces.
   - Narrowed in round 1: this does not make the next RESUME's committee recent. The certified restart needs RESUME(E′) from committee(`tip.openTerm`), which is recent only if appenders kept the tip close to the current term (section 7). If appends stalled, it is as old as S2:204 already allows.

What it buys:
1. **[proven: by construction] Prefix work survives a pure hatch or dead-mode landing.** Nodes appended under L become the new E.
2. **[proven: conditional on appenders, on no OTHER_SENTINEL or ordinary landing intervening, and on the tip passing the carry re-checks] The backlog after a pure resumable landing is bounded.**
   - The backlog is at most the closings formed since the last append, plus those formed during RESUME formation.
   - It is not the cumulative gap back to the old E. This repairs A's hatch-cycle growth law and B's absorbing miss, for hatch and dead-mode cycles.
3. **[assumed] The old-committee availability condition (S2:204) is relaxed only conditionally.** If honest parties append each closing while its committee is live, a later recovery does not need a committee older than the tip's. Otherwise S2:204 applies unchanged.

### 5.6 Settlement jobs (repairing A's O(n) MISS break)

[assumed: proposed; S3-R07 and C2-R06 are A's]

**Validity.** A job is **valid** iff `job.epoch == PrefixWord.epoch`, or `job.endHeight ≤ restoredHeightAt[job.epoch + 1]` (O(1)).
- [proven: conditional on C2-R21's monotone restore floor] A restart restores the newest L1 checkpoint (C2:227), and later restarts never restore below an earlier one (C2-R21 forbids rolling back below a published root). So a job whose landing ended at or below the first later restored height is on the kept chain; one ending above it belongs to a voided landing and stays invalid.
- [open: A, C4] A DAO rollback below a published root (C2-R14 case (ii)) breaks the monotone premise; that upgrade must then also invalidate jobs above its floor.

**Two times per job (round 3).** Round 2 used one field, `landedAt`, defined as "the value of `lastLanded.at` that the landing's own settlement uses", for both ROLE_HORIZON and `landableFrom`. For a hatch carry that value is stale: a hatch moves `lastLanded` "with `lastLanded.at` and `lastLandedTerm` untouched" (C2:156), so it can predate every carried term's start, and a ROLE_HORIZON test at a time before `termStart(t)` always passes. A job now stores two fields (5.2):
- `consumedAt = block.timestamp` of the consuming transaction (the anchored landing, or the hatch or dead-mode landing that carries). This is the time at which S3-R07 applies MISS "within ROLE_HORIZON of the term start" (S3:100) for a landing's own tail.
- `landedAt = ` the consuming transaction's pre-write `lastLanded.at`, for every job kind (round 5; round 4 used the post-write value for carries). With `consumedAt` it gives the `landableFrom` input of each term, through `splitTerm` (5.2, step 3): the first landing that reaches a carried term t is the consuming landing for an anchored job; the first non-hatch landing after a hatch carry, whose pre-write value equals `landedAt` because a hatch leaves `lastLanded.at` unchanged (C2:156); and for a dead-mode carry, the dead-mode landing itself for t ≤ T_d (pre-write value `landedAt`) and the next landing for t > T_d (pre-write value the dead-mode landing's `block.timestamp`, which is `consumedAt`). [assumed: proposal to C2-R06, closing O7's pre/post question] C2:269's `lastLanded.at` is read before the landing's own write. [assumed: proposed reading of C2:269] A landing "reaches" t when its post-write `lastLandedTerm` is at or above t, whether or not it writes t's record. Round 4 noted that under a post-write reading the job could drop `landedAt`; that no longer holds, because a dead-mode carry needs both values.

`settlePrefix(jobId, bodies)` requires a valid, open and **drainable** job and walks backwards from `job.nextId`. A job is drainable iff `lastLandedTerm ≥ job.termHi`, where `termHi` is the highest term a node of the job closes or opens. For an anchored job this holds at once [assumed: the landing's own `lastLandedTerm` is at or above every term on the consumed path, whose openings precede its end block]. For a dead-mode carry it holds at once iff the dead-mode landing's own `lastLandedTerm` (the term of its last sentinel segment) is at or above `termHi`. Both orders are reachable: committees can keep appending TIMEOUT and TERM_END nodes at lock L during the stall after the dead-mode object's reference, so the tip can lie in a later term than the object's last term, or in an earlier one. A later tip makes the job wait, as a HATCH_CARRY job does. For a HATCH_CARRY job it holds after the next landing that writes `lastLandedTerm`, which the hatch does not (C2:156). For each VC body (from the append transaction's calldata or event; the witness is not needed):

2. ## 6. Attack walks

### 6.1 The four prototypes

**P1.** Blocked. [proven: conditional on 5.7's units, S2:204's availability premises, no OTHER_SENTINEL or ordinary landing intervening, and section 7 (f)'s append-time window]
1. After L (`headClass = RESUMABLE`), honest parties append RESUME(X→Y) as a child of E, while its `l1Ref` (the block of L's landing, at or above the floor) is within the EIP-2935 window of the append; the `hatchTip` disjunct holds (5.3 check 6).
2. They append the TERM_END nodes in calls of at most 10M (V43: 10 calls with fresh H records, 6 when duty-recorded; 5.7).
3. They land with the anchor at the last node and the first timely block. The landing re-checks only that no foreclosing family-2 record exists at the root context; the age of `l1Ref` at landing does not matter (round 3, 5.3).
4. Each transaction fits, and nodes are monotone (I-P1).
5. If a pure hatch or dead-mode landing intervenes, the tip carry keeps the work (5.4), provided the RESUME root passes the carry re-checks; if it fails them, E is kept and P1 returns for that cycle as today. If an open-empty or no-committee sentinel intervenes, the work dies and P1 returns as it is today (section 7).
6. Round 4: V_MAX no longer wedges the restart. A carried tip opened at view V_MAX still admits RESUME(E′) and TERM_END (5.3 check 3, N9c), and a multi-segment dead-mode landing (if C2 admits it) still carries (5.3, N3b).

**P2.** Blocked. [proven: conditional on 5.3's checks and S3c(a) as at S2:138]
1. The RESUME(X→Y) node does not move E.
2. RESUME(Y→Z) fails `parent == E` as a node, and fails "first and closes E" in a tail.
3. A second RESUME closing X is the same context and slot 3. As a node it is refused by insert-once `prefixAt`; as a tail it is no longer checked against slot 3 (round 2) and may land instead of the node, in which case the first landing chooses, as S2:198 has it. At one redraw count the pair yields at least 2Q−m S3c(a) keys; across redraw counts no bound is derived (5.3 check 5). Either way only one of them lands, because:
4. The landing that consumes a RESUME moves the head (C2:181) and sets `headClass′ = ORDINARY`, so no further RESUME lands as a node or tail at that epoch and head. The same-block rule (5.2) excludes RESUME-consuming landings, and every later landing in that L1 block has an ORDINARY pre-state, so a RESUME landing never leaves a RESUMABLE head even within one block (round 4).
5. If a pure hatch or dead-mode landing consumes it by tip carry, a new RESUME must close E′ at a new head, after that new I4-listed landing.
6. After a C2-R14 restart that restores a RESUMABLE head, the consumed RESUME(X→Y) names an `l1Ref` below `restartL1Number`. Its re-append reverts `PrefixRefFloor`, and its re-landing as a tail reverts on the same floor [proven: textual, C2:228, C4:242, with 5.3's checks]. No new RESUME node can be appended at the restored head, because no post-floor `hatchTip` equals its lock. A fresh post-floor tail RESUME needs A1 after the restart, and keys that did not sign the old one, or S3c(a) equivocators (S2:128). So RESUME is one-use across restarts, and the restored head's certified exit is the open restart opening (C7:105, C4-R12) (5.5, N1b, N14). The blocks the voided landing landed or locked are voided by the restart itself (C2:233).

**P3.** Blocked. [proven: conditional on 5.5]
1. REPLACE(X→Y) lands, so `headClass′ = ORDINARY`. The same holds when the REPLACE is a node on an anchored path (`HAS_REPLACE`, 5.3).
2. RESUME(Y→Z) at L fails `NotResumable` as a node and as a tail.
3. `headClass` leaves ORDINARY only at a certificate-free landing, whose first block at L+1 voids every certified Y block above L (5.5), or at a C2-R14 restart below the REPLACE landing, which voids those blocks itself (C2:233) [proven: textual]. The restart exit therefore gives no RESUME over a surviving Y block. Round 5 closes the same-block form of this exit: a restart in L1 block N after a hatch-class landing in N leaves `hatchL1 = N` (C2:228), and round 4's same-block rule would then have kept RESUMABLE for a holder landing O on the restored head; the rule is now off while `restartL1Number == block.number`, so O sets ORDINARY and no RESUME node or tail RESUME closing O's E′ is admitted (5.2, N17).
4. Keys locked on certified Y blocks above the landed end keep their locks; reset keys enter ATTESTING in the generation opened by E′ (5.5), so there is no deadlock.
5. Same-block variants (round 4, N16). If constructible (reachability open, as for C3's T50, C3:517: the REPLACE closes E with its lock at the hatch's end, which is on L1 only from block N, so its votes were signed before N with a lock honest keys refuse, S2:134): a hatch, then REPLACE(X→Y), then an ordinary landing, all in L1 block N: the REPLACE landing is replacement-classified and sets ORDINARY, so the ordinary landing's pre-state is ORDINARY and the same-block rule does not apply; RESUME(Y→Z) with `l1Ref = N` is refused although `hatchTip[N]` matches. **P4.** Blocked. [proven: conditional on I-P1 and O1]

### 6.3 New surfaces checked

- **Tip griefing.** [assumed: T11 and S1 sampling] An adversary's deeper branch needs Q of each later committee. Whichever branch is carried is empty, below a head that the carrying landing already replaces. A deeper adversarial branch whose root later fails a re-check (a foreclosing record) makes the carry skip and keep E, today's behaviour: a liveness cost only, with the same Q-per-committee price (O11). A future-term tip is refused by check 4 [proven: by construction].
- **Stale proof at the tip carry.** The hatch or sentinel journal omits E′, so a tip moved after proving does not revert the landing [assumed: C3/C7]. The next holder landing proves against the stored E′, because `entryOpening` is read from storage at landing.
- **Storage spam.** [assumed] Every node needs a quorum witness and pays 2–6 fresh slots of state gas (5.7). Dead nodes are never read again.
- **Stale RESUME node (round 3).** [proven: by construction] A RESUME node whose `l1Ref` has since left the EIP-2935 window can still anchor and be carried, because there is no landing-time window re-check (5.3). So it never strands slot 3, and round 2's slot-3 wedge is gone. Honest keys could not have repaired a stale node anyway: a RESUME with a fresh `l1Ref` at the same scope and `lockHeight` differs in a signed field other than `redraw`, so S2-R09's protection refuses it (S3:445), and signing it would be S3c(a) for every key that signed the first (S2:128, S2:138). Past the window, a RESUME not yet appended can only be a tail (P1-limited, as today).
- **Settlement-job griefing.** [proven: by construction] Bodies are bound by id, and a wrong body reverts. A job cannot be advanced except along the consumed chain. **Ring filling** [assumed]: holding all 8 ring entries open needs 8 anchored or carrying landings with no drain in between; any caller can drain them in bounded calls. HATCH_CARRY jobs, and dead-mode carry jobs whose `termHi` is above the dead-mode landing's own `lastLandedTerm`, are not drainable until a later landing writes `lastLandedTerm` at or above `termHi` (5.6), so 7 such carries in a stall exhaust what carries may use (one entry is reserved, 5.3) and later carries are skipped (E kept, as today), a liveness cost only. The reserved entry is always free for an anchored landing, whose own `lastLandedTerm` write then makes the carry jobs drainable.

## 7. Liveness

**Claim** [proven: conditional on (a)–(f)]. Assume:
- (a) S2:204's premises hold for the committees whose closings are appended: they are live when the closing forms, their pins exist, and all of this is within ROLE_HORIZON;
- (b) an honest appender includes each L1 transaction within Δ;
- (c) A-T1 inclusion;
- (d) w_rd keeps one node at most 10M;
- (e) no open-empty or no-committee sentinel landing and no ordinary landing intervene between the recovery landing and the anchored restart, and the job ring has two free entries at a carry;
- (f) (narrowed in round 3) the RESUME **node's append** lands within the EIP-2935 window of its `l1Ref`: 8,191 L1 blocks, about 27 h at 12-s slots and 4.55 h at 2-s slots (C2:189). This is an append-time admission rule only (5.3 check 6). The later appends, the anchored landing and the carry have no window condition. The landing-time re-check and round 2's slot-3 wedge are removed (5.3, 6.3, N8, N10). A RESUME not appended within the window is tail-only and P1-limited, as every RESUME is today. Honest keys cannot re-sign it with a fresh `l1Ref` at the same scope and lock (S3:445, S3c(a)). So past the window, the certified restart at that head waits for another certificate-free landing, which mints a new head and a new `l1Ref`. A conflicting fresh-`l1Ref` RESUME exists only with S3c(a) equivocators (at least 2Q−m at one redraw count, 5.3 check 5).

Then:
1. Every formed closing becomes a node within Δ of its formation, or within a bounded number of priced calls of it.
2. **Holder restart.** If no landing intervenes, a landing anchored at the tip, with the first timely block, fits in about 4.18M + 2·w_rd plus the tail (5.7).
3. **Hatch or dead-mode intervention.** If a pure one intervenes, the tip carry sets E′ to within about Δ of the last formed closing. The next restart then needs only:
   - RESUME(E′) from committee(`tip.openTerm`);
   - the closings formed while that RESUME forms;
   - one landing.
4. Under (e), nothing grows across hatch and dead-mode cycles. That is the bound A and B lacked.

**[open] What remains conditional.**
- **Open-empty and no-committee sentinel landings.** They keep E and kill live nodes, as every non-carrying landing does. If they recur during a long stall, the prefix grows as today and P1 returns. RPT is no worse than today here; extending the carry to them needs a RESUME attestability argument C3-R09 does not give (round-1 finding N2).
- **Repeated certificate-free landings.** If hatch or dead-mode landings recur faster than RESUME formation, append, proof and first block, the certified path stays down. Certificate-free progress continues meanwhile.
  - This is the **pre-existing** C3 A-HONEST-RESUME bound, because a RESUME at L dies at any landing (C2:181).
  - RPT does not worsen it. The per-cycle work is O(Δ/TERM), not O(n): one RESUME node (≈ 3.08M at redraw 0, ≈ 3.43M + 2·w_rd at redraw 2), one H node per closing formed in the cycle (≈ 877k with a fresh `ClosingRecord`, ≈ 485k when duty-recorded), and one anchored landing (≈ 4.18M + 2·w_rd), plus the ≈ 74k carry (5.7).
  - It must stay disclosed with its existing C3 text, marked "independent of prefix length after hatch or dead-mode landings".
- **No appends during the stall.** If nobody appends during the stall and committees then go dark, S2:204's old-committee condition returns unchanged. That case is no worse than today.
- **Pre-A1 REPLACE.** REPLACE nodes need A1, as REPLACE records do; a REPLACE fork's prefix can be appended only after A1.
- **A post-A1 REPLACE record strands a RESUME node's descendants** (N6), as a REPLACE record forecloses an unrecorded RESUME tail today. The carry then skips such a tip and keeps E (N12); it does not fall back to another branch (O11).
- **Window at append** (condition (f)): a RESUME not appended as a node within its `l1Ref` window is tail-only; once appended, the node does not expire (N8, N10).
- **Restart.** After a C2-R14 restart, no RESUME node is appendable at the restored head and the pre-restart RESUME is never re-landable (5.5). The certified exit is the open restart opening (C7:105, C4-R12); RPT gives no bound for it. V_MAX adds no restart condition (round 4, 5.3 check 3, N1b).
- **Hatch-only stalls and the job ring.** HATCH_CARRY jobs, and dead-mode carry jobs whose `termHi` is above the dead-mode landing's own term, drain only after a later landing writes `lastLandedTerm ≥ termHi`, so at most 7 consecutive such carries are kept, and later ones are skipped (E kept) until such a landing (6.3).
- **F nodes wait for finality.** Each F on the path costs up to FB_CONTEST, 3 blocks and 30 s.

## 10. Open review findings at publication (A's internal round 5)

**[open: A, with B and J review]** Five internal repair rounds took the design from 4 High and 5 Medium to the findings below; each round's attackers replayed P1 to P4 and the N-schedules. This note is a proposal, not an adopted rule: no spec section changes until B (S2) and A (C2, C3, C5, C8) adopt the per-owner changes in 5.8 and J has attacked it. The remaining findings, verbatim in substance:

1. **Medium** (5.5 'P3 block on L1' (lines 287-290), 6.1 P3 step 3 and P2 step 4, 5.3 'RESUME in a tail' (line 217)). The memo tags the P3 block [proven] and says it 'rests on L1 alone; honest-key policy is not needed'. The argument has a gap. It says headClass leaves ORDINARY only at a certificate-free landing whose first block at L+1 voids every certified Y block above L. An open-empty or no-committee sentinel landing S is such a landing, but it keeps E (S2:221-222), so the Y generation continues. New schedule: REPLACE(X->Y) lands (ORDINARY). Later, S lands (OTHER_SENTINEL, E still in Y). Y blocks are then certified on S.end, and S.end is lastLanded on L1, so honest votes are allowed. C(Y_{S+2}) locks Y_{S+1}. A tail RESUME(Y->Z) closing E at lock S.end passes every L1 check: it is first, it closes E, it clears the floor, and headClass is OTHER_SENTINEL. For tails there is no A1 or hatchTip check on L1. That RESUME voids certified Y_{S+1} with no landing. The same holds after a RESUME landing followed by S, so P2 step 4 ('no further RESUME lands') is also true only per head. What stops it today is lock intersection (S2:134; no key is reset at an OTHER_SENTINEL landing, so at least 2Q-m locked keys would have to equivocate) and honest-key policy. L1 does not. *Proposed fix:* Retag the P3 block, and P2 step 4, as conditional on S2:134 locks and on no reset at OTHER_SENTINEL landings (S2:200), and delete 'rests on L1 alone' for this schedule. Add a vector: REPLACE, then an open-empty landing, then certified Y on S.end, then a tail RESUME, with the expected admission on L1 and refusal by locked keys. Alternatively, make the tail-RESUME admission at OTHER_SENTINEL require that no replacement- or RESUME-classified landing has occurred since the last hatch-class landing (that needs a stored bit, priced), and state the remaining cost.
2. **Low** (5.2 same-block rule (line 146) vs 5.5 RESET 'Branch per L1 block' (line 278); N15 open-empty variant). New schedule in one L1 block N: hatch H (RESUMABLE), then open-empty landing S (OTHER_SENTINEL, since the same-block rule now covers holder landings only), then holder non-recovery landing O on S.end. O's pre-state is OTHER_SENTINEL, not RESUMABLE, so the same-block rule does not apply and O sets ORDINARY. 5.5 chooses the branch from the block's last trigger, which is H, and applies the hatch branch 'whatever landed after it in the block'. Reset keys therefore enter RESET, where the only object they may sign is a RESUME closing O's E' with l1Ref = N. That RESUME is C3-R09-attestable (hatchTip[N] = O.end) but L1 refuses it as a node and as a tail (NotResumable, ORDINARY). The certified path is stuck until another certificate-free landing. O is constructible on the same premise as T25's O, which the memo keeps landable. Neither 5.5's enumeration ('a holder landing kept RESUMABLE ... or an open-empty or no-committee landing') nor N15 covers this order. *Proposed fix:* Choose one of two fixes. (a) Extend the same-block rule: a holder non-recovery landing whose pre-state is OTHER_SENTINEL, with hatchL1 == block.number and restartL1Number < block.number, keeps OTHER_SENTINEL. The tail RESUME is then admitted; argue that this does not reopen the replacement-then-open-empty case. (b) Make the RESET branch depend on the block's final headClass as well: if any landing after the last trigger left ORDINARY, take the ordinary branch. Add the hatch -> open-empty -> holder variant to N15.
3. **Low** (5.3 'Recovery classification' (line 206); 5.4 'A carried REPLACE path' (line 235)). Both still say observers select the RESET branch from `Landed.headClass` (the landing kind). Round 5 made the branch a per-block last-trigger rule that also needs the new `Landed.replaced` flag, because a REPLACE landing and an ordinary landing both report headClass = ORDINARY. headClass alone cannot pick the REPLACE branch, so the text contradicts 5.5 and 5.8. *Proposed fix:* Reword both passages: the branch is taken from the block's last trigger, identified by `Landed.headClass` and `Landed.replaced` (5.5). Keep the 'never from pathHasReplace' clause.
4. **Low** (5.6 step 3, HATCH_CARRY bullet (line 311)). The bullet argues that the first non-hatch landing after the carry has pre-write lastLanded.at equal to landedAt, 'because only non-hatch landings write lastLanded.at and a C2-R14 restart bumps the epoch, which leaves the job valid only if its hatch is kept'. C2:228 shows the restart itself writes `lastLanded.at = restartTimestamp`. A kept, still-valid HATCH_CARRY job (endHeight <= restored height) is therefore followed by a landing whose pre-write `at` is restartTimestamp, not landedAt. The [proven] tag's condition list does not exclude this. It is disclosed only separately, as O7 (c). *Proposed fix:* Drop the restart clause from the justification. Add 'no C2-R14 restart between the carry and the first reaching landing (O7 (c))' to the [proven: conditional on ...] list of this bullet and of the dead-mode carry bullet.
5. **Low** (6.4 N11c case (a) and N11d case (a); 5.6 dead-mode carry bullet). N11c (a) and N11d (a) assume a single dead-mode landing of four segments, terms 176-179 (T_d = 179, records written for 176-179). N3b states this condition, but these two fixtures give unconditional expected values (skip 176-179, splitTerm = 179). Add the one-segment expectation: T_d = 176, the job skips 176, writes 101-175 and 177-179 at T0 and 180-190 at tau_d (N11c), or at T0 under O7 (b) (N11d).
6. **Medium** (research/long-prefix-admission.md 5.3 line 171 (`Landed` gains `bool replaced`), 5.5 line 278 (branch per L1 block), 5.8 R06 line 383 and C8 line 394, section 9 round-5 row (line 659)). `Landed` already has a `bool replaced` field: C2:361 (§10) and C8:244 both declare `Landed(..., uint8 zkLeavesUsed, bool replaced)`. The memo nonetheless says `Landed` "gains" `replaced` in round 5, and redefines it as `isReplace` (= `LandInput.replace`, a REPLACE VC in the tail or a HAS_REPLACE anchor). C2 does not define the existing field, and C2:177 calls a dead-mode landing "a replacement landing (dead mode included)". If the existing field keeps that broader meaning, the round-5 rule breaks. That rule picks the RESET branch per L1 block from the last trigger and identifies a replacement-classified trigger by `Landed.replaced`. An observer would then put a dead-mode landing (and possibly an S1 D40(3) open-empty or no-committee replacement, S1:390) on the REPLACE branch (ATTESTING) instead of RESET. The memo's own branch table and N2/N15 expect RESET for these cases. This is a silent dependency on an existing C2/C8 field whose semantics the memo assumes but does not cite. *Proposed fix:* Cite C2:361/C8:244 and state that the proposal defines the existing `replaced` field as `isReplace` (a REPLACE VC consumed, in the tail or on the anchored path), false for dead-mode, hatch and D40(3) sentinel replacements, tagged as a proposal to A/C2 and C8. Alternatively, add a distinct field (e.g. `replaceVc`) and leave `replaced` alone. Either way, add a vector: a dead-mode landing that is the last trigger in its block must select the RESET branch whatever `replaced` reads.
7. **Low** (5.3 "Recovery classification" (line 206); 5.4 "A carried REPLACE path" (line 235)). Both places still say observers select the RESET branch from `Landed.headClass` ("the landing kind") alone. Round 5 changed 5.5 so the branch is chosen per L1 block from the block's last trigger, read from `headClass` together with `replaced`, because `headClass = ORDINARY` cannot distinguish a replacement landing from an ordinary one. A same-block-kept holder landing also has `headClass = RESUMABLE` without being a trigger. So the 5.3 and 5.4 text contradicts 5.5 and the 5.8 S2 proposal (line 418). *Proposed fix:* Reword lines 206 and 235 to point to 5.5's per-block last-trigger rule (`headClass` plus `replaced`, never `pathHasReplace`). Keep the carried-REPLACE point: such a landing is a hatch or dead-mode trigger, so it takes the RESET branch.
8. **Low** (5.6 step 3, HATCH_CARRY bullet (line 311) and dead-mode carry bullet (line 310); "first writer wins and it is always a landing" (line 308)). (1) The HATCH_CARRY bullet argues that the first non-hatch landing's pre-write `lastLanded.at` equals `landedAt`, "because ... a C2-R14 restart bumps the epoch, which leaves the job valid only if its hatch is kept". That does not follow. A restart that restores at or above the hatch keeps the job valid, and it still writes `lastLanded.at = restartTimestamp` and `lastLandedTerm = firstPermittedTerm − 1` (C2:228). The next landing's pre-write `at` then differs from `landedAt`, and the job can become drainable with no landing reaching the carried terms. This is O7 (c), but the [proven] tags on both carry bullets list conditions that exclude only O7 (b), not (c). (2) Term records are neither restored nor reset at a restart (C2:229). A record written by a voided landing, or by a voided job drained before the restart, therefore persists into the new epoch. An epoch-2 job over the same terms (N7's repeat variant) skips it under write-if-absent. So "the first writer ... is always a landing" is false across epochs, and N7 does not state the record outcome. *Proposed fix:* Remove the restart clause from the HATCH_CARRY argument. Add "and no C2-R14 restart between the carry and the reaching landing (O7 (c))" to the [proven] conditions of both carry bullets. Qualify "always a landing" to "within one epoch", note that writes made before a restart by voided landings or jobs persist and are skipped (C2:233's leak class), and add the skipped-record expectation to N7.
9. **Low** (5.7 gas table, RESUME node row (line 348), REPLACE node row (line 350) and "Round-5 landing reads" row (line 354)). Yet the RESUME row itemises its "about 20k" of reads without this read, and the round-5 row prices the extra read only for unanchored landings. The append-side cost of the round-5 change is therefore missing, including for H nodes whose parent is E. *Proposed fix:* Add `prefixMeta[E]` (about 2.1k) to the itemised reads of the RESUME and REPLACE node rows, and to H/F nodes appended as children of E, or extend the round-5 row to cover appends with `parent == E`. Restate the totals, or note that they are within the rounding of "about 20k".
