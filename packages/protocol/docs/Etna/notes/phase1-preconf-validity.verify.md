## Overall

The summary is safe to rely on for its central thesis and for essentially all contract-level, timing and gate claims: 65 of the 67 indexed claims I checked quote the code faithfully at the cited lines, and the "L1 wins / no local slot-right check / no bond" narrative in sections 9-10 is borne out by `checkProposer`, the Go/Rust gossip validators, the insert paths and the derivation code. Readers must re-check three things. (1) Section 4.4 step 11 and Open Question 2 are wrong: Go persists the *embedded* block-hash signature from the envelope (not the wire signature), so the response-topic verification over `BlockHash` is consistent and Q2 is resolved. (2) C48 understates a real asymmetry: the Go proposer gates on the operator's *sequencer* address, which only coincides with the whitelist's on-chain proposer check when both keys are the same. (3) C16 misattributes the `slotInEpoch >= 2` condition to the `eth_call` (it gates only the lookahead push), and the JWT row omits that authentication is optional. A designer of a permissionless successor should additionally note the missed facts above, chiefly: the Inbox ring-buffer capacity gate, the absence of any pause switch on `propose`/`anchorV4`/whitelist, the unwired `PRECONF_DOMAIN_SEPARATOR`/`DISPUTE_PERIOD` constants, the zero-beacon-root and zero-genesis fallbacks in operator selection, and the fact that a Go node without `--l1.beacon` rejects all gossip forever.
