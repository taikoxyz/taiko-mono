### Storage growth: a narrower bound, not a price solution

**Proven conditionally on the specified origin binding and writer gates:** let `N` be the number of accepted Etna segments after activation in one canonical history. A segment contributes one authenticated origin shared by all its blocks. A fresh pin can record only the current origin; a fresh checkpoint must authenticate a recorded origin or permanent pin. Thus these segments introduce at most `N` new pin value words and `2N` checkpoint value words: **at most `3N` additional 32-byte values**. Duplicate callers cannot multiply the same record. Discarded speculative branches do not persist in this accepted state.

This is not a bound on total database size or all protocol state: preserved legacy state, trie overhead, signals, caches, receipts, stages and other contracts are outside it. It is linear growth with accepted history, not bounded lifetime storage. Actual storage, synchronization and proving costs remain unmeasured.

**Proven pricing gap:** charging only `pinCurrentOrigin()` would leave ordinary positive-timestamp `revealCheckpoint` able to create two checkpoint words without pinning. A tariff on these APIs also would not price all application state. The [alternatives analysis](../design/checkpoints.html) therefore requires exact payment, sink, duplicate, refund, queued-transaction and compatibility rules before adopting pricing. Pruning pins would remove the nonexpiring forced recovery promise unless a replacement mechanism preserved it. **No tariff or pruning is adopted.** A measured state budget and an explicit policy decision remain launch gates.

### Rent: pipelining does not remove the cost floor

**Proven conditional arithmetic under the unchanged rent and segment bounds:** let `λ` be the long-run accepted-segment rate, `b` the accepted L2-block rate and `r` the encoded-body byte rate, all per second. The caps imply:

```text
λ ≥ max(b / 60, r / 122880)
rent per day ≥ 86400 × 0.05 × max(0, λ − 1/900) ETH
```

For each interval `Δ`, the rent is at least `0.05 × (1 − Δ/900)` ETH; truncation at zero and wei rounding can only raise that lower bound. Sum over successive acceptances and divide by elapsed time. Boundary terms vanish in the long-run rate. Irregular timing, bursting and pipelining do not remove it; this is not a claim that a feasible schedule attains the bound.

- Sustaining **one accepted block/s** requires `λ ≥ 1/60`, hence **at least 67.2 ETH/day in rent alone**.
- Sustaining **10,000 encoded body bytes/s** requires `λ ≥ 10000/122880`, hence **at least 346.7625 ETH/day in rent alone**. Headers and encoding consume some of those bytes; this is not a 10,000 user-payload-byte claim.

Gas, dual proving, staging, final DA and optional publication duties add costs. These are long-run lower bounds, not measured daily bills. Crucially, R4 literally specifies **soft issuance**: it does not by itself imply `b=1` accepted block/s. The first example applies to a service intended to retain continuous one-second blocks canonically without unbounded lag or systematic abandonment. That service goal must be stated instead of smuggled into the requirement. The [operating-profile analysis](../design/assurance.html#operating-profile) keeps this distinction explicit.
