## What the alternative does better

**Proven source observations:** the peer specifies committees that execute and attest preconfirmed blocks, carries certificates and lock votes across sequencer handoffs, and provides recovery modes when those participants disappear. It gives users a stronger conditional pre-L1 ordering promise than our unreserved soft branches. That protection depends on committee composition, redraw and replacement rules; it is not Ethereum finality.

Its [confirmation-level table](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/preconf.html#L72-L78) separates the object a user holds from its promise. Its [parameter dependency chain](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/parameters.html#L16-L37) and [five invariants](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/arguments.html#L27) make interactions easier to challenge. Its reserve analysis considers delayed settlement rather than assuming an operator's refundable stake also pays each future landing. It explicitly analyzes committee blanking and Sybil concentration, including cases where productive revenue grows faster than stake share.

The peer's [advisory intent/proof gossip](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/landing.html#L150-L153) supplies a concrete communication path between independently operating provers. Its [local signing policy](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/preconf.html#L43-L53) distinguishes receipt-time policy from deterministic block validity. These are useful even without adopting its committee.

## Decisions and claims we disagree with

### 1. Leaving shared contracts unchanged does not satisfy R1

**Proven source discrepancy:** the peer [freezes shared implementations as well as addresses](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/bridge-migration.html#L63-L75). R2 freezes addresses, while allowing compatible upgrades. Existing [Bridge pause authorization](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/961bbd8ff55a0f66f44ad04160eb51638d655b66/packages/protocol/contracts/shared/bridge/Bridge.sol#L555-L556), [SignalService pause authorization](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/961bbd8ff55a0f66f44ad04160eb51638d655b66/packages/protocol/contracts/shared/signal/SignalService.sol#L200-L201), and [owner-only ERC20 remapping](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/961bbd8ff55a0f66f44ad04160eb51638d655b66/packages/protocol/contracts/shared/vault/ERC20Vault.sol#L195-L206) remain operational privileges.

**Trace:** an authorized pauser calls Bridge.pause; ordinary send/process paths stop; arbitrary users cannot unpause. Cost is one privileged transaction. No forgeable ZK proof is needed. This is an R1 violation even if canonical block production continues. Keep the custody addresses, but enumerate and remove runtime admin powers through compatible upgrades. Our migration already takes that approach. It still requires an actual inherited-selector/live-layout audit before launch.

### 2. A timeout cannot silently erase forced requests in an unproven legacy tail

**Open correctness gap:** peer [M2 abandons the tail after24 hours while M4 promises forced obligations survive](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/bridge-migration.html#L89-L94). The legacy Inbox already [pays forced fees and advances the queue at proposal time](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/961bbd8ff55a0f66f44ad04160eb51638d655b66/packages/protocol/contracts/layer1/core/impl/Inbox.sol#L704-L719), before proof.

**Trace:** an eligible old proposer consumes Alice's paid request into a proposal; that tail remains unproven; the migration timeout discards it. The request is behind the retained queue head, its fee was paid, and Alice's L2 action never finalized and disappears from the surviving history. Preserving the pending queue alone does not recover it. The participant may gain the consumed fee or censorship; no new migration attack bond is specified. Require complete proof-backed draining, as ours does, or explicitly reconstruct/requeue those obligations and fund their processing without double-paying. Our choice can delay activation when historic proof capability is unavailable; that is a real cost.

### 3. The proposed public Frame gate is not yet a successful-landing lock

**Proven interface discrepancy:** [the gate pins68-byte calldata](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/frame-transactions.html#L44-L52), inherited from the PDF's fixed-word `proposeFor`, but the specified `landFor(address, LandInput)` contains dynamic proof/segment data. The fixed gate rejects that R7 payload. Generalize and fully bind the encoding; changing just the selector is insufficient.

**Proven counterexample under the researched EIP semantics:** after payment approval, a stale-parent or invalid-proof SENDER frame can revert while the shared account nonce remains consumed. The attacker pays for that included failure; honest competitors at the old nonce become invalid although nothing landed. The peer's research recognizes nonce burning, but its design says a stale public-shape landing becomes invalid. A terminal VERIFY protects successful progress under the draft's consensus rules but violates the specified public propagation policy. A public prefix solution needs a demonstrated complete prevalidation and resource argument. Our general ordinary public fallback still permits gas-paying losers; we cannot claim a solved public, cost-free race either.

### 4. Planned handoff gaps do not meet continuous one-second issuance

**Proven discrepancy in the illustrated schedule:** the peer's [handoff](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/preconf.html#L108-L110) runs from a last block at termEnd−1 to the next leader's first at termEnd+3. Its two-second TERM_END grace makes this a planned multi-second gap with all parties healthy, not merely an outage exception. R4 needs a safely pipelined handoff or an explicit cadence qualification. Backdating block timestamps does not shorten issuance intervals. Our open branch model avoids mandatory term handoffs, but its one-second execution target remains unmeasured.

### 5. Preserve proof diversity through outages; expose the actual committee risk

**Acknowledged design tradeoff:** [after two hours the peer permits one ZK leaf plus a certificate](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/landing.html#L100-L101). A single broken backend plus a colluding certifying quorum can then authorize a false root; elapsed time says nothing about soundness. This is disclosed, not a newly discovered exploit inside an honest-quorum assumption. We retain two proofs and accept the corresponding liveness dependence. Also distinguish a permanently unavailable proof implementation from operators going offline: the former can require an upgrade in either design.

**Acknowledged availability tradeoff:** the peer [permits a quorum-private certificate to stall progress without slashing until replacement](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/limitations.html#L21). Objective publication covenants can verify exact byte preimages and deadlines without proving their EVM execution. They have funding/censorship false-default risks, which ours documents, but those risks do not imply that every byte-publication obligation is impossible.

**Acknowledged confirmation tradeoff:** [redraw and FALLBACK exceptions](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/blob/32e78bb2159e0bfb002e65b006a9b50945e0d736/packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/limitations.html#L20-L34) can revert locked blocks with fewer or zero slashable keys. The within-one-committee bond-at-risk figure is not an unconditional reversion-cost floor or user compensation. It should travel with those exceptions wherever applications see the confirmation label. Our soft service has an unconditional reversion-cost floor of zero and is deliberately weaker; the new client assessment says so explicitly.

## What we did not import, and our own costs

**Proposed decision:** do not import a canonical committee, exclusive term lease, timeout-based proof downgrade, shared-nonce public-progress guarantee, or fiat abandonment of legacy obligations. None is necessary to adopt the useful communication, specification and accounting ideas above.

**Proven limitations of our rules:** a mature alternative can replace a perfectly honest soft branch without slashing an unrelated owner. Public staging adds at least360 seconds before eligibility and requires data twice; bonded fragment publication adds further calldata expense. Every early canonical winner pays rent, including honest small operators. A60-second acceptance cadence sinks about67.2 ETH/day at the proposed parameters, before proving and DA. Bounded forced inclusion does not protect arbitrarily large bridge transactions. Retained checkpoint pins and public history impose long-term storage costs. These are not resolved by pointing out defects in the competing design.

**Open:** whether users prefer stronger committee-conditional soft ordering over simpler permissionless settlement is a product/security decision. Actual proof, network, gas, state-growth and funding measurements remain launch gates. The peer's quantified cartel analysis is stronger than ours; our identity-independent rent theorem is narrower and conditional on a funded competitor. Neither establishes enduring decentralization.

## Reciprocal review and our response

The [peer author’s review of our snapshot8811f85](https://github.com/taikoxyz/taiko-mono/pull/22188#issuecomment-5926119441) arrived during this comparison. It says the peer is adopting our current-root guard, durable pin/reveal path, strict parser bounds and updated100% coinbase allocation. Those are the peer’s stated intentions; they are not assumed present in the reviewed peer commit.

**Accepted criticisms:** the one-blob body cap implies very low throughput at long landing intervals; our rent charges honest winners; full fragment calldata has a large recurring cost; four-item forced processing gives long delays behind a paid backlog; recent L1 origins expose soft blocks to shallow reorgs; hardware/order-flow advantages and losing proving work remain concentration risks. These are material operating limits. The [new stress section](../design/assurance.html#operating-profile) makes the numerical implications visible, and the reference’s opening status now states that production feasibility is unresolved. We did not increase caps or slash fees without redoing their resource/security arguments.

**Necessary qualifications:** one blob is a per-segment limit, not a universal bytes-per-second cap: earlier sealing and more acceptances increase throughput at more cost. Fifteen-minute submission is not universally economically optimal; it depends on actual willingness to pay for earlier settlement, which has not been demonstrated. A candidate reorg is free of a general rollback slash, not free of proving, DA, gas and applicable rent. Local execution validity and a precisely bounded publication duty are real guarantees; they are not a shared soft fork-choice rule, canonical ordering assurance or compensation. If that stronger meaning of preconfirmation is required, our current design does not supply it and its R4 cadence claim must not be used to imply otherwise.

**Origin policy:** no minimum age was required in our revision. An older origin is allowed, but a48-second age consumes48 of the60-second block-lead allowance, leaving12 seconds for production. The new assessment exposes the selected origin and this tradeoff. A mandatory maturity requirement would need an explicit coordinated revision; no finite delay eliminates all L1 reorg risk.

**Status:** the previous loop met its stated specification stopping criterion under named assumptions. The reciprocal review is not dismissed because it uses accepted-risk categories. It raises a serious deployment-feasibility challenge that remains open. No claim is made that these document/client additions solve throughput, market sustainability, broad forced exits or soft ordering.
