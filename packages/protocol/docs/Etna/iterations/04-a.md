## Concrete attack traces

### A1. Forge a withdrawal checkpoint from a soft or staged branch — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker controls every publisher, custodian and stager; victims hold escrow in the existing Bridge/Vaults. No broken Ethereum, cryptography, execution relation or malicious DAO upgrade is assumed.

1. Build or sign a branch assigning the attacker a signal authorizing a large withdrawal.
2. Collect custody signatures or stage a descriptor claiming the desired terminal root.
3. Attempt to write that root through SignalService, or submit a segment whose proof does not authenticate it.
4. Attempt to process the withdrawal against the forged root.

**Gate/defense:** staging performs no checkpoint call (`staging.html:120`). Only Inbox/Anchor provenance writes checkpoints; the complete accepted execution relation authenticates the state root and the anchor's exact origin (`index.html:114–116,121–151`; `migration.html:75–81,127–129`). Old checkpoints cannot be overwritten (`migration.html:80`). A soft signature has no root authority.

**Cost/gain:** optional signatures/collateral plus staging and failed-call gas; custody gain **0** under the stated premises. **Severity/exploitability:** a successful unspecified implementation omission would be Critical, but this trace is **not exploitable in the specified mechanism**. Minimal design fix: none identified; preserve these checks in both programs and the custody migration.

### A2. Copy a proof and redirect the beneficiary or consume a sponsor bounty — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker sees a valid proof-bearing submission or an accepted worker's staged task.

1. Copy the full data, proof and statement.
2. Replace beneficiary with the attacker, or replace `jobId`/`stageId` with a more valuable funded offer.
3. Relay the modified statement before the honest transaction.
4. Try to collect the original worker's bounty or forced-fee credit.

**Gate/defense:** the statement digest binds producer, stage, beneficiary, task, job and reward fields; the producer separately signs the exact statement (`index.html:115,133–151`; `staging.html:128–156`). A copied unmodified proof preserves the beneficiary. A newly generated proof/authorization for the same execution with other payment fields is permitted, but a different first child cancels the original job and refunds its sponsor instead of paying the attacker (`roles.html:97–101`; `accountability.html:39–41`). Initial reward authorization is exactly empty, without an arbitrary payout callback (`index.html:117`).

**Cost/gain:** copying costs gas; rebinding requires new proofs and self-authorization. A copied honest reward yields attacker gain **0**. The attacker may earn only its own proof-bound, funded canonical processing rewards. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted High reward theft **defeated**; ordinary competitive job cancellation is permitted behavior, not theft. Minimal fix: none identified.

### A3. Name a victim as producer and slash its collateral — defeated

**Preconditions:** Alice has live publisher claim X; attacker can prove a different branch Y and knows X's signature.

1. Construct Y with Alice as `producer` or beneficiary.
2. Try to accept Y without Alice authorizing its exact statement.
3. Supply X and a valid Y block-membership path to canonical-conflict evidence.
4. Withdraw the anticipated report credit.

**Gate/defense:** naming a beneficiary does not attribute assent. Mandatory producer authorization recovers Alice or an active immutable key owned by Alice, and the successful authorization identity is retained historically (`staging.html:124,154–157`). Conflict evidence retrieves the immutable accepted receipt and requires producer=collateral owner, revision/immediate-parent/height equality, different hashes, publisher kind, open evidence window and a real six-level leaf (`accountability.html:21–29`; `staging.html:160–181`). A victim's absent signature fails before acceptance and before slashing.

**Cost/gain:** prepared proofs plus rejected-call gas; victim slash/report gain **0** without key compromise. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted High collateral theft **defeated**. Minimal fix: none identified; do not infer producer identity from fee recipients or re-evaluate mutable ERC-1271 policies.

### A4. Multiply a fragment publication deposit or drain a reserve repeatedly — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker controls reporter/responder accounts and has signed claims against one or multiple positions for the same digest.

1. Open duplicate challenges or many distinct same-digest position challenges.
2. Publish the bytes once and attempt to collect every deposited Q.
3. Report equivocation and availability default against the same position in alternate order.
4. Repeat evidence or release the same reserve, then withdraw all credits.

**Gate/defense:** a position admits at most one lifetime challenge. First global revelation earns only the selected pending challenge's Q; other timely-satisfied challenges refund Q individually (`accountability.html:14,16–19`; `roles.html:120–121`). Each position has only one 10-TAIKO reserve across all offenses. A prior slash cancels a pending challenge and refunds untouched Q; an already settled ETH outcome remains settled (`accountability.html:19,31–37,43,59–72`). State-first guarded pull accounting keeps failed delivery claimable without duplicating credits (`roles.html:118–121`).

**Cost/gain:** attacker supplies Q for its own challenges and pays gas; self-publication merely returns its own deposit through responder credit. Self-slash loses 9 TAIKO per position after the 1-TAIKO report credit. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted High escrow drain **defeated**. Minimal fix: none identified; per-escrow asset conservation must be enforced, not netted across contracts.

### A5. Cure a worker default with late canonical progress or revision retirement — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker accepts a feasible job and reserves 1,000 TAIKO, but deliberately does not complete by deadline D; parent remains unconsumed and revision live through D.

1. Wait until D passes without applicable success or cancellation.
2. Consume the parent at D+1, or arrange a later honest revision retirement.
3. Ask settlement to inspect only the current head/revision and cancel rather than default.
4. Recover the worker bond.

**Gate/defense:** immutable `parentConsumption` and dated first `revisionRetirement` records decide the historical outcome. Late exact completion, late competing consumption and late retirement cannot erase the default (`index.html:62`; `roles.html:99–101`; `accountability.html:38–41`). Exact completion before D pays the worker; different timely consumption or retirement cancels; otherwise default is once-only slash/refund.

**Cost/gain:** forfeiture of 1,000 TAIKO, with at most 100 TAIKO recovered through self-report and 900 TAIKO irreversibly sunk, plus gas/proving costs. Sponsor bounty is refunded. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted Medium/High bond evasion **defeated**. Minimal fix: none identified; historical receipt retention is mandatory.

### A6. Omit forced bytes or alter the public codec while preserving the proof task — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker is a producer/prover; parent has an eligible forced prefix, including a malformed or state-invalid request.

1. Supply only the queue hash and a private witness for a rejected request, omitting its raw bytes from DA.
2. Move a valid forced transaction behind a discretionary nonce/balance-changing transaction and classify it as state-invalid.
3. Alternatively change coinbase, origin, anchor, header outputs, fragment splits or manifest order after staging.
4. Present a proof for the attacker-selected weakened interpretation and withdraw rewards or use its checkpoint.

**Gate/defense:** Inbox constructs the authenticated required-record hash itself (`index.html:126–150`). `forced[]` includes all raw inputs, hashes to exact queue metadata and binds explicit outcomes. Executed requests follow the successful first anchor immediately in the first block before discretion (`codec.html:128–132`). Body RLP, exact signed envelopes, fixed execution profile, all 21 header fields, gas/fees, reserved system identity, public activation witnesses, FR and MR are normative (`codec.html:7–16,21–44,47–123,135–171`). Changing any execution input changes data/context; current-segment payment/stage/task hashes cannot cycle back into execution (`staging.html:59–60`; `codec.html:125,171`). Both programs must enforce the same relation, not choose client-specific acceptance (`index.html:121,137–151`).

**Cost/gain:** proofs/staging/failed-call costs; theft or fraudulent checkpoint gain **0** under faithful relation enforcement. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted Critical proof-relation bypass **defeated at design level**. Minimal fix: none identified. Component hashes passing below do not establish implementation or proof-backend soundness.

### A7. Recover all force spam fees, or use free donations to block exits — defeated

**Preconditions:** attacker can fund arbitrary enqueue addresses and control processing beneficiaries, reporter identities or unsolicited token transfers.

1. Fill 1,000 pending force requests from an empty queue.
2. Prove/process its own requests, attempting to recover all enqueue costs.
3. Alternatively donate token/ETH dust and try to make an equality-to-assets assertion block unrelated withdrawals.
4. Attempt to withdraw the sink or donated surplus through an owner/reporter selector.

**Gate/defense:** enqueue price grows with pending count; processing transfers exactly half to the proof-bound beneficiary and half to a permanently nonwithdrawable sink, once per request (`index.html:171`; `roles.html:117–118,218–219`). Solvency is liabilities≤held assets, not equality, separately per escrow; donations create no claim and no owner sweep (`roles.html:118`; `accountability.html:43`).

**Cost/gain:** 1,000 unconsumed enqueues cost 10.5 ETH, of which at least 5.25 ETH cannot be recovered even by a self-processing cartel, plus gas/proof/DA costs. Interleaving below pending=50 costs at least 1 ETH/1,000 with 0.5 ETH sunk; the larger number must not be applied to that strategy. Donations cost the donor and create attacker withdrawal gain **0**. **Severity/exploitability:** attempted High fee/escrow drain **defeated**. Minimal fix: none identified; external MEV can offset attack expenditure, so this is not a proof of universal spam unprofitability.

### A8. Sybil or pipeline canonical conflicts avoid a service slash — real scoped residual, not custody theft

**Preconditions:** a cartel owns publisher account A and producer account B, or signs a descendant with an earlier accepted bucket base. Users voluntarily rely on provisional service X.

1. A signs valid X with live fragment reserves.
2. Stage a valid conflicting Y through B, or a later segment with a different immediate parent, without a breached sealed covenant.
3. Wait the mandatory public-age interval, then let Y win the L1 race.
4. Satisfy any fragment serving challenge and attempt to avoid owner-conflict slashing.

**Gate/scope:** the owner/immediate-parent predicate intentionally fails in these cases (`staging.html:180–181`; `accountability.html:29,34,163`; `roles.html:84,219–222`). The stage-age gate still requires actual complete Y data 360 seconds before acceptance, and custody roots still need a complete valid proof. X's soft state rolls back; that is permitted provisional behavior. No global economic-owner detector or ancestry walk is specified.

**Cost/gain:** additional addresses are cheap; refundable duty capital incurs opportunity cost, Y incurs actual staging/landing/proving expenses. The avoided conflict slash can be 10 TAIKO per affected position; possible trading/ordering gain is external and not bounded by custody escrow. **Severity/exploitability:** **informational residual economic limitation**, publicly reproducible in the specified scope; not a new proof/custody defect or an assertion that R6 passes. A mandatory economically effective cross-owner detector is not a proposed minimal fix: it would need a separately justified identity/resource assumption. Judge the stated anti-exclusivity, proportional liabilities, actual-data lead and irreversible fee friction against the intended R6 scope.
