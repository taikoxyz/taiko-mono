## Findings

**No confirmed Critical, High, Medium or Low design vulnerability was found in this liveness pass.** Consequently there is no R05-B-01 finding to assign a severity to. This is not a convergence declaration, an implementation-readiness verdict, or evidence that the measured launch gates pass. In particular, no result below treats an unavailable circuit, impossible gas envelope or absent migration witness as acceptable at launch.

The important distinction is that accepted-chain progress is conditional, while successful soft-branch inclusion and continuous profitable service are not promised. The current design contains a concrete public replacement path, immutable bounded forced work, a fresh-context route after rent decays, and explicit fail-closed launch conditions. The attempted counterexamples below did not find an omitted mechanism necessary to those scoped claims.

## Concrete attempts and current defenses

All paths/lines below are relative to `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/` and refer to the immutable snapshot. Each link supplies the relevant HTML anchor.

### B-A1 — lock the chain by disappearing with all incumbent services

**Preconditions:** Every current publisher, custodian, optional worker and private frame endpoint coordinates; an independently funded prover still satisfies A-ARCHIVE/A-PROVE/A-INCLUDE.

1. Stop delivering an incumbent soft branch and stop assigned proof jobs.
2. Refuse stage or producer signatures for that branch; accept outstanding jobs before going offline.
3. Attempt to make a newcomer wait for incumbent approval, job expiry, a service bucket, or DAO appointment.

**Attacker cost/gain:** Existing accepted duties may lose 10 TAIKO per position and 1,000 TAIKO per applicable job; no bond is needed simply to stop an unbonded service. The attacker can destroy its own soft service and waste clients' provisional work, but obtains no canonical lock.

**Result:** Rejected as an R1/R3/R7 falsification. The entrant may construct a different child from accepted state, self-authorize as producer, stage complete data and submit through an ordinary public transaction. Old stages and jobs confer no reservation. No claim that a missing *global* prover or archive can be replaced mathematically is made.

**Evidence/defenses:** `staging.html:230–232`, [recovery](../design/staging.html#recovery); `roles.html:54–62`, [role matrix](../design/roles.html#role-matrix); `arguments.html:78–79`, [L1](../design/arguments.html#l1). Failure of all capable provers or all archival state sources is an explicit environmental failure, not an undisclosed DAO gate.

### B-A2 — wait until rent is zero so every proof is expired

**Preconditions:** Current head is unchanged for at least 900 seconds; attacker wants rent's decay horizon and origin expiry to coincide permanently.

1. Let origin/stage O/S of a pending honest candidate expire.
2. Point out that the rent is finally zero only after this candidate becomes unusable.
3. Try to force recovery to alternate forever between unaffordable fresh proofs and expired free proofs.

**Attacker cost/gain:** No intrinsic protocol expenditure is needed to wait; the hoped-for gain is a permanent admission deadlock. A separate competitor that actually advances the chain pays the current rent and proof/DA costs.

**Result:** Rejected as an R1/R6/R7 falsification. The rent clock remains old while a *new* origin, body, stage and proof are constructed. For example, reference time 0, origin 1008, stage 1128, maturity 1488 and acceptance 1608 give zero rent, origin age 600 and stage age 480. An adversary can reset rent only by actually accepting a successor, which is progress and also consumes mandatory FIFO work once due. It cannot reset the clock with a stage, failed action or quote.

**Evidence/defenses:** `roles.html:266–280`, [rent](../design/roles.html#rent); `index.html:159–161`, [rent](../design/index.html#rent); `arguments.html:120–121`, [L4](../design/arguments.html#l4). This longer recovery schedule must not be advertised as the positive-rent 660-second scenario.

### B-A3 — make every proof stale with new force requests or stale snapshots

**Preconditions:** Attacker can enqueue and can win some or all canonical races with valid minimal segments; victim request has finitely many predecessors.

1. Append a request immediately before an honest proof lands.
2. Repeatedly choose a queue snapshot before the victim's maturity.
3. Win with empty-discretionary segments, reuse an old origin, or introduce new spam to keep the victim behind the queue.

**Attacker cost/gain:** Enqueue fees plus L1 costs; winning segments also require complete stage/republication, both proofs and current rent. Filling 1,000 slots from empty without consumption costs 10.5 ETH in force fees, of which 5.25 ETH cannot be recovered even by controlling processors. Interleaving can reduce price, but must consume work.

**Result:** Rejected as an R1/R6/R7 falsification. A child's required interval comes from its parent's immutable snapshot, so later enqueues do not move an in-flight target. The unique cut is checked against actual enqueue block numbers. A continuing chain must eventually select origins beyond the victim's maturity because of the 900-second age bound. Thereafter each accepted child removes the next up-to-four due predecessors; later arrivals cannot get ahead. Permanent halt without anyone meeting proving/funding/inclusion premises remains outside the conditional theorem. Large *earlier* paid backlog causes finite, potentially long delay; no uniform short FIFO SLA is asserted.

**Evidence/defenses:** `index.html:181–186`, [forced inclusion](../design/index.html#forced); `arguments.html:85–95`, [L2](../design/arguments.html#l2); `roles.html:285–287`, [competition](../design/roles.html#competition). The claimed conditional bound correctly names accepted-segment spacing Δ, not Ethereum slot spacing.

### B-A4 — combine bootstrap activation with a full forced prefix

**Preconditions:** Attacker fills the new queue during DRAINING with maximum-size entries, hoping the first beginEtna witness plus mandatory forced bytes exceeds the calldata fallback cap.

1. Queue at least four mature 2,048-byte requests before activation.
2. Require the first Etna segment to carry the maximum activation witness.
3. Attempt to remove both blob-free activation and subsequent forced progress, or replay empty-cut bootstrap indefinitely.

**Attacker cost/gain:** Ordinary escalating queue fees and gas; intended gain is a launch deadlock or permanent request deferral.

**Result:** Rejected as an R1/R7 falsification. Exact H0 has a zero force cut and cursor; the first successor must carry an empty forced array while preserving queued records and escrow. Its one-anchor body has the stated conservative 17,724-byte bound. That successor must take the actual post-activation origin snapshot, and segment two resumes ordinary FIFO. The sentinel exception is restricted to the exact stored initial head and is consumed once. The real activation witness's execution cost is **still an open measured hard gate**; the byte proof does not certify the 1,000,000-gas limit.

**Evidence/defenses:** `migration.html:290–330`, [bootstrap](../design/migration.html#bootstrap); `codec.html:123–124`, [anchor](../design/codec.html#anchor). An actual over-budget launch witness would block this revision; no waiver was assumed.

### B-A5 — keep data private, then exploit a later paid challenge

**Preconditions:** Attacker controls every holder of a signed version-2 fragment and has its live 10-TAIKO reserve; honest client lacks complete bytes.

1. Sign at issuedAt=1010 with publishBy=1250.
2. Withhold until a challenge opens at 1249.
3. Attempt to reveal at 1848, collect Q and avoid the original duty; alternatively publish only a blob stage or a bare digest.

**Attacker cost/gain:** Capital reserve and gas; late default loses 10 TAIKO, or net 9 TAIKO if self-reporting. The attacker can delay this provisional branch and may value that delay above the penalty. It cannot receive the proposed late Q payout or make unavailable data locally valid.

**Result:** Rejected as an R3/R6/R7 falsification. Challenge deadline is min(openedAt+600,publishBy)=1250. Direct default needs no challenge. Actual matching fragment calldata plus exact ordered FR preimage is required; the immutable first receipt distinguishes timely and late publication. One publication pays at most one selected Q. A holder may remain silent before the agreed deadline without breaching that deadline; no global gossip-time guarantee is stated. Canonical acceptance independently requires 360 seconds of actual complete staging.

**Evidence/defenses:** `accountability.html:17–37`, [fragment publication](../design/accountability.html#fragment-publication) and [challenge](../design/accountability.html#challenge); `staging.html:118–120`, [acceptance](../design/staging.html#acceptance). Ten TAIKO is not unlimited service insurance; its calibration remains open.

### B-A6 — squat all jobs, buckets or signatures; erase an earlier default

**Preconditions:** Attacker has enough TAIKO to accept jobs or open many buckets and can later cause canonical parent consumption.

1. Accept every advertised job and stop working; buy many buckets or rotate owner/key addresses.
2. Ask that other workers wait for assignment expiry.
3. After a job deadline, consume the old parent and claim that the original task was merely superseded.

**Attacker cost/gain:** 1,000 TAIKO locked per accepted job; 600 per bucket. Default self-reporting still loses 900 TAIKO per job. The attacker may disrupt the optional sponsorship market but cannot buy a canonical veto or erase a missed deadline.

**Result:** Rejected as an R1/R3/R6 falsification. No global ticket capacity exists. Exact historical parentConsumption and revisionRetirement timestamps decide whether cancellation was timely; late parent consumption does not erase default. Existing positions retain immutable verification keys and release deadlines. No promise of Sybil-resistant market share is inferred from those reserves.

**Evidence/defenses:** `roles.html:75–90`, [registry](../design/roles.html#registry); `roles.html:98–108`, [jobs](../design/roles.html#jobs); `accountability.html:56–60`, [jobs](../design/accountability.html#jobs).

### B-A7 — congest L1 or exploit shorter slots until publication/proof windows close

**Preconditions:** Attacker can compete for L1 resources or influence builders; the attacked bound is the stated 120-second inclusion model.

1. Delay stage or final inclusion, then make the origin expire at 900 seconds.
2. Delay a fragment beyond its 240-second duty and induce honest default.
3. Deny the private frame endpoint, or let an unpinned origin leave BLOCKHASH history at 2-second slots.

**Attacker cost/gain:** Ethereum resource purchase, censorship opportunity cost or builder compensation; these cannot be priced exactly from this design alone. Successful congestion can waste proving/staging work, slash an objectively late honest duty and interrupt soft service.

**Result:** Not a valid falsification *within* the named bounded inclusion/publication premises. These adverse outcomes are explicitly admitted when those premises fail. At 12/6/4/2-second steady intervals, 256-block lookup spans 3072/1536/1024/512 seconds; the shortest still permits a prompt pin within the assumed 120-second inclusion window. This is a feasibility calculation, not a real availability guarantee. A funded public ordinary acceptance path remains when private frame transport is unavailable. Pinning a fresh origin and rebuilding is available after an old candidate expires.

**Evidence/defenses:** `index.html:68–70`, [origin](../design/index.html#origin); `index.html:175–177`, [frames](../design/index.html#frames); `arguments.html:20–31`, [assumptions](../design/arguments.html#assumptions); `accountability.html:203–204`, [limits](../design/accountability.html#limits). Launch measurements must demonstrate capacity for the actual workload; claiming that a fixed-size action can always buy inclusion under arbitrary congestion would be incorrect, and is not the current claim.

### B-A8 — DAO inactivity strands old custody or migration forever

**Preconditions:** Either the installation bundle is complete and valid, or mandatory legacy DA/proving/state evidence is absent. The attacker attempts to conflate these cases.

1. Freeze governance after the committed installation and turn off all old service operators.
2. Attempt to require an owner to select a final root, release mature bond exits, resume a paused shared contract or approve activation.
3. Alternatively withhold a historical blob required by finite drain and demand a timeout that skips it.

**Attacker cost/gain:** No additional expenditure to stop an already controlled operator; historic withholding may prevent migration. Intended gain is an operational governance veto or unsafe omission of a legacy obligation.

**Result:** Rejected as a hidden post-install R1/R2/R7 gate. Public bounded drain/activation and owner-authorized ordinary asset exits are specified; pause, resolver, quota and ownership-acceptance routes are disabled or detached. Missing necessary historical DA/proving is a real **launch blocker**, explicitly not a permission to activate. Governance's initial upgrade authorization is the allowed R1 exception, not an ongoing duty. A later incompatible L1 hard fork is not promised to work forever without software changes.

**Evidence/defenses:** `migration.html:196–210`, [authority](../design/migration.html#authority); `migration.html:227–245`, [migration](../design/migration.html#migration); `migration.html:407–446`, [drain adapter](../design/migration.html#drain-adapter). The actual deployment/layout/legacy-proof manifest is unverified; this pass establishes no production migration success.

### B-A9 — dominate ordinary canonical ordering by paying rent or using Sybils

**Preconditions:** Attacker has faster proving, valuable private order flow or external ordering benefits; alternatively no honest entrant is willing to pay positive rent.

1. Repeatedly win with minimal valid staged branches, invalidating an honest publisher's richer soft branch.
2. Rotate producers/beneficiaries to avoid owner-linked conflict liability.
3. Pay each early rent when profitable, or wait 900 seconds in the absence of a funded competing entrant.

**Attacker cost/gain:** At D=660, each capture costs at least 0.013333333333333334 ETH in irreversible rent plus ordinary costs, *if* A-COMPETITION(D) holds. With no competing early entrant, zero-rent capture is possible. Sufficient MEV/external value can exceed all costs.

**Result:** Rejected as a falsification of the precisely bounded R6 mechanism; it defeats a stronger fair-market-share guarantee that the current design does not make. Every owner pays the same price; copying a beneficiary or using Sybils recovers no sink. The inequality G<Σq is mathematically valid but is conditional economic deterrence, not demonstrated real-market profitability. Ordinary soft fairness is absent; fixed forced items retain B-A3's progress guarantee.

**Evidence/defenses:** `roles.html:275–289`, [competition](../design/roles.html#competition); `arguments.html:118–121`, [L4](../design/arguments.html#l4). R6 acceptance should preserve the explicitly named competitor/value model and must not advertise it as unconditional decentralization.

## Limits and disposition

No unmet normative liveness mechanism was demonstrated in this pass. The open measured launch gates remain material: maximum-load execution/proving/publication capacity; both required proof backends; activation MPT byte/gas fit; archive and legacy proving availability; deployment compatibility; and the real economic ability to fund early competition and bonded fragment calldata. If any fails, the relevant advertised workload or this revision cannot launch unchanged. They were not converted into assumptions that waive the hard gate.

No new Critical/High was found. Convergence is **not** claimed.
