## 2. Independent verdict

## 4. Rejected attempted attacks

### A. Repeatedly append forced transactions to invalidate every in-flight proof

1. An honest prover starts a child of H.
2. An attacker enqueues another request just before landing, attempting to increase required work.
3. The child still derives its interval from H's frozen cut/time; the new tail cannot alter it. The child's own origin fixes a snapshot for the following child.

Rejected by `index.html:183–186` ([forced](../design/index.html#forced)) and `codec.html:128–132` ([forced](../design/codec.html#forced)). The attacker pays enqueue fees and gas but obtains no proof-target mutation. This does not assert a low universal queue delay.

### B. Keep landing cheap segments with an old snapshot to starve one fixed request

1. The attacker stages short valid children that reuse an old origin and omit the victim from their snapshots.
2. It repeatedly consumes the current head, trying to keep the request forever ineligible.
3. Eventually the origin exceeds the 900-second age cap. Fresh accepted origins must include the older queue entry; after maturity the mandatory prefix removes up to four predecessors per successor.

Rejected under continued canonical progress by `index.html:70,183–186` ([origin](../design/index.html#origin), [forced](../design/index.html#forced)) and `arguments.html:84–95` ([l2](../design/arguments.html#l2)). Later enqueues cannot increase the victim's predecessor count. An attacker may buy a large earlier backlog, and arbitrary accepted-head spacing yields no short wall-clock guarantee; those limits are explicit. Four declared one-million-gas requests plus the one-million-gas anchor fit the ten-million first-block limit (`codec.html:129`, [forced](../design/codec.html#forced)); proving affordability is still unmeasured.

### C. Squat every job or stage, withhold proofs, and make the DAO appoint a replacement

1. An attacker opens many stages and accepts all visible funded jobs.
2. It withholds proofs or supplies impossible descriptors.
3. A funded outsider constructs its own child, stages it and self-authorizes its statement. Neither the existing stager nor assigned worker has an exclusive right.

Rejected by `staging.html:89–95,229–232` ([registry](../design/staging.html#registry), [recovery](../design/staging.html#recovery)) and `roles.html:98–108,237` ([jobs](../design/roles.html#jobs), [competition](../design/roles.html#competition)). Spam costs L1 resources; abandoned applicable jobs risk their independent 1,000-TAIKO reserves. It can waste resources or remove subsidies, but cannot consume a finite global admission capacity. If no willing capable prover remains anywhere, progress halts as A-PROVE/A-FUND explicitly allow.

### D. Combine zero-rent waiting with proof expiry to lock recovery forever

1. Incumbents disappear immediately after H accepts.
2. An entrant waits until rent becomes zero at age 900; its old origin or stage expires.
3. The entrant takes a fresh origin and constructs/stages/proves another child of the still-current H. Rent remains zero throughout this new work.

Rejected by `index.html:159–161` ([rent](../design/index.html#rent)), `staging.html:113,230` ([lifecycle](../design/staging.html#lifecycle), [recovery](../design/staging.html#recovery)), and `roles.html:280` ([rent](../design/roles.html#rent)). For example, a fresh origin at 1900, stage at 2080, maturity at 2440 and landing by 2560 leaves origin age 660, below 900. If another valid child consumes H first, canonical progress has occurred. This argument needs a fresh feasible bounded schedule, not merely eventual inclusion after arbitrarily long delays.

### E. Reset rent with spam, or reclaim it through self-paid L2 fees

1. An attacker tries to reset the clock with a stage, job, failed submission or donation.
2. Alternatively it rapidly accepts self-beneficiary segments and attempts to recover rent as coinbase income.
3. Only successful canonical acceptance resets the clock. Rent enters a separate L1 nonwithdrawable ETH sink; L2 coinbase transfers cannot withdraw it.

Rejected by `roles.html:244–268,276–282` ([rent](../design/roles.html#rent)) and `index.html:119,159–160` ([landing](../design/index.html#landing), [rent](../design/index.html#rent)). Immediate additional children cost 0.05 ETH each and still require actual proofs, data and forced work. Self-paid L2 fees are internal transfers under the 100% coinbase rule; they must not be added to irreversible attack cost. The claimed positive capture-cost bound requires A-COMPETITION(D), and profitable domination above that bound remains possible.

### F. Reveal late, erase default, withdraw collateral, or multiply challenge rewards

1. A bonded signer hides a fragment until its absolute deadline passes.
2. It then publishes, rotates its key, or arranges several matching challenges to recover penalties or collect multiple Q payouts.
3. Immutable first-publication times preserve the miss; historic keys remain verifiable; each position is slashed at most once. One first publication earns at most one selected Q, with other timely satisfied challenges individually refunded.

Rejected by `accountability.html:17–37,61` ([fragment-publication](../design/accountability.html#fragment-publication), [challenge](../design/accountability.html#challenge), [accounting](../design/accountability.html#accounting)) and `roles.html:77–89` ([registry](../design/roles.html#registry)). At the exact deadline publication is timely; default requires strictly later time. Release occurs strictly after evidence close and first settles admitted expired challenges. Hidden signatures first disclosed after the evidence horizon can escape punishment, but that is an explicit finite-duty limit, not erased admitted evidence. Censorship can cause an honest service default when the inclusion premise fails.

### G. Cancel a worker's already missed deadline by landing a different child later

1. A worker defaults while its task parent remains current.
2. It subsequently lands a different child, then asks settlement to treat the old job as superseded.
3. The immutable parent-consumption timestamp is later than the deadline, so the prior default remains.

Rejected by `roles.html:99–106` ([jobs](../design/roles.html#jobs)) and `accountability.html:56–60` ([jobs](../design/accountability.html#jobs)). Timely competing consumption cancels innocently; late consumption or revision retirement cannot waive a historical miss. Fresh-stage job deadlines also include maturity plus 120 seconds and explicit origin/stage freshness guards. Optional jobs can become unavailable for old pipelined candidates; raw submission remains open (`staging.html:195–196`, [timing](../design/staging.html#timing)).

### H. First reveal a private winning branch, or slash an innocent losing publisher

1. An attacker attempts to disclose its winning body only with the final proof.
2. Alternatively it names an honest publisher as producer or beneficiary on a competing accepted block to slash that publisher.
3. First reveal lacks a 360-second-old actual-data receipt. Producer attribution requires exact authenticated assent; beneficiary naming is insufficient.

Rejected by `staging.html:118–121,154–157,180–181` ([acceptance](../design/staging.html#acceptance), [authorization](../design/staging.html#authorization), [block-tree](../design/staging.html#block-tree)). A mature public competing branch can still win and invalidate honest proving work. Cross-owner or different-immediate-parent conflicts can escape the scoped slash; neither case is claimed to guarantee soft finality.

### I. Shorter L1 slots destroy origin authentication or make fixed timers expire early

1. Change L1 production intervals from 12 to 6, 4 or 2 seconds.
2. Attempt to shrink a supposed slot-based timeout or prevent any origin pin before history expires.
3. Timers remain seconds, and HeaderStore pins an available historical execution header. At the illustrated intervals the 256-block lookup spans 3072, 1536, 1024 or 512 seconds, respectively, all exceeding the stated 120-second inclusion budget for prompt pinning.

Rejected for these requested intervals under prompt funded header acquisition/pinning by `index.html:68–70,215–216` ([origin](../design/index.html#origin), [dependencies](../design/index.html#dependencies)) and `codec.html:75–77,108` ([l1-header](../design/codec.html#l1-header), [anchor](../design/codec.html#anchor)). This arithmetic is a compatibility check, not a slots-to-seconds consensus rule or a guarantee about arbitrary future forks. No proposer lookahead was found.

### J. Freeze migration, abandon forced work, or replay the empty bootstrap interval

1. After installation an attacker tries to extend legacy tails, commit many pending forced proposals, or activate before the last old obligation is proved.
2. It alternatively attempts to keep reusing H0's empty force interval to bypass requests queued during DRAINING.
3. Tails are frozen, only one forced drain proposal may be pending, activation checks READY and the exact certificate, and only the exact initial head has the sentinel exception. Consuming it installs an ordinary real-origin snapshot.

Rejected by `migration.html:234–244,307–330,407–420` ([migration](../design/migration.html#migration), [bootstrap](../design/migration.html#bootstrap), [drain-adapter](../design/migration.html#drain-adapter)). The variant decreases with each successful finite-drain transition. Old fee claims remain separate and do not block activation. Missing archived old blobs, unavailable valid legacy proofs, or a failed deployment preflight block activation safely and are named launch dependencies, not new malicious role privileges. New requests queued during a blocked drain have no unconditional service deadline (`migration.html:330`).

### K. Reuse addresses while corrupting historical Bridge state or retaining owner control

1. Attempt to preserve proxy addresses but reset message status, bump SignalService VERSION, retain a pauser/owner-mint path, or overwrite checkpoints.
2. Attempt an owner-only runtime rescue after the DAO becomes inactive.
3. The specified upgrades preserve exact mappings/namespaces and message encodings, prohibit conflicting checkpoint overwrite, disable operational selectors and fallback routes, and detach quota dependencies.

Rejected as a permitted implementation of this design by `migration.html:59–82,142–158,196–212,217–222` ([checkpoint](../design/migration.html#checkpoint), [slots](../design/migration.html#slots), [authority](../design/migration.html#authority), [history](../design/migration.html#history)). Snapshot source checks confirm the existing checkpoint ABI and VERSION=1 map domains. No authenticated live deployment or generated old/new layout was available here; the actual compatibility claim is therefore open rather than empirically proven.

A-LIVE(Δ) supplies the spacing needed for a wall-clock FIFO bound; the fixed-prefix proof alone supplies only progress in accepted successors.
