## Attempted counterexamples

### C1 — profitable repeated capture despite a willing entrant

**Medium, feasible stated residual; tests R6.** Prerequisites: an actor can produce both required proofs, fund publication/republication and L1 inclusion, and realize consolidated external value `Gnet` per captured parent. An honest entrant satisfies the specification's illustrative `D=660` competition premise. Ordered actions: (1) privately prepare a valid minimal/discretionary-censoring successor; (2) publish its complete context and data, then wait the mandatory 360 seconds; (3) land it before the honest rich branch; (4) repeat on successive parents, processing every mandatory forced prefix. Split producer, payer and beneficiary over Sybils if desired.

Cost: actual proof/DA/gas plus at least `q(660)=0.013333333333333334 ETH` sunk per win; 100 such wins sink at least `1.3333333333333334 ETH`. Gain: for the symbolic choice `Gnet=0.02 ETH/head` after all ordinary external costs but before rent, 100 wins leave approximately `0.6666666666666666 ETH` profit. Own L2 sender-to-coinbase fees are internal transfers and are excluded from that gain. Cheap/minimal proofs can waste the rich branch's proving expenditure and exclude its ordinary transactions; the mandatory FIFO is still processed.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/index.html:161–164`; `roles.html:276–279`; `codec.html:104`; `arguments.html:170–172`. No cap or rotation prevents this; the specified consequence is the inequality `ΣGnet < Σq`, not guaranteed diversity. **No failed hard requirement:** R6 asks for concrete anti-monopoly economics, which the rent supplies; an unconditional anti-monopoly claim would be falsified. Exploitability is Medium because the actor needs sustained proof/DA capital and sufficient external value, and cannot forge custody or indefinitely omit eligible forced work through this tactic.

### C2 — zero-rent monopoly and rent-induced entrant retreat

**Medium, feasible stated residual; tests R1/R6/R7.** Prerequisites: incumbents have usable archives/provers; no independent entrant is ready *and willing to pay* before head age 900. This is compatible with the existence of a capable prover or some funded user: those weaker premises do not imply A-COMPETITION. Ordered actions: (1) leave the current head unchanged; (2) choose a fresh origin and stage a fresh valid candidate sufficiently late that it remains fresh at a zero-rent landing; (3) accept at age ≥900; (4) repeat. For example, staging with origin around head age 600 permits maturity around age 960 while origin and stage remain well inside their own 900-second caps.

Cost: zero protocol rent, ordinary proof/DA/gas and any private L1 ordering payments. Gain: every canonical win and its ordinary ordering value without a positive rent lower bound. Conversely, a target 60-second accepted-head cadence charges about `0.046666666666666667 ETH/head`, or `67.2 ETH/day` before ordinary costs. A small entrant with expected revenue below its incremental cost rationally retreats to the zero-rent schedule, weakening the very willingness premise used by the capture theorem.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/roles.html:281–283`; `arguments.html:25,27,120–121`; `staging.html:113,230`. **No failed hard requirement:** R1's objective entry remains open and a fresh zero-rent path exists; R6 has a conditional price rather than a permanent monopoly exclusion. R7 does not promise profitable proving. The proposed rate's affordability and adoption are unverified economic premises. It would be incorrect to reuse `Δ=660` for entrants deliberately waiting for zero rent.

### C3 — Sybil conflicts and strategically timed publication

**Medium, feasible stated residual; tests R3/R6.** Prerequisites: one economic actor controls bonded publisher owner A and independently self-authorizing producer B; users accept explicitly revocable soft results. It possesses complete correct bytes and can fund timely fragment publication and mature stages. Ordered actions: (1) A gives selected users a valid soft branch X; (2) the actor prepares conflicting Y under B; (3) publish X's exact fragments/FR preimages by each `issuedAt+240` and publish Y's complete context for at least 360 seconds; (4) authorize and land Y under B before X; (5) users discard X's unsafe suffix. A can delay general fragment publication until near its deadline without default; publication by any informed recipient could instead accelerate it.

Cost: refundable A service reserve/opportunity cost, timely fragment calldata, Y proof/DA/gas and rent. B requires no TAIKO stake. Gain: soft rollback value, selective information advantage, or ordinary censorship without a same-owner conflict penalty. The predicate fails exactly at `receipt.producer == bucket.owner`: B is not A. A pipelined claim rooted at an earlier base also misses the immediate-parent equality even without B. This cannot cure an independently missed X publication deadline.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/staging.html:154,180–181`; `roles.html:85,88`; `accountability.html:39,47,205,209`; `codec.html:165`; `index.html:81–82`. **No failed hard requirement under the stated bounded duty:** same-owner conflict and publication defaults are objective; common economic ownership and global gossip time are explicitly outside them. Treating certificates/address counts as Sybil-resistant soft finality would fail R6, but this candidate does not specify that guarantee. Medium reflects realistic cryptographic non-attribution and bounded soft exposure, without a false-root path.

### C4 — bonded service entry and rational publication default

**Medium, feasible stated residual; tests R3/R6/R4.** Prerequisites: independent parties lack guaranteed customer payment or challenge funding, and the TAIKO/ETH exchange value is variable. Ordered actions: (1) an entrant opens a 600-TAIKO bucket; (2) maintains one bonded promise/second, or four fragment promises/second for larger records; (3) finances each exact fragment's additional calldata publication, in addition to staging/final DA; (4) exits or reduces service if customers do not cover capital and publication cost. Efficient full-bucket schedules peak at 37,200 TAIKO for one promise/second and 147,000 for four, before sealed covenants, delayed release transactions or a separate custodian.

An alternative selfish action is to promise but skip publication when no other holder will finance it, then accept a slash. Let `pT` be ETH value per TAIKO. A full 32,768-nonzero-byte fragment has a stated data-floor component of 2,097,152 gas, or `0.04194304 ETH` at 20 gwei, before overhead. A colluding first self-reporter recovers 1 TAIKO, so forfeiting costs `9pT` plus evidence gas. Skipping can be economically preferable when `9pT + evidenceGasCost < publicationCost`; using only that floor gives the indicative threshold `pT < 0.004660337777… ETH/TAIKO` before additional costs. External-first reporting instead loses all 10 TAIKO. Timely publication by a recipient would discharge the duty and block this default branch.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/roles.html:55–56,65,76,231,288–289`; `accountability.html:28,61`; `arguments.html:173–175`. **No failed hard requirement:** objective entry/exit and bounded slashing exist, and an unbonded raw producer does not inherit this entry cost. No profitable custody equilibrium or unlimited-MEV deterrence is supplied. R4's continuing healthy optional service depends on willing providers. Severity is Medium: capital excludes low-budget continuous services economically, and the bounded penalty may rationally be paid; custody validity remains independent.

### C5 — paid backlog creates long censorship delay, but cannot hold a fixed victim forever

**Medium, feasible finite-delay residual; tests R6/R7.** Prerequisites: a censor can pay L1 enqueues before a victim and wants delay worth more than its irreversible expenditure. Ordered actions: (1) from an empty queue, enqueue 1,000 bounded requests without intervening processing; (2) the victim appends at index 1,000; (3) producers process only the required four-item FIFO prefix per accepted segment, while retaining optional ordering control; (4) new attacker requests may append but cannot acquire an earlier index than that victim.

Gross entry fees for the 1,000 predecessors are `50×0.001×(1+…+20)=10.5 ETH`; even if the actor owns every processing beneficiary, at least `5.25 ETH` is sunk, plus enqueue/proof/DA/gas. The victim's quote at pending=1,000 is `0.021 ETH`. There are 251 necessary four-item prefixes through the victim. Substituting `p=1000, Δ=660` into the conservative stated bound gives `1020+(1+251)×660=167,340 seconds`, approximately 46.48 hours. This is a conservative conditional upper bound, not an exact attainable delay or a measured throughput assertion. The tactic can nonetheless manufacture substantial delay and additional pin/reveal waits. With interleaved processing below 50 pending entries, the attacker can lower gross fees to 1 ETH/1,000 requests but must actually remove FIFO work; that does not sustain the same fixed-victim predecessor backlog.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/index.html:184,186–190`; `codec.html:117–121`; `roles.html:286–288`; `arguments.html:88–96,177`. **No failed hard requirement:** R7 requires forced inclusion and the exact mandatory prefix supplies eventual proved processing under progress premises; no uniform short deadline is required or promised for arbitrary paid backlog. The no-expiry/no-owner-void and nondecreasing fresh origins block permanent starvation of a fixed eligible item during continuing canonical progress. Medium reflects expensive but economically possible delay; claiming the sink makes all censorship unprofitable would be unsupported.

### C6 — everyone declines loss-making forced work or subsidized proof jobs

**Medium, feasible stated incentive residual; tests R1/R3/R7.** Prerequisites: fees/compute/DA/rent exceed expected external receipts and no altruistic/custom-funded prover will bridge the deficit. Ordered actions: (1) queue ordinary low-priced requests; (2) the mandatory prefix grows to four expensive state-valid transactions; (3) incumbents decline to prove another successor, while outsiders also decline the negative-margin workload; (4) head advancement and the forced workflow halt until an economically willing participant returns. At minimum force fee, four requests provide only `0.002 ETH` total processor credit. That is not a protocol reimbursement formula for dual proof generation, republication, L1 gas or the contemporaneous rent. Source L2 gas credited to a chosen coinbase is separate and must be counted explicitly rather than assumed to fund the L1 beneficiary.

A related sponsor/worker trace: a worker locks 1,000 TAIKO and spends proving effort; an incumbent lands a different eligible child, possibly the same execution with a different beneficiary, by its deadline; the job cancels and returns bounty to sponsor and bond to worker. No bounty pays the worker's wasted compute. Repeated legitimate wins can discourage workers without paying job-default slashes. Incumbent costs are its actual mature winning proofs/DA/gas/rent; possible gain is market exit or external censorship value. There is no contractual exclusivity to buy, and a late consumption cannot erase an earlier worker default.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/index.html:125,129,190,213`; `roles.html:17,54,97–109`; `accountability.html:56–60`; `codec.html:104`; `arguments.html:24–25,29`. **No failed hard requirement under A-FUND/A-PROVE's willing-participant scope:** there is no admission authority or stuck job reservation. Economic willingness is a real premise, distinct from solvency and objective access. Medium because no adversarial false proof is needed, but the trace fails the stated willingness premise rather than bypassing an available, funded honest submission.

### C7 — cheap default reporting is not guaranteed to attract a reporter

**Medium, feasible stated residual; tests R6.** Prerequisites: a genuine live default exists, but neither customer nor monitor values enforcement beyond its direct bounty. Ordered actions: (1) signer misses the absolute publication deadline; (2) an observer obtains the valid claim before evidenceClose; (3) when its expected evidence gas/handling cost `C` exceeds `pT` (the 1-TAIKO reporter reward), every profit-seeking observer declines; (4) evidenceClose passes and the unknown/unreported signature cannot revive the released reserve. The actor retains collateral despite an objectively provable breach. Its gain is avoided publication cost or other external benefit; its risk is a funded reporter or a prior admitted challenge forcing settlement.

Cost: the signer's refundable service collateral and ordinary registration expenses; no invented report cost is assumed away. Gas cost is symbolic because evidence methods have not been benchmarked. Self-reporting is optional and loses 9 TAIKO, so an attacker normally prefers no report. Direct evidence needs no ETH Q deposit, which reduces but does not remove the incentive issue.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/accountability.html:28,34,61,210`; `roles.html:59,83,87,218`; `arguments.html:25,149`. **No failed normative predicate:** valid permissionless evidence and a specified reward/false-accusation rule exist; deterministic detectability is conditional on timely presentation and is not actual economic detection. Medium because expected enforcement can be zero without breaking signature authentication. Calibration must include reporter gas and willingness, not just a per-position penalty.

### C8 — optional checkpoint omission and ring expiry

**Low for the permanent-veto attempt, blocked; finite availability/funding residual is Medium. Tests R1/R2/R3/R7 and the anchor-removal follow-up.** Prerequisites: no participant voluntarily pins/reveals a useful origin; producer excludes discretionary checkpoint calls; a later timestamp collides with its EIP-4788 ring position. Ordered actions: (1) accept valid anchorless blocks without pin/reveal; (2) advance timestamps to overwrite the desired temporary origin, potentially by one ≥8191-second jump after a long halt; (3) make the user's fixed-positive-timestamp reveal fail; (4) continue omitting ordinary checkpoint service.

Cost: ordinary valid segment proof/DA/rent costs, no checkpoint duty collateral. Gain: defer bridge usability and force users to pay workflow costs; no forged root. The fixed-timestamp reveal indeed fails. **The permanent-veto continuation is blocked by specified predicates/actions:** a funded user enqueues a valid `pinCurrentOrigin()` transaction, which must execute in the mandatory prefix and pins the *processing* origin, not an expired request-time origin; after learning that exact header, enqueue `revealCheckpoint(0,header)`. The pin removes ring expiry. A signal set before the pin's enqueue survives in the append-only remote signal history at a sufficiently later authenticated origin. A dedicated sender, valid nonce/balance/fee cap and enough ordinary execution gas remain necessary; a rejected/reverting request does not magically produce a pin.

Evidence: `packages/protocol/docs/Etna/design/checkpoints.html:19,50,86,91–96,108–109,121–125,128–129`; `arguments.html:125–128`; `codec.html:109–114`; `migration.html:219,339–344`. Zero subsidy and two queue passes can leave substantial finite delay/cost (C5/C6), but current-hash pinning prevents the claimed expiry monopoly.
