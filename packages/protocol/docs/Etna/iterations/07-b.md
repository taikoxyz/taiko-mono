## Findings and residual risks

### 07-B-M1 — paid predecessors can make checkpoint recovery slow even with healthy progress

**Severity:** Medium residual degradation. **Exploitability:** direct and permissionless with funded L1 enqueues; finite delay, not permanent starvation. **Requirements:** R1, R3, R7, with R2 bridge-claim latency affected.

**Locations:** `design/index.html:184–189`; `design/arguments.html:88–96`; `design/checkpoints.html:109`, `:124`; queue pricing in `design/roles.html:287`.

**Preconditions:** an initially empty queue; the attacker gets 52 requests enqueued before the victim's pin request; accepted successors process no more than the required four and are spaced 660 seconds apart. The 660-second spacing is one permitted illustrative progress schedule, not a measured production cadence. The attacker need not own the checkpoint writer or forge a proof.

**Trace:**

1. Enqueue 52 bounded requests before the victim. Their actual execution can be cheap or deterministically rejected; each still occupies one FIFO entry.
2. Victim enqueues a correctly funded nonce-isolated `pinCurrentOrigin()` as entry 52.
3. Once the victim is due in the snapshot, twelve successors remove entries 0–47; the thirteenth removes 48–51; the fourteenth processes the pin.
4. Fourteen 660-second successor intervals are 9240 seconds. Initial queue maturity/snapshot waiting may add more. Thus the ring's 8191-second timestamp collision threshold is not a usable forced-pin latency bound.
5. The pin nevertheless stores the **processing** origin. A later timestamp-zero reveal still works. Later malicious arrivals cannot add predecessors to this already queued pin.

**Cost/gain:** starting empty without intervening consumption, the first 50 enqueues cost 0.001 ETH each and the next two 0.002 ETH each: **0.054 ETH** plus L1 gas. At least **0.027 ETH** is unrecoverable even if the attacker captures every processing reward. Proving/rent/DA costs are additional if the attacker also supplies the accepted segments. Gain is hours of delay to a targeted user's recovery/claim workflow, without compromising roots or buying a permanent queue reservation.

**Disposition:** retain as a measurable censorship-delay risk. The existing monotone FIFO and escalating fee prevent the proposed infinite overtaking attack; they do not establish a short universal recovery SLA. This trace does **not** invalidate processing-time pinning. Calibrate backlog costs/capacity and report queue-dependent latency before launch; no custom EIP hook or mandatory pin is needed to resolve the ring-expiry concern.

### 07-B-M2 — durable checkpoint recovery is narrower than forced execution of every Bridge operation

**Severity:** Medium residual censorship exposure. **Exploitability:** conditional on sustained control of discretionary L2 ordering and a user's required operation exceeding force limits. **Requirements:** R1, R2, R7. This is a scope limit, not a claim that R2's unchanged ABI is absent.

**Locations:** `design/checkpoints.html:121–129`; `design/arguments.html:95`, `:127`, `:177`; `design/index.html:184`, `:211`; ordinary block limit `design/codec.html:59`.

**Preconditions:** a valid bridge operation fits ordinary block execution but its supported signed transaction needs more than 2048 raw bytes or more than 1,000,000 gas; discretionary producers censor that operation while continuing valid forced-prefix processing. For example, a legitimate message's fixed target work may require more than the forced gas cap. Availability of a complete state proof is assumed, not the issue.

**Trace:**

1. The user forces the bounded pin and reveal successfully; a durable authenticated checkpoint now exists at the existing SignalService.
2. The user's subsequent complete claim/processing transaction exceeds a force resource bound.
3. Enqueueing the bytes cannot make that larger operation mandatory: the forced codec permits a resource-restricted rejection rather than increasing the gas/byte allowance.
4. The incumbent continues valid successors, processes all supported FIFO entries, and omits the larger discretionary operation.
5. The pin/reveal path has succeeded but cannot by itself force that complete application action. Splitting proof preparation or using a wrapper may help particular messages, but is not a universal replacement: fixed message contents, gas needs, and Bridge caller/owner semantics must still hold.

**Cost/gain:** ordinary cost of retaining canonical ordering, including the specified rent/proof/DA; no special checkpoint attack cost or forged state required. Gain is selective censorship of out-of-envelope operations while the chain and checkpoint store remain live. Under a willing independent producer who chooses that operation and gets its applicable candidate accepted, censorship ends; the specification does not guarantee such a branch wins.

**Disposition:** explicitly retain the candidate's existing supported-operation limitation. Do not advertise “any forced pin can reveal a checkpoint” as “every bridged asset operation is now forceable.” No universal protocol fix is asserted here; increasing limits or adding a caller-compatible segmented operation requires a separate resource and interface design. This report does not demand such a change as an unstated R2 condition.

## Concrete adversarial traces and rejection analysis

The following attempts supplement M1/M2. Each states the resource and assumption boundary rather than declaring every environmental outage a vulnerability.

### T1 — expire a fixed-timestamp forced reveal

- **Severity/exploitability:** Medium temporary failure of a chosen request; direct with enough paid backlog/timestamp advancement. Permanent checkpoint-lock variant rejected. **R1/R2/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** origin H recorded at L2 timestamp 100000; H has neither a pin nor a persisted checkpoint. Victim signs `revealCheckpoint(100000, headerH)`.
- **Steps:** hold it behind paid FIFO work; advance to a block timestamp 108191 with a different authenticated origin; both timestamps have residue 1708 modulo 8191; process the reveal after overwrite.
- **Result:** the exact historical read fails; ordinary revert gas is paid. The attacker cannot revive H by claiming its stale ring value. A replacement forced selector-only pin records the processing origin H2, then `revealCheckpoint(0, headerH2)` succeeds independently of further ring overwrites. For a signal sent before the pin enqueue, monotone origin/snapshot rules put the processing origin after that signal's L1 write; preserved append-only signal storage permits a later-state proof.
- **Cost/gain:** backlog fees as M1, plus any canonical capture costs; gain is a failed obsolete reveal and extra recovery rounds, not an everlasting lock. A timestamp jump can cause collision after one successor, so an “8191 blocks” safety claim would be false; the candidate expressly avoids it.
- **Evidence:** `design/checkpoints.html:19`, `:93–109`; `design/arguments.html:126–128`.

### T2 — nonce, balance, basefee or gas grief the delayed pin

- **Severity/exploitability:** Low/ordinary transaction failure where the victim reuses a nonce or underfunds; funded dedicated-sender halt rejected. **R1/R3/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** victim queues a type-2 pin from a dedicated EOA, uses no competing transaction/authorization, funds its full gas purchase, chooses a fee cap covering the fixed 1-gwei basefee maximum, and supplies sufficient measured execution gas.
- **Steps:** attacker places discretionary transactions before the pin, raises the fee by filling blocks, attempts to spend the sender nonce, or supplies four maximum-gas forced predecessors.
- **Result:** mandatory forced execution precedes discretionary transactions; no forged sender signature changes the dedicated EOA's nonce; basefee is capped; four 1M declared-gas forced entries fit the 10M block. An attacker cannot force the pin to become invalid by these steps. If the victim signs another same-nonce transaction or lacks funds, outcome 2 is an ordinary validity result, and success was never promised for it. Contract execution out-of-gas is likewise an outcome, not successful pinning.
- **Cost/gain:** attacker pays ordinary queue/canonical costs without obtaining the proposed exclusion. Victim failure costs its original queue fee and any executed-call gas. Neither TAIKO collateral nor future Bridge reimbursement funds this transaction automatically.
- **Evidence:** `design/codec.html:100–105`, `:117–120`; `design/checkpoints.html:109`, `:121–125`. The stated 1M pin/reveal envelope still needs a gas benchmark; this review does not fabricate one.

### T3 — remove every relayer, or strand a newcomer without L2 ETH

- **Severity/exploitability:** Medium availability/funding exposure; no privileged-role deadlock established. **R1/R3.**
- **Preconditions:** every incumbent relay stops. Separate cases: (a) a capable user has funded L1 and L2 transaction accounts plus header/proof data; (b) nobody can fund a valid L2 call, even if someone holds ETH on L1.
- **Steps:** refuse all pin/reveal/Bridge relay requests and stop producing their ordinary calls.
- **Result:** in (a) the user can submit the supported force transactions itself; no relay signature, registration, caller subsidy or DAO is required. In (b) the ordinary paid pin can fail; the specification does not contain automatic L1-to-L2 gas sponsorship. A bridge deposit may itself need a revealed checkpoint, so L1 wealth alone must not be silently treated as a funded L2 sender. The candidate explicitly requires a funded valid ordinary transaction; failure of that premise is not an omitted relay authorization rule.
- **Cost/gain:** withholding one's own service has no mandatory slash absent an accepted duty; gain is delayed service until a funded participant acts. No custody extraction.
- **Evidence:** `design/checkpoints.html:50`, `:124`, `:129`; `design/roles.html:61–62`; `design/arguments.html:128`.

### T4 — erase accepted data or historical proof witnesses

- **Severity/exploitability:** High conditional availability loss if all required archives disappear; not a new protocol exploit under A-ARCHIVE. **R1/R2/R3/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** actual temporary DA retention has ended and every reachable holder loses the canonical reconstruction data or required historical state witness. A valid proof and persisted root remain.
- **Steps:** discontinue all archives; a new prover tries to reconstruct the accepted head or a user tries to build the bridge proof from hashes alone.
- **Result:** reconstruction/claim construction fails; pins preserve authenticity, not preimages or MPT witnesses. There is no legitimate timeout that manufactures those bytes. If one authenticated source survives, public entry and normal proof validation recover without incumbents. This is explicitly the failure consequence of A-ARCHIVE, not a missing timeout or DAO rescue selector.
- **Cost/gain:** cost depends on acquiring control of every relevant data copy and is not quantified by on-chain fees; refusing one's own archive is cheap but insufficient if another survives. Gain can be a halt to new proving/claims, without a valid forged withdrawal.
- **Evidence:** `design/arguments.html:18`, `:24–26`, `:106`; `design/staging.html:114`, `:230`; `design/checkpoints.html:124`, `:137`.

### T5 — activate with absent/wrong standard contracts or divergent execution engines

- **Severity/exploitability:** High launch failure if the prerequisite is violated; accepted-candidate bypass rejected under the normative migration relation. **R1/R2/R4/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** counterfeit/missing EIP-4788 runtime, a builder skipping its zero-root legacy call, or a guest omitting system state writes.
- **Steps:** deploy code while paths disagree; attempt to certify the terminal legacy root and activate; attempt an empty first Etna block with unchanged root; use a missing oracle read as a zero-root legacy allowance.
- **Result:** migration requires parity before canonical deployment, authenticated actual runtime/implementation state before activation, and both guards already installed in the certified legacy root. Missing/malformed reads revert; standard no-code execution is not redefined as a Taiko consensus exception. Empty blocks still prove both standard system effects. A faithful implementation cannot certify the proposed absent-code boundary. Faulty engine/proof implementations can break these conditions and must be caught before launch.
- **Cost/gain:** unauthorized attacker cannot amend the immutable manifest or forge the installation proof; a bad deployment/upgrade can halt or split implementations, which is the stated implementation/upgrade risk. No exploit cost is fabricated for bypassing sound certification.
- **Evidence:** `design/migration.html:228–236`, `:242`, `:321`, `:481–484`; `design/codec.html:109–114`; `design/checkpoints.html:53–74`.

### T6 — freeze migration then rely on DAO inactivity

- **Severity/exploitability:** Low for the attempted operational-admin lock; archive/prover-loss variants have High availability impact under failed premises. **R1/R2/R3/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** required installation is complete; old proposal/force tails have been frozen; all incumbents and DAO stop, but a funded capable newcomer has the actual legacy data and both required proving artifacts.
- **Steps:** attacker abandons assigned jobs, refuses legacy proof submission, tries to append drain work, and refuses to call activation.
- **Result:** anyone proves finite historical work and commits/proves the deterministic forced suffix; new legacy enqueues fail; no committer owns a proof right; activation is objective and public. H0 comes from the certificate and cannot be caller-selected. If legacy DA or either required prover artifact is absent, the explicit failure mode is blocked activation, not a DAO-only runtime switch. Missing installation before freezing is separately forbidden by preflight/order rules.
- **Cost/gain:** abandonment may incur existing liabilities but cannot retain a canonical lease; drain calldata/proof and activation gas fall to the newcomer. Gain is delay until that newcomer completes finite work.
- **Evidence:** `design/migration.html:241–259`, `:321–344`, `:421–434`, `:458–460`.

### T7 — use stale origins/stages and zero-rent waiting to trap recovery

- **Severity/exploitability:** Low wasted candidate work; permanent lock rejected. **R1/R3/R6/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** last accepted head at time 0; previous candidate's origin/stage expire; no incumbent acts; newcomer can obtain fresh authenticated headers and prove.
- **Steps:** wait until rent reaches zero at 900, then attempt to reuse the old proof; after its rejection, acquire a fresh origin at 1000, stage by 1120, mature at 1480, and land by 1600.
- **Result:** stale proof is correctly rejected. Fresh origin age at landing is 600 seconds, below 900; stage age is 480, within 360–900. Unchanged parent's rent remains zero. Refreshing execution context/body/proof, rather than merely changing salt, supplies a valid constructive recovery. A competitor accepting first is canonical progress, not a frozen head.
- **Cost/gain:** attacker obtains wasted stale preparation only; fresh entrant pays actual proof/DA/L1 costs, with zero protocol rent in this schedule. No identity can reset the rent clock through a failed action or a stage.
- **Evidence:** `design/staging.html:113`, `:118–120`, `:230`; `design/roles.html:267–281`; `design/arguments.html:121`.

### T8 — withhold bonded fragments or abandon accepted proof jobs

- **Severity/exploitability:** Medium soft-service degradation is possible; canonical-veto variant rejected. **R3/R4/R6/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** malicious publisher reserves a position and issues at t=1000; a worker accepts a funded exact live job; another capable entrant can work from the accepted state.
- **Steps:** publisher withholds until 1239, publishes exact fragment/FR by 1240, or misses that deadline entirely; worker never produces its assigned proof; attacker offers impossible staged outputs hoping the entrant must use them.
- **Result:** timely 1239 publication satisfies the actual duty and can delay recipients before then. Missing the deadline yields objective once-only 10-TAIKO liability if reported within its evidence horizon. The optional job's absence never reserves the parent. A backup may build a different valid child, stage it, prove and submit; invalid/unavailable stages impose no choice obligation. The 900-block unsafe horizon may suspend soft confirmations rather than invent valid data. No claim of uninterrupted one-second service survives all service providers withholding.
- **Cost/gain:** timely selective withholding has no slash; missed position loses 10 TAIKO, or 9 net if self-reported; applicable worker default loses 1000, or 900 net if self-reported. Gain is provisional disruption and wasted user/prover work; penalties are not unlimited MEV insurance.
- **Evidence:** `design/accountability.html:28–38`, `:56–61`, `:203–210`; `design/staging.html:229–233`; `design/index.html:194`.

### T9 — grind empty proofs, spam stages, or keep appending forced requests

- **Severity/exploitability:** Medium competition/resource degradation; infinite starvation of a fixed supported FIFO item rejected under continuing progress. **R1/R5/R6/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** adversary can afford canonical candidates and arbitrary paid stages/enqueues; victim already has a finite queue index.
- **Steps:** win small empty-discretionary segments repeatedly, reuse an old origin, append requests just before honest proofs land, or occupy apparent stage IDs.
- **Result:** staging grants no reservation, duplicate stage IDs cannot change receipt time/content, and each final proof must contain the immutable parent-required force prefix. New arrivals cannot move that proof's target. Reused origins expire after 900 seconds, so indefinite progress eventually snapshots the victim as due. Once eligible, each successor removes up to four predecessors. Same-block rapid progress pays 0.05 ETH for every subsequent head. Empty discretionary work does not exempt forced work.
- **Cost/gain:** paid spam and valid faster proofs can waste other entrants' inspection/proving effort and dominate discretionary ordering; rent and queue sinks remain real. They cannot keep a particular eligible supported request unprocessed forever while satisfying continued progress. If no honest competitor races before 900 seconds, a monopolist can pay zero rent; this is explicitly outside the positive capture-cost premise.
- **Evidence:** `design/staging.html:90–94`; `design/index.html:162–164`, `:186–189`; `design/roles.html:276–289`.

### T10 — break the one-second service through origin acquisition or required proof lateness

- **Severity/exploitability:** Medium availability/performance exposure; no demonstrated slot-coupled normative deadlock. **R4/R5/R7.**
- **Preconditions:** ordinary funded live service with authenticated parent and current origin; compare 12/6/4/2-second L1 production. Separate failure case: required local context is missing or maximum execution cannot finish in time.
- **Steps:** seal segments early at resource limits, demand new origins every L2 block, or present signed blocks before the observer can validate their origin/system writes.
- **Result:** the candidate permits origin reuse, fixes the origin before execution, and does not require a new L1 header per one-second block. It rejects the demand for per-L2-block L1 progress. Missing context yields UNKNOWN, not false soft confirmation. Gas/proof throughput can still violate the advertised healthy target; that is an unmeasured launch condition and may require reducing resource limits. Both proving backends are required for liveness, so failure of either stops acceptance until capability returns or a reviewed upgrade changes policy.
- **Cost/gain:** invalid or unavailable soft payloads can waste bounded validation resources but cannot acquire canonical status. Actual hardware/network degradation cost is environmental; no on-chain price is asserted. Gain can be loss of soft service, not forged validity.
- **Evidence:** `design/index.html:69–82`, `:129`, `:194`; `design/codec.html:7–16`, `:103`, `:164–166`; `design/arguments.html:20–24`, `:53`.

## Verification record and final boundary

Arithmetic checked independently in scratch: 52 predecessor fees = 54,000,000,000,000,000 wei; their half-sink = 27,000,000,000,000,000 wei; `ceil(53/4)=14`; `14*660=9240`; `100000%8191=108191%8191=1708`; maximum reveal calldata = 1636 bytes and the specified conservative raw envelope = 1845 bytes, below 2048. The fresh zero-rent recovery schedule above leaves 300 seconds of origin margin at its latest landing. These checks establish arithmetic only, not measured execution gas, a valid Ethereum proof fixture, or an end-to-end runtime test.

The two encoding fixture documents were read in full. Their stated toy bodies and synthetic roots are expressly not valid execution/activation proofs; they cannot close the missing launch measurements. No prior “pass,” “reviewed specification,” or author readiness assertion was used to conclude this round.

**Recommended disposition:** no new Critical/High revision required from this angle; retain M1 and M2 as explicit Medium scope/economic risks, and preserve the concrete funded dedicated-sender plus processing-time-pin recovery strategy. Do not waive canonical-code/parity/certificate/gas preflight. No new anchor transaction, mandatory pin, or custom pre-execution SignalService hook is justified by the rejected ring-expiry attack.
