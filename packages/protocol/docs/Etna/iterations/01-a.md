# Round 01, angle A — financial safety

- Reviewer model: `gpt-6.1-sol`.
- Inspected design base: `f0a548458` plus migration encoding-only follow-up `585d2a183`, as supplied by the coordinator; no git operations performed. The current 474-line migration page, including certificate fields at lines 245–251 and the exact checkpoint-hash expression at line 337, was inspected. An executable installation-state digest/witness relation remains a validation obligation; no missing unseen edits are alleged.
- Scope: full `README.md` requirement ledger, all five `design/*.html` pages and `design/architecture.md`; selected current SignalService, Bridge, BaseVault, ERC20Vault, BridgedERC20 and legacy bond source. No production implementation, transactions, external systems, executable exploit code or previous reviews were used.
- Interpretation: reversible soft preconfirmations are permitted; second-based targets are conditional on the stated execution/network/inclusion assumptions; historical P2P withholding is not directly provable on-chain. Those accepted boundaries are not defects or an impossibility verdict.

## Verdict

No direct custody-theft, unbacked-credit mint or double-payment exploit was demonstrated against the normative rules. One Low specification defect is demonstrated below. Three additional numbered items are open validation gates, with severity recommendations describing their possible impact or requirement importance, **not confirmed vulnerabilities**. In particular, there are **zero confirmed Critical or High findings** in this report.

The draft correctly separates proof-authenticated custody from advisory services. It also provides distinct escrow, single-use liability positions, immutable parent-consumption receipts, proof-bound beneficiaries, bounded recovery bytes, pull credits and historical message preservation. These are material protections. They remain specification commitments rather than evidence of a correct executable circuit or migration.

## Numbered findings and gates

### 1. Low — an unsolicited donation falsifies the stated asset-equality invariant

**Classification:** demonstrated specification defect; conditional availability hazard if the erroneous equality is implemented as a runtime assertion. No theft demonstrated.

**References:** `design/roles.html:101–106`, section `#accounting`, asserts `heldTAIKO = ...` and `heldETH = ...`; `design/arguments.html:51`, S4, repeats equality. In contrast, `design/accountability.html:29`, `#accounting`, correctly says liabilities never exceed held assets.

**Preconditions:** the registry holds correctly funded liabilities. TAIKO permits an ordinary transfer to its address, as expected for the established token. Alternatively, ETH can be delivered independently of the registry's deposit entrypoint, including a forced transfer under applicable Ethereum semantics.

**Concrete trace:**

1. Let held TAIKO and total accounted TAIKO liabilities both be 6,000 TAIKO.
2. An outsider transfers one token-wei directly to the registry without invoking `depositTAIKO`.
3. Held assets become `6,000 × 10^18 + 1`; free balances, reserves, credits, bounty escrow and sink are unchanged.
4. The displayed equality is false, although solvency is preserved. The ETH equality has the analogous one-wei counterexample.
5. If a future withdrawal/settlement asserts equality against the raw token/native balance, every otherwise valid call can revert until the specification supplies a surplus treatment. The present draft does not specify such an assertion, so that freeze is conditional, not demonstrated deployed behavior.

**Attacker cost/gain:** one token-wei or wei plus transfer gas; no direct financial gain. A runtime-equality implementation could create third-party grief value far exceeding the donation.

**Requirements affected:** R3 objective exits and R6 conserved, objective accounting if the equality is enforced. The current inconsistency itself is not a demonstrated failure of either hard requirement.

**Smallest fix:** make all pages use `accountedLiabilities <= heldAssets`; define any difference as uncredited surplus. Keep internal conservation equations between ledgers exact. Deposits must credit only the actual delta attributable to that deposit, and donations must create no caller withdrawal entitlement. Do not make operations depend on raw-balance equality.

### 2. Critical potential impact — executable root/DA binding remains a custody validation gate

**Classification:** open validation gate, not an exploit of the stated design. The attack below requires an implementation or circuit omission explicitly forbidden by the draft.

**References:** `design/architecture.md:15`; `design/index.html:103–112`, atomic acceptance and exact blob relation; `design/migration.html:75–81`, checkpoint predicates; `design/arguments.html:15–16,39,47–48`, A-CRYPTO/A-IMPLEMENTATION and S1/S3. Baseline: `contracts/shared/signal/SignalService.sol:173–181,284–295`; `contracts/shared/bridge/Bridge.sol:335–341`; `contracts/shared/vault/BaseVault.sol:53–61`; `contracts/shared/vault/ERC20Vault.sol:438–450,507–522`.

**Preconditions:** a future verifier accepts a proof while failing to bind a terminal state root, exact accepted parent, authentic anchor header, code/revision domain, or actual published blob/calldata bytes to the execution statement. This condition is not established by this review.

**Concrete hypothetical trace:**

1. The attacker obtains a valid execution proof for task A from accepted head H.
2. Suppose the implementation leaves `endStateRoot` outside the authenticated public-input relation, or accepts a program/domain other than the intended revision.
3. The attacker substitutes root R of an attacker-constructed remote state containing the legitimate remote SignalService address and a forged sent-signal slot for a withdrawal message m from the remote Vault.
4. Inbox accepts the under-bound statement and publishes R through its authorized `saveCheckpoint` call.
5. Bridge verifies an account/storage proof against R. BaseVault sees the expected remote Vault provenance in m, and the existing Vault release path transfers canonical escrow or mints an unbacked representation to the attacker.
6. Replay protection prevents replaying m; it cannot make this first forged m authentic. Removing quotas leaves no discretionary throttle, as intended by R1.

An independent DA variant proves body A while posting blob B. It need not immediately forge a state root, but prevents public derivation and new-prover reconstruction. The exact in-circuit KZG/vector relation and current-transaction `BLOBHASH` checks specified at `index.html:110` defeat this variant if correctly implemented.

**Attacker cost/gain:** under a binding omission, one reusable proof, crafted witnesses and L1/bridge gas; gain can approach available custody. Under a fully sound, correctly bound verifier, this trace requires violating A-CRYPTO and is not an available attack.

**Requirements affected:** R2 authentic reusable bridge checkpoints and R7 proof plus matching data. No failure of their normative rules is demonstrated; executable closure remains open.

**Recommendation / smallest closure:** retain the present mandatory bindings. Produce a fixed revision/program/public-input schema and independent relation review, including the Anchor system transition and full blob-to-body relation. Negative conformance vectors must vary one field at a time: both chain IDs, contract addresses, old head, output root/hash, revision/program ID, force cursor/outcomes, beneficiary/job fields, data encoding, padding and actual blob hash. Verify that the only checkpoint written is the exact proven terminal EVM state root. A generic statement that the prover checks EVM execution cannot close this gate.

### 3. Critical potential impact — preserved addresses do not establish preserved legacy custody authority

**Classification:** open deployment/migration validation gate; conditional hypothetical loss, not a confirmed migration bug. The design explicitly rejects the unsafe intermediate states in this trace.

**References:** `design/architecture.md:41`; `design/migration.html:139–155,188–218,225–242,342–350,377–393,428–437`. Baseline: `contracts/shared/bridge/Bridge.sol:208–212,335–402`; `contracts/shared/signal/SignalService.sol:63–73,265–295`; `contracts/shared/vault/BridgedERC20.sol:112–125,181`; `contracts/layer1/core/libs/LibBonds.sol:58–88,148–168,202–205`.

**Preconditions:** an actual retained proxy/router/wrapper differs from the assumed deployed layout or authority inventory, or a future migration implementation resets replay state, leaves a historical mint path live, or accepts an unauthenticated installation-state certificate. No live deployment facts were inspected here.

**Concrete hypothetical trace A — replay-state loss:**

1. A genuine historical withdrawal m has already released 100 canonical tokens and is marked DONE at the retained destination Bridge.
2. A storage-incompatible upgrade shifts/clears the destination `messageStatus` mapping, while preserving a valid old checkpoint or received-signal cache.
3. m now reads NEW at the new mapping location.
4. A holder of m and its old proof calls public processing. Signal authentication still succeeds; m releases another 100 tokens and becomes DONE in the new map.
5. The same proxy addresses were retained throughout, so an address-only check would miss the loss.

**Concrete hypothetical trace B — residual wrapper authority:**

1. A legacy token wrapper retains an owner or migration-pair mint path not represented in the migration inventory.
2. The old authorized party creates an unbacked wrapper balance.
3. It bridges that balance back through the legitimate Vault burn/message path.
4. A sound proof correctly authenticates the maliciously minted balance and genuine return message; the canonical Vault releases backing belonging to other users.

The normative draft disables these paths and requires active migration liabilities to be resolved, so neither trace establishes a defect in those rules. A malicious future DAO upgrade is additionally covered by the explicitly accepted A-UPGRADE trust boundary; this is not a claim to defeat that authority cryptographically.

**Attacker cost/gain:** old proof replay costs ordinary bridge gas and yields the repeated message value. Residual authorized minting costs mint/bridge/proving gas and can drain the corresponding canonical escrow. Amounts depend on unknown deployed state.

**Requirements affected:** R1 upgrade-only governance and R2 unchanged custody/message/storage semantics. These remain deployment-dependent, not passed by a source layout table alone.

**Recommendation / smallest closure:** keep activation fail-closed until the authenticated finalized-state manifest and full selector/layout inventory are available. Rehearse DONE replay, received-cache proofs, permit nonces, paired token migrations, old pending-owner paths, unused initializers, legacy routed selectors, and the one-way syncer switch against exact deployed bytecode/storage. Verify every original bond and forced fee once in its original denomination; the drain's best-effort legacy slash is not a new guaranteed collectible bond. Check the installation-state certificate's encoding and actual proxy implementation/authority/storage witnesses, rather than accepting its digest as an unaudited assertion. No new custody address is required by this closure.

### 4. Medium — capital-proportional duties and zero exclusivity do not yet establish R6's economic deterrence

**Classification:** open economic validation gate with a concrete feasible concentration strategy; not direct theft or a fundamental impossibility result. The draft already labels this weakness open.

**References:** `design/architecture.md:31–37`; `design/roles.html:48–58,165–193`, especially `#anti-monopoly`; `design/accountability.html:23,29,31–40`; `design/arguments.html:52,106,115–116`.

**Preconditions:** one actor has lower proving cost, larger capital or better builder access than competitors. Optional customer demand and TAIKO collateral valuation are not fixed by the protocol. No assumption of distinct owners behind addresses is made.

**Concrete trace:**

1. Honest publisher/prover P seals a transaction-rich public segment extending H and begins its target 180-second proof.
2. M builds a smaller valid segment from H, includes every required frozen forced request, and obtains its proof first. M needs no service bucket or raw-prover TAIKO bond.
3. M lands first through ordinary public submission or builder access. P's statement becomes stale. M receives only the fees/rewards validly bound to its accepted statement; it does not steal P's job bounty, because a different timely consumption cancels that job.
4. Repeat at subsequent heads. Eligible forced requests advance, but ordinary discretionary order flow and revenue can remain concentrated with M. P can pay repeated unproductive proving costs despite open admission.
5. If P additionally promises one bonded body per second, its seven-day 100-TAIKO-per-position history requires approximately 60.48 million TAIKO locked continuously, before extra fragments/custodians. M can operate unbonded canonical proving and avoid that service capital burden. Per-owner double-sign rules do not identify M's other addresses.

**Attacker cost/gain:** computation, DA/L1 fees and optional builder payments. Gain is valid accepted-block fees, forced-request fees and discretionary MEV, less these costs; no positive-profit claim is established without measurements. Each actual self-slash still loses 90% of its reserve: 90 TAIKO per position or 900 TAIKO per worker job plus gas. This proves no self-mint, not that external gain is smaller than the loss.

**Requirement affected:** R6's concrete anti-monopoly economics remains unverified. R1's open admission and mandatory FIFO survive this trace. R4 service viability is exposed to capital/throughput costs but not disproved.

**Recommendation / smallest closure:** define the economic guarantee that R6 must demonstrate, then supply a capital/proof-cost/revenue sensitivity model and measured workload distribution. Compare small-entry operating capital and same-actor role combinations with the unbonded canonical path. Adjust reserve/history parameters only with an explicit finite exposure budget, and separate deterrence from insurance. Publishing calibrated limits on extractable-value deterrence is necessary; simply adding per-address caps, auctions or a canonical committee would not close this gate and could add liveness dependencies.

## Judge disposition requested

Correct finding 1 in the normative accounting text. Keep gates 2–3 visibly blocking implementation readiness without treating their hypothetical assumption failures as new Critical exploits. R6 economics needs a concrete closure criterion and evidence; the permissionless API alone does not prove it. R4/R7 performance and R2 deployed compatibility remain unverified. This review supports neither an all-requirements pass nor a fundamental incompatibility verdict.
