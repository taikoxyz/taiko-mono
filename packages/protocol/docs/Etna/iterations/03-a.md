## Verdict

**No confirmed Critical, High, or Medium financial-safety finding against the normative specification at this snapshot.** This is a scoped, conditional design review, not implementation verification, a deployment audit, or proof that the entire project has converged. No finding is suppressed merely because the document acknowledges it; the rejected traces below identify the actual rule that defeats each attempted exploit.

Custody depends on complete proof enforcement and authenticated checkpoint provenance, rather than service collateral. Service penalties and rewards have finite, separately escrowed funding. Those are appropriate boundaries: ten TAIKO is not advertised as insurance against arbitrary MEV, and optional-role outages cannot grant anyone a withdrawal root.

The specification provides an actual defense against an active holder privately withholding a candidate until its canonical acceptance: complete matching execution data must be published through L1 DA at least 360 seconds earlier. The objective publication lead is universal, rather than contingent on a custodian, challenger, or voluntarily signed covenant. It gives a funded entrant access to the execution inputs before canonical eligibility under the stated retrieval/proving assumptions. It does **not** punish all earlier private computation, guarantee that the honest soft branch wins, or establish global economic identity. These residual limits do not constitute a custody exploit or a falsification of the stated objective surrogate.

## Concrete financial attack traces examined

These are falsification attempts, **not confirmed findings**. Each includes the preconditions, attack steps, cost, intended gain, requirement at risk and the specified defense. Severity is therefore “not assigned / rejected trace,” rather than an invented exploitable vulnerability. No specification fix is required for these traces; future implementations must preserve the cited rules.

### A1 — false withdrawal checkpoint through captured soft roles

**Preconditions:** attacker controls publishers, custodians and offered workers, but cannot forge a complete execution proof, break Ethereum, or install a malicious upgrade.

1. Sign an apparently bonded candidate with an attacker-chosen state root containing a fabricated Bridge signal.
2. Stage or certify that candidate and attempt to install its root in SignalService.
3. Process a withdrawal against the fabricated signal to release retained Vault escrow.

**Cost:** optional service collateral, signatures and L1 gas/DA; capital is not a proof substitute. **Intended gain:** arbitrary escrow or wrapper minting. **Requirements at risk:** R1, R2, R7. **Defense:** neither staging nor service testimony has checkpoint authority; atomic acceptance requires the complete valid relation. Anchor provenance and full EVM state-root proof verification remain mandatory (`index.html:114–121,137–151`; `migration.html:75–82`; `arguments.html:48–50`). **Result:** rejected before root publication. **Severity/minimal fix:** none; preserve those admission/provenance rules.

### A2 — copied proof redirects forced-fee credits or job bounty

**Preconditions:** attacker sees an honest valid final action or proof and can relay/front-run it.

1. Copy the proof-bearing submission.
2. Replace beneficiary with the attacker, or replace jobId/stageId to claim a different escrowed job.
3. Submit first and attempt to withdraw the honest worker's reward or processed forced fees.

**Cost:** relay/front-run gas; changing statement fields requires a new complete proof and producer authorization. **Intended gain:** the existing job bounty or half of processed forced fees. **Requirements at risk:** R3, R6, R7. **Defense:** beneficiary and job/stage fields are proof-bound; exact producer assent binds the entire statement; job settlement additionally matches its immutable task/stage/job/worker (`index.html:115–117,135–137,171`; `staging.html:154–157`; `roles.html:93–101`). A newly computed independent proof can legitimately race with its own beneficiary, but cancels rather than steals the other job's bounty. **Result:** copied proof cannot redirect funds. **Severity/minimal fix:** none.

### A3 — manufacture victim-producer conflict evidence

**Preconditions:** innocent publisher X has a live reserve and attacker can stage/prove a conflicting valid Y.

1. Put X's owner into Y.producer or beneficiary without obtaining X's authorization.
2. Land Y and present its block membership as evidence against X.
3. Collect a reporter reward while destroying X's reserve.

**Cost:** proof/DA/gas and a forged authorization if seeking the claimed gain. **Intended gain:** 1 TAIKO reporter payout plus grief against X. **Requirements at risk:** R6 and role safety under R3. **Defense:** acceptance requires canonical EIP-712 producer assent; immutable receipt attests the checked owner/key identity. Conflict evidence requires producer equality, exact immediate parent/revision/height, an authenticated statement preimage and six-level real-block membership (`staging.html:123–157,159–181`; `accountability.html:21–29`). Merely naming a fee recipient proves nothing. **Result:** rejection at admission or evidence authentication. **Severity/minimal fix:** none.

### A4 — repeat slash and multiply responder payouts

**Preconditions:** attacker can generate many authentic conflicting signatures using its own position, or open several funded same-digest challenges.

1. Present successive conflict pairs against the same position to collect repeated reporter credits.
2. Reveal one fragment and attempt to collect Q from every pending same-digest challenge.
3. Withdraw reserve, reward and refunded escrow as if each still existed independently.

**Cost:** 600 TAIKO per bucket and Q per admitted challenge, plus gas. **Intended gain:** unbacked TAIKO/ETH through duplicated allocations. **Requirements at risk:** R3, R6. **Defense:** a position is slashed only once; selected response earns exactly one Q; other timely satisfied challenges refund their openers via bounded lazy settlement; accounting moves amounts rather than leaving duplicated reserves. Self-slash recovers at most 1 of 10 TAIKO (`roles.html:110–121`; `accountability.html:18–19,37,43,58–78`). **Result:** no value creation; self-default loses 9 TAIKO plus gas. **Severity/minimal fix:** none.

### A5 — late child erases proof-worker default

**Preconditions:** worker accepted a feasible job and missed its deadline while the parent remained unconsumed.

1. Wait until after deadline without completing.
2. Consume the parent using a different beneficiary/job or let a collaborator consume it.
3. Settle as an innocent superseded worker and recover the bond.

**Cost:** 1,000 TAIKO locked and late proving/DA/gas. **Intended gain:** avoid a 1,000-TAIKO slash; potentially take an expired bounty. **Requirements at risk:** R3, R6. **Defense:** immutable parent-consumption acceptedAt and revision-retirement times are compared to the deadline; a late child or late exact completion cannot cure the historical default. Bounty returns to sponsor (`roles.html:96–103`; `accountability.html:38–42`). **Result:** default remains slashable; self-reporting recovers at most 100 and sinks 900 TAIKO. **Severity/minimal fix:** none.

### A6 — active-holder private withholding until first canonical reveal

**Preconditions:** attacker is an active bonded publisher, has accepted H, and can privately compute valid Y; honest peers extend soft X.

1. Keep Y's complete executable data/context private while preparing its proof.
2. First publish Y and its proof together when consuming H, attempting to surprise honest provers and invalidate X's work.
3. Alternatively, put only a hash in an earlier stage, choose a fresh salt, or rely on a signed fragment certificate to simulate prior actual publication.

**Cost:** private proof work and canonical DA/gas; a compliant attack also pays prior full DA and waits 360 seconds. **Intended gain:** private execution-input/proving head start, MEV or wasted competing proof work. **Requirements at risk:** R6, with R7's data obligation also relevant. **Defense:** every accepted context must have an immutable matching stage at least 360 seconds old; blob staging checks actual transaction BLOBHASH, calldata staging hashes full bytes, final proof binds the complete staged body and all execution-affecting context, and final action republishes it. Salt cannot backdate a receipt (`staging.html:8–12,57–61,90–95,111–121`; `index.html:153–162`). **Result:** first-reveal-at-acceptance fails even when no holder signs a PublicationClaim and nobody challenges it. **Severity/minimal fix:** none for that trace.

**False-positive/false-negative analysis:** canonical age is an eligibility condition, not an accusation of intent. Its rejection has no confiscatory false positive for a legitimately too-young candidate. An authentic sealed covenant missed because of censorship can slash an honest signer; this is expressly conditional service-default semantics. Prior selective gossip refusal followed by a timely response, private computation before staging, unknown common ownership, pipeline base mismatch and evidence hidden past expiry can escape particular slashes. Detection of those broader behaviors can be zero. The universal data-age condition still applies to their final candidate. A mature public alternative may win and cause a reversible soft rollback; the review did not relabel that permitted behavior as theft.

### A7 — self-enqueue refunds the entire censorship price

**Preconditions:** attacker controls force sender and processing beneficiary and has enough proving resources to win every applicable segment.

1. Enqueue many requests ahead of a victim using split identities.
2. Process its own requests and claim all admission fees back.
3. Recycle the recovered money to maintain or jump the backlog.

**Cost:** at least half of total charged ETH fees is irreversibly sunk, plus gas, proving and two DA publications. **Intended gain:** near-free queue congestion/censorship. **Requirements at risk:** R6 and R7 forced-inclusion closure. **Defense:** fee escrow is credited once, half to beneficiary and half to a permanent sink; immutable FIFO prevents later arrivals overtaking a fixed request. With 1,000 unconsumed entries from empty, 10.5 ETH is charged and 5.25 ETH sunk. With interleaved consumption below 50 pending, the corresponding minima are 1 ETH paid and 0.5 ETH sunk; the document correctly distinguishes them (`index.html:170–175`; `roles.html:218–219`; `arguments.html:73–80`). **Result:** complete refund loop rejected; economically profitable abuse via external MEV remains possible and is not claimed impossible. **Severity/minimal fix:** none for the proposed free/refundable-loop attack.

### A8 — duplicate legacy fee collection or unauthenticated migration head

**Preconditions:** adversary can call public drain/activation functions and submit witnesses, but cannot violate the committed manifest or forge pinned legacy/installation proofs.

1. Commit the same frozen forced entry repeatedly or replay its proof to receive its original ETH fee twice.
2. Activate before pending work or required L2 upgrades are proved.
3. Retain old root-writer authority and overwrite the new accepted checkpoint.

**Cost:** invalid-call gas or a real required proof. **Intended gain:** duplicate old fees or escrow via a fabricated boundary root. **Requirements at risk:** R1, R2, R3, R7. **Defense:** one pending forced proposal, exact cursor/old head, once-only advance and proof-completion credit; READY requires all frozen work finalized; installation is atomic with upgrade; activation checks the authenticated finite drain and installation state, seals it in the same L1 action and changes authority by an irreversible phase predicate (`migration.html:228–245,340–357,380–396`). Historical fee solvency cannot debit unrelated TAIKO bonds. **Result:** rejected by explicit transition checks. **Severity/minimal fix:** none.

## Remaining evidence and review limits

1. **Generated programs and execution encoding:** the document requires full body/KZG, selected Ethereum execution, anchor, force, block-tree and public-input bindings. This review treats those constraints as normative, not optional hints. It has not executed generated programs or proved their soundness. Exact engine/parser artifacts, fork schemas, keys/code hashes and cross-language vectors remain launch work. A future implementation omitting a stated binding would be a real implementation bug, but is not evidence that this specification authorizes the omission.
2. **Migration facts:** the frozen-address and layout design is substantive, but no live proxy manifest, storage snapshot or historical-data availability was authenticated here. A deployment that fails those preflight conditions cannot claim R2 ready merely by retaining address labels.
3. **Economics:** capital-proportional reserves, zero exclusive leases, FIFO and irreversible fee sinks supply concrete mechanisms. They do not ensure small operators win races. Reporter reward may be below gas cost, response front-running can take Q while still publishing the bytes, and simultaneous service demand or censorship can exhaust the conditional response budget. The design explicitly requires funded observers/responders and admits false service defaults outside those bounds. The judge must preserve these qualifications rather than demand either unlimited MEV insurance or an identity oracle.
4. **Fixture verification:** `encoding-vectors.md:3–39` specifies one consistent EtnaPublication type literal, all-uint256 field order and EIP-712 domain, matching `staging.html:199–214` and `accountability.html:10–15,96–105`. Independent Keccak recomputation was attempted, but `Crypto.Hash.keccak` and a local `cast` executable were unavailable. No hash computation or encoder test pass is claimed; no package was installed. This is a verification limit, not a detected fixture error.
5. **Convergence:** this report alone neither establishes two clean independent rounds nor dispositions from the other reviewers. Financial safety has no confirmed finding in this review; broader requirement and convergence verdicts remain with the judge.
