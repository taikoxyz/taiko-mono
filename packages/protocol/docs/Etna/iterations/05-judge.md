### J05-01 — Medium, accepted: zero-rent ordinary capture remains possible

**Source:** R05-C-01, A's realizable domination limit, B-A9. **Exact preconditions:** a dominant producer can fund valid DA/proving/L1 work, choose fresh contexts, and win the eventual zero-price ordering race; no independent valid rent-funded competing transaction consumes the parent before age 900. Capability of another prover alone does not imply its willingness to pay earlier rent. No invalid execution, withheld required DA, identity forgery or violation of Ethereum inclusion is necessary.

**Concrete counterexample to unconditional monopoly deterrence:** (1) a parent accepts at time 0; (2) the actor selects origin 480, stages around 500, and proves the exact forced-compliant child; (3) its stage is mature by 860; (4) it lands at 900 with origin age 420 and stage age 400, paying q=0; (5) repeat from the new head, winning each zero-price race. At 900 the normal proof, stage, freshness, producer and FIFO checks all still apply. This timing was independently recomputed.

**Cost/gain and exploitability:** mechanically feasible in this economic regime; 0 ETH additional rent per captured head, ordinary stage/republication/proof/L1 and possible builder costs remain. Positive net order-flow/fee/MEV value can sustain it. Under the *different* A-COMPETITION(660) regime it instead must win by 660 and sink at least 0.013333333333333334 ETH/head; 100 such wins cost at least 1.3333333333333334 ETH in rent. External value above that cost can still make capture profitable. Neither regime guarantees every race can be won.

**Requirement judgment:** R6 is affected; **no current R ID is violated within the stated bounded guarantee**. R1 gives open objective entry, not free or guaranteed winning entry. Explicitly accept this Medium residual risk because the specification now supplies the mechanism previously missing: a mandatory identity-independent charge for every winner, a reset on every accepted head, an irreversible sink, a precise net-gain threshold, and nonblocking zero-floor recovery. The guarantee is positive cost and negative profit only in its stated competitor/value regime. Acceptance does not assume that regime exists in every real market or waive the mandatory charge on applicable early wins. Empirical viability/calibration remains unverified; descriptions must retain these conditions.

**Evidence:** `index.html:159–161` [rent](../design/index.html#rent); `roles.html:275–282` [economics](../design/roles.html#rent); `arguments.html:27,118–121,166` [assumptions](../design/arguments.html#assumptions), [L4](../design/arguments.html#l4). **Novelty:** the exact zero-floor tactic follows the new rent rule; the underlying concentration risk was already tracked. This is not a renewed absence of an ordinary-control mechanism.

### J05-02 — Medium, accepted: continuous bonded soft service has a capital and publication barrier

**Source:** R05-C-02; overlaps the capital/cost portion of prior J02-03. **Exact preconditions:** customers prefer a continuous bonded branch; a large provider can finance overlapping reserves and mandatory FR/calldata publication, while a smaller entrant can finance only limited buckets. No customer or challenger is required to subsidize the entrant. This is a market-concentration trace, not an adversarial authorization privilege.

**Concrete steps:** (1) both providers enter objectively with 600 TAIKO for a 60-position bucket; (2) each one-fragment block consumes a distinct 10-TAIKO reserve and needs publication within 240 seconds; (3) the small provider exhausts its positions while prior reserves remain locked through the evidence horizon; (4) the large provider maintains overlapping buckets and publication funding, attracts continuous bonded order flow, and may remain dominant. One promise/second has the specified 37,200-TAIKO peak; four promises/second 147,000, before extra custodians/covenants and delayed releases.

**Cost/gain and exploitability:** collateral is refundable but financing cost is real. A maximum 32,768-byte nonzero fragment has a 2,097,152-gas data-floor component, 0.04194304 ETH at 20 gwei, before metadata/storage/execution costs. Smaller fragments cost less; several signers share exact publication. The incumbent may gain coinbase income and private order flow; profitability and actual customer preferences remain unmeasured. No lawful dominance slash exists.

**Requirement judgment:** R3/R4/R6 are affected; **no current R ID is violated by this trace alone**. Explicitly accept Medium concentration risk because duties, backing, rewards, exits and failure consequences are concrete, no global ticket capacity can be bought, and unbonded validated service plus raw canonical entry remains open. R4 is the defined healthy issuance target, not a promise that every600-TAIKO entrant can operate indefinitely. This disposition does not certify that any service market will support the chosen workload: actual one-second execution, dual-proving throughput and publication capacity/funding remain launch gates. Adding an identity oracle or assuming free service would not be a supported mitigation.

**Evidence:** `roles.html:75–91,206–230,288` [registry](../design/roles.html#registry), [parameters](../design/roles.html#parameters), [competition](../design/roles.html#competition); `accountability.html:61` [cost](../design/accountability.html#accounting); `codec.html:176` [unbonded display](../design/codec.html#preconf). **Novelty:** mandatory fragment calldata adds a new cost component; the broader capital concentration limit is tracked, not a new Critical/High.

### J05-03 — Low, accepted: an earlier paid backlog can cause long forced-transaction delay

**Source:** R05-C-03; consistent with A X10/B-A3. **Preconditions and steps:** starting empty, an attacker pays to enqueue 1,000 bounded requests before the victim, without processing them during that construction; the victim then enters behind them. Once all are due/in the relevant snapshot, at most four execute per accepted successor, so reaching the victim requires 251 successors. At the illustrative Δ=660 this component is 165,660 seconds (about 46 hours); the document's conservative snapshot/maturity bound totals 167,340 seconds. Neither cadence nor experienced delay is measured.

**Cost/gain:** the bulk backlog costs 10.5 ETH in force fees, at least 5.25 ETH sunk even if the attacker captures every processing beneficiary, plus enqueue and any self-proving/DA/rent/builder costs. The victim also pays its applicable queue quote. The gain can be delay of a valuable operation. Later arrivals cannot add predecessors, so the trace is not a permanent veto under continued progress. Keeping backlog low by interleaved processing changes both cost and delay and cannot claim the same 1,000-entry standing prefix.

**Requirement judgment:** affects R7 practical censorship escape; **no current R ID is violated** because the specified guarantee is finite eligible FIFO progress, not a uniform short SLA for an arbitrary paid prefix. Accept Low with explicit backlog sensitivity. `index.html:181–186` [force](../design/index.html#forced); `arguments.html:87–95,171` [bound](../design/arguments.html#l2); `roles.html:286` [price](../design/roles.html#competition). This is already acknowledged throughput risk, not a missing ordering rule.
