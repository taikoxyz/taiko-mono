## Rejected counterexamples

All paths below describe hypothetical state transitions in this documentation's model. Costs are protocol-denominated amounts where fixed; actual proving, DA, transaction and opportunity costs remain unmeasured.

### X01 — soft-role cartel installs a withdrawal root (R1, R2, R3, R7)

Preconditions: an actor controls every current publisher, custodian and worker, but not the DAO upgrade authority and cannot forge a validity proof or L1 header.

1. It signs a soft block containing an invented token balance and obtains every advisory custody signature.
2. It attempts to publish that soft root through staging, a challenge response or a direct SignalService call.
3. It asks the retained Bridge/Vault path to release escrow against that root.

**Rejected at step 2:** staging and service evidence cannot call the checkpoint path; the L1 syncer is the phase-appropriate contract, and ACTIVE publication requires atomic Inbox acceptance of the complete proved transition. On L2, the retained Anchor syncer alone supplies the origin checkpoint and the relation requires exact authenticated origin equality. Checkpoint entries reject conflicting overwrite. A fictitious account proof against the actor's own root does not authenticate that root. Step 3 therefore has no trusted root for its forged signal. Cost: role collateral/opportunity cost and failed-call gas; no authorized escrow release. Violated gates: authenticated writer, complete relation, exact parent/origin and checkpoint correspondence. References: `migration.html:24–26,75–82` (#boundary, #checkpoint); `index.html:119–123,142,156` (#landing, #verifier-interface); `staging.html:119–120` (#acceptance); `arguments.html:55–57`.

### X02 — migration replays an already released message or creates a second wrapper (R1, R2)

Preconditions: a valid historical message has already become DONE; its canonical-token key and wrapper mapping exist.

1. A relayer resubmits the same old message after cutover using a preserved trusted checkpoint or signal cache.
2. It tries to reset status through an unused initializer, old fork fallback or owner path.
3. It requests another mint/release, possibly through a new wrapper for the existing key.

**Rejected:** the message hash, enum values, counter/status namespace and VERSION=1 cache/checkpoint namespaces are preserved. Old initializers and generic fallback paths are explicitly disabled; existing canonical keys cannot acquire duplicate wrappers. A preserved cache proves the signal, not permission to ignore DONE. Cost: relaying/failing-call gas; impact is no duplicate release. Violated gates: terminal message status, disabled administrative selectors and preserved canonical mapping. References: `migration.html:142–154,177–183,197–210,217–222` (#slots, #inventory, #authority, #history). A broken historical checkpoint or incompatible actual proxy would violate A-LEGACY/preflight and block launch, not be silently blessed.

### X03 — old/new writers race across activation (R1, R2, R7)

Preconditions: finite legacy work exists and an actor can call every permissionless drain/activation entrypoint.

1. It attempts ACTIVE while a frozen proposal or forced request remains unproved.
2. Alternatively, it supplies a different boundary root or installation digest and asks activation to seal it.
3. After a valid activation it calls the legacy writer or replays activation to replace genesis/reset clocks.

**Rejected:** READY, exact terminal counters/checkpoint/cut, complete migration-proof input and installation-state relation are mandatory. Sealing and phase/head installation are one L1 transaction. The SignalService phase predicate removes legacy authority once ACTIVE, and repeated activation reverts. An unfunded/unavailable old witness leaves the launch blocked safely. Cost: attempted calls/proofs and gas; no alternate custody root or erased legacy obligation. References: `migration.html:234–245,307–320,407–415,444–458` (#migration, #bootstrap, #drain-adapter). This argument does not assume cross-chain atomicity; all required L2 installation state is proved in the certified legacy end.

### X04 — first segment skips or erases the DRAINING Etna queue (R2, R7)

Preconditions: requests 0–8 were admitted into the new queue during DRAINING with paid fees; activation's certified legacy terminal head is sound.

1. The caller selects H0 cut/cursor equal to the new tail to pretend those requests were consumed.
2. Or it submits a first child that consumes no requests and then repeats the zero-cut exception forever.
3. It withdraws their fees as surplus or attempts to publish a zero-origin checkpoint.

**Rejected:** H0 is constructed with exactly cursor/cut/time=0 and its certificate-bound legacy fields; callers cannot choose it. The first real segment is the only consumer of this H0, proves beginEtna against a real post-activation origin and installs the uniquely checked origin queue cut. The next children consume 0–3, 4–7, then 8 when due. Zero origins are recognized only for the exact initial hash/segment-zero parent and are never checkpoint roots. Tail, request mappings, fee liabilities and enqueue times are preserved. Cost: paid enqueue fees and eventual processing gas; impact is a documented one-segment deferral, not erasure. Violated gates: exact bootstrap hash, segment increment/current parent, real ACTIVE origin, unique snapshot and fee escrow. References: `migration.html:290–330` (#bootstrap); `index.html:66,183–186` (#state, #forced); `codec.html:127–132` (#forced).

### X05 — copied proof changes beneficiary, job or accountable owner (R2, R3, R6, R7)

Preconditions: an actor sees another party's valid staged context and final proof/authorization.

1. It copies the final action and substitutes its own beneficiary/job ID or names an innocent publisher as producer.
2. It tries to reuse the original authorization because the execution-only task hash is unchanged.
3. After a genuine producer's key revocation it attempts to erase that accepted producer's historical liability.

**Rejected:** the execution head/task intentionally excludes payment assignment, but the complete final statement, proof input and producer signature bind that assignment. The receipt records successful exact producer authorization/key identity; beneficiary naming is not assent. Revocation prevents unused future acceptance but does not rewrite accepted receipts/keys. Copying the unchanged action preserves its original recipient; a new self-authorized proof for the same public data is permitted competition, not reward theft. Cost: proof/gas/rent if it instead makes a valid competing action; impact on original job is the specified timely cancellation/refund, not stolen bounty. References: `index.html:62–64,119–122,140–142`; `staging.html:57–60,124–157` (#encoding, #authorization); `accountability.html:39–47,57–59` (#canonical-conflict, #jobs).

### X06 — publication creates multiple Q payouts or late data earns Q (R3, R6)

Preconditions: two independently reserved claims promise the same FR/index; each has its own admitted challenge and Q deposit.

1. A responder first publishes the exact complete fragment and FR preimage through challenge A.
2. It replays the bytes through challenge B and attempts to receive Q again.
3. Alternatively, it withholds past A's signed publishBy and answers within an imagined extra 600-second grace period.

**Rejected:** one first receipt funds at most one selected timely response. Challenge B settles individually from the immutable receipt, refunding its opener if timely; a duplicate still checks bytes but creates no publication reward. Deadline=min(opening+600,publishBy), never a fresh grace period. If publication is late for A, Q returns to A's opener and A's reserved default persists; the bytes may satisfy B only if B's own deadline allows it. At the deadline publication is timely; default requires strictly later time. At 20 gwei opening basefee, each Q is 0.176 ETH, backed by its own deposit; no subsidy is minted. A self-response merely reallocates the actor's own Q and costs gas. References: `accountability.html:17–37,75,88–91` (#fragment-publication, #challenge, #multiple, #state-machine); `roles.html:125–126` (#accounting).

### X07 — equivocation/default/conflict evidence multiplies one reserve (R3, R6)

Preconditions: one 10-TAIKO position is implicated in many signatures, an absolute-publication miss, a funded challenge and/or an owner-authorized canonical conflict.

1. Several reporters submit different valid offenses in competing L1 order.
2. The first successful reporter consumes the position reserve.
3. Later reporters try other offenses or another conflicting signature; the owner attempts release of the same collateral.

**Rejected:** all offenses share the same once-only reserve. First slash zeros the selected reserve and moves exactly 1 TAIKO to the applicable reporter/opener and 9 to the permanent sink. Pending Q is refunded on the prior-slash cancellation path; settled ETH outcomes are not allocated again. Later evidence creates no extra payout and slashed collateral cannot be released. For two independently funded publisher positions, at most two reserves may be slashed; that is a backed 20-TAIKO limit. Self-report cost is at least 9 TAIKO per position plus gas; external reporting leaves the signer the full 10-TAIKO loss. References: `roles.html:82–88,123–126` (#registry, #accounting); `accountability.html:28,34–37,49–55,61` (#fragment-publication, #challenge, #offenses, #accounting).

### X08 — a worker's late parent change erases default or the sponsor takes back escrow (R3, R6)

Preconditions: a job is voluntarily accepted with its separate 1,000-TAIKO bond, fixed bounty, live exact parent/stage and objective deadline d; that parent remains unconsumed through d.

1. The worker misses d.
2. At d+1 it lands another child (or an exact but late completion), then presents current-head staleness as an innocent cancellation.
3. The sponsor changes the task or cancels accepted funding before settlement.

**Rejected:** settlement checks the immutable first parent-consumption receipt and first revision-retirement time, not only today's head. Neither late consumption nor late retirement cancels a prior miss. Accepted sponsor terms/bounty cannot change. Default refunds the actual sponsor bounty and allocates the reserved worker bond once (100 TAIKO reporter, 900 sink). In contrast, a different child accepted on or before d cancels without slash; copied exact proof accepted on time pays the bound worker. Cost of intentional default is up to the 1,000-TAIKO bond, or net 900 for colluding self-reporters, plus gas/work; no unbacked bounty. References: `roles.html:95–108` (#jobs); `accountability.html:56–61` (#jobs, #accounting); `staging.html:195–196` (#timing). Freshness acceptance requires both origin and stage survive d+120; it does not certify an impossible sponsor output or subsidize every pipelined task.

### X09 — donation, callback or cross-contract balances break exit conservation (R2, R3)

Preconditions: valid TAIKO/ETH balances and outstanding claims exist; an actor can donate assets and a recipient can reject native/token delivery.

1. The actor donates extra ETH/TAIKO without acquiring a credited claim.
2. It tries to make an equality-to-held-assets check block withdrawals or to claim that donation as surplus.
3. A recipient rejects delivery or tries a repeated withdrawal/settlement to retain both reserve and credit.

**Rejected:** coverage is liabilities≤held assets separately for each contract/asset, never equality and never netting an asset held elsewhere. Donations remain uncredited, with no owner sweep. A slash/settlement cannot leave the same amount in reserve and credit. State-first guarded pull withdrawals preserve claimability on failed delivery and cannot redirect another person's entitlement. Exact received TAIKO amounts are required; rebasing/fee-on-transfer assumptions are not introduced. Cost: donated amount remains unclaimable; delivery failure can delay that recipient's withdrawal but not checkpoint acceptance or prior penalty accounting. References: `roles.html:112–126` (#accounting); `accountability.html:61` (#accounting); `migration.html:131–132,203–204,243,411,419` (#interfaces, #authority, #migration, #drain-adapter). The legacy drain separately requires sufficient original forced-fee escrow and keeps old bond units/withdrawals; it cannot fund ETH fees from unrelated TAIKO.

### X10 — controlled forced-queue processing recovers all spam fees (R3, R6, R7)

Preconditions: one actor controls requester and winning proof beneficiary, and the queue begins empty.

1. It enqueues 1,000 requests without intermediate consumption, paying F(p)=0.001 ETH×(1+floor(p/50)).
2. It processes those requests itself and claims both halves of their fees or replays outcomes to get another credit.
3. It appends more entries before a victim's proof lands to change that proof's required prefix or jump the victim.

**Rejected:** total fee is 10.5 ETH; at most 5.25 ETH can return to the bound beneficiary, with the other 5.25 permanently sunk. Each fee escrow is debited once on exact cursor/outcome processing. New entries cannot change a parent-frozen prefix or become earlier queue indices. Empty-discretionary heads still must process the eligible required work. Cost: at least 5.25 ETH in fee sink for this particular backlog strategy, plus enqueue/DA/proving/landing expenses and canonical rent. Interleaving consumption to keep pending<50 reduces 1,000 fees to 1 ETH and sink to 0.5 ETH; the larger number is not applied to that different strategy. External revenue may exceed either cost. References: `index.html:181–186` (#forced); `codec.html:127–132` (#forced); `roles.html:121,285–287` (#accounting, #competition).

### X11 — admission clock resets without progress or the actor withdraws rent (R1, R3, R6)

Preconditions: a current head has clock r; the actor controls producer, fee beneficiary, sender, payer and many owner addresses.

1. It stages, registers jobs, donates ETH or submits a failing/stale proof to reset r and price out entrants.
2. Alternatively, it wins and tries to claim charged q as beneficiary/coinbase/DAO income rather than refund of excess.
3. It pipelines immediate accepted children under different identities to seek the old decayed quote.

**Rejected:** only complete canonical acceptance resets r. Failure atomically rolls back price/credits/receipt/checkpoint. Charged q enters the separate 100% nonwithdrawable sink; excess belongs only to the immediate caller. Every extra child at the same timestamp faces q(0)=0.05 ETH regardless of identity. A positive-rent frame funds its individually controlled actual sender; the shared sender/nonce example is limited to zero SENDER value, preventing a shared unpartitioned value pool. Cost: k additional same-timestamp accepted children sink k×0.05 ETH plus normal costs; invalid ordinary actions may pay gas but no rent. References: `index.html:159–161,175–177` (#rent, #frames); `roles.html:240–280` (#rent); `arguments.html:108–121`.

### X12 — zero rent and expired origin create an admission deadlock (R1, R7)

Preconditions: no one advances a head for 900 seconds, so q=0; the actor has only an expired old origin/stage/proof.

1. It attempts the old action at zero rent, treating the quote as a freshness waiver.
2. After rejection it asserts that a DAO must restore liveness or refresh the old receipt.
3. A funded outsider instead selects a recent authenticated origin, executes/stages a new child, proves it and waits 360 seconds while this parent stays unchanged.

**Rejected as a deadlock:** step 1 fails independent origin/stage checks. Step 3 is an open path and still pays zero rent if no competing child consumed the parent. Example: head accepted at t=0; fresh origin at t=899; stage at t=1000; maturity at t=1360 leaves origin age461 and stage age360, both within900. Inclusion/proving budgets must fit the remaining window. A competing valid child means canonical progress, with its new quote. Cost: new proof/DA/L1 fees; impact is longer recovery and lost old work, not permanent reservation. References: `staging.html:113,118–120,230–232` (#lifecycle, #acceptance, #recovery); `roles.html:280–282` (#rent); `arguments.html:120`.

### X13 — L1 cadence secretly controls duties or force snapshots (R5)

Preconditions: the same protocol runs with 12-, 6-, 4- or 2-second L1 production and ordinary EVM history behavior.

1. The actor treats a 360-second stage lead or 240-second publication promise as a fixed count of current slots.
2. At 2-second blocks it waits 900 seconds to pin a selected header, beyond BLOCKHASH history, and claims all origins are unusable.
3. An entrant pins the selected header while available and retains that immutable authenticated tuple for later eligibility.

**Rejected:** the actual gates use elapsed timestamps and block identity, with no CL future schedule or slots-to-seconds conversion. A 256-block lookup is approximately512 seconds at a constant2-second interval, so step2 is a failed operational choice, not a permitted late pin. Header pinning may occur during staging/proving and A-INCLUDE(120) fits this illustrative lookup opportunity when scheduled early. At the other constant intervals the corresponding windows are3072/1536/1024 seconds; these illustrative numbers never replace the block-count check. Cost: pin gas; impact of not pinning is rebuilding, not a future-proposer role gate. References: `index.html:68–70,215–216` (#origin, #dependencies); `codec.html:74–77,108` (#l1-header, #anchor); `arguments.html:20,53,155`. Irregular production or unbounded censorship is not covered by a claimed constant wall-clock history window.

### X14 — maturity or resource bounds force a greater-than-one-second soft issuance interval (R4, R7)

Preconditions: a healthy service has complete parent/origin data, adequate measured execution hardware and funding, and the staged/proving pipeline remains within its local unsafe horizon.

1. The actor argues a publisher must wait for L1 stage inclusion/proof completion before signing each next block.
2. It fills the segment byte/gas cap early and argues the publisher must then wait for a new L1 slot.
3. Or it presents unavailable parent/origin state as a successfully checked soft confirmation.

**Rejected as a rule-level coupling:** one-second issuance is local, provisional execution; sealing/staging/proving older segments runs concurrently. Origins may repeat while admissible, so an early resource seal does not itself require a new L1 header. Missing dependencies are UNKNOWN, and reaching the900-block speculative limit stops confirmations rather than extending exposure indefinitely. Cost: service collateral and mandatory fragment-calldata costs, plus execution/proving capacity; loss is possible soft-service suspension/rollback. No measurement was supplied to prove worst-case actual one-second performance. References: `index.html:73–82,190–191` (#preconf, #flows); `codec.html:108,171,174–177` (#anchor, #manifests, #preconf); `arguments.html:23–25,80–82,154`. An unconditional one-second service during total outages is not established by these rules.

## Counterexamples that remain realizable within the explicit limits

These are not rejected traces, but do not contradict the scoped normative theorem or manufacture a waiver of a specified gate.

- **Profitable early domination:** with an honest ready competitor by D=660, (1) an actor races every head successfully by D, (2) pays at least 0.013333333333333334 ETH per win, (3) realizes net external value 0.02 ETH/head before rent. For 100 wins it sinks at least 1.3333333333333334 ETH but obtains 2 ETH net value before rent, leaving at most about0.6666666666666666 ETH profit in this simplified scenario. The rent mechanism supplies concrete cost and a G<Σq deterrence threshold, not a guarantee of diverse winners. Without a willing funded early competitor it can wait 900 seconds and pay zero. `roles.html:275–282` and `arguments.html:118–120` expressly define this boundary. Treating A-FUND or prover existence as A-COMPETITION would be an unsupported argument; this review does not do so.
- **Hidden evidence expires:** (1) a signer misses a duty, (2) every holder hides its authentic signature until after evidenceClose, (3) the unreported reserve releases. Punishment can be zero despite a historical breach. Clients must stop calling it bonded. Already admitted challenges remain settleable; canonical proof/stage/checkpoint rules do not expire with these reserves. `accountability.html:34,61,210`; `roles.html:82,86`. This is finite service coverage, not an undisclosed custody shortcut.
- **Timely public data, losing soft branch:** (1) X is validly signed/executed and its fragments published on time, (2) another owner's fully staged mature Y wins with a sound proof, (3) clients roll back X. No owner-linked conflict offense exists merely because X lost; forced FIFO still applies to Y. Consumers bear provisional application risk. `staging.html:180–181`; `arguments.html:64–65,131–143`.
- **Honest objective default:** (1) a signer accepts publishBy, (2) a reorg/censorship/funding failure prevents canonical publication on time, (3) an authentic reporter settles its10-TAIKO default. The predicate proves noncompletion, not malicious intent. This lies outside the stated timely-inclusion/funding conditions and is not silently excused. `accountability.html:203–205`; `arguments.html:28,141`.
