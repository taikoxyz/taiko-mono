## J03-01 — Medium: the inner execution payload format is not defined

**References:** `design/index.html:31–64,115–121,137–151,160–162`; `design/staging.html:16–61,90–95,118–120`; `design/accountability.html:9–11`; `design/migration.html:124–130`.

The reviewed specification precisely defines `StageContext`, its context/task hash, deterministic stage ID, outer statement and proof digest, blob field-element packing, producer authorization, accepted block-hash tree, and publication-covenant type. It also normatively requires all execution inputs to be public before eligibility. Those are substantive safeguards.

It does **not** define the inner `canonicalBody`/manifest object to which those safeguards refer:

- `index.html:64` lists categories of manifest fields but provides no complete body/block/manifest struct, exact field order, field widths, array layout, version interpretation or transaction/system-envelope representation.
- `staging.html:60` calls the block sequence self-delimiting under a “public versioned encoding,” but neither that page nor the other reviewed files supplies or identifies the complete encoding.
- `manifestRoot` is an input in statements, stages and duties, and the duty text says a manifest commits ordered fragment lengths/digests. There is no exact tag/hash/tree construction fixing what is hashed, fragment boundaries, and the relationship between that fragment manifest and the execution body.
- The outer blob stream fixes a version word, length and padding around body bytes. This does not determine how those bytes decode to the ordered blocks, anchors, transactions and outputs. The publication digest fixture fixes a different object and cannot serve as the body fixture.
- The system-anchor behavior is specified semantically, but `migration.html:130` still leaves its complete engine encoding to be specified. The body format must either derive that envelope unambiguously or reference an exact normative representation; proof/engine implementation and its later audit are separate tasks.

**Concrete consequence and exploitability.** Two independently developed clients can choose different ABI tuple layouts or manifest-root constructions while following every presently enumerated field and using canonical `abi.encode`. They can disagree on decoding the same published bytes, reconstructing the task, computing `manifestRoot`, or generating the required proofs. A funded entrant then needs the incumbent implementation's unstated format convention to reproduce work. Hash collision resistance only fixes bytes; it does not select their unspecified interpretation. Fragment-serving validation also cannot be reproduced independently without the manifest construction.

No forged proof, stolen custody, hash collision or demonstrated chain-wide exploit follows solely from this omission. A faithful implementation of one well-defined complete format can make the intended mechanisms sound. That is why **Medium specification/interoperability severity**, rather than High or Critical, is supported. The issue nevertheless blocks a complete implementation-ready design: A-IMPLEMENTATION cannot mean “implement the unspecified format correctly,” and A-RETRIEVE cannot itself supply the missing public decoder.

This is distinct from A's valid observation that hypothetical code omitting an expressly required binding is not a specification exploit. The required *semantic completeness* is written; the normative *representation and root function* are absent. Neither a future parser benchmark nor a claim that the proof binds `bodyHash` fills them.

**Required correction, within the documentation-only task:**

1. Choose one exact versioned canonical body and per-block schema, with integer widths, field order, array/byte encoding, bounds and malformed/noncanonical rejection rules. State which Ethereum typed envelopes and header/fork rules are referenced, and how the first/system anchor envelope is generated or represented without private authorization.
2. Fix the precise `manifestRoot` construction, domain/tag and fragment split/order/length rules, and its exact relationship to `bodyHash`, the staged context, soft claims and blob payload. Exclude self-referential hashes or private late-selected execution fields explicitly.
3. State which data are carried versus deterministically derived, and show that an independent reader can reconstruct all execution inputs from public data plus the authenticated parent/history. The same decoded execution must apply to calldata and blob transport.
4. Add a small reproducible documentation fixture for the chosen serialization and commitments, with boundary/rejection examples sufficient to expose ordering, padding, missing-fragment and cycle ambiguities. This requires no production contract/client implementation or proof benchmark.

**Disposition:** fix and review the concrete specification. Do not simply mark this risk accepted while declaring R6/R7 fully passing. Generated proof programs, cross-language implementation tests and gas/proving measurements can remain future launch work once the format exists.

## C-01 — selective serving is real; the claimed new High is not established

### Exact promise and cost

C correctly identifies the following written limits. An early publisher `DutyClaim` promises public recovery of its exact fragment through the funded challenge mechanism; an included challenge has a 600-second response period. A sealed context's `issuedAt+240` publication covenant is separate and optional. X need not receive that latter covenant merely because its fragments were signed. Cross-owner or mismatched-immediate-base canonical conflicts are outside the bounded attribution predicate. These facts are not denied or converted into a universal dissemination assurance.

Thus an active publisher can selectively deny X to some peers, answer a valid challenge before its actual deadline, and preserve X's reserve. Its 600-TAIKO bucket is locked and later refundable to the extent unslashed; X uses its own 10-TAIKO position. Y's raw producer can self-authorize under another owner with no additional service bond. Y still costs actual staging, proof work, complete republication and L1 fees. If the attacker answers X's challenge, it earns that challenge's Q but must publish the complete matching bytes and pay response gas. Q is the purchased publication fee, not an unbacked protocol reward. No general profitability conclusion follows from these costs.

This is a **600-second recovery promise, not an absolute public-publication promise starting at issuance and not a promise to preserve X's canonical ordering**. Applications must not display the stronger guarantee. A publisher that fails the admitted serving deadline is punishable; a publisher that accepted and missed the sealed deadline is separately punishable even after late disclosure. A compliant response does not prove earlier P2P broadcasting.

### Why C's consequence does not establish the claimed violation

1. **Y is no longer privately unavailable at its opportunistic landing.** In C's own trace, Y's full data/context are public around t=60 and cannot become eligible before about t=420. A funded entrant can retrieve and prove Y without the original holder's assent under the specified premises. This is the universal rule that also applies to zero-collateral producers, not an optional covenant offered only by cooperative attackers.
2. **X has an honest-data-holder path in the stated precondition.** C requires a user to have validated X with its complete bytes. That user can relay those bytes to other provers or answer an admitted challenge; any responder may do so. The publisher therefore does not have cryptographic exclusive possession of X. If the recipient does not relay, temporary selective market access can remain, but the trace cannot assume that the protocol prohibits or needs the attacker's authorization for this public recovery.
3. **The identified rollback can occur with X public immediately.** With X and Y both publicly staged and mature, either valid child can win. Withholding X is not necessary for Y to consume their parent or invalidate an X proof. C establishes an opportunity to favor one provisional branch's dissemination, not a promised right for the other branch to survive. Its proposed gain—Y ordering, soft rollback and wasted competing work—overlaps the accepted mature-branch race and economic risks rather than bypassing the intended public-data predicate.
4. **No finite post-issuance X deadline generally precedes a competitor.** Y may already be mature when X is issued. Requiring X publication by issuance+240 would improve X's recovery time but would still permit Y to settle first. The proposed “early enough to precede a competing branch's earliest eligibility” property needs an additional canonical priority/reservation restriction, or cannot hold generally. It is not supplied merely by shortening X's recovery deadline.
5. **The attributable conflict component is already scoped and dispositioned.** A same-owner, same-immediate-parent, same-height conflict is punishable with exact accepted membership evidence. Distinct-owner and pipeline misses overlap J02-02. This judgment does not claim that the narrow predicate links an economic actor's Sybils or enforces a general promise never to reverse any future soft history.

**Decision:** C's selective-serving sequence is a valid limitation of the offered service. It is **not a new High**, nor a demonstrated missing mandatory duty under the permitted provisional-state/objective-surrogate interpretation. It is not rejected merely because the design mentions it: the independent public-data admission gate, third-party recovery path, actual contracted deadline and absence of canonical priority explain the result. Adding an absolute per-fragment publication covenant is a possible stronger service, with additional publication/capital cost; it is not necessary to prevent first private disclosure in the canonical action, and it would not guarantee X beats an already-mature Y.

If a broader product requirement demanded every bonded X become publicly retrievable within a fixed interval from issuance, independent of challenge, the present optional covenant would not satisfy that different promise. This report does not attribute that promise to the existing signed duty or silently advertise it. The allowed requirement interpretation must remain the explicit one below.

## R6: prevention, punishment and the “any participating party” boundary

The acceptable interpretation is neither blanket immunity for active holders nor a claim to slash anyone who privately computes a block. It consists of these independently testable obligations:

| Actor/action | Mandatory prevention or attributable penalty | Precise limit |
|---|---|---|
| Any canonical producer, including a raw zero-collateral submitter or an active service holder using another address, attempts first disclosure of the complete candidate at acceptance | Universal matching actual-data stage, 360-second age, exact-context binding, full republication and proof checks reject the action. No service signature or reporter is needed for that admission rule. | This is prevention, not confiscation of nonexistent collateral. It depends on closing J03-01's exact public interpretation. Earlier compliant private computation is not the prohibited first-reveal event. |
| Any funded publisher/custodian fails an authenticated serving duty | Anyone can admit a funded challenge; an unmet included deadline slashes the reserved position once. Another holder's timely exact publication fulfills the promised public availability. | No claim of personal server uptime, universal P2P delivery, or receipt before the agreed response deadline. |
| Any holder accepts a sealed publication covenant and misses it | Immutable earliest-publication lookup or absence supplies permissionless default evidence; late publication cannot cure it. | The covenant is optional and sealed-context-specific. This mechanism alone would be insufficient to protect canonical admission from an unsigned withholder; the universal stage gate supplies that protection. |
| Publisher signs conflicting reserved promises or authorizes a conflicting canonical block within the specified owner/base/height scope | Original signatures and, where needed, authenticated accepted block membership slash the reserved positions. Revocation cannot erase historical assent. | Pipeline and distinct-economic-owner attribution misses remain the accepted Medium limit; victim-beneficiary naming cannot frame an innocent publisher. |
| Worker accepts a funded proof job and defaults while it remained applicable | Historical parent-consumption/revision-retirement records determine objective cancellation versus a 1,000-TAIKO default. | Raw proof producers make no deadline promise by merely having the ability to prove; a lost canonical race is not automatically misconduct. |
| Stager publishes generic data, or an unbonded relay/prover stops participating | Staging grants no canonical reservation, checkpoint, subsidy or exclusive admission right; anyone can construct, stage and prove a replacement. | Mere publication or raw relay creates no invented service reserve. An unbound actor cannot be slashed for an undertaking it never authenticated. It still cannot waive canonical DA/proof/FIFO conditions. |

This meaning of “any participating party” covers each attributable accepted duty across its roles, without a privileged exempt signer, and universally prevents the targeted private-first-reveal canonical action. It does **not** promise punishment for an unobservable historical P2P decision that nevertheless complied with the actual public-availability deadline. Punishing every valid losing/winning soft branch would add a different guarantee and could frame honest participants. An accepted interpretation cannot be used to excuse a bypass of universal data eligibility or an actual authenticated deadline breach.

**Predicate-specific FP/FN:** under correct complete format/crypto/L1 enforcement, a first-reveal canonical candidate is rejected deterministically. A known authentic covenant missed and reported within its evidence window defaults deterministically; a late stage cannot erase it. An unmet admitted fragment challenge similarly defaults given timely reporting/settlement. Hidden signatures, evidence after expiry, selective earlier service followed by timely recovery, and cross-owner/base conflicts can yield zero detection of those broader behaviors. Honest censorship, excessive fees or failed observation/inclusion premises can yield 100% false attribution of *malicious intent* while still proving an actual contracted service default. No empirical error rate is asserted. Under the named funding, observation and inclusion bounds, a conforming feasible response avoids false service default.

**Economics:** zero exclusive lease/ticket capacity prevents role purchase from becoming a veto; additional simultaneous duties require additional reserve; self-reporting loses at least 9 of 10 TAIKO per position or 900 of a 1,000-TAIKO job; each force fee has a 50% irreversible sink; fixed FIFO predecessors cannot be overtaken. Starting-empty 1,000-request backlog fees are 10.5 ETH with 5.25 ETH sunk, while interleaved processing below 50 pending lowers those figures to 1 ETH and 0.5 ETH and consumes FIFO work. These are concrete bounded costs. They neither ensure dispersed market share nor make every MEV-funded attack unprofitable. J02-03 remains accepted with exactly that limitation.
