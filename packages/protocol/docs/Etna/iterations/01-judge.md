## J-01 — High: an active publisher can cause the targeted private-branch disruption without a punishable breach

**References:** [architecture, Chosen boundary and Claims to try to falsify](../design/architecture.md), lines 7 and 47; [core preconfirmation/landing rules](../design/index.html), lines 71–73 and 103; [publisher duties](../design/roles.html), lines 49 and 77; [offenses and detection limits](../design/accountability.html), lines 19–23 and 118–122; [W1](../design/arguments.html), lines 87–96.

**Reproduced trace.** M opens a funded publisher bucket, signs valid public branch X from accepted H, and promptly serves every promised byte. Honest users execute X and an honest worker begins proving it. M privately constructs conflicting Y from H, issues no DutyClaim for Y, and lands Y's sound proof and complete DA before X. M may actually control the same publisher owner and proof-beneficiary address; a Sybil is unnecessary to perform the attack. Y consumes H, X's proof becomes stale and X's soft users roll back.

The accuser possesses signed X and an accepted Y receipt, but the offense predicates require two signed claims, an unanswered admitted challenge, or a missed applicable proof job. X's bytes remain available. Y has no signed data duty and is public at landing. Neither a position-equivocation proof nor an availability default exists. A timely replacement also cancels an innocent X worker's job liability; it does not reimburse its wasted work.

**Exploitability and severity.** M pays computation, DA and L1 race costs and may gain MEV, private-order-flow advantage or strategic destruction of competing proof work. No positive net profit is asserted without measurements. The demonstrated fact is **zero additional withholding penalty** while the exact active-holder disruption requested in the brief occurs under sound proofs, healthy delivery of X, and working Ethereum. This is High because an explicit adversarial requirement has no applicable defense or adverse consequence, not because a soft reorg steals finalized custody.

**R1–R7 impact.** R6 fails. R1's public entry and R7's atomic settlement can both operate correctly during this attack. R4 permits soft rollback; J-01 does not add hard preconfirmation finality to R4. The attack does not forge the R2 checkpoint or disprove finite forced FIFO progress.

**Timeout adjudication.** The existing timeout is a valid objective surrogate for the duty it actually specifies: failing to publish matching bytes within 600 seconds after an included funded challenge. Under the stated response/inclusion assumptions, such a missed duty is detected deterministically. This does not make its coverage universal. Serving X, never signing Y, or revealing challenged bytes before the deadline gives this strategy a 100% false-negative worst case for earlier withholding. The user's permission to use surrogates authorizes a practical evidence mechanism; it does not authorize silently replacing the requested attack coverage with an unrelated narrower duty.

The problem therefore needs a mechanism change or an explicitly authorized scope relaxation. More precise wording is necessary for honesty but is **insufficient to turn R6 into a pass**.

**Repair conditions.** A revised mechanism must identify the enforceable obligation or admission predicate that changes this trace, name the attributable actor, give permissionless evidence/payout rules, and analyze honest races, Sybil ownership, delayed inclusion and all-holder failure. A partial same-owner rule must not infer authorization merely because Y's proof names that owner as beneficiary: a malicious prover can choose another address as beneficiary. Proof-bound payment prevents later substitution; it does not authenticate that recipient's consent to Y. Punishment needs an explicit owner/key authorization or equivalent authenticated provenance. Such a rule must still disclose cross-owner bypass rather than calling it universal accountability.

Do not punish every losing X publisher: an outsider could then win Y and slash an honest broadcaster. Do not assume custody attestations give X canonical priority when the admission rule does not check them.

## J-02 — Medium: permitted early sealing exposes a dependency on L1 header cadence

**References:** [architecture, Canonical data and Forced FIFO](../design/architecture.md), lines 12–14 and 23, plus the byte bound at line 29; [core origin, soft issuance and resource rules](../design/index.html), lines 59–64, 71, 128 and 134–139.

**Reproduced trace.** With healthy 12-second L1 production and adequate execution/proving, a publisher uses the newest usable origin O. It admits a valid large transaction so the resulting block fits the 122,880-byte segment bound but leaves insufficient encoded space for the next empty block's header/anchor. The allowed early-seal rule closes that segment. One second later, a child segment requires origin O′ strictly newer than O, but Ethereum has not yet produced such a header. A fabricated future header is not locally verifiable; the next VALID_IN_VIEW issuance must wait. Repeated near-full segments exhaust any finite stash of older usable origins.

**Exploitability and severity.** The sender pays ordinary L2 fees and needs the publisher to admit the permitted large block. Large calldata can approach the byte bound while fitting the specified block gas limit; an eventual encoding must give the exact byte vector. No censorship, slow prover, partition or broken cryptography is needed. This is bounded soft-service degradation, not a permanent accepted-chain halt, so Medium is appropriate.

**R1–R7 impact.** R4's one-second healthy issuance rule is not established for all admitted workloads. R5 has no discovered CL lookahead or forbidden slot timer, but its broad claim of cadence independence needs qualification until this dependency is removed. R7's data-plus-proof atomicity and R2 custody are unaffected.

**Required correction.** Specify either (a) admission/packing reserves for system and empty blocks through the promised origin-acquisition interval, with honest definitions of transaction admission/backpressure, or (b) an origin-reuse rule with a new queue-snapshot/progress argument. The fixed 600-second freshness bound may help that argument; do not simply remove advancement without reviewing all consumers. A measured faster engine alone cannot repair absent headers. A's observation that early sealing reconciles 60 blocks with the aggregate gas cap is correct, but does not answer this distinct origin-exhaustion trace.

## J-03 — Medium economic gate: concrete deterrence remains uncalibrated and costly to small services

**References:** [architecture, Resource and economic baseline](../design/architecture.md), lines 24 and 31–37; [role entry and anti-monopoly limits](../design/roles.html), lines 48–58 and 163–194; [liability bounds](../design/accountability.html), lines 23 and 29–40; [arguments, economic and FIFO limits](../design/arguments.html), lines 52, 66–71, 106 and 115–116.

The arithmetic is established: one separately reserved 100-TAIKO promise per second with 604,800 seconds of evidence liability requires approximately **60.48 million TAIKO** continuously reserved, plus active issuance, extra fragments, and any separate custodians. The indivisible opening bucket also requires 6,000 TAIKO even when fewer than 60 positions are used. Splitting ownership does not reduce the aggregate collateral for an equal promise count, but it bypasses the same-owner double-sign link. A raw canonical prover need not incur any of these optional service costs.

A better-funded or faster prover can repeatedly land smaller valid successors before transaction-rich public competitors, while processing all mandatory forced entries. This consumes competing computation and can concentrate discretionary revenue without violating admission or proof correctness. Open rights, zero exclusivity and FIFO remove particular protocol barriers; they do not establish a calibrated economic deterrent to this strategy.

**Severity adjudication.** C-02's High monopoly implication is not established: the record lacks token-liquidity, opportunity-cost, workload, demand, market-share and profitability evidence. Cross-owner independence was never guaranteed. Nevertheless the cost mismatch and missing calibration are material to the expressly required R6 economics. This remains a Medium **blocking evidence/design gate**, not an accepted risk that completes R6. R1 is not falsified merely because participation costs capital; R3's objective entry/exit remains mechanically specified, while practical service replacement and R4 sustainability need this evidence.

**Force-fee recycling, merged from C-03.** A flooder/prover adding 1,000 requests to an empty queue locks 10.5 ETH in escalating force fees, plus actual enqueue gas. If it later wins all processing proofs, the 10.5 ETH returns to its proof-bound beneficiary. Its net cost is computation, DA, enqueue/landing gas, capital time and fees captured by outsiders, not a 10.5-ETH burn. A victim behind those 1,000 entries needs at least 251 consuming successors. This is an economic scenario for calibration, not indefinite bypass of a fixed FIFO item. Later arrivals cannot increase the victim's predecessors.

C-03 omits a term when quoting the draft's conservative bound. With p=1,000 and Δ=360 seconds, the specified expression is

`720 + (1 + ceil(1001 / 4)) × 360 = 91,440 seconds = 25.4 hours`.

That is an illustrative conditional upper bound under a stated maximum successor spacing, not an observed delay, lower bound, or universal forced-inclusion SLA. Neither a processing reward nor a refundable queue fee was specified to be a burned congestion charge. A nonrefundable component is one possible adjustment only after comparing recovery incentives and net attack cost; the judgment does not mandate it without that analysis.

**Required closure.** Define the intended deterrence target, legitimate small-service workload and maximum working capital. Model same-actor publisher/prover/worker/flooder combinations, proof-cost asymmetry, MEV benefit ranges and price/liquidity sensitivity. Justify reserve granularity, retention, rewards and queue pricing against those results. Do not substitute address caps, a claimed unbiased lottery, or zero exclusivity alone for this evidence. No universal economic-owner diversity theorem is demanded.

## J-04 — Low: unsolicited assets contradict the published raw-balance equality

**References:** [roles, Conservation](../design/roles.html), lines 101–106; [arguments S4](../design/arguments.html), line 51; compare the correct inequality in [accountability, Conservation](../design/accountability.html), line 29.

An outsider can transfer one token-wei directly to the registry, or deliver unsolicited ETH, without creating any internal credit. Raw held assets then exceed the unchanged sum of reserves, credits, bounties, free balances and sink. The equality in roles/S4 is false, although the registry remains solvent. If later implemented as a raw-balance equality assertion, this could freeze legitimate settlement/exits cheaply; no such runtime assertion or deployed freeze has been demonstrated.

Use `accountedLiabilities <= heldAssets` consistently, define the difference as uncredited surplus, and retain exact conservation between internal ledgers. Deposits credit only their attributable received delta. Donations confer no withdrawal entitlement, and settlement must not depend on raw-balance equality. This is Low now; its potential R3 exit/R6 accounting consequence is conditional on an unsafe future implementation.

## What must change next

1. **Resolve J-01 through a substantive mechanism revision before another convergence claim.** A nonexclusive public-data staging rule is a viable research candidate: actual blob/calldata publication receives an immutable L1 timestamp; staging grants no head reservation, sequence priority or checkpoint; final acceptance still contains the complete matching data and valid proof in one action. This changes the observable transcript and therefore is not excluded by revision 0's indistinguishability argument. It has **not** been reviewed as a fix in this round.
2. **Make any staging revision precise enough to falsify.** Specify exact content/manifest/parent binding, global first-publication semantics, cryptographic attribution of signed deadline duties, deadline and expiry interactions, reorg behavior, archive assumptions, fees/duplicate publication, and all-holder recovery. A proposed 120-second public age or 240-second signed publication deadline must be analyzed against the actual proving window. Public age alone does not punish every earlier private computation or every resulting stale competitor; M may precompute before staging. Late-duty punishment only covers a duty whose signer, content and due time are objectively bound. Report this residual FN scope instead of claiming automatic R6 closure.
3. **Resolve J-02 in normative admission/origin rules and J-04 in all accounting statements.** Review the revised FIFO proof if origins can be reused. Preserve immediate execution validation and explicit UNKNOWN.
4. **Supply J-03's quantitative model and choose defensible parameters.** Account for fee recycling, small-entry capital, optional-service participation and same-actor role combinations. Do not represent general market diversity as already proved.
5. **Retain the implementation/deployment/performance gates with concrete closure evidence.** Circuit/public-input conformance, exact live migration and benchmarks remain required for readiness. Documentation review does not imply those experiments were run.
6. **Review the changed mechanism in the next independent round.** Repeating unchanged W1 reports cannot produce progress or two clean rounds. Do not accept the High as a mere documented risk, and do not declare all literal requirements impossible to avoid investigating a materially different construction.

## Precise negative result and smallest relaxation

The proven negative result is narrow: **with revision 0's always-open admission, optional duty transcript and no extra publication/order/authorization evidence, the chain cannot distinguish histories that present the same served X claims and accepted Y data/proof solely by whether Y was publicly gossiped earlier.** No on-chain classifier of that unchanged transcript can reliably punish one history while exempting the other. The result is about available evidence, not about every possible permissionless based protocol.

It is **not** a theorem that literal R1–R7 are mutually inconsistent. The user permits objective surrogates. A permissionless additional publication predicate changes the evidence. A separately justified ordering/attestation mechanism may change the sequencing assumptions. Both require full liveness/accountability analysis; neither is ruled out merely by calling this candidate “strict based.”

Literal R7 requires the canonical action to carry data and proof together. It does not, by itself, establish the stronger rule that no copy of the data may have appeared publicly earlier. A generic prior DA transaction which grants no canonical order is therefore worth testing against R7, while acknowledging its duplicate cost and any wider user-intent interpretation. A prior action that reserves canonical sequence/head rights is a different, stronger change and needs explicit scrutiny of the intended replacement of separate propose/prove phases. This judgment does not silently approve either construction.

The smallest **scope relaxation preserving revision 0 unchanged** is to require punishment only for objectively breached, preexisting signed service obligations, expressly allowing valid private winners and their unpunished soft rollback. That would narrow the active-holder guarantee; it is **not authorized merely because timeouts and false-negative analysis were allowed**. Until a revised mechanism meets the brief or the user explicitly accepts that precise narrowing, the proper disposition is **R6 fail; not ready**, with the other gates reported as above.
