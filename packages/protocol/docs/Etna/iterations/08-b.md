## Verdict

**No new Critical or High normative liveness defect established in this pass.** R1–R7 have conditionally adequate specification mechanisms under the explicitly stated execution, proving, funding, inclusion, archive and migration premises. This does not certify the premises or make soft confirmations final. In particular, both proof backends, maximum-cost forced operations, migration compatibility and sustainable one-second service remain unverified.

The anchor-removal recovery mechanism survives the principal expiry attack: forcing a reveal against an old timestamp can fail indefinitely, but forcing `pinCurrentOrigin()` records the origin of its eventual processing block. A subsequent timestamp-zero reveal has no ring deadline. No incumbent signature or DAO operation is needed for that route. The fixed forced prefix and origin freshness together stop an advancing chain from indefinitely avoiding a particular admissible request.

Feasible **Medium residual risks** remain: selective withholding within the publication deadline; repeated loss of ordinary proofs/soft branches; expensive paid queue congestion; and profitable concentration despite rent. Total loss of required proving or archived history can have **High availability impact**, but is an expressly identified premise failure rather than a newly discovered missing escape predicate. No safety bypass should be added to conceal those failures.

## Concrete attempted counterexamples

### B1. Withhold a branch, reveal only at acceptance, then invalidate public work

**Prerequisites:** attacker can execute/prove a valid branch Y from current H and fund L1 DA/gas/rent. Honest workers prepare X from H. No broken cryptography is needed.

**Actions:** (1) compute Y privately; (2) withhold its complete body/context; (3) submit Y's full proof and bytes before X lands; (4) attempt to consume H and strand X.

**Resources/cost and gain:** normal proving, publication and admission costs; potential ordering revenue, discarded honest proofs, and rollback of an unsafe suffix. Avoiding service registration avoids optional service penalties, not canonical checks.

**Result:** the first-disclosure-at-landing step is **blocked**. Acceptance requires an actual matching stage at least 360 seconds old, complete context matching, fresh origin and full same-mode republication: `staging.html:90–93,118–120`; `index.html:122,165–168`. A hash-only blob assertion, alternative private manifest, or fresh salt cannot provide old actual-data eligibility. The acyclic execution/context relation is explicit at `staging.html:57–61` and `codec.html:159–165`.

**Remaining feasible variant:** stage Y publicly, wait 360 seconds, then win normally. An attacker can also selectively gossip a bonded fragment until just before `issuedAt+240`, publish it and its exact FR preimage on time, and incur no default. Honest nodes lacking dependencies return UNKNOWN, not a fabricated confirmation. If a signed duty really misses the deadline, anyone with timely evidence can slash its 10-TAIKO reserve; a colluding reporter still loses 9 TAIKO net. See `accountability.html:26–29,203–210`; `roles.html:288`.

**Requirements/severity:** attempted R6/R7 bypass fails; R4 service can degrade during the expressly permitted disclosure interval. **Medium residual**, not a missing publication predicate: the design does not promise universal one-second gossip or selection of X. No custody root is authenticated by that soft promise.

### B2. All incumbent services disappear or reserve every available proof job

**Prerequisites:** reconstructible accepted H; L1 inclusion and a new funded prover capable of both required backends. All existing publishers/custodians/workers either disappear or accept and abandon jobs.

**Actions:** (1) incumbents stop producing/serving; (2) old stages expire and workers withhold proofs; (3) outsider reconstructs H, selects a fresh origin, builds/stages a child, proves it and submits without any incumbent authorization; (4) settle abandoned jobs independently.

**Resources/cost and gain:** each abandoned accepted job locks 1,000 TAIKO and defaults if the parent/revision remains applicable through its deadline; self-reporting recovers only 100. The attack can destroy a selected soft service and waste sponsor time, but cannot buy a canonical reservation. A replacement funds computation, DA and gas; positive rent is at most 0.05 ETH, or zero after an unchanged parent ages 900 seconds.

**Result:** permanent incumbent lockout is **blocked** by open staging/submission and self-authorized producer entry (`roles.html:15–17,53–62`; `staging.html:154–155,229–232`). Jobs never gate raw submission (`roles.html:99–109`). A concrete idle-chain recovery schedule can pin a recent origin, execute a small recovery block, stage it and wait maturity while proving. Recovery block timestamps may leave a gap (`codec.html:61`); there is no need to recreate every missed one-second block. For example, relative to a fresh origin O, pin by O+120, stage the small executed candidate by O+240, mature at O+600 and land by O+720; parallel proving within 180 seconds fits. This illustrative construction presupposes available parent state and a valid block timestamp in `(parentTime,O+60]`, and is not a measured SLA.

**Requirements/severity:** no R1/R3/R7 specification failure. **Medium residual** soft-service outage and funded replacement delay. If *every capable prover*, including potential entrants, lacks either required backend, progress halts: both proofs are mandatory (`index.html:129`; `arguments.html:24`). That has **High availability impact under an explicit failed premise**, not an incumbent veto or a newly missing timeout bypass. DAO action cannot manufacture a sound proof.

### B3. Exhaust the 900-second proof window and trap zero-rent recovery

**Prerequisites:** current H remains unchanged; a candidate fixes origin time O. Attacker delays its own data, optional worker, or transport; an honest payer hopes to wait for zero rent.

**Actions:** (1) stage at O+541; (2) wait the required 360 seconds; (3) attempt acceptance at O+901; (4) try a new stage salt or zero-rent quote to revive the proof.

**Resources/cost and gain:** one unusable stage plus optional computation; delayed user progress and wasted proving/DA. Abandoning an accepted feasible job additionally risks its fixed worker bond. Mere publishing of unusable work has no global reservation effect.

**Result:** the candidate really is infeasible: stage maturity starts after origin expiry. This interaction is explicitly specified (`staging.html:113,118–119`), so neither restaging identical execution nor paying rent cures it. **Permanent recovery deadlock is blocked:** build with a fresh origin/context, stage/prove again, and retain zero rent while H remains unchanged (`roles.html:281`; `arguments.html:121`). No current-parent clock reset occurs on reads, stages, jobs or failed submissions (`roles.html:267–268`). If someone changes H, a valid successor already made canonical progress.

An accepted job cannot begin with this already-impossible freshness schedule: both origin and selected stage must survive `deadline+120`; the deadline is `max(acceptance+300,stage+480)` (`roles.html:100`; `staging.html:195–196`). A raw candidate may remain viable when a new optional job is conservatively unavailable; off-protocol funding/raw proving is explicitly permitted.

**Requirements/severity:** no R1/R3/R7 deadlock. **Medium residual** repeated expiry under congestion/prover delay, expressly conditional on inclusion/proving/funding; **Low** generic unusable-stage spam because selection is discretionary and every publication costs L1 resources. Normative repair is unnecessary for this trace; measuring the actual joint timing budget is necessary before launch.

### B4. Append force requests during proving; then churn empty proofs using old snapshots

**Prerequisites:** request Q is enqueued at t with finite earlier backlog p. Attacker can enqueue more requests and win proof races.

**Actions:** (1) append requests immediately before each honest proof arrives, hoping to change its mandatory input; (2) alternatively accept empty-discretionary successors with repeatedly old origins; (3) try to keep Q forever below an unadvanced cut or behind later requests.

**Resources/cost and gain:** every enqueue pays `0.001 ETH*(1+floor(pending/50))`; canonical churn needs valid proofs, mature actual DA and rent. Filling 1,000 entries from empty without processing costs 10.5 ETH, at least 5.25 irrecoverable even when the attacker later receives every processor credit. Interleaving to keep pending below 50 instead costs at least 1 ETH/1,000 entries, with 0.5 ETH sunk; the larger example must not be applied to that strategy (`roles.html:287`). Delay or ordinary-ordering revenue may justify these costs.

**Result:** both perpetual-starvation constructions are **blocked while canonical progress continues**. The parent fixes exact due work; later arrivals do not invalidate it. Origin freshness eventually forces a snapshot at/after t+120, and every child consumes up to four earliest due items. Raw bytes and deterministic outcomes are proof-bound; discretionary transactions cannot precede the prefix (`index.html:184,187–190`; `codec.html:117–121`). A child cannot shrink its first-block gas limit to avoid four bounded forced requests.

The remaining congestion is real. Under a separately justified accepted-successor bound Δ, processing occurs by `t+1020+(1+ceil((p+1)/4))*Δ`; p=9 and Δ=660 gives 3,660 seconds. Later arrivals cannot increase Q's predecessor count. This is conditional, not a universal fast-force SLA (`arguments.html:88–96`). Reverting application calls still count as execution attempts; a bad nonce or inadequate balance can cause proved rejection, not guaranteed success.

**Requirements/severity:** no R7 moving-target or indefinite advancing-chain starvation failure. **Medium residual** purchased backlog and bounded force capacity; arbitrary-size bridge calls are outside the explicit 2,048-byte/one-million-gas escape. Funding and measurement of worst-case forced work remain premises, not proof supplied by nominal bounds.

### B5. Let checkpoint timestamps expire before every forced reveal

**Prerequisites:** origin H was recorded at L2 timestamp u, never pinned/revealed. Malicious producers can delay discretionary reveal transactions or the chain is offline long enough for later timestamp advancement.

**Actions:** (1) let a later block at u+8191 overwrite that ring residue; (2) process `revealCheckpoint(u,headerH)` only after expiry; (3) repeat against newly selected fixed timestamps to strand bridge users.

**Resources/cost and gain:** delay through ordering dominance/paid queue congestion, or merely exploit a long outage; no forged hash is needed. Gain is delayed inbound checkpoint availability and extra user gas. A timestamp jump can collide after one successor, so counting 8,191 blocks would be unsound.

**Result:** the fixed-timestamp reveal **does fail**, as specified (`checkpoints.html:19,94,108`). But indefinite checkpoint starvation via that expiry is **blocked** by the following concrete fallback: (a) use a dedicated funded sender with sufficient fee cap and no competing nonce use; (b) force the four-byte `pinCurrentOrigin()` call; (c) when processed, obtain H2 from its authenticated processing header/pin state; (d) retrieve headerH2; (e) force `revealCheckpoint(0,headerH2)`. The permanent pin does not expire. No sender must predict H2 in the first request (`checkpoints.html:86,109,123–125`; `arguments.html:126`). The worst allowed 1,536-byte header has a specified ≤1,845-byte signed type-2 reveal envelope, within the queue limit. Its actual ≤1M gas feasibility still requires measurement.

**Requirements/severity:** no R1/R2/R7 normative deadlock from ring expiry. **Medium residual** recovery delay, duplicated calls and archive/funding dependence. This closes the specific expiry problem, not every bridge operation's size or execution requirements. A missing permanent-pin predicate would have been a blocker; it is present.

### B6. Pin a checkpoint predating the victim's L1 signal

**Prerequisites:** a source signal S exists on L1 before the user enqueues a valid forced pin Q. Attacker controls canonical ordering and chooses the oldest admissible origins.

**Actions:** (1) continue choosing origins before S; (2) eventually process Q while trying to pin such an old origin; (3) force the user to reveal a root in which S is absent.

**Resources/cost and gain:** valid successor production, rent and ordering control; desired gain is censorship without violating literal pin semantics.

**Result:** **blocked** when the pin is processed. Q becomes mandatory only under a parent snapshot at/after its enqueue, which follows S. Child origin number/time cannot decrease. Therefore the processing block's exact recorded origin is at/after Q's enqueue and S. Persistent signal storage supports proof against that later root (`index.html:70,187–188`; `arguments.html:127`; `migration.html:219–220`). Exact same-origin header equality is required, not merely a nonzero root (`codec.html:69,111`). A soft pin/reveal on a losing branch is rolled back and cannot be treated as settled (`arguments.html:59`).

**Requirements/severity:** no R2/R7 bypass. **Low residual** wrong-view/stale client handling if clients disregard the specified canonical/finality checks. Surviving append-only signal history and retrieval of a matching state proof remain explicit premises; arbitrary future storage migrations are not covered (`checkpoints.html:124`).

### B7. Shorter L1 slots erase the origin before the proof lands

**Prerequisites:** L1 production interval changes among 12, 6, 4 and 2 seconds; attacker hopes a 900-second origin lifetime wrongly assumes 256 L1 blocks are always longer than that.

**Actions:** (1) select O; (2) delay authentication until its direct BLOCKHASH lookup expires; (3) attempt acceptance or force emergency DAO intervention to restore the old header.

**Resources/cost and gain:** delayed pinning, omission of a chosen pin transaction, or an operational mistake; no future proposer schedule is needed. Potential gain is expiry/wasted proof work.

**Result:** late initial authentication fails safely. The nominal 256-block opportunities at the four regular intervals are 3,072/1,536/1,024/512 seconds respectively; these are schedule illustrations, not protocol conversions. The intended route pins the header while lookup is available and retains it immutably (`index.html:68–70`; `codec.html:76–78`). At two-second production, a fresh pin included within the stated 120-second assumption fits comfortably inside that opportunity. Proof landing can use the retained tuple after direct BLOCKHASH expiry, still subject to the independent 900-second origin-age bound. After an outage, pin a fresh header and rebuild instead of relying on unavailable old pin data.

**Requirements/severity:** R5 survives 12/6/4/2-second timing; no role assignment or deadline uses a future CL schedule. **Low residual** late pin acquisition within the supported timing model; broader censorship violates A-INCLUDE. A future header layout/size outside the explicit parser bounds needs a reviewed revision (`checkpoints.html:95`); that is a stated compatibility limit, not a hidden slot dependency.

### B8. Freeze migration after a withheld historical blob or abandoned forced-drain commit

**Prerequisites:** finite legacy tails are frozen; required L2 installations are committed. Consider separately (a) public historical witnesses and compatible proving artifacts survive, and (b) a necessary old blob/state witness is irretrievably unavailable.

**Actions:** (1) attacker commits the next forced-drain proposal then stops; or (2) a historical data holder withholds the only remaining witness after retention; (3) try to make activation depend on that actor, erase the obligation by timeout, or collect new Etna force fees during the blockage.

**Resources/cost and gain:** (a) one ordinary commit transaction, with no exclusive worker right or commit reward; (b) monopoly over surviving historical data or prior archive failure. Gain is migration delay; no invented checkpoint should be accepted to escape it.

**Result:** **(a) blocked as an incumbent reservation attack.** The complete deterministic pending proposal remains available, any prover completes it, and no caller can choose its bytes or extend frozen tails. Each historical proof or forced commit/proof decreases the finite-work potential (`migration.html:423–434`). L1 activation is permissionless, verifies exact READY state and installation proof, seals and installs H0 atomically (`migration.html:321,426`).

**(b) feasible safe halt, explicitly stated.** Missing data is not a malformed-source proof; no sound prover can reconstruct absent witnesses merely from commitments (`migration.html:249,259,431,460`). The new force queue rejects in DRAINING before collecting fees or liabilities (`migration.html:330–337`). Legacy obligations remain outstanding rather than silently disappearing. The specification already requires archive/proof checks before freezing and an explicit missing-data rehearsal (`migration.html:247–249,529`).

**Requirements/severity:** no newly missing R1/R2/R7 transition predicate identified. **High availability impact if the explicit historical-data/proving premise fails**; this is an unverified deployment/migration blocker, not a passed real-world recovery test. The finite potential proves absence of internal infinite work, not existence of a proof or a bound on archival recovery. Do not label migration unconditionally live or count an unavailable witness as an invalid queue entry.

### B9. Repeated cheap winners keep the chain advancing but censor ordinary service

**Prerequisites:** attacker can fund fast minimal applicable proofs and obtain canonical first ordering; honest ordinary transaction-rich proofs are slower. Alternatively, no honest entrant is willing to fund a positive rent.

**Actions:** (1) stage a sequence of small valid candidates; (2) repeatedly win before a competing ordinary branch; (3) pay rent from external ordering value, or wait until each price reaches zero; (4) repeat across Sybil producers and let optional soft branches lose.

**Resources/cost and gain:** with a genuine funded included competitor by each parent age D=660, every capture sinks at least 13,333,333,333,333,334 wei; 100 captures sink 1.3333333333333334 ETH plus DA/proving/gas. Rapid children at the same L1 timestamp each sink 0.05 ETH. Self-generated L2 fees returning to the same coinbase are internal transfers, not extra deterrence (`roles.html:276–279`). External gains above rent can make capture profitable. Without A-COMPETITION(D<900), waiting until zero eliminates this positive rent lower bound (`roles.html:282–283`).

**Result:** **feasible**, explicitly scoped. Public staging supplies a proving opportunity, not fair winning probability. Neither address count nor collateral establishes independent owners. This degrades ordinary user/prover service and can provoke repeated soft rollbacks. It cannot reset rent by mere staging or keep a *fixed eligible forced request* unprocessed while advancing, because B4 still applies.

**Requirements/severity:** **Medium residual R6 economic concentration/R4 service degradation**, not an R6 claim of equal-share security or a new canonical lock. R6 has a concrete identity-independent mechanism, costs, decay, split and rationale; effectiveness is conditional and unmeasured. If the intended product requires guaranteed honest ordinary-ordering share or every soft branch to land, that stronger property is not supplied here.

### B10. Erase a worker default by consuming its parent late

**Prerequisites:** attacker has accepted a feasible job, with deadline d and 1,000 TAIKO reserved; no competing consumption or revision retirement occurs by d.

**Actions:** (1) withhold the promised proof through d; (2) at d+1 consume the old parent using another producer/beneficiary; (3) ask settlement to treat today's changed head as innocent cancellation; (4) withdraw the worker bond.

**Resources/cost and gain:** reserved bond, alternative proof/DA/rent and gas; intended gain is blocking paid service while avoiding its default cost.

**Result:** **blocked** by immutable parent-consumption time. Only a consumption or retirement at/before d cancels; later consumption cannot erase default. Exact timely task/stage/job/worker completion has precedence. Any caller settles without DAO judgment (`roles.html:101–107`; `accountability.html:56–60`). Proving competitions before d can innocently cancel the job and waste work, but do not reserve canonical admission or create a free malicious-default waiver.

**Requirements/severity:** no R3/R6 failure. **Low residual** race costs inherent in voluntary nonexclusive jobs; the separately feasible economic risks are B9. Slashing is bounded, once-only and objectively attributable, not a promise to reimburse every sponsor's consequential loss.

## Boundaries and disposition

**Normative missing predicates:** none established by the attempted traces. In particular, the review did not substitute an imagined implementation for current-origin pinning, timestamp-zero reveal, frozen FIFO work, deadline history, zero-rent rebuilding, or migration gates: each has an explicit rule cited above.

**Stated residual risks:** B1's pre-deadline silence and mature alternative branches; B3's rebuilding costs; B4's paid finite congestion; B9's concentration; finite evidence horizons and expensive voluntary services. These are feasible outcomes requiring economic/product acceptance, not rejected traces.

**Unverified premises:** existence and throughput of both required proving backends; full verifier/circuit relation; worst-case ordinary pin/reveal/forced and L1 envelope gas; sustainable one-second execution; adequate staged blob retention; reconstructible accepted state; honest funded inclusion; canonical EIP runtime/client/prover parity; exact deployment ownership/storage and historical legacy witnesses. The supplied vector files are component fixtures, explicitly not a valid end-to-end segment/proof or migration attestation (`codec-vectors.md:3,60,87`; `encoding-vectors.md:43`). No implementation, gas, deployment or launch check was run or represented as passed.

The specification provides a reviewable recovery route from incumbent outage and ring expiry. It does not provide a recovery oracle for erased historical data, a proof-production fallback when both required proof capabilities do not exist, or an unconditional service-latency guarantee.
