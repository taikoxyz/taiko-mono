# Etna adversarial review, round 01, angle C: censorship and monopolization

Reviewer: **gpt-6-sol**. Scope: revision-0 `README.md` R1–R7, `design/architecture.md`, and all five `design/*.html` pages. This is a specification review, not an execution benchmark or a deployed-contract audit. “Proven” below means a trace follows from the proposed rules under its stated preconditions; ETH/TAIKO market values, L1 inclusion, actual prover speed and cartel market share remain **unmeasured**. Findings are independent of any earlier review disposition.

## Findings

### C-01 — An active canonical participant can withhold a valid competing branch without a punishable duty (High; R6 blocker)

**Preconditions.** An honest publisher gives users an executed, provisional branch X from accepted head H. A competing economic participant M can construct and fund a valid segment Y from H and get Y's proof/data included before X's proof/data. M need not operate a registered publisher or custodian and may use a distinct paying address. This requires neither a forged proof nor a dishonest service signature.

**Trace.** (1) X is gossiped and locally marked `VALID_IN_VIEW`. (2) M keeps Y and its proof private during the proof window, possibly omitting a targeted unforced transaction or taking MEV. (3) M lands Y's complete canonical DA and sound proof through `submitSegment`. (4) Y consumes H; X becomes stale and its soft users roll back. If M has signed a data duty, M can still reveal the bytes at landing or answer a later challenge within 600 seconds. No missed response, same-position equivocation or missed accepted job is established.

**Cost/gain and evidence.** M pays proof computation, DA and an included L1 action, and risks a losing race; these amounts are not measured. Gain can be censorship of unforced flow, MEV and rival proof waste. The L1 record proves Y's eventual validity and publication, not its earlier P2P absence. A delayed but honest public broadcast can yield the same contract-visible record. The design explicitly leaves raw submitters unbonded and calls private reveal-before-deadline unslashable. A 100-TAIKO position cannot deter an actor who did not commit one, and even a signed position has no default if timely challenged bytes appear. The 10% reporter/90% sink split correctly prevents profitable *self-slashing*, but is irrelevant when no slash predicate holds.

**Precise references.** [`architecture.md`, “Chosen boundary” and “Claims to try to falsify”](../design/architecture.md); [`index.html#preconf`](../design/index.html#preconf) and [`index.html#landing`](../design/index.html#landing); [`roles.html#boundary`](../design/roles.html#boundary); [`accountability.html#offenses`](../design/accountability.html#offenses) and [`accountability.html#limits`](../design/accountability.html#limits); [`arguments.html#withholding`](../design/arguments.html#withholding). The draft already recognizes its W1 instance. This review affirms it as a requirement failure under the brief's demand to punish a participating party's withholding, rather than treating an acknowledged gap as an accepted relaxation.

**Smallest fix/decision.** A timeout surrogate is sound for a **preexisting, attributable, funded publication promise**: challenge, allow bounded response, slash only a missed deadline. It cannot punish M's pre-landing secrecy when M made no such promise, or when M reveals on time. To cover every canonical participant, the protocol needs an enforceable prior commitment/publication duty and a rule preventing its bypass by a later private branch; that changes the selected always-open, one-action proof race and needs its own liveness and R7 analysis. The smallest relaxation preserving this candidate is to narrow the punishable guarantee to breached *voluntary signed service obligations*, and expressly permit valid private winners and soft rollback. No such relaxation is presumed authorized, and no impossibility theorem for every possible R1–R7 design follows from this trace.

### C-02 — Seven-day position locks make sustained one-second bonded service capital intensive, while Sybil ownership bypasses owner-level equivocation (High; R3/R6 economic gate)

**Preconditions.** Clients value “bonded” one-second publishing or custody claims, and each such signed content identity consumes a fresh 100-TAIKO position. Buckets are indivisible deposits of 60 positions/6,000 TAIKO; `evidenceClose` is issuance end plus 604,800 seconds. There is no identity test between collateral owners.

**Trace.** A continuously active publisher uses about one position per second and, at steady state, has roughly 604,800 simultaneously locked positions: **60,480,000 TAIKO** before fragment multiplicity, publisher plus custodian duplication, ETH response funding, gas or proof capital. A publisher serving only a few clients must still open a whole 6,000-TAIKO bucket. A well-capitalized M can instead divide those funds across owner addresses and keys, sign rival blocks from the same H under separate owners and avoid the *same-owner* double-sign rule; each address's own position is funded, but neither is slashed for cross-owner conflict. If the bytes are served on challenge, the availability rule also does not slash. M can buy a larger share of visible soft services and privately route traffic, while new small providers face the full minimum bucket and long lock.

**Cost/gain and evidence.** The 60.48-million figure is arithmetic from the proposed one-position/second and seven-day liability, not a measured attainable supply or price threshold. M must acquire/lock that capital and pay operating costs; splitting identities does not reduce aggregate reserved TAIKO for the same number of signed promises. M can gain order flow, fee share, MEV and stronger odds of selling the default soft view, none priced in the design. The 100-TAIKO cap is an exposure cap, not evidence that it exceeds the value of double-signing, withholding or induced soft reorgs. Open canonical proving means a small honest prover remains *legally* admissible; it does not make a continuous bonded soft service economically competitive.

**Precise references.** [`roles.html#registry`](../design/roles.html#registry), [`roles.html#parameters`](../design/roles.html#parameters) and [`roles.html#competition`](../design/roles.html#competition); [`accountability.html#offenses`](../design/accountability.html#offenses); [`architecture.md`, “Resource and economic baseline for role pages”](../design/architecture.md); [`arguments.html#limits`](../design/arguments.html#limits). The draft discloses the 60.48-million-TAIKO weakness and cross-owner limit, but its R6 anti-monopoly gate still lacks a credible priced response.

**Smallest fix/closure.** Specify the target service workload, maximum economically acceptable working capital, credible MEV/soft-reorg benefit range, token liquidity and opportunity cost, then choose reserve granularity/duration against those numbers. Permit smaller independently backed positions/buckets if fixed 60-position prepayment is unnecessary; retain one reserve per simultaneously collectible obligation. Do not replace economic analysis with an address cap, lottery or a claim of distinct owners. Cross-owner collusion is not objectively slashable without a different public commitment rule or an explicit independence assumption.

### C-03 — A cartel can recycle most escalating force fees when it proves its own flood (Medium; R6/R7 delay economics)

**Preconditions.** M can afford a large run of valid bounded `force` calls before the victim's call, and can win or coordinate the subsequent proof-bearing segments. Another honest prover remains free to race and may capture fees; the trace is strongest where M already dominates proving or L1 inclusion. Each forced request can be a small raw transaction that deterministically rejects in canonical state; execution success is not needed to clear it.

**Trace.** M appends N=1,000 requests while the queue is otherwise empty. The proposed fee `0.001 ETH × (1+floor(pending/50))` locks **10.5 ETH** across those enqueues, plus L1 calldata/gas. The victim's next bounded request pays 0.021 ETH. The frozen FIFO correctly places the victim behind M's 1,000 entries, and every accepted successor clears at most four, so at least 251 successors are needed to clear the 1,000 entries and then process the victim. M constructs/lands successors carrying just the required prefix, making itself the proof-bound beneficiary and receiving its own force fees back as those entries clear. The net fee component approaches zero if M wins all those proofs; actual loss is enqueue/landing gas, proof/DA expense, capital time and any fees won by outsiders. The victim's fee may even flow to M. With the illustrative A-LIVE Δ=360-second interval, the draft's conditional bound for p=1,000 is `720 + ceil(1001/4)×360 = 91,080 seconds` (25.3 hours); that is a *possible allowed pace under assumptions*, not an observed delay or a promised SLA.

**Precise references.** [`architecture.md`, “Forced FIFO”](../design/architecture.md); [`index.html#forced`](../design/index.html#forced) and [`index.html#parameters`](../design/index.html#parameters); [`roles.html#competition`](../design/roles.html#competition); [`arguments.html#l2`](../design/arguments.html#l2). The FIFO proof correctly prevents a fixed victim request from being skipped forever under A-LIVE. This finding is the narrower economic point: paying the processing beneficiary does not make the escalating fee a burned congestion cost for a vertically integrated flooder.

**Smallest fix/closure.** Model the fee's net cost when enqueuer and beneficiary are one actor, and measure enqueue/proof gas against the delay and MEV value. If the fee is meant to deter flooding, direct a calibrated nonrefundable portion away from the batch beneficiary or make the actual processing cost non-recyclable, while preserving adequate incentives for independent provers. State clearly that the queue gives per-request eventual progress under A-LIVE and a backlog-dependent bound; no uniform short user SLA follows under arbitrary paid backlog or all-prover outage.

### C-04 — First-accept proof jobs let an incumbent occupy the bounty and cancel its own liability through a colluding canonical successor (Medium; R3/R6)

**Preconditions.** A sponsor posts a useful immutable task T from current head H. M can reserve 1,000 TAIKO, win the first job acceptance and later land another valid child of H before the 300-second deadline. No rule links distinct worker/submitting owners, and other provers cannot accept the already accepted job.

**Trace.** M accepts T first, making M the only proof-bound recipient of that job's bounty. Honest workers can still submit any canonical segment, but completing T with M's job ID pays M, while changing the beneficiary no longer completes that accepted job. M or an affiliate lands a small competing valid child of H that omits the targeted discretionary flow. The first-consumption receipt objectively marks T “competing consumption”; the sponsor receives its bounty back and M's worker reserve is released with **zero slash**. M repeats on new offers/heads. A legitimate, unrelated competing successor would deserve the same cancellation, so merely slashing all superseded workers would create false penalties.

**Cost/gain and evidence.** M temporarily locks 1,000 TAIKO per accepted job and pays acceptance plus ordinary proof/DA/L1 costs for the competing child; the locked principal returns. M can deny honest workers access to the sponsor's subsidy and continue censoring unforced transactions if its competing proof races win. If another funded entrant wins T first or out-proves M, the attack fails. This is a grief path against *optional subsidized proving*, not an exclusive canonical veto; the design's statement that `submitSegment` stays open is correct but insufficient for the job market's intended replacement function.

**Precise references.** [`roles.html#jobs`](../design/roles.html#jobs) and [`roles.html#role-matrix`](../design/roles.html#role-matrix); [`accountability.html#jobs`](../design/accountability.html#jobs); [`index.html#landing`](../design/index.html#landing); [`arguments.html#l1`](../design/arguments.html#l1).

**Smallest fix.** Keep a sponsor's exact-task bounty open to any successful proof beneficiary until canonical consumption/expiry, even if one or more workers separately accept deadline liabilities. Multiple independently reserved workers may pledge to try; the first exact accepted proof earns the bounty, while timely competing consumption releases each innocent worker. This removes first-accept control of reward access without claiming that a bond can prove common ownership of a competing prover. Benchmark whether the remaining open bounty is enough to attract a backup after a worker disappears.

## Verdict and smallest relaxation

Revision 0 does **not** pass R6 as the stronger brief is written. The decisive trace is C-01: an economically active canonical submitter can keep a valid branch private until proof-bearing landing without ever entering a punishable duty. The problem is structural to this candidate's combination of unrestricted L1 winner selection, advisory preconfirmations and service-only bonds. C-02 through C-04 add specific economic paths; their profitability and frequency require measurements, so they are not asserted as empirical cartel outcomes.

The narrowest stated relaxation that preserves the selected R1/R7 architecture is: **punish objectively missed, preexisting signed publication/proof-service duties, including withholding that persists past a funded on-chain challenge deadline; do not require punishment merely for a private valid branch that first appears with complete proof and data at landing.** This preserves the user's allowed objective timeout surrogate and still punishes a participating *duty signer* that defaults. It does **not** satisfy a demand to punish every economically active submitter's historical pre-landing withholding. If that broader demand remains hard, redesign canonical ordering/public commitment before a pass verdict; do not infer a mandatory hard preconfirmation finality requirement or declare the literal seven requirements impossible from the present counterexample alone.
