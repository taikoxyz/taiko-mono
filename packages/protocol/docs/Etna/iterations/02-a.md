## Verdict

No confirmed Critical or High custody, solvency, reward-theft, or false-slashing exploit was established from the specified rules. Two Medium design issues and one Low accounting-specification issue remain. R6 cannot receive an unconditional pass: the publication signature grammar is contradictory, individual active-holder refusal is not penalized when someone else reveals the bytes, and strong monopoly deterrence remains explicitly unestablished. This is not a claim that existing soft branches must be irreversible, that private computation must be prohibited, or that a chain can progress with zero capable provers.

The canonical first-disclosure-at-landing attack fails the mandatory matching actual-data receipt and 360-second age check. Atomic R7 still holds at specification level: the canonical action carries the complete matching data and proof. Prior generic publication is not an unproved canonical proposal or a reservation, and literal R7 does not prohibit it.

## Findings

### A02-01 — Medium: contradictory EIP-712 publication type names make covenants non-interoperable

**Evidence.** `design/staging.html:199–209` explicitly says the exact EIP-712 type name is `PublicationClaim` and defines `PUBLICATION_TYPEHASH` from `PublicationClaim(uint256 bucketId,uint256 position,uint256 revision,bytes32 baseHeadHash,uint256 issuedAt,bytes32 contextHash)`. `design/accountability.html:10` and `:103` instead prescribe the exact type name `EtnaPublication`; `design/roles.html:85` agrees with `EtnaPublication`. Staging is authoritative for commitments at `staging.html:5`, while accountability governs evidence and reserved liabilities. These are different signed digests, even with identical fields and domain. The stated authority division does not resolve the contradiction.

**Preconditions.** A publisher client follows the staging page's normative digest and an evidence verifier follows accountability's normative digest. Its bucket reserve is otherwise valid and live. This is a permitted divergence in the design, not a claimed bug in deployed code.

**Trace.** (1) Open a publisher bucket and sign a sealed-context covenant using the `PublicationClaim` digest. (2) A client following staging authenticates it and displays the promised publication deadline. (3) Miss that deadline. (4) A funded reporter submits the original signature to an evidence verifier following `EtnaPublication`. Signature recovery does not authenticate the bucket key, so evidence reverts. (5) After the evidence horizon, the signer releases its unslashed reserve. The reverse page interpretation creates the symmetric interoperability failure.

**Cost and gain.** A bucket requires 600 TAIKO refundable collateral and registration gas; the affected position's nominal exposure is 10 TAIKO. The signer avoids the 10-TAIKO default penalty and can avoid timely-publication expense; no custody theft or reward mint follows. An honest signer or reporter can also lose availability of the expected enforcement path. Mismatched signature verification rejects evidence; it does not by itself establish a false slash of a victim.

**Requirements.** R3 duty specification and R6 objective evidence; R7 acceptance safety remains independently protected by staging and proof checks.

**Minimal fix.** Choose one exact type name and canonical full type string, use it in all three pages and every interface comment, and publish a shared digest/signature vector. Keep the struct name distinct only if that distinction is explicitly specified and consistently used.

### A02-02 — Medium: globally satisfied publication challenges do not penalize an individually active holder that refuses service

**Evidence.** `design/accountability.html:17` permanently satisfies a digest's duty when any party publishes it. `:18` refunds every other timely satisfied challenge and rejects future challenges after global revelation. `:34` states that a response by any holder defeats the accusation. `design/roles.html:54` says custodians retain and serve signed chunks, while the actual enforcement predicate only needs somebody's reveal. `accountability.html:159` acknowledges selective serving followed by a timely response can have 100% false negatives for prior gossip behavior. The additional trace here is refusal by the challenged holder throughout the challenge and thereafter, with all compliance performed by a different holder.

**Preconditions.** A valid active custodian has signed a funded, live claim. Another holder or public archive has the bytes and is willing to reveal them. No impossible task, missing data, L1 censorship, expired evidence, or hidden signature is needed.

**Trace.** (1) Custodian A signs a claim and remains active but refuses every request for its bytes. (2) A user opens A's valid paid challenge. (3) Independent holder B reveals matching bytes before the deadline and earns Q. (4) A's reserve stays intact. (5) A continues refusing service; no subsequent challenge can be opened for that data key because the global reveal is permanent. If A has several claims for that digest, all timely challenged claims are satisfied. A neither responds nor supplies an authenticated act acknowledging the included request.

**Cost and gain.** A pays bucket registration/signing costs and collateral opportunity cost, with zero slash. It saves ongoing storage/bandwidth and its own ETH response expenditure while continuing to advertise collateral-backed custody until clients apply an additional unstated policy. B gets the legitimate challenge deposit; no escrow multiplication or theft is involved. Any optional customer payment to A is external to consensus and is not assumed in this trace.

**Requirements and severity scope.** This is a concrete requirement gap if the requested defense must cover an actual individually active holder refusing its service. It is not a violation of the narrower specified global-publication duty: that duty was satisfied. Universal staging still ensures canonical bytes were public before acceptance and protects against the original first-reveal-at-landing trace. Those availability guarantees do not establish holder-specific accountability. Severity is Medium because the impact concerns optional service reliability and penalty coverage, not custody-root integrity or canonical liveness.

**Detection limits.** Under the current predicate, false negatives for A's own refusal are 100% in this trace even with a funded timely reporter and healthy L1. False positives for the narrower missing-global-reveal predicate remain zero under its funding/inclusion assumptions; malicious-intent attribution still admits 100% worst-case false positives under censorship. A previously unknown private gossip refusal cannot be inferred merely from later publication.

**Minimal fix.** Specify whether the insured duty is global publication or individual signer participation. To meet an individual active-holder duty, add a bounded included challenge that requires a challenge-specific authenticated response from the obligated immutable key, relayed permissionlessly with matching complete bytes, or another precisely specified signer-attributable timeout/attestation surrogate. Other holders may rescue global availability without automatically erasing that separate missed signer obligation. Keep total slash capped at the existing reserve; publish the false-positive/false-negative conditions, including censorship and key outage. Such a surrogate proves objective response participation, not historic universal P2P receipt. If only global publication is intended, explicitly accept this requirement relaxation instead of calling individual holder refusal punishable.

### A02-03 — Low: combined ETH conservation equation omits pending forced-request fees

**Evidence.** `design/roles.html:108–115` defines `accountedETH = pendingChallengeDeposits + unpaidETHCredits + permanentForcedFeeSink`, requires asset-specific coverage in each contract and says internal transfers conserve accounted balances. `design/index.html:135` stores ETH fees when a force request is queued; `design/roles.html:201–212` allocates 50% to beneficiary credit and 50% to the sink upon processing. Neither term in the displayed equation reserves the pending queue fee before processing.

**Trace.** (1) Enqueue one request with a 0.001-ETH fee. (2) It remains unprocessed. The listed accounting components do not change, although held ETH rises and the queue stores the fee. It therefore looks like uncredited surplus under the supplied equation. (3) Process the request and create a 0.0005-ETH beneficiary credit and 0.0005-ETH sink entry. Listed accounted ETH increases by 0.001 ETH without a new deposit, contrary to the displayed internal-conservation claim.

**Cost and gain.** One minimum fee plus enqueue gas produces the discrepancy. No attacker gain or demonstrated insolvency follows: surplus sweeping is expressly forbidden and processing still requires a valid proof. This is an accounting definition omission, not a confirmed withdrawal exploit.

**Requirements.** R3/R6 fee accounting, and R7 forced-work settlement.

**Minimal fix.** Add `pendingForcedFeeEscrow` to the Inbox's asset-specific equation. Enqueue moves received fees into that reserve; processing debits it once and credits the exact beneficiary/sink split. Specify surplus separately and retain no-sweep and pull-payment rules. Do not net this Inbox reserve against ETH held by the challenge registry.

## Financial invariants that resisted falsification

- Custody root authority is exclusively proven Inbox → L1 SignalService and circuit-checked Anchor → L2 SignalService. Stage records, custody signatures, worker registration and arbitrary beneficiary naming have no checkpoint authority (`migration.html:73–82`; `staging.html:120`).
- Calldata bytes and current transaction blob versioned hashes are matched exactly; the canonical proof must establish the full body/KZG/execution relation and all statement fields (`index.html:114,125–127`; `staging.html:118–120`). Unimplemented circuit conformance is an open verification task, not an assumed missing-check exploit.
- Producer authorization is mandatory and binds the complete statement, stage and immutable key ID. Accepted history stores its successful authentication; revocation or beneficiary choice cannot frame an innocent owner or erase attribution (`staging.html:124–157`).
- Historical first publication, stage times, parent consumption, and revision retirement do not reset through ordinary operation. Late proof or parent replacement cannot rewrite a worker's already-missed deadline (`index.html:62`; `staging.html:111–115`; `accountability.html:37–40`).
- Job bounty and worker reserve settle separately. Early competing consumption returns both parties' escrow; absence of timely success, replacement or retirement slashes exactly once. A copied proof preserves worker beneficiary; the worker's voluntary failure grants no canonical veto.
- Reserving every position independently, once-only slashes, asset-separated pull credits, exact received-token amounts, state-first transfers and no surplus/sink sweep prevent the illustrated self-report mint, duplicated response payout and donation-induced withdrawal lock (`roles.html:108–118`; `accountability.html:18,42`). Pending forced-fee reservation still needs the explicit A02-03 repair.

No Critical/High-free convergence claim follows from this single review. The judge should resolve the Medium issues and the exact active-holder/R6 acceptance criterion before marking the ledger passed.
