## 2. Independent verdict

**No validated security finding was established.** The attempted custody theft, unbacked payout, false slashing, deadline erasure, DA substitution and forced-prefix bypass traces fail explicit normative checks in this snapshot. This is a bounded design review result, not evidence of implementation correctness or deployment compatibility.

The admission and settlement mechanisms have conditional arguments under the named cryptographic, implementation, L1, upgrade, data, proving and funding assumptions. Actual one-second execution, sustainable dual-proof throughput, complete transaction gas and byte budgets, authenticated migration compatibility and practical economic deterrence remain open. In particular, the rent establishes a cost inequality under a ready funded competitor and a bounded external gain; it establishes neither owner diversity nor an unconditional monopoly prohibition. No convergence or readiness conclusion is made.

## 4. Rejected attempted attacks and surviving limits

### A. Forge a withdrawal root through a captured soft-service role

1. An attacker operates every publisher/custodian and signs a plausible block or availability claim with a fabricated withdrawal state root.
2. It tries to use that signature or publication receipt as a SignalService checkpoint.
3. It then presents a storage proof to the retained Bridge/Vault to release escrow.

**Rejected:** soft signatures/stages cannot publish checkpoints. Acceptance requires the complete dual-proof relation and an exact current-parent transition; L1 SignalService accepts only the Inbox provenance path, and L2 only the proved Anchor path. The required checkpoint is the full execution state root. `design/index.html:119–126` ([#landing](../design/index.html#landing)); `design/migration.html:75–81` ([#checkpoint](../design/migration.html#checkpoint)); `design/staging.html:120` ([#acceptance](../design/staging.html#acceptance)). Snapshot source corroboration: SignalService.sol:174–183 and 284–296 authenticate the syncer and remote state proof; BaseVault.sol:53–61 authenticates Bridge context and the source Vault. Role capture has no extra custody privilege. A broken verifier, malicious upgrade or unsound legacy root can still cause theft, but those failures cross the explicitly named A-CRYPTO/A-IMPLEMENTATION/A-UPGRADE/A-LEGACY boundaries.

### B. Replace published bytes with a private body or private ordering manifest

1. Stage an innocuous hash/descriptor or one blob.
2. Privately execute different bytes, rejected forced inputs, transaction order or anchor context.
3. Land the private execution under the old staging receipt.

**Rejected:** calldata staging hashes actual complete bytes; blob staging binds the actual current transaction's versioned hash. Final acceptance requires same-mode republication and a proof of the full body/KZG/context relation. The codec includes exact headers, raw transactions, anchor calldata and rejected forced bytes, plus distinct FR/MR/block-tree domains. `design/staging.html:90–93,118–120` ([#registry](../design/staging.html#registry), [#acceptance](../design/staging.html#acceptance)); `design/codec.html:21–44,128–132,135–171` ([#body](../design/codec.html#body), [#forced](../design/codec.html#forced), [#manifests](../design/codec.html#manifests)); `design/index.html:170–172` ([#da](../design/index.html#da)). Stage-time blob semantic decoding is not claimed. Exact KZG circuit enforcement remains an implementation/audit obligation; a one-point probabilistic surrogate is not permitted by the normative relation.

### C. Front-run proof submission to steal its bounty or force-fee payout

1. Copy a worker's complete proof and statement from a pending transaction.
2. Replace its beneficiary, job ID, producer or stage ID with the attacker's values.
3. Attempt acceptance and withdrawal of the sponsor's bounty or forced fees.

**Rejected:** these fields are included in the complete statement/public input and exact producer authorization. A copied unchanged proof can help the worker succeed but preserves its bound beneficiary. Reproving another valid assignment is allowed; it can win and cancel the original job, but cannot claim the original bounty without its exact task/stage/job/worker receipt. `design/index.html:126–142` ([#verifier-interface](../design/index.html#verifier-interface)); `design/staging.html:124–157` ([#authorization](../design/staging.html#authorization)); `design/roles.html:96–106` ([#jobs](../design/roles.html#jobs)). The competing race can waste honest work and DA expenditure; cancellation is an explicitly specified market outcome, not escrow theft.

### D. Manufacture collateral, drain sinks or make donations freeze withdrawals

1. Self-challenge several claims for one fragment, self-report defaults, and request duplicate payouts.
2. Reuse the same slashed position or forced-fee record.
3. Send unsolicited ETH/TAIKO and try to claim the surplus or defeat a raw-balance equality check.

**Rejected:** every position/job/deposit has separately backed accounting, each reserve slashes once, one first targeted publication receives at most one Q, compatible challenges refund individually, and processed force fees are debited once. Per-contract liabilities are bounded by held assets, not required to equal them. Donations create no claim; sinks and surplus have no sweep. Self-slashing loses 90% of TAIKO; self-processing loses the forced-fee sink; canonical rent is entirely sunk. `design/roles.html:113–126` ([#accounting](../design/roles.html#accounting)); `design/accountability.html:32–37,61` ([#challenge](../design/accountability.html#challenge), [#multiple](../design/accountability.html#multiple), [#accounting](../design/accountability.html#accounting)); `design/index.html:159–160,182` ([#rent](../design/index.html#rent), [#forced](../design/index.html#forced)). Failed pull transfers preserve the claim. Reentrancy and token-receipt semantics must still be faithfully implemented; no executable implementation was verified.

### E. Frame an innocent publisher by naming it as producer or beneficiary

1. Select a valid fork conflicting with Alice's signed soft block.
2. Name Alice in the canonical statement without her assent.
3. Use the resulting receipt to slash her reserve.

**Rejected:** acceptance verifies Alice's exact EIP-712 authorization or her objectively self-registered immutable key. Historical evidence uses the successfully checked receipt, not arbitrary beneficiary naming or a mutable ERC-1271 result. `design/staging.html:124–157,180–181` ([#authorization](../design/staging.html#authorization), [#block-tree](../design/staging.html#block-tree)); `design/accountability.html:39–47` ([#canonical-conflict](../design/accountability.html#canonical-conflict)). Key compromise remains the owner's explicit key-security risk. Separate owner addresses and different immediate-parent contexts can evade this particular conflict offense; that limitation is stated and does not bypass DA/proof admission or an already signed publication duty.

### F. Rotate keys, change declared issuance time or reveal late to erase default

1. Sign a fragment duty, then rotate the service key or sign again with a later issuedAt.
2. Open a late challenge to seek a fresh 600-second grace period.
3. Publish after the original deadline and claim Q or release collateral as satisfied.

**Rejected:** bucket keys/context are immutable; issuedAt changes a semantic signed deadline and is equivocation on the same position. The response deadline is capped by issuedAt+240. First-publication records are immutable, late data cannot cure the miss or earn expired Q, and release settles already admitted defaults first. `design/roles.html:77–89` ([#registry](../design/roles.html#registry)); `design/accountability.html:14–15,25–37,49,61` ([#obligations](../design/accountability.html#obligations), [#fragment-publication](../design/accountability.html#fragment-publication), [#challenge](../design/accountability.html#challenge), [#offenses](../design/accountability.html#offenses), [#accounting](../design/accountability.html#accounting)). Honest L1 censorship can produce an objective service default; late challenge funding does not promise adequate response time. Evidence hidden until after evidenceClose can escape punishment, as explicitly disclosed at `design/accountability.html:210` ([#limits](../design/accountability.html#limits)).

### G. Erase a proof-job default with a later head change or retirement

1. Accept a job and miss its deadline while its parent remains unconsumed.
2. Later consume that parent, or retire the revision.
3. Settle as an innocent competing-race cancellation.

**Rejected:** settlement reads the immutable parent-consumption and revision-retirement timestamps. Only events at or before the deadline cancel; timely exact completion takes precedence. Later events cannot erase default. Freshness guards leave the stage/origin valid through deadline plus 120 seconds. `design/roles.html:99–106` ([#jobs](../design/roles.html#jobs)); `design/accountability.html:56–60` ([#jobs](../design/accountability.html#jobs)); `design/staging.html:195–196` ([#timing](../design/staging.html#timing)). An impossible output, underfunded ETH payer or slow worker can still fail a voluntarily accepted job; local execution and funding diligence are required. Jobs are optional and reserve no head.

### H. Censor a fixed forced request through moving or permanently old snapshots

1. Append a request just before another party's proof lands, hoping to invalidate its required workload.
2. Alternatively, keep accepting cheap discretionary-empty segments with an old origin/cut.
3. Or insert discretionary execution first to make the victim transaction invalid.

**Rejected:** mandatory work comes from the immutable parent snapshot; new appends cannot move it. Queue-cut boundary checks determine the unique tail at the authenticated origin. A progressing chain cannot indefinitely reuse an origin older than 900 seconds. Once due in a snapshot, every successor processes the first up to four eligible records; rejected records also require exact public raw bytes and proved classifications. The forced prefix executes immediately after the first anchor, before discretionary work. `design/index.html:181–186` ([#forced](../design/index.html#forced)); `design/codec.html:128–132` ([#forced](../design/codec.html#forced)); `design/arguments.html:85–95` ([#l2](../design/arguments.html#l2)). The fee and processing sink impose real cost, but an arbitrary earlier paid backlog or prover outage defeats a uniform short SLA. “Processed” is an execution attempt or deterministic validity rejection, not guaranteed application success.

### I. Activate on an arbitrary root, skip old obligations or repeat the empty bootstrap

1. Choose a convenient legacy end root, omit pending forced work, or activate before required L2 upgrades are proven.
2. Reset the new queue or repeatedly exploit the empty H0 force interval.
3. Use a pre-activation origin or call beginEtna repeatedly to install roots.

**Rejected:** activation independently matches the exact READY drain certificate and verifies the migration relation under pinned keys; H0 is fully derived from that certificate. Activation is irreversible, queues/escrow are preserved, the zero-origin sentinel applies only to the exact stored initial head, and only the first successor consumes an empty interval. It snapshots the real queue for later children. beginEtna proves Inbox ACTIVE under the identical authenticated post-activation origin and flips its own one-way phase. `design/migration.html:234–245,269–330,407–420,448–458` ([#migration](../design/migration.html#migration), [#bootstrap](../design/migration.html#bootstrap), [#drain-adapter](../design/migration.html#drain-adapter)). Missing historic data, unsupported legacy proof programs, incompatible wrappers or oversized activation witnesses safely block launch; they are not silently cured by DAO assertion. The constructive 17,724-byte bootstrap body does not prove its gas cost.

### J. Recover a legacy administrative mint/pause/root path after activation

1. Invoke inherited owner mint, pause, pending-owner acceptance, unused initializer, resolver remapping or an old router fallback.
2. Use the authority to mint/release assets or freeze an entrant's operations.

**Rejected at specification level:** every cited path is expressly disabled or removed through storage-compatible upgrades, including wrapper migration authorities and initialization variants. Quotas are detached; a dormant non-upgradeable quota utility is not treated as upgraded. No generic old-code fallback is permitted. `design/migration.html:168–187,196–212` ([#inventory](../design/migration.html#inventory), [#authority](../design/migration.html#authority)). Snapshot source checks confirmed that these are real legacy powers: EssentialContract.sol:149–175,207–209; ERC20Vault.sol:200–252; BridgedERC20.sol:112–141,181. Their existence in old source does not demonstrate a bypass of the proposed upgraded implementation. Whether every live proxy/wrapper can satisfy the required replacement is open, not assumed to have been demonstrated.

### K. Stall canonical admission by resetting rent without progress or waiting for zero with an expired proof

1. Stage candidates, create jobs or make failed submissions to repeatedly reset rent.
2. Attempt to reclaim rent through coinbase, forced fees or another controlled address.
3. Wait 900 seconds and relay a stale proof at zero rent.

**Rejected:** only a successful fully valid accepted successor resets the external rent clock; all rejected actions roll back. Charged rent has no beneficiary and cannot fund the caller's excess credit. Zero rent does not waive origin/stage expiry; a fresh context can be staged/proved while an unchanged head remains at zero. `design/roles.html:248–268,278–282` ([#rent](../design/roles.html#rent), [#competition](../design/roles.html#competition)); `design/index.html:159–161` ([#rent](../design/index.html#rent)). Fast valid churn is possible, but costs Pmax per additional same-timestamp head and processes mandatory FIFO work. A nominal L2 fee paid between attacker-controlled sender and coinbase is an internal transfer; `design/roles.html:276` explicitly accounts for this under the 100% share.

**Surviving disclosed economic counterexample:** without a ready willing funded competitor, an incumbent can wait 900 seconds between accepted heads and pay zero rent; with sufficiently valuable MEV it can profitably pay every early quote. This falsifies an unconditional monopoly-prevention claim, which the design does not make. The positive cost result is conditional on A-COMPETITION(D<900), and deterrence further requires consolidated external gain below the sunk rent. `design/roles.html:275–282` ([#competition](../design/roles.html#competition)); `design/arguments.html:118–120` ([#l4](../design/arguments.html#l4)). This assumption supplies economically willing competition, not an identity oracle or enforced owner rotation. Calibration remains open.

### L. Force an L1-slot-dependent failure or unsafe soft finality

1. Shorten L1 production intervals from 12 to 6, 4 or 2 seconds.
2. Try to expire a header lookup while a seconds-based role assumes a fixed number of slots.
3. Or treat an executed one-second provisional block as a finalized bridge withdrawal right.

**Rejected conditionally:** timers and rent are in seconds, identity is by authenticated earlier execution header/block number, and header pinning does not consume future proposer schedules. At two-second intervals the 256-block BLOCKHASH horizon is roughly 512 seconds, longer than the modeled timely 120-second pin inclusion; this is an illustrative scheduling check, not a consensus bound on arbitrary missed-block or inclusion behavior. Origins can be reused; a small segment need not wait for a new L1 header. Soft validity, L1 acceptance and Ethereum finality are expressly separate, and a competing mature branch may cause rollback. `design/codec.html:75–77,108` ([#l1-header](../design/codec.html#l1-header), [#anchor](../design/codec.html#anchor)); `design/index.html:73–82,215–216` ([#preconf](../design/index.html#preconf), [#dependencies](../design/index.html#dependencies)). One-second worst-case execution and sustained proving are unmeasured; a total outage removes soft service instead of manufacturing confirmation.

## 6. Actual checks and limits

Performed:

- Complete independent frozen-input reading and recovery of tool-truncated ranges; source/anchor and exact ABI cross-checks described above.
- A self-contained Ethereum Keccak-256 calculation, first checked against known empty-string and `abc` answers, independently reproduced **19 fixture outputs**: four publication EIP-712 values, body/block/record hashes, FD/FR/MR, two fragment boundary digests, fragment publication key/binding, and five migration/bootstrap/head values. Checked the 320-byte FR preimage, 384-byte MR preimage, 224-byte certificate, and 384-byte Head ABI lengths. All matched snapshot fixtures.
- Recomputed rent at ages 0/360/420/660/899/900/901, including upward rounding, the 100-capture lower bound, same-timestamp reset consequences and expiry-aware zero-rent recovery.
- Recomputed the 1,000-enqueue 10.5-ETH backlog fee, 50% irreversible cost, Q at 20/30 gwei (0.176/0.256 ETH), the 3,660-second example FIFO bound, and the 17,724-byte minimal bootstrap body bound.
- Checked challenge/default strict inequalities and immutable historical job-deadline cancellation at before/equal/after boundaries; checked 256-block history opportunities at 12/6/4/2-second intervals (3,072/1,536/1,024/512 illustrative seconds).
- Confirmed public refreshed-main source at `31df8fe8ef7c027840abf122ec36f87c41c3ce94:MainnetInbox.sol:45–46` sets 100% basefee to coinbase. The normative checkout's legacy source still sets 75%; the design expressly distinguishes the two source snapshots and retains historic recorded percentages. No deployment percentage was inferred.
- No third-party crypto package was available; no package was installed. Calculation code used synthetic public fixture values, no signature/key generation, and was deleted after completion.

Not established:

- No implementation, verifier, prover backend, engine or migration program was executed or audited end-to-end. The conjunction requires both RISC0_RETH and SP1_RETH artifacts for liveness; at least one sound complete relation for safety does not resolve a shared relation-specification bug.
- No complete valid block/proof vector, KZG commitment computation, real activation MPT witness or L1/L2 integration test was produced. The fixtures check component encoding/hashes, not execution validity.
- No gas measurement for full staging, fragment response, both proof verifications, forced processing, activation anchor, migration bundle or maximum calldata envelope. Proposed bounds and the 4-million-gas Q reference are not benchmarks. Actual activation must fit both the byte cap and 1-million-gas anchor limit.
- No one-second maximum-gas execution or sustained prover-throughput benchmark; no measured retrieval, inclusion or header-acquisition bound. The D=660 scenario must include complete actual acquisition/work, rather than silently borrowing the illustrative arithmetic schedule.
- No empirical TAIKO/ETH valuation, reporter profitability, custody equilibrium, MEV bound, rent adoption or market-share evidence. A funded competitor who chooses to pay early is an additional economic premise, not supplied by permissionless entry. Unlimited gain can exceed every stated price.
- No live proxy/address/owner/treasury/storage/codehash manifest, historical-message replay, active-wrapper migration audit or proof-program compatibility rehearsal. A-LEGACY and A-UPGRADE are material custody assumptions.
- No verification of deployed EIP-8141 semantics/public-builder policy. Optional frame guards cannot substitute for the ordinary payable public fallback, which preserves protocol admission if the optimization is unavailable.

These limits define the reach of the no-validated-finding result. They do not assert implementation readiness, deployability, unconditional R1–R7 performance, or completion of independent judge review.
