## Result

**No new validated Critical or High liveness defect was established. No new validated Medium defect is asserted.** This is a focused falsification result, not readiness or an all-requirements pass. There is one conditional resource-composition observation for the first-block calldata fallback, detailed below; it requires an actual authenticated activation witness before promotion to a concrete finding.

The strongest specified defenses are substantive: no role or job reserves canonical admission; a fresh entrant may rebuild from the accepted state; every accepted segment has prior actual public DA; forced work is a fixed bounded FIFO prefix; origin freshness prevents indefinitely freezing the queue snapshot; service exits and job settlement use immutable evidence. Merely writing these defenses as assumptions would not cure a missing rule, but the relevant rules are present here.

I do not infer that proving, inclusion, archives or financing are free. Nor do I demand honest soft branches always win, punishment for unobservable intent, or unlimited MEV insurance. Those would exceed the assignment. Conversely, no disclaimer was used to excuse an absent hard gate.

## Concrete attack traces

### B-01 — Incumbent role and worker squatting

**Preconditions:** attacker can fund arbitrarily many registry buckets and win every visible job acceptance. One new honest entrant can obtain accepted history and finance both required proofs and L1 transactions.

1. Attacker registers many publisher/custodian keys and accepts each sponsored job.
2. Attacker stops producing proofs, refuses requests and revokes its producer keys.
3. Entrant constructs a child from the accepted head, with its own producer authorization and `jobId=0`; it stages the full context and data, proves it and submits after maturity.
4. Attempt to reject step 3 for lack of an incumbent signature, job slot, ticket, or DAO approval.

**Result/defense:** step 4 has no specified admission predicate. A copied execution context permits an independently authorized statement, and a new context may ignore unusable soft work. Revocation affects only that owner's unused authorizations. Existing jobs settle independently; none is exclusive.

**Cost/gain:** 600 TAIKO per bucket and 1,000 TAIKO per accepted job are locked, plus L1 costs. Applicable abandoned jobs irreversibly lose 900 TAIKO even under self-reporting; a competing timely child instead objectively cancels them. Attacker can degrade a customer's selected optional service or consume a sponsor's procurement opportunity, but obtains no canonical veto.

**Gates:** R1, R3, R6, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** canonical-halt attempt rejected by design; optional service disruption remains possible. **References:** `roles.html:15–17,51–60,95–104,216`; `staging.html:60,154–156,228–231`; `accountability.html:38–42`. **Minimal fix:** none for the canonical attack; do not represent procurement availability as guaranteed chain liveness.

### B-02 — Active publisher withholds the execution payload until landing

**Preconditions:** attacker is an active bonded publisher and capable prover; it privately constructs candidate Y while users follow X.

1. Attacker computes Y and its proof without publishing Y's actual body/context.
2. It submits Y with full DA and proof, attempting to consume X's accepted parent immediately.
3. It supplies either a bare earlier hash, an unrelated blob receipt, or a fabricated descriptor as evidence of prior availability.

**Result/defense:** step 2 fails without a 360-second-old matching actual-data receipt. The receipt descriptor must match the final context and the final proof checks body/header/manifest/forced-input/KZG relations. An earlier mismatched blob cannot be repaired only in the final statement. Changing execution order, system anchor or forced bytes changes the context and restarts maturity.

**Cost/gain:** invalid ordinary submission pays gas; an invalid frame can consume builder simulation resources. A valid competing branch instead pays earlier staging plus final DA costs and reveals its full execution inputs for the public lead interval. It may then win, waste X's proof and cause a soft rollback; that residual is real but differs from the forbidden first-reveal trace.

**Gates:** R6, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** first-reveal canonical attack rejected; mature public competitor remains exploitable as the expressly provisional race. **References:** `index.html:114–162`; `staging.html:57–61,90–95,118–121`; `codec.html:42–44,108,123–132,170–177`; `accountability.html:160–163`. **Minimal fix:** none for first reveal. The judge must assess bounded economic defenses without pretending all mature-branch losses are slashable.

### B-03 — Withhold fragments until a paid challenge, then reveal late enough to impair soft service

**Preconditions:** attacker signs a valid live fragment-serving claim; an observer has the signature and can pay Q. No separate broken publication-by covenant need exist.

1. Attacker selectively refuses the fragment before staging and before a challenge.
2. Observer opens the funded challenge at time c; deadline is c+600 seconds.
3. Attacker or a colluder publishes exact bytes at c+600, collects Q and avoids the availability slash.
4. Alternatively, attacker reveals at c+601 and attempts to cure the expired default.

**Result/defense:** step 3 succeeds within the deliberately specified serving duty; it can impair the chosen soft branch. Step 4 does not cure the default. A signed sealed covenant supplies a different absolute deadline, and canonical admission independently requires the mature public body. Unknown soft dependencies are not confirmed, and recovery may start from accepted state instead of waiting for this signer.

**Cost/gain:** honest challenger pays Q for a timely revelation. At opening basefee 20 gwei, Q is 0.176 ETH; responder pays publication gas from its own ETH. Attacker may earn Q if selected as responder, so this is not an incentive to answer every P2P request promptly. A default loses up to 10 TAIKO, with only 10% recoverable by the recorded opener; repeated observers cannot multiply insurance. No invented promise of personal server uptime is used.

**Gates:** R3, R4, R6. **Severity/exploitability:** concrete optional-service degradation, already within its stated duty semantics; not a newly validated canonical-liveness defect. **References:** `accountability.html:14–20,35,156–166`; `roles.html:53–58,121`; `staging.html:215–225,229–231`. **Minimal fix:** if the product requires earlier absolute block-level publication, specify and price that additional duty; it is not supplied by the existing 600-second response promise.

### B-04 — Forced-inclusion starvation by append races and cheap empty proofs

**Preconditions:** victim has a fixed queue index v. Attacker can enqueue repeatedly and win valid successor races; canonical progress continues.

1. Victim enqueues at time t behind p predecessors.
2. Attacker appends immediately before each honest proof lands, hoping to move its required prefix and make it invalid.
3. Attacker instead lands minimal segments, repeatedly reusing an old origin whose queue cut excludes v.
4. After the origin expires, attacker refreshes it but attempts to omit v or process new attacker requests first.

**Result/defense:** step 2 cannot change the parent-frozen work. Step 3 eventually fails the 900-second origin age check. With a snapshot time at least t+120, v is mature and in the exact authenticated cut. Step 4 must remove the next up to four due FIFO entries, including all earlier due entries before v; later arrivals cannot add predecessors. Outcomes 0–2 cannot hide missing input bytes, and a valid reverting request still counts as an execution attempt.

**Cost/gain:** 1,000 enqueues into an initially empty undrained queue cost 10.5 ETH in fees, with 5.25 ETH irrevocably sunk even if attacker receives every processing credit; transaction, proof and duplicated DA costs are additional. Interleaved processing below 50 pending reduces fees to 1 ETH and sink to 0.5 ETH, but processes old FIFO entries. The attack can buy a large finite backlog or preferential discretionary order. It does not perpetually hold one already-enqueued victim behind a fixed prefix.

**Gates:** R6, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** indefinite FIFO starvation attempt rejected under canonical progress; paid finite congestion remains possible. **References:** `index.html:170–175`; `codec.html:127–132`; `arguments.html:73–80`; `roles.html:217–219`. **Minimal fix:** none for this attack. Do not market a uniform short forced-inclusion SLA: with p=9 and conditional Δ=660 seconds the document's conservative bound is 3,660 seconds; with p=999 it is 166,680 seconds.

### B-05 — Exhaust proof freshness or manufacture a worker default

**Preconditions:** attacker offers or manipulates timing around a voluntary proof job; honest worker observes the specified admission rules. L1 inclusion remains within I=120 seconds for applicable funded transactions.

1. Sponsor offers a candidate with a nearly expired origin/stage, hoping the worker accepts an impossible deadline.
2. If a job is accepted, attacker consumes its parent with another valid child before the deadline, hoping to slash the displaced worker.
3. Alternatively, a defaulting worker arranges parent consumption only after its deadline, hoping today's changed head erases the miss.

**Result/defense:** admission computes `max(acceptedAt+300, publishedAt+480)` and checks both freshness limits through deadline+120. Step 2 cancels the job rather than slashing an honest loser. Step 3 cannot rewrite `parentConsumption` or its timestamp; historical timely versus late consumption is explicit. An impossible execution output remains a diligence risk before acceptance, not an oracle adjudication problem.

**Cost/gain:** sponsor locks the bounty; an accepting worker locks 1,000 TAIKO. No job acceptance stops another proof. Winning the parent race can waste computation, but is itself canonical progress and pays its own proving/publication costs. Repeated failures outside A-PROVE/A-INCLUDE can expire honest work, an acknowledged limitation rather than a missing timer.

**Gates:** R1, R3, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** proposed false-default/exit evasion rejected. **References:** `staging.html:113,193–197`; `roles.html:95–104`; `accountability.html:38–43`; `arguments.html:68–71`. **Minimal fix:** none. In particular, pipelined work older than the 480-second job threshold may need raw independent funding; this is explicit, not a hidden mandatory subsidy.

### B-06 — Challenge spam locks honest collateral forever

**Preconditions:** attacker holds valid signatures for all of an owner's positions and can finance Q for each challenge.

1. Open each challenge at the last allowed retention timestamp.
2. Re-submit the same obligation repeatedly, attempting to extend the response deadline.
3. Add more same-digest signers/positions, hoping a response loops over all of them or pays Q repeatedly.
4. Delay settlement until after the release timestamp, then claim pending state prevents any exit.

**Result/defense:** each position admits at most one challenge and immutable deadline. Last opening is `openedAt+60+1200`; its deadline is `openedAt+1860`, earlier than ordinary `evidenceClose=openedAt+3660`. Duplicate attempts do not extend it. Shared revelations and challenge settlement are lazy and bounded; `releasePosition` resolves expired duties before releasing the unslashed reserve. Slashed positions cannot be used to create further liability.

**Cost/gain:** attacker funds one Q per admitted challenge plus gas. Timely publication spends one selected Q; other satisfied deposits refund. There can be finite monitoring/publication overhead, but no unbounded extension for a fixed position. Underlying transfer failure leaves a pull credit claimable.

**Gates:** R1, R3, R6. **Severity/exploitability:** indefinite exit lock rejected. **References:** `roles.html:75–83,119–121`; `accountability.html:14,16–19,43`. **Minimal fix:** none; implementations must preserve the explicit bounded-settlement and strict-after boundary.

### B-07 — Faster L1 blocks or a lost private relay stop all progress

**Preconditions:** L1 interval changes to 2 seconds, while funded inclusion remains within 120 seconds; private frame builders disappear. Historical header/data access remains available.

1. Attacker withholds optional frame relay service and tries to make a shared nonce unusable.
2. Honest entrant constructs a fresh origin and publishes/pins it while preparing a bounded candidate.
3. Entrant uses ordinary `submitSegment` instead of the optional frame envelope.
4. Attacker argues that by final landing the origin is outside `BLOCKHASH`'s 256-block lookup window.

**Result/defense:** final landing reads the immutable earlier pin, not a fresh historical lookup. At nominal intervals 12/6/4/2 seconds the lookup spans 3,072/1,536/1,024/512 seconds. Even at 2 seconds, a 60-second observation plus 120-second included pin leaves 332 seconds of nominal margin. These figures are an operational check, not protocol slot constants. Failure to pin before expiry requires resealing against a fresh origin; timely permissionless pinning is a specified dependency. Ordinary submission has identical canonical checks, although losing transactions may pay gas.

**Cost/gain:** attacker can remove its own relay or spend L1 resources on congestion; it cannot impose its nonce or private builder as a mandatory entrant credential. Congestion beyond the declared inclusion/funding assumptions can halt/expire work, which no in-rollup rule can repair.

**Gates:** R1, R5, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** mandatory-relay/slot-coupling attack rejected under stated assumptions. **References:** `index.html:67–69,164–166,203`; `codec.html:74–77,108`; `arguments.html:20–29,45–46`. **Minimal fix:** none; test actual header pin scheduling at every intended interval before launch.

### B-08 — DAO inaction during or after finite migration

**Preconditions:** the complete authorized installation has occurred with authenticated state, public historical data and immutable compatible proving programs. Every old operator and the DAO then disappears; a new funded prover remains.

1. Attacker leaves pending historical proposals and frozen forced requests at installation.
2. It attempts to append more old work, claim the single pending forced drain slot exclusively, or require an old whitelist identity for proof completion.
3. Entrant proves the finite suffix, commits/proves remaining deterministic forced entries, and supplies the exact activation certificate.
4. Attacker attempts to require an additional DAO signature to seal legacy or start the first Etna anchor.

**Result/defense:** tails are frozen; pending work is not caller-owned; proof completion is permissionless; each successful operation decreases finite remaining work. Activation and sealing are atomic, and the first anchor uses a public proof of the new Inbox ACTIVE storage field. No post-installation DAO decision selects the root. Missing historical DA or compatible legacy proving artifacts blocks launch under explicitly stated preconditions; it is not cured by inventing an admin exception.

**Cost/gain:** attacker can leave real finite paid work and force the entrant to pay for its completion; no recurring lease or refundable claim buys indefinite new work after the freeze. There is no liveness claim when no capable prover or archived inputs remain.

**Gates:** R1, R2, R3, R5, R7. **Severity/exploitability:** inaction/role-squatting trace rejected given installed migration premises. **References:** `migration.html:227–245,345–358,380–396,431–438`. **Minimal fix:** none demonstrated; manifest and end-to-end drain rehearsal are mandatory launch evidence.

## B-OBS-01 — First-anchor calldata fallback needs a composed size check

**Classification:** conditional resource observation, not a validated Medium/High vulnerability. Potential impact is a first-segment calldata liveness failure if the required activation witness is large and blob operation is unavailable or excluded by the deployment's DA choice. Normal one-blob operation is a specified defense. This review has neither an authenticated launch manifest nor an actual large valid activation witness, so it does not assert this precondition holds on the intended deployment.

**Relevant rules:** `codec.html:22–43,117–124,127–132` permits beginEtna calldata up to 16,384 bytes, includes all forced raw bytes in `forced[]`, and duplicates executed forced transactions in the first block's transaction list. `index.html:170–173` mandates up to four due requests, each up to 2,048 bytes. Calldata body cap is 32,768 bytes (`index.html:161`; `codec.html:42,201`), and queued requests may already exist when activation occurs (`migration.html:235,242`).

**Conditional trace:**

1. The authentic first anchor needs a valid 16,356-byte ABI payload, within the 16,384-byte bound; it also satisfies the 1,000,000-gas limit. This is a condition to demonstrate, not a witness supplied by this report.
2. Before the initial snapshot, an attacker queues four state-valid 2,048-byte transactions that are due in that snapshot. They each fit the 1,000,000-gas request bound.
3. A first calldata-mode segment must include the anchor and all four executed requests in block zero, and retain each request's raw bytes and receipt hash in the forced records.
4. Just those components occupy at least `16,356 + 2×4×2,048 + 4×32 = 32,868` bytes, already over 32,768 before the anchor's transaction envelope, header or any RLP/container metadata. Splitting discretionary work does not remove the mandatory first-block prefix.

**Cost/gain:** with an initially empty queue and no other entries, four force fees total 0.004 ETH; at least 0.002 ETH remains sunk if attacker later receives every processing credit. Enqueue and L2 execution gas are additional. If the large-proof and calldata-only preconditions hold, this could prevent the first segment using that fallback. It does not demonstrate a stop of blob-mode settlement.

**Current defense and minimal clarification:** migration already demands actual first-anchor byte/gas checks and worst-case forced processing measurements (`migration.html:438`; `codec.html:124,214`). Explicitly make that preflight test the **combined** first body under the intended DA fallback, including four maximal executed requests and duplicate forced records. If it does not fit, select and review a smaller activation witness/profile, an explicitly deterministic bootstrap queue rule, or a launch rule requiring usable blob transport for activation. Do not assume that independently fitting the anchor and force prefix proves their sum fits calldata. This is a concrete check refinement, not a demand that all possible Ethereum tries fit the chosen activation cap.
