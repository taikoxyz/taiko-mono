## Findings

### R05-C-01 — zero-rent tail permits sustained ordinary capture without an anti-monopoly charge (Medium)

**Preconditions.** A dominant producer/prover can pay ordinary DA, proving and L1 costs and has a fresh, eligible candidate for each parent. Other capable participants exist but do not submit an included, rent-funded valid transaction before parent age 900 seconds, for example because their expected per-segment revenue is below the positive rent plus duplicate data/proving cost. The cartel wins L1 ordering at the zero-price boundary, potentially through a builder payment; no L1 censorship or invalid proof is assumed.

**Trace.** 1. After a parent is accepted, the cartel selects a real origin around parent age 480 and stages a complete child around age 500, satisfying the producer signature, proof and any mandatory FIFO prefix. 2. The stage matures around age 860; at age 900 its stage and origin are still below their 900-second expiry limits. The cartel waits until the specified rent becomes zero. 3. It submits the full data and proof, and its transaction is ordered before any later or same-block zero-price attempt. 4. The new head resets the rent clock. Repeat. A separate producer address is optional; the cartel never needs a publisher bucket, a proof job, an exclusive ticket, or a forced-queue request.

**Cost/gain.** Additional Etna admission rent is **0 ETH per captured head** in this trace. The attacker still pays staging, final DA, proof and L1 inclusion costs (plus any builder payment) and may gain ordinary fees, private order flow, MEV, or censorship value. It can rationally repeat while that net gain is positive. The specified positive lower bound of `q(D)` applies only under `A-COMPETITION(D<900)`, which explicitly includes a competitor willing and able to pay the positive quote and obtain inclusion. The existence of a capable or zero-rent-funded entrant alone does not establish that premise. For comparison, the example `D=660` would charge at least `0.013333333333333334 ETH` per early win, and a rapid age-60 winner pays `0.046666666666666667 ETH`; neither price is incurred here.

**Requirement and rule disposition.** This limits **R6**'s anti-monopoly economics and can narrow practical **R1** entry without creating an authorization gate. It does not falsify the stated conditional `ΣGnet < Σq` arithmetic: its precondition is absent. The candidate expressly admits zero-rent capture and market concentration at [roles.html:275](../design/roles.html#competition) and [roles.html:281](../design/roles.html#rent), and defines the stronger competition premise at [arguments.html:27](../design/arguments.html#assumptions). The exact rent reset/floor is at [index.html:159](../design/index.html#rent) and [arguments.html:110](../design/arguments.html#l4). Eligibility still requires an actual mature stage, complete proof and forced work; this is **not** a hash-only staging, fake-rent-refund or proof bypass. No new normative safeguard is missing from the stated bounded theorem. Whether this limited theorem satisfies the hard R6 anti-monopoly goal is a design judgment; real costs, competing revenue and the prevalence of a ready funded entrant are launch measurements, not established facts.

**Exploitability.** Mechanically feasible under the stated preconditions. Economic attractiveness is unresolved. This is a specification-level limitation of a rent that decays to zero, separately from calibration of its `0.05 ETH` peak.

### R05-C-02 — small bonded publishers face a scale barrier absent from canonical entry (Medium)

**Preconditions.** Users prefer the optional *bonded* one-second soft service; a large provider can fund sustained reserves and mandatory L1 fragment publication, while a small entrant can fund one bucket but not continuous overlapping buckets and data publication. No customer, challenger, or sponsor is required to subsidize the small entrant.

**Trace.** 1. Every signed block fragment consumes one reserved 10-TAIKO position and must appear with its FR preimage in L1 calldata by `issuedAt+240`, even if the final segment uses a blob. 2. A 600-TAIKO bucket issues at most 60 positions over 60 seconds, but its reserves stay locked until more than 3,600 seconds after issuance ends. 3. At one one-fragment promise per second, the specified just-in-time peak is 37,200 TAIKO; at four fragments per second it is 147,000 TAIKO, before a separate custodian or sealed-context covenant. 4. A capital-rich publisher can maintain a continuous branded bonded branch and internalize publication operations while smaller providers repeatedly fall back to short windows or visibly unbonded service. Users' ordinary client selection can concentrate soft order flow even though every address remains eligible to prove and submit canonically.

**Cost/gain.** Collateral is refundable if duties are satisfied, but its financing cost is real. Each published 32,768-byte nonzero fragment has a 2,097,152-gas calldata component, or `0.04194304 ETH` at 20 gwei, before fixed execution/storage overhead; smaller fragments scale down in byte cost. The incumbent gains possible coinbase fees, private order flow and reputation; the small entrant faces the same per-fragment ETH cost with much less working capital. No offense or slash follows from lawful dominance.

**Requirement and rule disposition.** This pressures the practical one-second bonded service under **R3/R4** and the concentration objective in **R6**, but it is not a whitelist or a canonical submission veto under **R1**. Exact reserve/peak accounting is at [roles.html:75](../design/roles.html#registry) and [roles.html:288](../design/roles.html#competition); the additional actual-publication cost and absent automatic subsidy are at [accountability.html:61](../design/accountability.html#accounting); unbonded display remains explicitly allowed at [codec.html:176](../design/codec.html#preconf). The design already identifies capital concentration as open. A sustainable customer-funded market and one-second execution/proving capacity need measurement. A rule promising equal bonded-provider market share would be a new requirement, not something inferable from open entry.

**Exploitability.** A plausible market concentration path, not an on-chain safety exploit. It deserves R6 economic adjudication and launch cost measurement, rather than a claim that an address cap would solve it.

### R05-C-03 — paid FIFO stuffing can defer a victim's forced attempt for many segments (Low)

**Preconditions.** The attacker can enqueue a finite backlog before the victim's force request, pay its ETH fees and L1 gas, and values delayed execution enough to bear those costs. Canonical segment cadence is finite. The victim's transaction fits the 2,048-byte/1,000,000-gas force limits and remains state-valid when reached.

**Trace.** 1. Starting from an empty queue, the attacker enqueues 1,000 bounded requests before the victim. 2. The victim enqueues after them and cannot jump ahead: the snapshot rule fixes indices and at most four due requests are processed per accepted segment. 3. Even after the snapshot and maturity delays, at least `ceil(1001/4)=251` accepted successors are needed to reach and process the victim. At the illustrative 660-second accepted-segment spacing, those 251 transitions alone take about 46 hours; this is a scenario, not a measured SLA. 4. Later malicious arrivals cannot increase the victim's predecessor count, so continued canonical progress eventually reaches it.

**Cost/gain.** The specified bulk quote totals `10.5 ETH` for 1,000 requests, with `5.25 ETH` irreversibly sunk if the attacker later controls every processing beneficiary and recovers the other half. Enqueue gas and any self-proving, staging, rent or builder costs are additional. Without control of the processors, the attacker's effective loss can approach the full fee. The gain is a long bounded delay of a time-sensitive victim operation; neither a free indefinite veto nor a violation of the FIFO proof follows.

**Requirement and rule disposition.** This limits the practical censorship escape in **R7**, while the formal finite-delay claim still holds under `A-LIVE(Δ)`. The four-item cap and fee are at [index.html:182](../design/index.html#forced) and [index.html:184](../design/index.html#forced); the conditional delay formula and explicit absence of a uniform bound are at [arguments.html:87](../design/arguments.html#l2) and [arguments.html:93](../design/arguments.html#l2). The candidate's own sink arithmetic is consistent. This is a priced, acknowledged throughput limit; its security significance depends on real forced-request value and sustainable accepted-segment spacing. It is not a missing FIFO mechanism.

**Exploitability.** Mechanically possible with a large paid predecessor set; profitability and observed delay are unverified launch/economic questions.

## Checks performed and limits

Read-only commands were `git status --short`, `git rev-parse HEAD`, `git ls-tree`, and `git show` of each frozen design/vector/README file, including line-numbered `git show | nl -ba` searches for report citations. A local Python integer calculation reproduced rent at ages 0, 60, 660, 899 and 900, the 1,000-request bulk queue fee (`10.5 ETH`) and sink (`5.25 ETH`), and the illustrative FIFO bound at `p=1000, Δ=660` (`167,340 seconds`, including the specification's conservative snapshot/maturity terms). These are arithmetic checks, not simulated L1 ordering, gas benchmarks, cryptographic vector verification, circuit tests or evidence of deployed economics. No contract/client test was run because this task neither changed nor audited production code.

The central distinction for the judge is that R05-C-01/02 are **current specification limits** of the chosen rent and voluntary bonded market. The rent formulas, publication deadlines and FIFO rules themselves survived the attempted bypasses above. Actual competitor budgets, extractable value, DA/fragment cost and sustained segment cadence are **launch measurements**; they cannot retroactively create a stronger anti-monopoly property in the specification. No convergence claim is made.
