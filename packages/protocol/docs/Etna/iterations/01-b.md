## B-01 — High: an active bonded publisher can invalidate public proving work without breaching any punishable duty

**Classification:** reproduced design-level attack and hard-requirement coverage failure; already anticipated as W1 by the draft, independently reconstructed here. It is not theft of finalized custody and does not halt the accepted chain.

**Exact evidence:** `design/index.html:26,71–73,103`; `design/roles.html:49,77`; `design/accountability.html:19–23,118–122`; `design/arguments.html:87–96`. Position equivocation and publisher double-signing both require two signed DutyClaims. Canonical submission requires no publisher claim. Availability default concerns failure to answer an admitted data request, not failure to select or publish a competing branch before proving.

**Preconditions:** M is an active publisher with a valid funded bucket. Honest nodes and a proof worker follow its public branch X rooted at accepted head H. M has ordinary proving capability and can win at least one open L1 submission race. Ethereum, proof soundness, network delivery of X, and all honest funding assumptions can hold.

**Trace:**

1. M signs and promptly serves every promised block of X. The honest worker starts proving X; users observe locally valid X.
2. M builds a conflicting branch Y from H in private. M signs no DutyClaim for Y and accepts no contradictory proof job. Nothing requires Y to inherit X's ordering or publisher certificate.
3. M generates a valid proof for Y and submits Y's complete canonical data and proof before X lands. M may use the **same address** as its known publisher owner and proof beneficiary: this trace does not need an identity-linking failure.
4. Y consumes H. X's proof is stale, provisional users roll back, and the honest worker loses completed or ongoing computation. Any early-parent-replacement cancellation returns the worker's job bond but does not reimburse that computation.
5. An accuser has the signed X claims and accepted Y receipt. The current slash predicates cannot combine those two objects: there is no second signed Y claim. Challenging X succeeds because M or another node has its publicly served bytes. Challenging Y requires a signed Y claim that does not exist; after landing Y is available anyway.
6. M repeats when worthwhile. A second address makes attribution harder, but is unnecessary for the initial exploit.

**Cost and gain:** ordinary computation for Y, DA and winning L1 submission fees; any normal publisher reserve remains locked on normal terms but is not lost. There is **zero additional objective withholding penalty** in this trace. Gain can be private-order-flow/MEV extraction or strategic destruction of a competitor's proving investment; exact profitability needs market measurement. A competitor cannot force reimbursement by funding more challenges because no defined offense exists.

**Violated requirement:** R6's requested objective deterrence against an active role holder privately building to invalidate competitors' blocks/proofs or cause soft reorgs. R4 does not imply irrevocable soft finality, and this finding does not add that requirement. R1 and R7 can still work exactly as specified while this attack succeeds.

**Why allowed surrogates do not close it:** the user allows response timeouts and attestations rather than direct proof of historical P2P nonpublication. The design genuinely meets that narrower service-default goal. But its surrogate predicates impose no adverse consequence at all on this active-holder trace. Honest availability attestations for X neither restrict Y's canonical admission nor make an attributable contradiction out of its accepted proof. Calling the 100% false-negative strategy a disclosed limitation does not establish a defense against the requested attack.

**False positives/negatives:** conditional zero false positives and zero false negatives for a correctly admitted *missed response* can coexist with 100% false negatives for this strategy. Penalizing every losing X would instead let M slash honest public publishers by winning Y; that is not an acceptable repair. No empirical rate is inferred.

**Minimal fix/disposition:** this needs a mechanism change, not larger response bonds or a shorter response timeout. At minimum, specify an enforceable relation among an active publisher's signed branch, an accepted competing segment and collateral; combining an accepted receipt with a contradictory signed obligation would close the same-owner instance if the duty explicitly covers it. That limited repair must be accompanied by an honest-race analysis and must not be advertised as closing Sybil/private-beneficiary bypasses. Full closure requires an enforceable public ordering/publication predicate, a separately justified attribution or attestation assumption that actually constrains harmful admission, or an explicitly approved narrowing of the withholding requirement. Evaluate any such change against R1/R7 and failed-holder recovery. Until then retain **R6 fail/not ready**, not “accepted Medium risk.”

## B-02 — Medium: early sealing can consume all usable origins and stall the one-second soft service

**Classification:** deterministic specification gap for a permitted workload; not a prover-speed benchmark failure and not a permanent canonical halt. A publisher can avoid it with admission/packing constraints that the draft does not yet specify.

**Exact evidence:** `design/architecture.md:12–14,23,29`; `design/index.html:59–64,71,111,128,134–139`. Every segment has a fixed authenticated origin strictly newer than its parent's. The total encoded segment may be 122880 bytes, with early sealing at the cap. Each next soft block needs the fixed origin context to become VALID_IN_VIEW. No rule reserves bytes for subsequent mandatory headers/anchors or limits a single block to a small fraction of the segment.

**Preconditions:** healthy 12-second L1 production, honest promptly pinned headers, adequate execution/proving capacity, and a live publisher whose current segment uses the newest usable origin O. The attacker can pay the normal fee for a valid large transaction admitted by the publisher. Choose its data length so one block fits the segment cap but leaves less room than the next empty block's required header/anchor/encoding. Large calldata can meet this condition without violating the 10-million block gas bound; an implementation's eventual encoding must instantiate the exact byte count.

**Trace:**

1. Immediately after a fresh usable O is selected, the publisher emits a valid block containing the admitted large transaction. Its segment is now full and must seal.
2. At the next one-second issuance point, an empty successor cannot fit in that segment.
3. A new segment must use origin O′ strictly greater than O. During the interval before the next L1 header exists, no such authenticated header is available. A fabricated future origin is UNKNOWN or invalid, not a locally completed preconfirmation.
4. The service waits for an L1 header, violating its one-second issuance target despite healthy execution hardware and no prover outage. Repeated early sealing creates a sustained capacity dependency: on a branch whose full blocks each consume a segment, segment production cannot exceed authenticated-origin production. A finite stash of old origins only postpones exhaustion; timestamps also limit using arbitrarily old ones.

**Cost and gain:** the attack pays ordinary L2 execution/data fees and needs the publisher to admit the block; no bond forfeiture, L1 censorship or proof failure is necessary. It buys a soft-service interruption. Canonical progression and forced FIFO remain available. This is bounded degradation, hence Medium rather than a permanent-halt High.

**Violated/incomplete requirement:** R4's at-most-one-second healthy cadence is not specified for all permitted early-seal cases. R5's claim that shorter L1 intervals do not affect the soft mechanism needs this qualification; no prohibited CL schedule was found. The issue cannot be repaired by simply buying a faster prover.

**Minimal fix:** define an admission and packing policy that reserves enough encoded space for empty/system blocks through the maximum promised origin-acquisition delay, and backpressure discretionary transactions before exhausting that reserve. Specify what “transaction admission” means for the ≤1-second user-latency target so queued traffic is not misleadingly counted as confirmed. Alternatively, permit origin reuse with a reviewed monotone snapshot rule; prove FIFO progress from freshness rather than requiring a unique L1 origin per segment. Any reuse change needs its own boundary proof. Merely documenting a worst-case execution benchmark is insufficient. A maximum L1 observation/pinning delay is an assumption, not a requirement to read CL slot schedules.

## Rejected halt traces and precise remaining limits

### Fresh force requests cannot repeatedly invalidate a sealed proof

Appending one request per second while an honest proof runs does not change that proof's parent snapshot. The boundary check chooses the exact queue tail at fixed origin O (`index.html:121–122`). An attacker cannot insert a lower index afterward. Each due FIFO item has a finite number of predecessors, and advancing canonical segments remove up to four. With the draft's conditional Δ bound, the stated conservative bound

`processedAt ≤ enqueueTime + 720 + (1 + ceil((predecessors + 1)/4)) × Δ`

survives the attempted append/empty-proof attack. An attacker can repeatedly invalidate **discretionary** branches by winning real valid successors, but those successors make canonical progress and cannot indefinitely skip a fixed mature forced item. This is not a proof of normal-mempool fairness.

The force fee is a capital lock and consumer payment, not necessarily a net burned spam cost: an actor that queues and later proves its own requests can recover the queue fees. For example, 1000 entries added to an empty queue lock `0.001 × 50 × (1+…+20) = 10.5 ETH`, before gas, and require 250 successor segments once mature. Replenishing behind an existing victim cannot increase that victim's predecessor count. Any economic calibration must therefore price queue capital, enqueue/proving gas, recovery and opportunity cost rather than count all 10.5 ETH as permanently lost. This is an economic launch question, not a demonstrated violation of the finite-predecessor progress theorem (`architecture.md:24`; `arguments.html:66–71`).

### Proof-window exhaustion is real but has no demonstrated permanent lock under the assumptions

A fixed origin can expire after 600 seconds; backup started too late can inherit an already-doomed statement. A funded honest entrant need not keep that statement: with an unchanged accepted head it can reselect a fresh origin, rebuild and prove. The illustrative 60-second production + 180-second proving + 120-second inclusion is 360 seconds; even a separate 120-second pin delay fits 480 seconds if work/origin choice is consistent. This is feasibility arithmetic, not measured liveness.

If a competitor keeps consuming the parent before inclusion, the honest proof is stale but the chain is progressing. If congestion repeatedly prevents any valid fresh proof landing, A-INCLUDE/freshness feasibility has failed; the draft already says this. No contract escape can manufacture a validity proof when every capable prover is offline. Do not report these acknowledged premise failures as new R1 exploits (`arguments.html:19,22,62–63`).

### Proof-job and challenge state races are bounded

A late incompatible child cannot erase a missed worker deadline because settlement uses immutable first-parent-consumption and revision-retirement timestamps. A before-deadline competitor cancels the voluntary job without holding canonical rights. Same-digest availability revelation is lazy per challenge, so many signers cannot force an unbounded reveal loop. Equivocation slashing cancels an outstanding challenge and refunds its untouched deposit; it does not create a second slash against the same position (`accountability.html:17,24–29`).

L1 censorship or an unaffordable fee increase can still cause an honest service default. The draft states the relevant assumptions and 100% worst-case false-positive rate; no new false-default exploit within its stated notice/funding/inclusion bounds was established.

### Migration is finite if its evidence exists; finite is not automatically affordable

Frozen tails and single-pending forced commits prevent the attacker from extending migration work after installation. The deterministic default source and timestamp lower bound prevent the committer from choosing arbitrary payloads or impossible two-block timing. Legacy data-fetch failure is deliberately not reclassified as malformed data: `packages/taiko-client/driver/chain_syncer/event/derivation/source_fetcher.go:55–67` distinguishes structural zero-blob fallback from retrieval failure; `packages/protocol/docs/Derivation.md:198–224` supplies forced/timestamp rules.

Required closure remains substantial: authenticate every old blob/witness; pin compatible immutable RISC0/SP1 programs; establish exact deployed ownership/layout; show upgrades can be installed without an unsafe intermediate state; rehearse one fresh unregistered prover completing the drain and activation. Missing evidence blocks activation; it is not permissionless runtime dependence on an inactive DAO. No supplied live deployment facts prove that those conditions hold (`migration.html:342–355,379–393,428–437`).

## Impossibility boundary and recommendation

No impossibility theorem for literal R1–R7 follows from this review. In particular, one-second *provisional* issuance and minute-long proof completion can coexist, and liveness may reasonably assume some funded capable prover and bounded L1 inclusion.

A narrower statement is proved: **given this candidate's acceptance rule, optional duty transcripts, and no extra publication/order/attribution evidence, an on-chain rule cannot distinguish two histories with the same accepted Y proof/data and the same fully served X promises solely by whether Y was gossiped privately or publicly before landing.** Both histories produce the same available inputs to the slashing predicate. This constrains the present transcript, not all timeout/attestation designs permitted by the user. Introducing an enforceable surrogate may change those inputs and requires fresh analysis.

Recommended disposition: retain not-ready status; require a concrete B-01 redesign or an explicitly authorized, precisely worded relaxation; close B-02 in the normative admission/packing rules; keep performance and migration facts as evidence gates. Up to eight review rounds and an evidence-backed eventual negative verdict are acceptable. Do not manufacture convergence by changing the meaning of withholding, demanding impossible universal prover availability, or relabeling a hard requirement failure as accepted risk.
