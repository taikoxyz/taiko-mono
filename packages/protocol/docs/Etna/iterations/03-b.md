## Verdict

**No new Critical, High, Medium or Low normative liveness vulnerability was established.** This is a clean review of this angle under the expressly permitted bounded assumptions, not an implementation-readiness or measured-performance result.

The active-holder withholding defense is substantive: the holder cannot canonically land a candidate without actual matching public data aged 360 seconds; after publication another entrant can use the execution context, select its own producer/beneficiary and generate its own proof without the holder's authorization. A holder that never publishes cannot reserve the head. Recovery can construct another child from accepted state. These are admission rules, not assumptions that the incumbent behaves honestly.

The design does permit temporary service failure, mature competing branches, wasted speculative proof work, paid queue backlog and concentration of ordinary order flow. I did not silently elevate those outcomes into irreversible preconfirmation requirements, a universal identity oracle, unlimited insurance, or liveness without a capable funded prover. The explicit limits remain relevant to the final judge.

## Concrete adversarial traces

These are tested attack hypotheses and residual scenarios, **not confirmed findings**. Each records the relevant cost, attempted gain, requirement and reason it does or does not falsify the design. No repair is required for a rejected hypothesis; the cited rules must survive implementation.

### B1 — active holder privately withholds an alternative branch

**Preconditions:** attacker controls an active publisher and enough proving capacity to build a conflicting valid branch Y from accepted H. Honest users follow provisional X. L1/DA assumptions hold.

1. Attacker privately constructs Y and its proof while continuing to offer X.
2. It tries to submit Y's full data and proof as Y's first publication.
3. The selected stage receipt is absent or younger than 360 seconds; canonical acceptance fails.
4. Alternatively it stages Y, withholds its producer signature/proof, and waits for maturity.
5. An entrant retrieves Y's exact context and data, constructs its own full statement/self-authorization, and proves it; it needs neither the original producer nor an optional job.
6. If Y is never staged, the entrant constructs and stages another child of H. The unavailable Y has no reservation.

**Cost/gain:** private compute and paid failed/staging transactions; a mature valid Y can still win and earn execution revenues or reorg soft X. No exclusive withholding veto or first-disclosure-at-acceptance advantage survives. The entrant must pay its own compute/DA/gas; fair race outcomes are not implied.

**Requirements:** R1, R3, R6, R7. **Evidence:** `staging.html:8–12,57–61,90–94,118–121,228–231`; `index.html:114,154–157`. **Disposition/severity:** rejected as a liveness vulnerability; no finding. **Minimal fix:** none to the specified rules.

### B2 — withhold until a late service response, or use another owner/base

**Preconditions:** attacker issued authentic funded DutyClaims, optionally a sealed PublicationClaim. The reporter knows the signature and can fund included evidence. Service collateral remains live.

1. Attacker selectively refuses P2P fragments.
2. A funded challenger opens the fragment challenge.
3. Attacker answers at the included deadline, avoiding the serving-default slash, or stays silent and loses the position.
4. For a sealed covenant it stages after `issuedAt+240`, then tries another stage salt to hide the miss.
5. Immutable first-context publication still proves default; late publication cannot cure it.
6. It can avoid the narrow owner-conflict slash by using a distinct producer owner or a mismatched pipelined base. Every accepted segment still has to satisfy universal stage age.

**Cost/gain:** compliant late response costs publication gas and earns the deposited Q; a deliberate missed duty costs 10 TAIKO, or at least 9 TAIKO net if self-reported, plus gas. Collateral caps do not insure the user's arbitrary loss. Early selective serving can favor private peers for a bounded interval.

**False positives/negatives:** serving-default detection is deterministic after an admitted unmet duty, conditional on known signatures/reporting. Historical gossip misconduct can be missed 100% when a timely response arrives; cross-owner/base conflict evidence can also miss 100% of that class. An honestly censored/unfunded responder can default; the mechanism claims breach, not malicious intent. These cases are explicitly analyzed, not advertised as zero-error intent detection.

**Requirements:** R3, R6. **Evidence:** `accountability.html:14–21,155–166`; `staging.html:180–181,211–225`. **Disposition/severity:** documented bounded-surrogate limits; no new finding under the permitted interpretation. **Minimal fix:** none required here; retain the limited promise in client displays and verdicts.

### B3 — all jobs and service buckets are squatted

**Preconditions:** attacker can post substantial TAIKO collateral and accepts all attractive published jobs before honest workers.

1. It opens many buckets and accepts offered jobs.
2. It abandons every job and refuses data service.
3. It asks the Inbox to wait for its lease, ticket, witness certificate or deadline.
4. There is no such canonical gate. A new funded entrant stages/proves a child and can land it immediately upon ordinary eligibility.
5. If the parent remains unconsumed at a job deadline, permissionless settlement slashes the worker. Changing the head only after the deadline cannot erase default.

**Cost/gain:** 600 TAIKO locked per bucket, 1,000 per accepted job; each job default loses 1,000 or 900 net under colluding reporting. Honest sponsors can lose time and subsidized availability, but cannot lose a reserved canonical right because none exists. A timely competing child cancels the abandoned job without penalty; that outcome itself establishes canonical progress.

**Requirements:** R1, R3, R6. **Evidence:** `roles.html:73–87,93–104,216`; `accountability.html:38–43`. **Disposition/severity:** rejected canonical deadlock; optional procurement grief remains priced and disclosed. **Minimal fix:** none.

### B4 — invalid staging, deterministic-ID front-running and expiry resets

**Preconditions:** attacker can fund L1 publications and observe an honest staging transaction.

1. It stages impossible outputs or many spam contexts.
2. It front-runs the honest deterministic ID, then tries to refresh its timestamp repeatedly.
3. Different content cannot occupy the same ID without defeating its hash binding; identical publication still has to supply actual matching data.
4. Duplicate stage calls return the original immutable receipt; they cannot delay maturity or overwrite history.
5. A new salt creates a separate interval, not a reset of the honest receipt or the context's first publication.
6. An expired context cannot revive by salt alone because its fixed earlier origin is also expired.

**Cost/gain:** attacker pays publication and storage gas per new record. It can consume paid bandwidth and optional inspection resources. It cannot require honest peers to prove every candidate or reserve H with an impossible descriptor.

**Requirements:** R1, R3, R6, R7. **Evidence:** `staging.html:60–61,90–95,111–115`. **Disposition/severity:** no normative veto; global paid L1 congestion remains an environmental cost. **Minimal fix:** none; bounded parsing/work selection is already specified.

### B5 — proof-window exhaustion and stale-parent churn

**Preconditions:** attacker has mature valid competing candidates. Honest participants satisfy the stated retrieval/proof/funding assumptions; accepted parent and origin are known.

1. Honest entrant builds, stages and proves a child.
2. Attacker consumes H before its proof lands, wasting the honest work.
3. Honest entrant rebases; attacker repeats.
4. Each successful interruption is itself a valid accepted successor and must meet the forced-prefix rule.
5. If attacker stops advancing H, a fresh honest candidate can complete the pipeline within the stated bounds; no immature/failed job locks it.

**Cost/gain:** attacker pays compute and staging/landing DA, potentially offset by block revenue/MEV; honest discretionary work can be repeatedly wasted. This is not a proof of an economically fair race. A monopoly on ordinary ordering remains possible without violating FIFO.

**Timing check:** illustrative `60+120+360+120=660` seconds leaves 240 seconds inside a 900-second origin budget before header-acquisition overhead. Origin pinning and correct context selection are necessary. An origin older than 480 seconds cannot accept a new conservative 300-second job with 120-second extra freshness, but raw submission remains open. No design promise covers every viable pipelined candidate with a job subsidy.

**Requirements:** R1, R3, R4, R6, R7. **Evidence:** `roles.html:96–104`; `staging.html:188–197`; `arguments.html:67–80,132–143`. **Disposition/severity:** permitted race/market limitation, not a new liveness finding. **Minimal fix:** none under the requested scope; do not market an unconditional latency or fair-selection guarantee.

### B6 — forced-inclusion starvation through empty proofs and queue flooding

**Preconditions:** victim successfully enqueues a bounded request at timestamp t with finite p predecessors. Attacker controls candidate selection and can fund arbitrary later enqueues.

1. Attacker repeatedly accepts cheap segments using an old origin/cut to omit the victim.
2. Within 900 seconds that old origin becomes invalid; indefinitely progressing history must advance origins.
3. Once origin time is at least `t+120`, its exact queue cut contains the victim and all predecessors are mature.
4. Each child consumes the next up-to-four due entries before discretionary work.
5. Attacker appends more entries; they are behind the victim and cannot increase p.
6. Refusing the mandatory prefix makes its proof/action invalid. If it stops progress entirely, a funded honest entrant can perform that finite prefix.

**Cost/gain:** a finite preexisting backlog delays the victim. Starting empty, 1,000 enqueues without processing cost 10.5 ETH in fees and sink at least 5.25 ETH even with all processor credits recovered, plus gas and proof/DA costs. Interleaving that keeps the queue below 50 instead sinks only 0.5 ETH per 1,000 requests, but cannot hold a fixed earlier victim behind those later arrivals.

**Bound check:** under an actual accepted-successor spacing bound Δ, processing occurs by `t+1020+(1+ceil((p+1)/4))*Δ`. For p=9 and Δ=660 this is 3,660 seconds (61 minutes). Δ is conditional, not derived from L1 slot duration or advertised as measured. Processing is an Ethereum-validity-checked attempt, not guaranteed success for an invalid nonce or insufficient fee.

**Requirements:** R1, R6, R7. **Evidence:** `index.html:168–175`; `arguments.html:73–80`. **Disposition/severity:** no fixed-victim starvation counterexample under the premises. **Minimal fix:** none; retain finite-backlog and valid-transaction qualifications.

### B7 — L1 congestion, deadline censorship and shorter slots

**Preconditions:** attacker pays for L1 capacity or controls some inclusion opportunities; honest services have finite budgets.

1. Delay staging or canonical inclusion until the selected origin expires.
2. Delay a valid fragment response past 600 seconds, or raise effective fees beyond Q's opening quote.
3. The honest service can default or rebuild even though it tried to comply.
4. This trace requires failure of the relevant bounded observation/funded inclusion assumption, not a protocol-reserved denial right.
5. Shortening slots alone does not change the seconds-based timers. Nominal 256-block history windows at 12/6/4/2 seconds are 3,072/1,536/1,024/512 seconds; prompt 120-second pinning still fits each.

**Cost/gain:** censorship/resource cost is L1-market dependent, not quantified by an invented security budget. Q funds one response at twice the reference quote; it is neither prepaid ETH for the responding account nor insurance against unlimited fees. A malicious reporter may collect 10% of a false-intent default, but cannot forge the actual missed deadline.

**Requirements:** R3–R7. **Evidence:** `accountability.html:158–160`; `arguments.html:20–29,45–46`; `index.html:203`. **Disposition/severity:** explicit bounded-liveness failure case, not a newly discovered normative gap. **Minimal fix:** none under permitted assumptions; assess actual launch funding and response resources separately.

### B8 — DAO disappearance during installed migration and all-incumbent outage

**Preconditions:** installation bundle is complete, the fixed manifest is correct, historical state/data are reachable and at least one funded prover can generate the specified legacy and Etna proofs.

1. Every incumbent and DAO stops operating.
2. A new entrant proves the frozen historical suffix and permissionlessly commits/proves each frozen legacy forced entry.
3. A malicious committer cannot add discretionary bytes, expand the frozen tail, collect a fee for commitment alone or monopolize the pending proof.
4. The entrant supplies the exact objective drain certificate and activates Etna.
5. Public ACTIVE-state witnesses enable the first system anchor; no second DAO signature is necessary.
6. Existing service failure after launch is handled through fresh staging/proving from accepted state and separate duty settlement.

**Cost/gain:** paid proof and transaction work; an adversary can withhold its own proof, but a capable replacement can recompute from public data. Historic data or a usable audited legacy proof program being genuinely absent blocks launch; the design does not silently discard that old obligation to manufacture progress.

**Requirements:** R1, R2, R3, R5, R7. **Evidence:** `migration.html:227–245,345–358,380–396`; `staging.html:228–232`. **Disposition/severity:** no post-install discretionary DAO liveness gate found. **Minimal fix:** none; actual manifest/program/data compatibility remains mandatory launch validation.
