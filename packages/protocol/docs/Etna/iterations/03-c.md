# Etna round 03, angle C — censorship and market capture

**Independent reviewer invocation:** `gpt-6-sol`, reasoning `high` (requested model). **Committed design snapshot:** `29cc3b060bf935c2a842e2b28f6f174f0652a00d`. **Scope read:** all six `design/*.html` pages, `design/encoding-vectors.md`, and the R1–R7 ledger in `README.md`. The design files and ledger matched the committed snapshot when reviewed. This review did not consult prior iteration reports, architecture, revision notes, or production implementation, and makes no launch benchmark claim.

**Verdict for this angle:** one new **High** normative gap in R6. The actual-data staging rule closes first disclosure *at canonical landing*, and the forced FIFO gives a conditional escape for bounded user transactions. Neither supplies a timely publication obligation for the *bonded soft block whose data an active publisher withholds*. A funded availability challenge can remain open until after an alternative staged branch has won. The optional sealed-context covenant need never be signed. R6 therefore does not yet pass, even though the first-reveal-at-landing subcase is addressed. No claim here requires universal identification of common owners, insurance against unlimited MEV, zero-prover liveness, or a ban on prior generic publication under R7.

## C-01 — A signed soft block can remain privately withheld until after a different branch settles

**Severity:** High. **Violated gate:** R6's required defense against active-holder private withholding and objective penalties. R3's publisher duty is affected. This is a *normative coverage gap*: the written rules expressly make the absolute sealed-context promise optional, while the available fragment challenge has a later deadline than the competing branch's earliest settlement. It is not a conjectured implementation bug.

**Preconditions.** A bonded publisher controls a bucket and gives a user a locally valid soft block X with the signed `DutyClaim` and complete bytes required for that user's check, but selectively withholds those bytes from independent proving peers. No other holder republishes X before Y settles; a recipient with the bytes *could* do so and collect the challenge reward if it has the means and chooses to act. The attacker can stage and prove a different valid branch Y from the same accepted head using its own funds. The user or an observer knows X's signed claim and can fund an immediate valid challenge; this makes the trace stronger than assuming nobody reports. The attacker uses a different self-controlled producer owner for Y, or a later mismatched immediate base, so the narrow same-owner conflict predicate is unavailable. A capable prover and bounded L1 inclusion exist; no permanent L1 censorship is needed.

**Attack trace.**

1. At `t=0`, issue the signed one-second block X to the selected user and withhold its claimed fragment(s) from the public prover market. Do **not** sign an optional `PublicationClaim` for X's eventual sealed StageContext. X remains a valid provisional block for the recipient, but unrelated peers cannot prove or stage that branch from the missing bytes.
2. Prepare a different valid execution branch Y and publish its complete body/context through `DARegistry` at `t≈60`. This satisfies the actual-data staging rule for **Y**; the rule does not require publication of the separately signed X. A staged candidate has no exclusive right, but the attacker can privately prepare Y's proof.
3. Let an observer challenge X's signed fragment at `t≈0–60`, locking the prescribed `Q` ETH. The response deadline is `openedAt+600` seconds ([accountability.html line 16](../design/accountability.html#challenge)). At `t≈420`, Y has aged 360 seconds and can be republished with a valid proof to consume the parent ([staging.html lines 7–12, 117–121](../design/staging.html#purpose)). X's soft users roll back; any X proof from that parent becomes stale.
4. At `t≈590` (or any time through the actual challenge deadline), publish X's matching fragment to answer the challenge. The responder gets Q; X's 10-TAIKO position survives. Repeating this for every challenged fragment discharges those serving claims. A disclosure after Y won does not restore the soft branch. The attacker never accepted the optional `issuedAt+240` sealed publication covenant, so no absolute publication default exists ([accountability.html line 20](../design/accountability.html#sealed-publication); [roles.html line 85](../design/roles.html#registry)).
5. If a reporter presents X against Y, `proveCanonicalConflict` requires Y's authenticated producer to equal X bucket's owner **and** an exact immediate base/height match. A different self-controlled owner address defeats the owner predicate without forging a signature ([accountability.html lines 21–29](../design/accountability.html#canonical-conflict); [staging.html lines 179–181](../design/staging.html#block-tree)). This false negative is acknowledged, and no identity oracle is assumed here.

**Attacker cost and gain.** The 600-TAIKO bucket (10 TAIKO for X's position) is refundable after its evidence window if all claims are answered; a separate owner key needs no added bond for raw canonical submission. Y costs a real L1 stage publication, duplicate final DA publication, proof computation/verification and transaction fees. If the attacker itself answers the fragment challenge, Q moves from the observer to that responder; it still pays response gas. With prompt funded challenges, the attacker avoids the 10-TAIKO slash by answering before `openedAt+600`. The gain is the ability to land the attacker's Y ordering and make X's signed soft transaction(s) and competitors' proof work stale; any extractable ordering value is additional and unbounded by the 10-TAIKO reserve. This is a feasible cost/gain path, not a claim that every Y wins or that all MEV exceeds gas.

**Why the existing defenses do not close it.** Mandatory stage age blocks *first disclosure of Y at landing* and gives the public 360 seconds to prepare a proof of Y. It does not make X available before Y can settle. A challenge's 600-second period is useful for eventual data recovery, but permits the active holder to wait out the canonical race if nobody else publishes X. The optional sealed covenant would punish a signer who actually made it and missed its deadline; making that distinct claim optional leaves no absolute publish-by duty for every bonded soft X. Same-owner conflict cannot be extended to common economic control without an identity premise. The FIFO escape can eventually force an admissible user's raw transaction, but cannot preserve X's one-second soft ordering or restore the wasted proof. These limits are explicit at [arguments.html §withholding, lines 95–114](../design/arguments.html#withholding) and [accountability.html lines 155–166](../design/accountability.html#offenses).

**Minimal design fix.** For every block that clients label a **bonded publisher preconfirmation**, require the signature to carry a bounded, exact body/fragment commitment and an objective absolute L1 publication deadline tied to that block's issuance, independent of a later optional sealed segment key or of a challenge being opened. Set the deadline and data path so a timely publication gives a funded peer the stated retrieval/proving opportunity in the ordinary 360-second maturation window; the present 600-second response horizon does not do so for the worked trace. Record first actual publication of the committed complete bytes (or all independently bounded fragments plus their ordered manifest) in an open calldata/DA path; use a signed timestamp constrained by the preexisting bucket window, and slash the reserved position if publication is late even when bytes later arrive or another owner wins. A later `StageContext` can still be constructed and staged nonexclusively; canonical acceptance still carries full data plus proof. Specify the extra positions/capital and gas that a one-second service needs. Calibrate the slash and/or required service tier against realistic extractable value; do not claim unlimited indemnity or that timely published X must win against an already mature rival. An alternative is to cease advertising bonded soft availability before actual publication and make that narrower user guarantee explicit, but then demonstrate how R4's useful one-second soft service and R6's active-holder defense are still met.

**Predicate quality.** This fix proves a missed signed publication deadline, not malicious intent or universal P2P nonreceipt. It may slash an honest signer whose publication is censored, unfunded or delayed beyond the bound (false positive for *intent*, not for the contracted on-chain service outcome). Under the named funding and inclusion premises, timely publication avoids that result. A signer can still compute privately before signing, disclose only just before the deadline, or avoid offering a bonded preconfirmation (false negatives for generic private behavior). Clients must never portray an unbonded signature as carrying this penalty. This bounded objective surrogate targets the concrete active-holder attack without demanding a universal identity oracle or irreversible soft finality.

## Limits retained without a new finding

The forced queue's four-item FIFO progress and half-fee sink withstand address splitting at the level of *protocol cost*: self-processing recovers at most half of each fee. Its delay grows with paid predecessors and accepted-segment spacing, so it offers no uniform short SLA under a large backlog ([index.html §forced, lines 170–175](../design/index.html#forced); [arguments.html §l2, lines 73–80](../design/arguments.html#l2)). An attacker may find queue flooding profitable if outside MEV exceeds the documented sink, gas and proof/DA costs. That is an economic calibration question, not a demonstrated bypass of FIFO. Likewise a better-funded prover/builder cartel can win many discretionary races, but the written rules grant it no exclusive admission ticket and force processing of due requests on each progressing chain. No fairness theorem or identity independence is claimed. The launch design still needs actual price, proof-throughput and maximum-gas evidence before describing the proposed constants as strong economic deterrence ([roles.html §competition, lines 215–222](../design/roles.html#competition)).
