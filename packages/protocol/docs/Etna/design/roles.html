Etna design / Roles and economics

Roles without admission authorities

Reviewed specification. Service promises are optional and never reserve the canonical head.

Assumed A-IMPLEMENTATION: unlabelled interface declarations, table cells and normative transition rules specify intended behavior, not deployed or tested code. Proven marks a logical consequence of those rules; Assumed names a dependency; Open marks an unresolved claim.

1. Separate a service promise from canonical admission

Proven, by the specified admission rule: any address can call payable submitSegment with an applicable proof, matching republished data, the required forced transactions, an eligible public staging receipt, mandatory producer authorization and the current canonical admission rent. The relaying caller needs neither a service key nor TAIKO collateral. The producer authorizes the exact statement with its own EOA key or an objectively self-registered immutable signing key; the caller may be a different payer. Service registration cannot delay, veto or replace this action. The first valid transaction in canonical L1 order extending the exact accepted head wins; ordinary losing transactions may pay gas. Rent buys no future reservation or preferential ordering.

An unbonded stager first publishes a complete candidate through the open DARegistry. It obtains no priority. A publisher offers users a signed provisional branch. A custodian promises actual public calldata publication of specified bytes by a fixed deadline. A worker can accept a funded proof job. These are separate, voluntary economic services. A role holder obtains no exclusive sequencing right, committee vote, mandatory relay position or governance privilege. The same address can perform every role, and different addresses are not evidence of different economic owners.

Assumed A-EXEC + A-L1 + A-ECON: someone remains able and willing to submit a proof-bearing successor at affordable L1 gas prices. All existing service providers can leave and a new address can still do so. If every capable prover everywhere stops, the chain halts safely until a prover returns. No DAO action restores mathematical proof production.

Proven, under the staging rule: a never-staged private branch cannot first reveal its body at canonical landing: a matching public stage must be at least 360 seconds old. Every bonded fragment promise also requires actual calldata publication by its signed issuance time plus 240 seconds, whether or not anybody challenged it. Open: already mature public competing branches can still replace provisional state, and private computation before any promise is not itself an offense. Fragment and sealed-context deadlines plus attributable same-owner canonical conflicts create bounded objective liability; they do not prove every peer received gossip. See public staging and accountability.

2. Role and accounting glossary

Stager
Anyone publishing the complete candidate data and context through DARegistry, paying ETH transaction/blob costs and obtaining no ordering right.
Producer
The EOA or objectively registered immutable-key owner authorizing a canonical segment statement. It need not be the proof beneficiary, payer or bonded publisher.
Publication-by covenant
A separate reserved PublicationClaim that promises the complete sealed context will have an actual public staging receipt by issuedAt +240 seconds.
Fragment publication duty
Every signed DutyClaim promises complete fragment bytes in calldata with the exact block-fragment manifest preimage by issuedAt +240 seconds. A challenge may fund earlier recovery but is not required to create this deadline.
Publisher
An optional signer distributing executed provisional blocks and their dependency data.
Custodian
An optional signer accepting a bounded, fragment-specific public-publication obligation, with opportunistic serving before publication.
Proof producer
Anyone computing a ZK proof; it need not submit the transaction or accept a job.
Submitter / payer
Anyone relaying and paying gas plus the current ETH canonical rent for a complete proof-bearing L1 action. Proof-bound beneficiaries cannot be replaced by copying the transaction; unused call value is credited to the actual caller.
Worker
A proof producer that voluntarily accepts a particular escrowed job and its deadline liability.
Sponsor
An address funding an immutable proof-job bounty. Sponsorship grants no protocol administration.
Challenger / reporter
Anyone opening a bonded availability challenge or presenting objective slash evidence.
Responder
Anyone publishing the complete bytes satisfying a live challenge; it need not be the signer.
Relayer
Anyone transporting gossip, proofs, transactions or existing Bridge messages without changing authenticated contents.
Duty bucket
An immutable registry record reserving 60 independent service positions for one owner, signing key, kind and branch context. A position is an obligation index, never an Ethereum slot.
Position
A separately reserved 10 TAIKO liability for one signed content identity; incompatible content identities for the same position are equivocation.
Content identity
The canonical hash of every semantic promise field, including bucket, position, branch context, block/manifest commitment, fragment index, digest, byte length and issuedAt. Both DutyClaim and PublicationClaim use issuedAt to fix an absolute publication deadline; changing it is an incompatible promise, not a harmless retry.
Free balance
Deposited TAIKO not backing a duty, job, payout credit or sink entry. It may be withdrawn immediately by its owner.
Sink
Tokens permanently excluded from withdrawable accounting. This is not an ERC20 burn, and there is no owner sweep.
Canonical rent
An identity-independent ETH admission price that decreases from 0.05 ETH to zero over 900 seconds since the last accepted head. Every accepted head restarts that clock; 100% of the charged rent remains in a nonwithdrawable ledger sink.
DAO
The sole upgrade owner; it is not a registry manager, scheduler, fee setter, pauser, slashing judge or recovery operator.

A contract account may own collateral and designate an immutable ECDSA service key. No citizenship, hardware vendor, TEE identity, current-member vote or permissioned signature is required.

4. Bucket state machine and bounded liabilities

Proven, by reservation: registration atomically moves exactly 60 × 10 × 1018 token-wei from free balance into positions. There is no overbooking and no reuse of a position in another bucket. Buckets have independent IDs, so a key's many parallel buckets require proportionate locked collateral. One owner may hold arbitrarily many buckets; this creates a capital cost, not identity independence.

  1. Open. Kind 1 is publisher, kind 2 is custodian; reject other values. The context is the committed revision and canonical base-head digest. Store immutable owner, signingKey, roleKind, revision, baseHeadHash, openedAt, issuanceEnd, claimDomainVersion, evidenceClose and 60 fresh positions. Set openedAt = block.timestamp, issuanceEnd = openedAt + 60, claimDomainVersion = 2, evidenceClose = issuanceEnd + 3600. Registration cannot backdate its start. There is no retentionEnd field or post-publication service interval.
  2. Issue. A valid EIP-712 claim uses the EtnaDuty domain with version "2" and binds chain ID, registry address, implementation revision, bucket ID, position, immutable context, semantic content identity and an issuedAt in [openedAt, issuanceEnd). The timestamp fixes publishBy = issuedAt +240 and is part of the semantic promise. Clients only call a claim bonded after verifying this preexisting reserve and the v2 rules in their L1 view. A claim against an unconfirmed registry view remains reorg-conditional; a v1 serving-only signature is not upgraded into a v2 publication promise.
  3. Publish. Every DutyClaim requires complete matching fragment bytes in calldata plus the exact FR manifest preimage, validated against the claim, at or before publishBy. Record the immutable first successful matching publication time. A signature and historical absence or late first receipt prove a missed absolute deadline even if the challenger first learns the signature later. Actual blob staging, a bare digest or a signed availability certificate does not substitute for this exact fragment-calldata receipt.
  4. Optional funded recovery. A valid challenge may open only while now < publishBy and no matching public receipt exists. Its immutable response deadline is min(openedAt +600, publishBy). Publication by that deadline satisfies it; neither opening a challenge nor revealing late restarts the absolute clock. The 600-second term is a ceiling and never grants 600 extra seconds beyond this 240-second promise. Challenges near the deadline can have very little remaining time. Detailed manifest, duplicate, fee and first-receipt rules are in accountability.
  5. Preserve evidence. Conflicting eligible signatures can be first disclosed until evidenceClose; actual historical signing time is unknowable. The owner cannot shorten the interval by stopping, announcing exit, rotating a key, changing its receiver or claiming it did not issue any signatures.
  6. Release. For each position, releaseAt = evidenceClose: every admitted v2 challenge deadline is before issuanceEnd +240, which is before evidenceClose. Strictly after releaseAt, objective settlement first resolves any pending expired challenge and then moves only unslashed residual collateral to the owner's free balance. Unsettled admitted defaults do not become withdrawable merely because a timer passed. Unknown signatures can still escape penalties if nobody supplies them before evidenceClose; the registry cannot enumerate unseen promises. Calls process one position at a time, with no scan of every bucket.

Each publisher additionally promises not to sign two different blockHash values for the same subjectHash = keccak256(abi.encode(revision, baseHeadHash, l2BlockNumber)) under the same collateral owner. Evidence can span keys or buckets, and slashes each involved live position once, provided both evidence windows remain open. Different fragments of the same block do not conflict. A new canonical base head permits a new provisional branch. One actor using different owner addresses escapes this link; the protocol does not claim identity independence.

Proven: a position's slash is capped at its own 10 TAIKO, once across all offenses. Repeated signatures or thousands of counterparties cannot manufacture more compensation. Slashing a position does not erase the remaining 59 positions' liabilities. One matching public fragment record can satisfy multiple eligible claims without yielding multiple publication rewards; a late first publication does not cure an earlier expired absolute duty or challenge. Different manifests are not interchangeable merely because they mention the same digest.

Open, inherent limitation: an old private signature presented after evidenceClose has no slashable balance. Clients must stop describing it as a currently bonded promise at expiry. Extending evidence retention only increases the capital lock; it does not prove publication time or eliminate delayed disclosure.

Assumed — canonical conflict evidence: a publisher's signed block X and a different owner-authorized accepted block Y form objective evidence only when the producer equals that publisher's collateral owner, revisions/L2 numbers match, and Y.oldHeadHash == claim.bucket.baseHeadHash. A bounded membership proof authenticates Y against the proof-bound blockHashesRoot. Slash X's still-live position once; a producer signature alone creates no new collateral, and someone else winning never slashes an honest publisher. The staging specification fixes membership encoding. This intentionally bounded predicate does not follow an arbitrary ancestor chain: pipelined claims rooted at an earlier accepted ancestor can miss this particular slash. That false negative is explicit; every canonical segment still independently needs its own mature stage.

Assumed — separate sealed-context covenant: once a complete candidate is sealed and its exact contextHash is known, a PublicationClaim can additionally promise publishBy = issuedAt +240. Either bucket kind may issue it, using a separate 10 TAIKO position and an in-window signed issuance time. Its canonical fields are bucketId, position, revision, baseHeadHash, issuedAt, contextHash, with EIP-712 type EtnaPublication in the EtnaDuty version "2" domain. The claim binds contextHash, not a chosen staging salt: getFirstPublication(contextHash) permanently records the first actual matching DA publication under any salt or relayer. A new stage ID cannot hide or reset that historical fact. Canonical eligibility and optional jobs separately bind their particular immutable stage IDs. The exact hash encoding and field types are normative in staging.html. Matching actual staging by the deadline, regardless of stager identity, discharges this sealed-context covenant; fragment duties separately require their exact calldata publication. Later staging cannot erase default; a valid signed claim plus objective receipt or absence evidence enables permissionless settlement. Any difference in either claim kind's fields, including issuedAt and therefore publishBy, is position equivocation. Reusing one position across distinct fragment and sealed-context promise kinds is also equivocation, not two independently insured claims. An early fragment digest is never substituted for the final blob versioned hash.

Service keys use secp256k1 recovery with canonical low-s signatures. Contract owners may designate these keys; authorization to spend the owner's deposit still comes from that owner. Rotating service keys means opening a new bucket with a new key. An existing bucket's verification key never changes and is not checked against a mutable ERC-1271 policy at dispute time. A compromised historic key remains a liability through its original deadline.

Assumed — producer identity lifecycle: any owner may call registerProducerKey(signingKey) to create a never-reused key ID with immutable owner/key. No service deposit is required. The owner may revoke that key for future admission; previously checked accepted receipts retain their producer, key ID and authorization hash, so revocation cannot erase historic conflict evidence. A direct EOA uses key ID 0. ProducerAuthorization(statementHash, stageId, keyId) authorizes an exact statement whose proof also binds producer and the ordered block-hash root. A payer cannot name an innocent publisher as producer without its assent. No generic ERC-1271 policy is reevaluated during slashing.

Each availability claim covers at most 32,768 bytes. A larger canonical RLP block record uses an ordered manifest of at most four separately reserved chunks. The codec fixes fragment boundaries and its exact block manifest root. A segment can contain up to 60 such records but its entire encoded body, including forced-input records, remains within 122,880 bytes; the cap is not granted separately to each block. A client checks every required chunk, digest, length, parent and executed payload before accepting a locally valid block. A fragment position is not an L2 height; a publisher may spend several positions on one block. An unbonded block can still be valid and canonically submitted.

5. Optional proof jobs never lock the head

A sponsor posts a TAIKO bounty and immutable task hash covering old head, terminal execution output fields (excluding the constructed head hash), ordered data commitment, L1 origin, forced outcomes and derivation revision. Precisely, taskHash = contextHash follows StageContext from staging.html and excludes stage ID, producer, beneficiary and job metadata. The offer separately fixes the exact live stageId; those metadata are bound in the full accepted statement, not recursively inside taskHash. It does not ask a worker to guess the output. Any worker can inspect and execute the task before accepting it. At acceptance, the registry stores the worker as proof beneficiary and the job ID is bound into the full proof statement. The sponsor cannot substitute a task or withdraw accepted bounty.

  1. Offered: fully escrowed offer, acceptance expires after 600 seconds; sponsor may cancel only while unaccepted. Anyone meeting the collateral condition may accept first.
  2. Accepted: the task parent must equal the current canonical head and the exact matching staging receipt/context must already exist. Reserve 1,000 TAIKO and set deadline = max(acceptedAt +300, stage.publishedAt +360 +120). Require both origin.timestamp +900 >= deadline +120 and stage.publishedAt +900 >= deadline +120, so a nearly expired origin or stage cannot itself make the job impossible. This is a freshness guard, not a proving-speed guarantee.
  3. Success: the exact task hash, separately fixed stage ID, job ID and worker beneficiary appear in the first accepted child of that parent with acceptedAt <= deadline. The worker receives bounty and its own bond. Any payer may have relayed this proof.
  4. Cancelled: a different first child consumes that parent by the deadline, including a competitor executing the same data but with a different beneficiary. Return bounty to sponsor and bond to worker. No worker is penalized merely for losing that canonical race.
  5. Defaulted: after the deadline, if the parent was not consumed by the deadline, the reserved worker bond is slashed once and bounty returns to sponsor. Later parent consumption cannot erase this miss. Anyone settles; 10% of actual slash goes to the first valid reporter and 90% to the sink.

Proven, conditional on receipt integrity: the Inbox retains an immutable parentConsumption[parentHash] = AcceptedReceipt receipt when consuming each accepted head. Job settlement uses that historical receipt, not merely the current head. This prevents a defaulting worker from changing the head after its deadline and claiming an innocent cancellation. An implementation revision retired before the deadline, making the task inapplicable, also has a dated objective cancellation receipt; a prior timely exact job completion takes precedence; arbitrary upgrades remain subject to A-UPGRADE.

Assumed A-EXEC + A-L1 + A-ECON: the accepted workload is feasible within its 300-second proving budget, data can be retrieved, and a transaction funded for both L1 gas and the then-current canonical rent can be included. The stage-aware maximum also leaves the assumed 120-second inclusion allowance after the 360-second public-data age gate. A worker must budget the bounded maximum 0.05 ETH rent or arrange a willing payer before accepting; the TAIKO bounty is not an automatic ETH conversion. A valid but late worker can be slashed for failing its voluntary deadline despite honest effort. A 120-second inclusion assumption and the publication-gas quote do not guarantee Ethereum will provide that service.

Proven — no rising rent on a live job parent: while the accepted job's parent remains current, the quote cannot increase with L1 time. A worker can instead budget that parent's acceptance-time quote; any reset requires consuming the parent, and the immutable receipt supplies the existing timely-success or timely-cancellation outcome. A reset after the job deadline cannot excuse its prior default. ETH availability for the actual payable sender remains a worker/payer funding obligation even when the bounty is sufficient in TAIKO market value.

Accepted market limit: after an origin is more than480 seconds old, the conservative300-second job plus120-second freshness margin cannot fit, even if a prepared raw proof remains eligible. Current-head-only acceptance can produce this condition for pipelined candidates. Jobs therefore do not guarantee every candidate a subsidy; independent raw proving and off-protocol funding remain available. See the exact timeline in staging.

Open economics: anyone can accept and abandon jobs at the stated bond cost; no job acceptance stops competing proof work. The 1,000 TAIKO default penalty must be priced against the particular task's potential grief value. A sponsor can offer an impossible output; local execution before acceptance, not a trusted job curator, is the defense. Offers create no compulsory work.

6. Conservation, pull payments and concurrent disputes

Proven invariant to implement and verify:

accountedTAIKO = freeBalances + dutyReserves + workerReserves
                + offeredOrAcceptedBounties + unpaidTAIKOCredits + permanentTAIKOSink
accountedETH   = pendingChallengeDeposits + pendingForcedFeeEscrow
               + unpaidETHCredits + permanentForcedFeeSink + canonicalRentSinkWei
accountedTAIKO <= heldTAIKO
accountedETH   <= heldETH

On force enqueue, the exact charged fee enters the Inbox pendingForcedFeeEscrow. Any excess msg.value becomes a separate caller pull credit. On proved processing, atomically decrement that request's pending fee and credit half to the bound beneficiary and the remainder to the irreversible sink; never create either allocation twice. Unprocessed fees remain escrow liabilities, not donations. Challenge deposits live in the service registry and are accounted separately.

On successful canonical admission, compute q from the current parent clock, allocate exactly q of msg.value to canonicalRentSinkWei and credit msg.value −q to the immediate caller. The current rent is a separate admission charge, not an L2 basefee or a forced-request fee. No producer, publisher, beneficiary, caller or DAO has a withdrawal right over rent. Rent is fully backed ETH in an irrevocable ledger sink under A-UPGRADE, not ETH destroyed by an EVM operation. The complete canonical transition, checkpoint publication, clock reset and these allocations revert together if any gate fails; reverted or invalid transactions create no rent credit or sink entry.

These equations show the combined components for readability; enforce the same asset-specific coverage separately in every escrow contract, never net a liability against assets held elsewhere. All quantities use base units; internal transfers conserve accounted balances. Unsolicited donations create uncredited surplus, not a claim, and must never make withdrawals revert by violating an equality-to-assets check. Surplus has no owner sweep. Credits and sink entries cannot also remain reserves. A slash zeros only its selected reserve, creates the reporter's 10% pull credit and increments the 90% nonwithdrawable sink. No new tokens, fractional promise multiples or unbacked user reimbursements are created. Use exact received amounts and support only the established TAIKO token's transfer semantics; do not silently introduce fee-on-transfer collateral.

All external payouts are pull withdrawals. State transitions and credit creation occur before external token/native transfers; failed recipient transfers leave the credit claimable. Any settlement relayer may help resolve a bucket, but may not redirect an owner's free balance or another recipient's credit. An immutable credited recipient may itself transfer its claim through an explicit authorization; signing-key rotation does not do so.

Every admitted position challenge has a distinct obligation ID and a separately reserved ETH deposit. Successful complete calldata publication with an exact validated FR manifest records a permanent first-fragment receipt. The selected timely responder receives one challenge deposit; other compatible challenges whose deadlines had not expired at that publication refund their deposits on individual bounded settlement. Direct publication without selecting a pending challenge creates no reward and lets matching timely challenges refund. Earlier missed absolute publication deadlines and challenge deadlines remain slashable. No implementation loops over every signer when revealing bytes. Duplicate slash evidence is idempotent and never allocates the same reserve twice.

The challenge deposit is Q = 2 × 4,000,000 × (block.basefee + 2 gwei) ETH-wei, quoted at challenge opening. Require msg.value >= Q, lock exactly Q, and immediately credit any excess to the opener so a changing inclusion basefee needs no exact send-time prediction. Underfunded calls revert before admission. A timely matching response pays all Q to the actual responder. Default refunds Q to the challenger and applies the position slash. An unnecessary valid challenge therefore buys public data; a self-challenge only moves one's own money and pays gas. Repricing after admission is forbidden. The deposit never extends publishBy. Fee growth, late discovery or censorship can leave insufficient time or funding: the signer must arrange public inclusion within its original 240-second promise, not rely on somebody else opening a timely subsidized challenge.

9. Anti-monopoly mechanisms and their limits

Proven: exclusivity duration is zero. Buying every service bucket, accepting every offered job or operating every visible publisher does not buy a veto over another address's proof-bearing submission. There is no global capacity of tickets that can be squatted. Each additional simultaneous reserved promise locks another 10 TAIKO for the fixed evidence period, and each proof job locks another 1,000 TAIKO. Reusing identities does not remove these capital costs.

9.1 A descending-price admission auction with no reserved winner

Assumed — normative mechanism: each canonical parent has one public ETH price. Any complete valid proof-and-data action can consume that parent immediately by paying the current price. No bid, reservation, reveal phase, chosen winner, queue or later activation transaction exists. L1 ordering still selects the first valid action. The price decreases with elapsed seconds and restarts after every successful canonical advance, regardless of caller, producer, beneficiary or common ownership. This Dutch admission auction makes repeated early capture costly without identifying economic owners.

Constants:
    Pmax = 50_000_000_000_000_000 ETH-wei
    decaySeconds = 900
State outside the execution Head and proof/task commitments:
    rentReferenceTime : uint48
    canonicalRentSinkWei : uint256

At bootstrap H0 activation:
    rentReferenceTime = checked uint48(block.timestamp)
At quote/admission:
    require block.timestamp >= rentReferenceTime
    a = uint256(block.timestamp) - uint256(rentReferenceTime)
    if a >= 900: q = 0
    otherwise:
        N = Pmax * (900 - a)       // checked uint256 arithmetic
        q = N / 900 + (N % 900 == 0 ? 0 : 1)
At successful submitSegment admission, atomically:
    require msg.value >= q
    canonicalRentSinkWei += q
    ETHCredit[msg.sender] += msg.value - q
    rentReferenceTime = checked uint48(block.timestamp)
    accept the proof-bound successor and publish its checkpoint/receipt
    emit CanonicalRentPaid(statementHash, oldHeadHash, msg.sender,
                           q, priorReferenceTime, rentReferenceTime)

Proven — exact state boundary: the complete proof, data, maturity, expiry, forced-prefix, producer-authorization and current-parent checks remain mandatory. Every accepted head, including a zero-rent or empty-discretionary head, resets the clock once. A rejected call, an unsolicited ETH transfer, a service registration, staging, a job, or a partial proof cannot reset it. The quote and allocations are recomputed from current state in the same atomic canonical action; any revert rolls all of them back. The constant product is bounded by 50,000,000,000,000,000 ×900; checked uint256 arithmetic and checked uint48 narrowing reject overflow rather than wrapping. A quote has no persistent effects.

Assumed — payment interface: quoteCanonicalRent() returns the current head hash, reference time, observed L1 timestamp and q. It is a snapshot, not a guaranteed reservation. For an unchanged parent q can only decrease as canonical L1 time increases. A caller may attach Pmax to cover every quote and receives the exact excess as its own pull credit. A changed parent invalidates the stale statement; the caller must build an applicable successor rather than treating the old quote as a ticket. The rent clock is outside Head, StageContext, taskHash and the execution proof, so waiting changes payment without changing executed L2 blocks or requiring a new proof, provided all existing freshness constraints still hold. The quote uses L1 seconds, never a proposer schedule, slot or epoch.

Assumed — transaction transport: ordinary payable submission is always available. A positive-rent Frame Transaction must fund actual call value from the sender that executes the Inbox call; a separate frame payer's gas authorization does not fund msg.value. The private terminal-success-guard construction can use each competitor's individually owned and funded contract account, with ordinary account authorization and no protocol allowlist. The shared-nonce example is restricted to zero-rent, zero-SENDER-value execution; there is no unpartitioned shared ETH pool. If a funded smart account is the immediate caller, excess credit belongs to that account and it can withdraw through its own authorization. Invalid guarded losers pay no protocol rent under the researched EIP assumptions; ordinary reverted losers pay gas but create no rent liability. See Frame Transactions research.

9.2 What the price proves

Assumed A-COMPETITION(D): for every contested accepted parent, at least one independent honest entrant can obtain applicable public data, complete execution/proving, fund the current rent and L1 gas, and submit an otherwise-valid successor that L1 includes by parent age D <900 unless another valid successor already consumes that parent. Its candidate remains fresh and satisfies the frozen forced prefix throughout that window. This assumption includes economic willingness, not merely available proving hardware. Canonical L1 timing/inclusion and no destructive upgrade remain A-L1/A-UPGRADE. A producer paying an L1 builder to exclude this entrant violates the inclusion premise; the rent alone does not prevent that.

Proven, conditional bounded capture cost: a participant that captures every contested parent before that entrant must consume each by age D. Since q decreases with age, each such capture irreversibly sinks at least q(D). Across n captures the cost is at least Σ q(Dᵢ), independent of the number of addresses it uses. If Gnetᵢ denotes its incremental fees, MEV and external benefit after ordinary gas/proving/DA costs but before this rent, capture has negative aggregate profit when Σ Gnetᵢ < Σ q(Dᵢ). A stronger sufficient condition is Gnetᵢ < q(Dᵢ) for every captured head. Under the100% coinbase fee rule, fees from the producer’s own L2 transactions are internal transfers, not an irreversible attack cost or external revenue. Gnet consolidates all addresses under common control and counts genuine external costs/receipts only; L1 rent and sunk force fees cannot return through L2 coinbase. This is an explicit economic deterrent for repeated early ordinary-ordering capture, rather than a claim that refundable service collateral alone deters it.

Assumed — concrete D=660 scenario: after a parent accepts, an entrant forms a bounded segment within 60 seconds and stages it within a further 120 seconds. That stage matures 360 seconds later, by parent age 540. Retrieval plus proving completes within that maturity interval, and the funded canonical transaction lands within the assumed next 120 seconds. Both origin and selected stage must remain within their independent 900-second freshness limits. Under these premises, q(660)=13,333,333,333,333,334 wei =0.013333333333333334 ETH. One hundred consecutive contested captures by those deadlines cost at least 1.3333333333333334 ETH in rent alone. These timing and net-gain bounds require measurement; they are not an Ethereum service guarantee.

Proven — rapid resets: after any accepted head, another accepted child in the same L1 timestamp pays Pmax. A chain of k additional immediate accepted children therefore sinks k ×0.05 ETH. The first child after a 900-second pause can pay zero; it still resets the next child's price. Empty-discretionary churn is not free: it needs its own valid proof, mature actual data, admissible origin and required FIFO processing, and each rapid subsequent advance pays the reset price. It may raise the next entrant's immediate cost, but cannot reserve that next head or reset time without an actual canonical advance.

9.3 Liveness and economic limits

Proven, conditional eventual admission: if no one consumes a parent for 900 seconds its rent is zero for every address. No unavailable auction winner can block another proof, and the DAO need never reset the clock. An old proof or stage may have expired by then: an entrant must obtain a fresh origin, execute/stage a fresh candidate and prove it within its own freshness window. If the parent remains unconsumed during that work the rent stays zero; if another participant consumes it, canonical progress already occurred. Under eventual affordable L1 inclusion and a willing capable prover, the rent introduces no permanent admission lock.

Open — market concentration remains possible: without a funded ready competing entrant, an incumbent can wait until zero and capture every head for no rent. With sufficiently large MEV, private order flow, external censorship value or hardware advantage, it can rationally pay every quote and still dominate. Address caps cannot identify common ownership, and this auction does not promise equal shares. The demonstrated result is a nonrecoverable identity-independent cost for sustained early capture under A-COMPETITION(D), with an explicit profit threshold; broader dominance is outside that theorem. The final independent reviews accept this bounded R6 mechanism while retaining concentration as a Medium risk; economic diversity is not a protocol invariant.

Open — honest throughput and entry cost: every winner pays rent, including an honest entrant. A hypothetical accepted-head cadence of 60 seconds pays q(60)=0.046666666666666667 ETH per head, approximately 67.2 ETH/day at 1,440 heads/day, before gas/proving/DA. This cadence example is not a measured sustainable market or a proof that users will fund it. Zero rent after a pause establishes eventual price accessibility, not continuous one-second soft service or sustained canonical throughput. The 0.05-ETH ceiling is an explicit proposed economic change from today's Taiko, chosen to make capture costs concrete; fee revenue, ETH purchasing power and small-entrant budgets must be evaluated before launch. Rent has no TAIKO oracle and changes neither L2's ETH native token nor the100% coinbase execution-fee rule.

9.4 Other griefing prices and collateral limits

Proven, under the forced-queue rules: canonical successors must process the eligible FIFO prefix. A winning builder cannot prefer newly arriving discretionary transactions over the required prefix. Congestion can slow progress, but later arrivals cannot jump ahead of an already queued request. See the canonical pipeline for the precise snapshot rule and workload bound.

Proven — irreversible queue price: the enqueue fee is F(p)=0.001 ETH ×(1+floor(p/50)) at pending count p. On processing, exactly 50% becomes the proof-bound beneficiary's pull credit and 50% becomes a permanent nonwithdrawable ETH sink. Starting empty, filling 1,000 requests without intermediate consumption pays 50 ×0.001 ×(1+…+20)=10.5 ETH. Even one actor controlling sender and beneficiary loses 5.25 ETH to the sink, plus enqueue gas, execution, proving and duplicate staging/landing DA costs. Splitting addresses does not recover the sink. With interleaved processing that keeps the queue below 50, the minimum fee for 1,000 requests is instead 1 ETH, with 0.5 ETH sunk; do not apply the large-backlog example to that strategy.

Proven — bounded net-cost model: for n independently slashed service positions, a self-reporting cartel recovers at most n TAIKO and irreversibly loses 9n TAIKO; an external reporter leaves the signer the full 10n loss. For a defaulted job, the corresponding self-report loss is 900 TAIKO. Honest service collateral is refundable, so its economic cost is opportunity cost plus transaction expenses, not a fictitious burn. For queue abuse, attacker net cost is at least 0.5 ×ΣF(p) + gas + proof/DA costs − external revenues when it controls all processing beneficiaries. Cross-owner producer Sybils can evade the owner-linked conflict predicate, but cannot evade per-position reserves or fee sinking. Open: MEV or other external revenue may exceed all these costs. This is concrete friction against griefing and reserved-role squatting, not guaranteed market diversity, fair race outcomes or unlimited MEV insurance.

Open — capital concentration: at one promise per second, 3,600 seconds of evidence exposure accounts for 36,000 TAIKO, plus bucket issuance overlap. Under just-in-time full 60-position buckets, one bucket opens every 60 seconds and remains locked through openedAt +60 +3600; ordinary overlap is 36,600 TAIKO and a coincident opening at the exact unreleasable deadline needs one extra 600 TAIKO bucket, a 37,200 TAIKO peak. At four fragment promises per second, one bucket opens every 15 seconds: the corresponding figures are 144,000 TAIKO for evidence, 146,400 with issuance overlap and 147,000 at that boundary. These are efficient-cadence model bounds, not a cap on voluntary extra deposits; delayed L1 release raises real capital needs. A separate custodian repeats the exposure. This lowers the earlier long-window proposal's barrier, but does not establish affordable entry for every participant or identity diversity. Evidence discovered after the one-hour horizon is an accepted limit of this bounded soft service, never a weakening of proof or Bridge custody checks.

Proven — alternatives rejected for this boundary: a sealed-bid auction that grants one future producer exclusive rights introduces a winner-availability gate and recovery duties, unlike the selected immediate descending-price admission. Commit/reveal phases add timing and withholding opportunities without identifying common owners. Per-address rotation/caps are Sybil-bypassable; an L1-random leader lottery needs an explicit manipulation/stake assumption; a mandatory committee changes the based-sequencing boundary and needs a threshold assumption. A flat admission fee would charge even indefinitely delayed recovery and offers no zero-price eventual entry. The chosen decay removes that permanent fee floor while pricing successive early capture. Optional customers may still choose publishers by transparent price, latency and historic performance, but reputation is a client policy, not consensus or a whitelist.

10. Worked liability example

Alice deposits 1,250 TAIKO. At timestamp 1,000 she opens one publisher bucket and one custody bucket, reserving 1,200 TAIKO; 50 stays free. Each issues only until 1,060 and accepts objective evidence through 4,660. Alice rotates her key at 1,020: both original buckets remain verifiable under their original keys. There is no 1,200-second post-issuance serving interval.

Position 7 of the custody bucket promises 20,000 bytes with issuedAt 1,030, so actual matching fragment calldata and the FR preimage are due by 1,270. At 1,100, while no matching publication exists, Bob opens a challenge at 20 gwei basefee, depositing 0.176 ETH. The response deadline is min(1,700,1,270)=1,270. A valid targeted response at or before 1,270 earns its responder 0.176 ETH and leaves Alice's 10 TAIKO reserved until release. If there is no timely publication, Bob reclaims 0.176 ETH and receives 1 TAIKO; 9 TAIKO goes to the sink. Another reporter with the same evidence earns zero. Alice cannot turn that slashed position into a second 10 TAIKO compensation claim.

If nobody opens a challenge, the original signature and a first-fragment lookup after 1,270 still prove default when publication is absent or late. A first successful publication at 1,280 records useful data but cannot erase the miss. If matching calldata was published at 1,260, later challenges are inadmissible, the publication promise is satisfied, and no perpetual private-server duty remains. Changing issuedAt to 1,040 on the same position would be a second incompatible signed promise, not an extension to 1,280.

Position 8 remains independent. A second pending challenge against it is unaffected by position 7's settlement. Strictly after 4,660, Alice withdraws only unslashed reserves after every admitted expired obligation has been settled. If position 7 was the sole slash, 1,190 TAIKO returns to free balance; the original free 50 makes 1,240 withdrawable. Internal accounting remains 1,250 =1,240 owner +1 reporter +9 sink. An unsolicited extra token increases held assets and uncredited surplus; it does not increase any of those claims.

A separate sealed publication promise signed at 1,030 is due at 1,270. Matching staging at 1,260 satisfies it regardless of who paid for staging; first staging at 1,280 is a default that later publication cannot cure. A worker accepting a job at 1,000 with an exact stage published at 900 gets deadline max(1,300,1,380)=1,380. A stage at 900 and origin at 850 pass the 900-second freshness checks because both remain fresh through deadline +120=1,500. The stage matures at 1,260, leaving the assumed inclusion allowance; no exclusive submission right exists during that wait.

11. Claims to test before implementation

  • Proven at design level: anyone can enter canonical proving without service membership; optional duties have bounded reserved liabilities and absolute public-fragment deadlines; historic key rotation does not erase evidence; deterministic settlement does not need DAO action. The canonical price is independent of owner and bounds early-capture cost under A-COMPETITION(D).
  • Assumed: A-CRYPTO, A-UPGRADE, sufficiently timely affordable L1 inclusion, at least one willing capable prover, and for the quantitative capture theorem a funded ready honest competitor before the price reaches zero. Optional soft services additionally need providers able and willing to fund mandatory fragment calldata publication.
  • Open: historical private computation and selective gossip before objective promises, concentration beyond the bounded capture theorem, economic calibration of both rent and collateral, and proving/response gas benchmarks. The final independent reviews accept these bounded mechanisms. Specification convergence does not establish measured performance, economic effectiveness or launch readiness.
