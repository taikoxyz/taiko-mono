Propose-with-proof: the landing

One L1 action, land, carries a range of certified blocks as blobs plus a ZK proof and finalizes them immediately: the checkpoint is written into the L1 SignalService in the same transaction, with two exceptions: a landing accepted with one ZK leaf in single-proof mode is provisional and gets its checkpoint after a second-leaf confirmation or 24 h, and a two-leaf landing that follows an unfinalized provisional record on its chain writes none and is subsumed by the one checkpoint written when the newest record it follows is finalized (§7; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Anyone may land any certified prefix. The blob bytes are a pure function of the certified chain, so any prover's proof is usable by any lander. Deadlines are economic only: the clock alone voids nothing, and no rule penalizes a holder for not landing (invariant I5; §6). This page is normative for the landing checks and for replacement (round 4, D9); the parameters page is normative for constants and the interfaces page for struct and evidence types. Sources: judge synthesis of the three B4 designs, aligned with the certificate page.

Invariants this page owns (round 4)

I2. A drawn term's lander reward exists at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself: no other owner action and no liveness event can remove the funds that pay the term's landers and attesters; reward debits are the only drain, reward credits to an owner with seats refill its own reserve first (round 5, R5S-4), the reserve is sized for two terms of exposure per seat, and an owner whose reserve falls below one term per seat lapses, so every draw it still holds is funded by at least one term per seat (round 6, R6S-2; §5, residual there). I4. Within the landing horizon (L14) and short of an L1 reorg deeper than the anchor age, nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement or forced batch accepted under A1, or 2Q(m) − m slashings, or, after a landing accepted with one leaf in single-proof mode, a DAO resolution of a proof conflict that voids that provisional landing and every block built on it (§7; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): no L1 record and no accusation voids certified blocks (§6). The objects this covers are every VC kind: TIMEOUT and TERM_END VCs are signed by committee(t) and a vote below a certificate the signer attested is S3b-slashable with no exemption (round 5, R5S-1); a FALLBACK VC is signed by a later committee and is accepted only after an L1 time gate, only while no live VC of committee(t) is recorded, and never lands a block above a certificate the landing verifies (round 5, R5S-5), and it is refused, or dead once recorded, below a certificate of committee(t) recorded for the view (round 6, R6S-3); REPLACE and RESUME VCs are accepted only at lastLanded after A1 and have no closing effect (round 4, R4H-5). I5. No accusation object exists: every slashing rule's accept set is signatures by the accused over conflicting messages, a signed object with an invalid preimage, a signed L1 reference that is false, or code on the key; no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing.

What today does and why it fails

Two actions: propose stores blob pointers behind a whitelist, one per L1 block; prove, hours later, verifies an aggregated proof behind a second whitelist, writes the checkpoint and advances finalization (00 §2). Image ids are owner-set booleans, so every prover release invalidates in-flight proofs. Nothing on L1 references the preconfirmed chain; derivation clamps timestamps to the landing time; a non-empty forced-inclusion queue reorgs the preconfirmed suffix; a prover that stops halts finalization until the ring buffer fills. This fails R1, R5, R6 and R7 and leaves TH1, TH2, TH7, TH11, TH13, TH16, TH17, TH20 and TH25 open.

1. There is no sequencer seal; the batch commitment is derived

The sequencer signs every block header (V1). No second signature over a batch exists: the batch-level commitment is a digest every party computes identically from the certified chain, and the committee's view change pins where a view ends. Consequences: no seal message to withhold (W5 has no object), no seal-equivocation offence, and a certified block is landable the instant its certificate exists. A lander may land any certified prefix [lastLanded.height + 1, L] spanning up to MAX_VIEWS_PER_LANDING = 4 consecutive views (one segment per view, each opened by its view change and ended by the next view change's lock, the last ended by the end certificate), so the fixed landing gas is amortized over several terms when gas is dear. proven

2. Canonical blob encoding (the DA commitment)

payload = 0x03 ‖ u32(numBlocks) ‖ Σ_i ( u32(len(rec_i)) ‖ rec_i )
rec_i   = headerCore_i ‖ seqSig_i (65 B) ‖ [viewChangeBytes_i if first block of a view] ‖ txListBytes_i
          (an FI block's record stops before txListBytes: headerCore ‖ seqSig [‖ viewChangeBytes]; round 6 fix pass)

headerCore is the non-derivable subset of the signed header and the block header: timestamp, coinbase, gasLimit, anchorNumber, anchor hash (the header's parentBeaconBlockRoot), carried certificate height and hash, termId, view, kind, vcHash, txListHash (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147: kind is added, the L1 state root and the seat are dropped). Every block carries vcHash here, and only here, so the guest rebuilds PH for every block from this record and the block header. txListBytes are the exact compressed bytes whose keccak is txListHash (the sequencer's own zlib stream, copied, never re-compressed). An FI block's record is headerCore ‖ seqSig [‖ viewChangeBytes if it opens a view] with no txListBytes: its transactions are derived from the entry's payload witness, which hashes to the entry's dataHash (forced-inclusion page §5, the guest rule), so the record is about 250 bytes and no FI block adds a blob to any landing (round 6 fix pass). Packing is today's coder: 31 bytes per field element, 130,044 usable bytes per blob, zero-padded, split in order. Every byte is a signed field, a signature, or bytes committed by a signed hash, so the blob versioned hashes are a function of the certified block set and the range with no encoder freedom: two landers of one range produce bit-identical blobs and any prover's proof is usable by any lander. A misencoded landing yields an end hash different from the certificate and is unlandable (self-harm only). proven

Batch size per second. The L1 blob budget is about 224 KiB/s at every slot time. Client policy targets about 20 KiB/s of compressed output per sequencer (about one eleventh of L1; the V10 bound below, 1,270,000 bytes or about 9.8 blobs per 60-s term), a value the DAO can raise only with the per-term exposure and the reserve re-derived (round 6, R6H-3; 32 KiB/s under the earlier 15-blob bound). Ranges are lander-chosen, so no per-term cap exists on-chain; the caps are per landing: 6 blobs (the per-transaction limit) and 256 blocks. An oversized landing is not includable, never slashable. Per block, validity rule V9 (certificate page) bounds the canonical record headerCore ‖ seqSig ‖ VC bytes ‖ txListBytes to 130,035 bytes, so that the framed single-block payload (0x03 ‖ u32(numBlocks) ‖ u32(len) ‖ rec, nine framing bytes) fits one blob of 130,044 bytes: any single certified block is landable alone at every slot time, and no record is larger than one blob, which is what makes the paid-landing count of §5 hold however a term's blocks pack (round 2, H1; round 3, H3 corrected the framing bytes; round 6, R6H-3). Landers size multi-block ranges with 4 framing bytes per further record. Per term, rule V10 bounds the sum of the records of the term's sequencer blocks to TERM_BYTES_MAX = 5 × LANDING_UNIT = 5 × 254,000 = 1,270,000 bytes (about 9.8 blobs): a certified term obliges landers to at most five paid landings at every slot time, which is what the per-landing reward below is sized for (round 3, H2; round 6, R6H-3). Forced-inclusion blocks and forced batches (view 254) are exempt from V10 in the guest and at attestation (round 4, R4H-6): FI blocks are derived from queue entries, their records about 250 bytes and carrying no payload, the entry's own blob or calldata being the payload witness, so they add no blob to a landing (round 6 fix pass), bounded by MAX_FI_PER_LANDING, and are never the sequencer's choice, so an exhausted budget can never make a sequencer block invalid through V8 and a MISS can never result from the budget. A transaction that is provable but pushes a block or term over its bound is simply not certifiable; MAX_ENVELOPE on the P2P layer equals the V9 number.

5. Sequential landing, pipelined proving, lander economics

Landing is sequential in height; proving is not. A block's proof depends only on its parent state, public on the certified chain, so provers prove blocks as they are certified and aggregate any range. Happy-path "landed" latency ≈ 2 to 3 minutes after a block assumed (block proofs ≤ 20 s pipelined, aggregation ≤ 90 s, one to two L1 blocks). Catch-up: 256 blocks and 6 blobs per landing, several landings per L1 block.

No prover role, registration, assignment or fee pool. The lander is paid in TAIKO on the existing L1 bond ledger from the bond of the sequencer whose blocks it lands: reward = blocks × r(τ, t) per segment, plus R_BLOB per blob for at most 15 blobs of the term, counted in landing order (round 6 fix pass: the five paid landings of a cap term at three blobs each; a later blob earns nothing, so the per-term exposure below is a hard bound for every landing pattern, whereas sixty one-block landings would otherwise have earned 60 R_BLOB, 7,206 per term, and 2 × 7,206 = 14,412 exceeds LAND_RESERVE), plus one per-landing component r_land(τ) for each landing a term needs (round 3, H2: a fixed 2 M-gas cost per landing cannot be covered by a per-block price when a term needs several landings). r ramps linearly from R_BLK_MIN (0.5 TAIKO) at landableFrom(t) to R_BLK_MAX (4 TAIKO, the marginal proving cost) over LAND_WINDOW; r_land ramps from 0 to R_LAND_MAX (1,300 TAIKO ≈ 0.2 ETH at the assumed rate, a 2 M-gas landing at 100 gwei) over the same window, and so does PIN_REWARD (L4). Ramps measured from landability (round 6, R6H-2): landableFrom(t) = max(termEnd(t), lastLanded.at), where lastLanded.at is the L1 time of the landing that the first range reaching t builds on, fixed in t's term record from then on, so every landing of a multi-landing term ramps from one origin; for a pin by recordAssignment(t), paid only for a term at or below the first unlanded term t′ of A1, it is read from the current lastLanded.at until a landing reaches t, and a pin of a later term earns nothing, since no range containing it can yet build on the current lastLanded. No term is charged for time during which nothing could land. The schedule covered: during an outage of any length nobody can land; afterwards the first landing builds on the pre-outage lastLanded, so the terms it reaches (at most four views) are charged the maximum ramp, each at most one term of exposure over all its landings, and every later backlog term is first reached by a range built on a landing made after the outage and starts a fresh ramp, so holders self-land their backlog terms at no debit and an absent holder's term is paid from a fresh ramp. A chain-wide outage therefore costs the maximum ramp only for the terms the first landing after it reaches, not the whole backlog. A1's replace window is unchanged (it already uses lastLanded.at, capped at termEnd + LAND_WINDOW_MAX): after an outage longer than A1's cap the backlog is replaceable at recovery (limitation L6, unchanged), so the ramp change moves no safety boundary. Per-landing reward rule (round 4, R4H-7; landing unit fixed in round 6, R6H-3): TermRecord tracks bytesLanded, the term's sequencer-block records landed so far (the quantity V10 bounds); landing k of term t (k counting the landings whose first segment belongs to t) is paid r_land only if k ≤ ⌈bytesLanded_after / LANDING_UNIT⌉, where LANDING_UNIT = 254,000 bytes is a constant at every slot time. The schedule covered: the sequencer chooses its block sizes and the lander its range boundaries. Every record fits one blob (V9), so a greedily packed landing that is not the term's last carries more than 390,132 − 130,044 = 260,088 bytes even at 2-s slots, where one L1 block carries LANDING_BYTES = 390,132 bytes (three blobs), and together with the record that did not fit it, which opens the next landing, more than 390,132 bytes. At most about 12,500 bytes of a term are not counted in bytesLanded (the framing, 4 bytes per record and 5 per landing over at most 60 blocks; at most 40 FI-block records of about 250 bytes, their payloads being the entries' own blobs or calldata; and the view changes those blocks open). So after a greedy lander's k-th landing of a term bytesLanded exceeds (k − 2) × 260,088 + 377,000, more than (k − 1) × 254,000 for every k ≥ 2, and every landing it makes is paid however the blocks pack. A term at the V10 cap therefore takes at most five greedy landings at 2-s slots, since four landings that are not its last already carry more than 1,040,000 of its at most 1,282,500 bytes, and two six-blob landings at 12-s slots, and whatever boundaries a lander picks it is paid at most ⌈1,270,000 / 254,000⌉ = 5 r_land: the r_land exposure of a term is at most 5 × R_LAND_MAX, and splitting a range earns nothing beyond ⌈bytesLanded / 254,000⌉ landings. The break-even fee at which a third party is paid in full is therefore ≈ 100 gwei per landing whatever the term's size and the slot time, since every landing a greedy lander makes is paid and R_LAND_MAX prices one 2 M-gas landing at 100 gwei; above it landing waits for the fee to fall or the holder to land at its own cost, and nothing is penalized while it waits (no rule reads the absence of a landing). A landing may cover four views so the fixed gas is amortized. Before landableFrom(t) the price is the minimum and the sequencer lands its own blocks (the lander's reserve debit and credit are skipped for a self-landing, round 5, R5S-4; net cost L1 gas plus the attester share, about 6 TAIKO per full term, offset in expectation by its own attester income). If its prover fails, the price ramps: a Dutch auction with the sequencer as reserve bidder. A sequencer that knows its prover is down shortcuts the ramp with a signed RewardBoost, capped at R_BLK_MAX.

The landing reserve (invariant I2; round 4, R4H-3, R4-C2, R4-C4). Reward-type debits (ramps, blob reward, boost, per-landing reward, attester share) are drawn from the holder's landing reserve, a ledger amount of LAND_RESERVE = 14,400 TAIKO per seat that registration and reactivation require on top of 1.05 × the seat floor: the registration bond is ≥ 1.05 × seats × B_SEAT + seats × LAND_RESERVE, 35,400 TAIKO per seat (re-derived once in round 6 for the per-seat line, horizon-length outages and the 2-s landing unit: R6S-2, R6H-2, R6H-3). Five rules make the reward exist at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself (which takes the whole ledger, reserve included) and short of the deliberate self-directed reward drain costed in the residual below:

  • Non-withdrawable. The reserve cannot be withdrawn while the owner has any active or pending seat; it leaves the ledger only through requestExit + EVIDENCE_WINDOW, like seat stake. withdraw is limited to the excess over 1.05 × floor + reserve (round 4, R4H-3: round 3 had funded the reward at registration and left the funds withdrawable at S(t) + 1). An owner can still move reserve to a withdrawable balance by landing its own terms with rewardTo a seatless address it controls (the residual below), but not below one term of exposure per seat without losing its draws (the lapse rule below), so the reserve is non-withdrawable down to seats × 7,161 (round 6, R6S-2).
  • Ring-fenced. Class-C debits never touch the reserve: MISS is taken from the 5 % buffer above the floor, then from seat stake down to the 50 % hard floor. The reserve is debited only by reward-type payments, so a timeout can never empty it (round 4, R4-C4).
  • Refilled from rewards (round 5, R5S-4). Any reward-type credit (ramp, blob reward, per-landing reward, attester share, PIN_REWARD) whose rewardTo resolves to an owner with seats is credited to that owner's reserveGwei first, up to its required level (seats × LAND_RESERVE), and only the excess to its withdrawable balance; when the lander's rewardTo and the holder are the same owner, the lander's debit and credit are both skipped. The credits this covers: a self-landing (no lander reward moves: self-landing costs L1 gas plus the attester share, about 6 TAIKO per full term, which the owner's own attester income offsets in expectation); a landing by another owner (its reserve is refilled by what the holder's reserve pays); the attester share (committee members' reserves are refilled by their committee income, which is where the honest drain of round 4's sizing went); a pin reward (the same). A credit to an address with no seats is the only one that leaves the reserve system; it needs no third party, since the owner can direct its own landings' rewards to a seatless address it controls, and the per-seat lapse line below is what bounds it (round 6, R6S-2).
  • Sized for concurrency per seat, honest drain included. The per-term reward exposure is 60 × R_BLK_MAX + 15 × R_BLOB + 5 × R_LAND_MAX + 60 × ATT_REWARD_PER_BLOCK + PIN_MAX = 240 + 15 + 6,500 + 6 + 400 = 7,161 TAIKO (round 5: PIN_MAX added, R5H-2, and sized in the fix pass; round 6, R6H-3: five paid landings; round 6 fix pass: R_BLOB paid for at most 15 blobs per term in landing order, so the sum is a hard bound for every landing pattern), realised only at the maximum ramp; LAND_RESERVE = 14,400 per seat is two terms of it (2 × 7,161 = 14,322 ≤ 14,400) and at most B_SEAT (20,000). On the honest happy path the reserve moves only by the attester share: the holder lands its own tail 2 to 3 minutes after term end and the lander's debit and credit are skipped; the attester share it pays (about 6 TAIKO per full term) lands in other owners' reserves, and the share it receives as an attester lands in its own, so the two cancel in expectation over committee draws assumed. The reserve is drawn down only when another owner lands the term (a failed prover, or a fee the holder will not pay), and that owner's reserve is refilled by the same amount; at the happy-path lag of 150 s a term left to a third party costs about 640 TAIKO for a term at the V10 cap landed in five landings of at most three blobs each (2-s slots), about 310 in two six-blob landings (12-s slots) and about 200 for a one-landing term (60 × 0.79 + blobs + landings × 108 + 6 + 33). An owner with CAP = 8 seats holds 115,200 TAIKO of reserve, about 16 terms of worst-case exposure, half of it above its lapse line (8 × 7,161 = 57,288): it lapses only at the ninth maximum-exposure debit with nothing refilled or topped up, and ReserveLow fires at the eighth. At the launch registry (8 of 72 seats) about 7 of its terms fall in one LAND_WINDOW_MAX (60 terms), more than 8 in about 22 % of windows and more than 16 in about 0.02 % (binomial, 60 draws at 1/9), so an owner whose own prover stays down through an hour of fees above break-even relies on the automatic top-up below, while a chain-wide outage, however long, charges an owner the maximum only for its terms that the first landing after it reaches, because the ramps run from landability (round 6, R6H-2). A single-seat owner (14,400) survives one maximum-ramp third-party landing (14,400 − 7,161 = 7,239, above its line of 7,161) and lapses at the second unless it tops up. Class-A stake is never touched by rewards (round 2, S5).
  • Lapse below one term per seat, with a warning that carries information (round 6, R6S-2, replacing round 5's per-owner absolute lines). An owner whose reserve is below seats × 7,161, one term of exposure per seat, lapses: DELAY_S-dated, not DELAY_REG, because a lapse, like every suspension since round 5, changes no rank and no E_t (R5S-2); stored in the two-interval store with its minimum length SUSPEND(3) kept, since the store's argument needs every interval at least ROLE_HORIZON + DELAY_S long; and ended only by a top-up to the required level (seats × LAND_RESERVE) plus reactivate(). The guarantee is checked at the draw, not only at registration: a seat drawn while its owner is lapsed at termStart(t) is a void view or an empty committee slot. The grinding check for the DELAY_S date: an owner that sees the seed and lapses itself can only void its own draws and empty its own committee slots after the seed is fixed, which never adds a cartel member to any committee (an empty slot lowers m and Q(m), and removing one of a coalition's own slots lowers its count by one while Q(m) falls by at most one, so no coalition gets closer to a quorum or to a blocking minority) and never moves another owner's rank (round 4's R4-C2 lever was a rank change, which no suspension is any more). ReserveLow fires once per crossing below (seats + 1) × 7,161, one term of headroom above the line (14,322 for one seat, 64,449 for eight), and is disarmed until the reserve is back at the required level seats × 14,400, so an owner sees one event per real drawdown instead of one per debit (round 5, R5S-4; the re-arm line is the required level, which the refill rule reaches on its own, rather than a multiple of the exposure: seats + 1.5 terms would exceed a one-seat owner's required level and never re-arm, and seats + 0.5 terms lies below the firing line and would re-fire on every debit). Keeping the reserve funded is a sequencer duty (roles page §1): the client tops up automatically through deposit() on the event, to the required level (a partial top-up leaves the event disarmed, so the next crossing would be silent), which takes at most one term per seat (seats × 7,239) when the event fires at its lowest. A catch-up after an outage charges the maximum ramp only to the terms the first landing reaches, at most four, even when several landings share an L1 block (R6H-2), so an owner at its required level lapses from the outage alone only if that range reaches more of its terms than it has seats (s × 14,400 − j × 7,161 falls below s × 7,161 only for j > 1.01 s): an owner with three seats or fewer can still lapse if the first post-recovery range reaches more of its terms than it has seats, and one with four or more cannot. Consequence: an owner cannot drain its reserve below one term per seat and keep its draws.

Residual, stated plainly. No L1 rule can guarantee funds for a term drawn before the debits that emptied the reserve, and the owner can still trigger those debits deliberately: it lets its own terms ramp to the maximum and lands them with rewardTo an address that owns no seats, so up to 7,161 TAIKO per term moves from the reserve to a freely withdrawable balance (the refill rule cannot tell such an address from a genuine third party). Since round 6 (R6S-2) that frees at most 7,239 TAIKO per seat while the owner keeps its draws, and every draw it still holds is funded by at least one term per seat; draining further crosses the per-seat line, which costs the owner its draws 22 minutes later (DELAY_S) for at least 36 h (SUSPEND(3)). The window is the terms drawn inside DELAY_S after the crossing plus the terms still unlanded at that moment (up to 34 h of drawn terms), funded only by what is left in the reserve; such a term is landed by its holder at gas cost, or replaced under A1 with no penalty to anyone. Honest operation does not reach this window (the happy path moves only the attester share, offset in expectation; round 5). A self-inflicted class-A slash (the owner submits S1 against its own conflicting headers) empties the ledger, reserve included, and unfunds the same window at the price of the whole ledger; it costs the owner its seats and, since round 5, changes no draw (limitations L20). The journal binds rewardTo for ordinary landings as for forced ones (round 2, S6: a copied proof with a swapped reward address would otherwise land identically), so proofs are per lander: a prover sells or gives a proof by proving for that lander's address, and the proofs topic carries the lander address. Attesters of the end certificate share ATT_REWARD_PER_BLOCK (0.1 TAIKO) per landed block, debited from each block's holder. open whether to index the minimum to block.basefee through a DAO-set rate constant.

6. Deadline and replacement

deadline(t) = termEnd(t) + LAND_WINDOW (30 min) is the economic deadline: the ramp reaches its maximum there for a term landable from its end (the ramps run from landableFrom(t), §5; round 6, R6H-2). LAND_WINDOW_MAX (60 min) caps the lag that chained late landings can build inside A1. The clock voids nothing by itself, and no rule penalizes a term for not landing (invariants I4 and I5). Round 4 removed the abandonment penalty S4a with its evidence deadline, outage gate, void waiver and escrow (R4H-5, R4-C3): a certified view is landable by anyone and paid from the holder's reserve (§5), so a holder cannot abandon a provable view; the only unlandable certified content is a guest-completeness bug, which is a false positive for any abandonment rule and is handled by replacement plus the client blacklist. Class C is MISS only. The withholding defence (TH5) rests on the worthlessness of uncertified blocks and on "certified implies landable by anyone", not on a penalty.

  • A1 replaceable (inbox predicate). With t' the first unlanded term: replaceableFrom = min(termEnd(t') + LAND_WINDOW_MAX, max(termEnd(t') + LAND_WINDOW, lastLanded.at + LAND_CHAIN_GRACE)) + REPLACE_GRACE (REPLACE_GRACE = 300 s; round 4 renamed it, its only remaining role is this formula). A replacement landing (or a forced batch) is accepted iff block.timestamp > replaceableFrom. Inputs are inbox storage and the block timestamp. The chain grace keeps a late parent from making its child replaceable at once; the maximum caps the lag chained late landings can build. Bonded landing intent (round 2, S3): anyone with a TAIKO ledger balance may call announceLanding(firstHeight, endHeight) with firstHeight = lastLanded.height + 1, locking ANNOUNCE_BOND = 2,000 TAIKO on the ledger (not ETH), while replaceableFrom has not passed; it defers replaceableFrom by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE, once per range. One deferral per range (round 5, R5-C3): the deferral is a single flag keyed on lastLanded, spent by whichever of a landing announcement and an accepted REPLACE or RESUME record (A3) comes first; the other buys nothing further, so the forced path is deferred at most once per range as a rule, not as a consequence, and the forced-inclusion bound stays 112 minutes. If an ordinary landing covering firstHeight follows, the bond is returned with it; if a forced or replacement landing lands first, the bond is forfeited: 40 % to that lander, 60 % burned. An announcement is refused once the head forced-inclusion entry has been due at the T_floor of the first unlanded term for longer than LAND_WINDOW (round 3, C3). A fake announcement therefore costs its poster 2,000 TAIKO and can delay the forced path by at most one grace period, and a landable backlog is never reorged while someone bonded is landing it. Nodes label confirmations above lastLanded "replaceable" from replaceableFrom.
  • A2 fork. When A1 holds in a node's L1 view, the current holder may reopen its current view (same term and view number) with a new opening object: a VC of kind REPLACE carrying an L1 reference, whose hash becomes the view's vcHash. Its first block has parentHash = lastLanded.blockHash. The reference is inside every REPLACE lock vote's signed bytes and inside the VC (round 3, S1). Attesters attest it iff A1 holds at that reference in their view, the reference is at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old and canonical, and the first unlanded certified term's deadline has passed. Every equivocation key (S1, S3a, S3c, fork choice, gossip dedup, slashing-protection records) includes the opening object, so the original tail and the fork are never equivocation. Client duty: re-include the void blocks' transactions in order, except transactions the fork's sequencer lists in the REPLACE VC as unprovable (a Q-signed quarantine list), which are dropped.
  • A3 L1 arbitration (landing-based; round 4, R4H-5 and R4-C3). Until a replacement (or a forced batch) lands, landing the original stays valid and pays the ramp; whichever lands first wins, and the loser is invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape. A recorded REPLACE VC has no closing effect: recordViewChange accepts a REPLACE or RESUME VC only if vc.lockHeight == lastLanded.height, vc.lockPhHash == lastLanded.phHash and block.timestamp > replaceableFrom at record time (A1 re-evaluated on inbox state; otherwise the call is refused), and the accepted record serves two purposes only: (i) it is the first-recorded VC for (t, v, closes) that the fork's landing must match under L4, and (ii) it is priority evidence for the forced path, spending the same single per-range deferral that a landing announcement spends (A1; whichever comes first, one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE in total; round 5, R5-C3). A REPLACE or RESUME record is dead once lastLanded.height > vc.lockHeight (round 4 fix, R4H-5 residual): the first-recorded rule ignores a dead record, so when the original view is landed on top of the record's lock, the original's own TIMEOUT or TERM_END VC for the same (t, v, closes) is recorded or landed as if no REPLACE had been recorded, and a dead record is no longer priority evidence; without this, a stale record would have refused the original view's closing VC at the next view boundary and forced a second replacement. A live fork's own later VCs close a different object (round 3, S6), so they never collide with the original's on L1. The lock reset of the certificate page (preconf §6) and the RESET attester state trigger on a landed replacement or forced batch only, never on a record; the RESET-state rule (only RESUME is signable under the old opening object) is unchanged, and it is the whole of the reset machinery: S3b has no exemption for any lock (round 5, R5S-1), because every mandatory post-reset message is a RESUME, which S3b never applies to, and no honest TIMEOUT, TERM_END or FALLBACK vote can sit below a certificate the same key attested outside a reset (an attester's lock is at least the carried certificate it verified at V3, and votes are monotone). A RESUME lock vote's l1Ref is the (number, hash) of the L1 block that contains the forced or replacement landing being resumed from (certificate page, normative). Round 3's recorded-REPLACE close was removed in round 4 because a stale record, formed before a backlog landing, closed a live view and voided fresh locked blocks with a single transaction and no proof (R4H-5), and because a record voided a whole certified backlog while proving was down (R4-C3). Dead-mode landings (view 255, no certificate) are replacements and obey A1.
  • Why descendants are void. Every certified block above lastLanded was built on a state L1 will never hold once a replacement lands; its proof asserts a parent root the inbox does not store. Unavoidable when the preconf chain runs ahead of L1; this design bounds when: only when a replacement lands, never by time within the landing horizon. The horizon is the smaller of ROLE_HORIZON (34 h) and the EIP-2935 window (27 h at 12-s slots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots); a range whose first term is older than it can no longer be landed and is replaceable. A global proving outage shorter than the horizon voids nothing.
  • Rarity. A replacement needs A1 (≥ 35 to 65 minutes without any landing) and a landed proof: a replacement needs a working proof system, so a proving outage on its own voids nothing (round 4, R4-C3 restored this). It happens when the head range is unprovable while a replacement is provable (a content-specific guest bug), or when a holder forks after an outage and its fork lands before the backlog. A few events per year, each voiding at most about an hour of blocks, with no penalty to anyone.

7. Verifier composition, degradation, rotation

ZK_K = 2 distinct ZK leaves of ZK_N (RISC0, SP1; target 3). TEE leaves never count (assumption A3, TH1). Each leaf keeps today's staticcall shape plus require(remote.code.length > 0) and {current, previous, previousValidUntil} image sets: a DAO upgrade moves the old set to previous with a two-hour overlap, or zero when flagged emergency (TH20). Single-proof mode, provisional landings (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): if block.timestamp − lastLanded.at > DEGRADE_AFTER (2 h), a landing with a non-empty certificate is accepted with one ZK leaf until the next landing, and such a landing is provisional: the inbox advances lastLanded, pays rewards and runs every deadline as for a normal landing, but writes no checkpoint to the L1 SignalService for it; it stores the provisional record (the start it extended: the height, block hash and state root of lastLanded at the landing and the forced-inclusion head at that point; the end height, end block hash and state root; journal hash, leaf id, landedAt) in a ring of PROVISIONAL_RING = 12 slots (the start fields were added in the fix pass of 2026-10-01: they are what a checkpoint and a rollback are taken from, below). The record becomes final, and its checkpoint is written, when either (i) anyone presents a proof of the same journal (same start state, same input commitments, same end state) from a different ZK leaf (confirmProvisional(k, journal, proof)), or (ii) DEGRADED_FINALITY = 86,400 s (24 h) elapse after landedAt with no conflict (finalizeProvisional(k), callable by anyone and done by the next landing that passes it). A conflict is a valid proof from a different leaf over the same start state and input commitments with a different end state (a journal equal to the record's in every field but the end block hash, the end state root and the end PH hash, presented through the same call): the inbox records it, emits ProofConflict(k, leafA, leafB) and enters CONFLICT, in which no landing of either mode and no checkpoint write except by finalizeProvisional of records preceding the conflicting one, which stays callable and writes at most the conflicting record's start, until a DAO upgrade names the sound leaf, rolls lastLanded back to the newest L1 checkpoint if needed (its reinitializer sets it to the newest checkpoint of the L1 SignalService, as initEtna does, so the two-leaf landings between that checkpoint and the conflicting record are replaced with it; by the finalization rule below that checkpoint is the start of the oldest record still unfinalized, so the reinitializer also restores the forced-inclusion head from that record's start word, sets fiClearTip to the restart time as M3 sets it to drainedAt, and clears the ring: the entries consumed by the voided landings are due again on the stall clock from the restart, like the entries requeued by the abandonment of the Shasta tail; the fees already credited for those entries, the rewards paid for the voided landings and the term records they settled are not clawed back, a bounded leak recorded in limitation L-PF), and the chain restarts from there, dead-mode style: the preconfirmed blocks built on the voided landing are replaced. The rollback stops at the parent of the conflicting record once every earlier record is final (finalizeProvisional stays callable in CONFLICT for the records that precede the conflicting one, whose windows are their own, so anyone can finalize those past their window before the upgrade), and otherwise at the start of the oldest unfinalized record, so it voids at most one DEGRADED_FINALITY of landings, whose terms lie inside ROLE_HORIZON, which is why the dead-mode restart needs no role of an older term. A later landing that starts from a provisional end state, with one leaf or two, does not finalize the earlier record: a sound leaf proving "from S these blocks lead to S′" says nothing about whether S was the true result of the blocks before it (a fabricated state trie is still a trie), so finalization by (i) needs a proof of the same journal, and the earlier record waits for (i) or (ii). Checkpoints are written in order. The rule, stated identically on every page: a two-leaf landing writes its checkpoint in its own transaction unless an unfinalized provisional record precedes it on its chain; then it writes none (it needs no confirmation of its own and stores no record) and is subsumed by the one checkpoint written when the newest record it follows is finalized. A conflict at record k voids k and everything landed after it, and the DAO's resolution restores the newest L1 checkpoint, so the two-leaf landings between that checkpoint and the conflicting record are replaced with it; until that record is finalized, such landings are labelled "landed (provisional)" like the record itself (§8). Finalization, run first by every landing or by finalizeProvisional alone, passes the records in order from the oldest while each is confirmed or older than DEGRADED_FINALITY and writes one checkpoint: the start of the oldest record still unfinalized (the end of the landing that precedes it, which the record stores), or lastLanded when no unfinalized record remains; the passed record's own end is never the checkpoint, because the two-leaf landings between it and the next record are proven from that end by two leaves and the next record's start covers them all, and a newer L1 checkpoint proves every signal an older one did, so the landings between two records need none of their own. This is why the record stores its start (fix pass of 2026-10-01): with the passed record's own end as the checkpoint, a two-leaf landing just after record k − 1 waited until record k, landed up to 23 h later, was itself finalized, about two windows, and a conflict on k rolled back to k − 1's end, about two windows of landings, beyond ROLE_HORIZON; with the start stored, a two-leaf landing waits at most one DEGRADED_FINALITY for its checkpoint and a conflict voids at most one, since the newest checkpoint is always the start of the oldest unfinalized record. The ring is bounded by construction: a one-leaf landing ends the mode and the next landing needs two leaves until DEGRADE_AFTER has passed again, so one-leaf landings are at least 2 h apart and at most DEGRADED_FINALITY / DEGRADE_AFTER = 12 records are unfinalized at once, the oldest being finalizable before a thirteenth is appended. The schedule this covers: a prover of the surviving leaf with an unsound proof, a colluding quorum that certified the false block, and the other leaf unusable for the whole window. What it buys: a false root in degraded mode needs one ZK bug, a colluding quorum (as before) and, in addition, that no honest prover of the other leaf can produce a proof over the same journal for 24 h; when the other leaf is unusable for that long (the premise of the mode) the window adds delay, not evidence, and the mode's safety rests on T11 as before: the journal's end block hash must equal the certified hash, whose header commits the state root that Q attesters executed, so a false root needs one ZK bug and a colluding quorum. Elapsed time is not evidence of soundness, which is why the mode's landings are provisional and not why they are safe. This is the one place attester execution carries part of state safety, and only after two hours of stalled landing. User-facing cost: in degraded mode, bridge withdrawals and every message that needs an L1 checkpoint wait up to 24 h; in CONFLICT every landing path, the forced batch included, waits for the DAO (limitation L-PF). Cost on L1: a provisional landing writes six fresh words of record (about 588k state gas, 6 × 97,920: the start height, the forced-inclusion head, the end height, leaf, time and status pack into one word, and the start block hash, start state root, end block hash, end state root and journal hash take one each) in place of the two checkpoint words (about 196k), which whoever finalizes it pays later; the ring's slots are overwritten after the first lap.

8. Races, ePBS timing, finality and the SignalService call

Primary shape, direct to builder: an expiry VERIFY frame, the lander's own nonce, the SENDER frame calling land, and a trailing status VERIFY; any revert (lost race, NotNext, bad proof, rotated image, out of gas) makes the transaction invalid: no gas, no blob fee. Public-mempool fallback: the shared-nonce gate G_LAND calling landFor; deployed only once EIP-8141 is Final, inert while its immutable address is zero; free only against same-nonce losers, since a stale copy whose parent moved is still valid and pays the early revert (Frame transactions; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Without EIP-8141: a type-3 transaction from the lander's EOA with deadline; a loser reverts for ≈ 25k gas plus its burned blob fee.

Levels, no slots: landed = in an L1 block the node has (checkpoint written; a provisional landing, and any landing whose chain holds an unfinalized provisional record, is labelled "landed (provisional)" until its checkpoint is written, §7); L1-safe = that block has one descendant (under ePBS the payload of N is canonical in practice once N+1 builds on it); L1-final = finalized per the node's beacon client (reported, never consumed by a contract); void = replaced or removed by an L1 reorg (inbox state reverts with L1; the range returns to CERTIFIED; no penalty). An Empty slot means the transaction was not executed; the expiry frame makes the stale copy free.

12. Attacks defended and not defended

  • TH1 forged proof: defended, bounded. Normal mode needs two distinct leaves; TEE never counts. Single-proof mode also needs a colluding quorum, and since its landings are provisional (§7; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01) a false root is checkpointed only if no honest prover of the other leaf produces a proof over the same journal within 24 h, while a proof with a different end state over the same inputs is a conflict that halts every landing until the DAO names the sound leaf. Not defended: a common-mode bug in the shared guest; only diverse guests resolve it (open).
  • TH2 prover cartel, W6: defended. The sequencer lands itself; otherwise the ramp attracts any newcomer with the certified data (held by ≥ Q attesters); a total outage delays, never voids.
  • TH4 gossiped ≠ landed: defended. L5 binds the end hash to a certificate; the guest chains every signed header from lastLanded to it.
  • TH5 W1/W3/W5: defended. Uncertified blocks are unlandable; provers rebuild blobs from committee-held data; no seal object. Not defended: withholding that still lands within the window costs only latency.
  • TH7 FI starvation: defended. L8 counts due entries at the anchored timestamp, kept fresh by the anchor age rule; a landing skipping a due request reverts, so the blocks are unlandable and replaceable (no penalty: the holder earns nothing from them); the forced batch bypasses rights after escalation.
  • TH8 bond griefing: defended. No penalty is accusation-triggered and none reads the absence of a landing (invariant I5): the only landing-path debit is MISS, applied by a Q-signed TIMEOUT VC against a leader of whose view Q attesters saw no new certificate for TIMEOUT seconds while holding no attested block still uncertified (round 5, R5H-1).
  • TH11 landing-race waste: defended with frames, degraded without. No third party can make a winner revert after payment: every check depends on readable state and the winner's calldata; FI fees are pull-based.
  • TH12 reorg: defended. A reorged landing returns the range to CERTIFIED with no penalty.
  • TH13 congestion: defended up to the break-even fee. Windows in seconds; under 2.7 M gas for a pinned term, and pinning is paid (PIN_REWARD, a ramp to PIN_MAX = 400 TAIKO) and bounded independently of churn (one tree version per walk, round 5, R5H-2 and the fix pass: about 1 M gas at 128 seats and about 5.4 M at 4,096 in the round-6 simulation, so a 10 M-gas 2-s-slot block carries the pin through 4,096 seats and well beyond; the 65,535-seat maximum, about 10.1 M, needs a block gas limit above 10 M and stays inside the 16.8 M per-transaction cap, and it is above the token supply anyway, 65,535 × 35,400 ≈ 2.3 B TAIKO of entry capital), so a stall never leaves a term whose walk no transaction can carry; blobs per landing sized to the block; the ramp pays a third party in full up to about 100 gwei per landing, whatever the term size and the slot time (R_LAND covers the fixed gas of each landing, and LANDING_UNIT makes every greedily packed landing a paid one; round 4, R4H-8 corrected the earlier per-term figure; round 6, R6H-3); above that, landing waits or the holder pays, and the wait costs no bond because no rule reads a missing landing.
  • TH16, TH17, TH20, TH25. Only the two gate shapes touch EIP-8141 and both fail closed; the anchor tip is checked on L1; image overlap in seconds; protocolParamsHash makes a mismatched guest unlandable (a liveness, never a soundness, failure).
  • Bounded, not prevented: reorg of a landable backlog after a stall (round 2, S3; round 4, R4-C3). Once replaceableFrom has passed and proving returns, the current holder may fork a REPLACE view, and any bonded party may land a forced batch, racing the backlog; whichever lands first wins, and if the fork or the forced batch lands first the stall's certified blocks are void. Defences: nobody is penalized (no rule reads a missing landing); a bonded announceLanding, or an accepted REPLACE record, gives the backlog priority for one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE; a REPLACE lock vote with a false L1 reference is class B (S3d); the replacer and forcer earn no penalty share (only 40 % of a forfeited announcement bond); landing the backlog pays the maximum ramp for the terms the first range after the stall reaches and a fresh ramp after them, and each holder self-lands its own backlog term at no debit (round 6, R6H-2); the RESUME view re-includes voided transactions before any forced entry (V8's first-block waiver, forced-inclusion page §4); confirmations are labelled "replaceable" from replaceableFrom. Residual: one reorg of the backlog per stall, bounded by the grace period, no penalty to anyone (limitation L6).
  • Not defended: poison content (a certified block no guest can prove) is handled by replacement plus a client-side blacklist fed by prover error reports.

13. Failure analysis

  • Sequencers. All offline: takeover produces blocks under backup views; certified ranges are landed by anyone; nothing is voided or penalized without a landed replacement; in dead mode any bonded address restarts from lastLanded. All malicious: unprovable content (replaced; the term earns nothing, no penalty), refusing to land (pay the ramp), spurious forks (attesters reject a REPLACE failing A1; the inbox refuses a REPLACE record whose lock is not lastLanded); they cannot land uncertified data or a false root.
  • Attesters. All offline: nothing landable, no deadline runs, nobody penalized; dead mode is the floor after 75 minutes. All malicious (≥ Q): they can certify a block only they hold (landable only if provable), refuse certificates (liveness only), or sign a false REPLACE certificate that voids provable blocks (bounded griefing).
  • Provers and landers. All offline: the sequencer lands itself; if it cannot, ranges accumulate CERTIFIED, un-voided; the first returning prover clears the backlog, paid the maximum ramp only for the terms the first landing after the outage reaches and fresh ramps after them (round 6, R6H-2), so an outage of any length costs an owner at most its terms among those. All malicious: they can only decline or race.
  • L1 builders. All refuse landings: the public-mempool shape needs no builder once the gate exists; before then a total boycott stalls landing but voids nothing. FOCIL does not cover blob transactions. Total L1 censorship is outside T1.
  • DAO never acts. Open-ended image windows never expire; single-proof mode and replacement keep the chain moving unless every ZK system is broken, and provisional landings become final by the 24-h rule with no DAO action (§7). The one state that waits for the DAO is CONFLICT, a valid proof against a landed one, which no rule can resolve objectively (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01; limitation L-PF).

14. False positives and false negatives

  • Deadline and replacement. FP: an honest sequencer's blocks hit a guest bug; after ≥ 35 minutes a replacement lands and it loses the term's revenue, nothing else. FN: a sequencer producing provable blocks but never landing pays only the ramp (intended); one producing unprovable blocks loses only the term's revenue, by design, since no rule can tell a guest bug from a refusal to land.
  • Certificate at landing. FP: a sequencer partitioned from every attester for TIMEOUT gets no certificate; its blocks are skipped by a TIMEOUT VC, never landable, it pays MISS (50 TAIKO) and no deadline runs. A leader held by an abstaining minority has its two real blocks attested by the honest attesters, who then never time it out: at worst its term is blank; a leader that gossips bodiless headers, withholds payloads or squats gets nothing attested, is timed out at 5 s and pays MISS outside the collusion and split cases of certificate page §1(d) (round 5, R5H-1: the trigger reads the attester's own attestations, not message arrival).
  • Reward ramp. FP: in a fee spike a lander is paid up to the maximum per block; bounded by the per-term lock. FN: a lazy cartel waits for the maximum; any defector undercuts.
  • Single-proof mode. FP: an idle chain enters it although nothing failed; harmless under T11, and since the landing it admits is provisional its checkpoint waits for a second-leaf confirmation, which any prover gives at once when both leaves work, or 24 h (§7). FN: an attacker cannot trigger it while any leaf works, and a landing it admits is never checkpointed on elapsed time while a conflicting proof has been presented.
  • Image overlap. FP: a prover two hours behind a release is refused. FN: a retired image trusted two hours longer; the emergency flag sets zero.

15. Robustness

6/4/2-s slots: no rule uses slots; blobs per block 10/7/3 so landers shrink ranges, and every greedily packed landing is still paid its r_land (LANDING_UNIT, §5); a 2 M landing is 20 % of a 10 M block; LAND_WINDOW spans 300/450/900 L1 blocks; the EIP-2935 window (13.6/9.1/4.55 h) always exceeds the hard deadline. Without Frame Transactions: losers pay ≈ 25k gas plus one blob fee; deadline stops stale landings; safety, liveness and latency unchanged. ePBS Empty slots: delay inclusion one slot and consume deadline seconds; cannot cause a replacement (that needs a landed proof). L1 EIP-4788 gaps: L1's beacon-roots contract is never read; L2's own EIP-4788 contract records L1 execution block hashes from L2 headers, keyed by L2 timestamp, and no landing check reads it (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). Partitions: a sequencer cut from attesters is "sequenced" only, no penalty; landers cut from L1 are covered by the window; provers cut from gossip range-sync from any committee member.

17. Alternatives rejected

A3); a withdrawable landing reserve (§5); a replacer share of a penalty (griefing incentive); hard 2-of-2 ZK with no degradation (halts until a DAO upgrade); permanent single-proof (puts the ZK-bug case entirely on attesters);
