Propose-with-proof: the landing

One L1 action, land, carries a range of certified blocks as blobs plus a ZK proof and finalizes them immediately: the checkpoint is written into the L1 SignalService in the same transaction. Anyone may land any certified prefix. The blob bytes are a pure function of the certified chain, so any prover's proof is usable by any lander. Deadlines are economic only: the clock alone voids nothing, and no rule penalizes a holder for not landing (invariant I5; §6). This page is normative for the landing checks and for replacement (round 4, D9); the parameters page is normative for constants and the interfaces page for struct and evidence types. Sources: judge synthesis of the three B4 designs, aligned with the certificate page.

Invariants this page owns (round 4)

I2. A drawn term's lander reward exists at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself: no other owner action and no liveness event can remove the funds that pay the term's landers and attesters; reward debits are the only drain and the reserve is sized for two terms of them (§5, residual there). I4. Within the landing horizon (L14) and short of an L1 reorg deeper than the anchor age, nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement or forced batch accepted under A1, or 2Q(m) − m slashings: no L1 record and no accusation voids certified blocks (§6). I5. No accusation object exists: every slashing rule's accept set is signatures by the accused over conflicting messages, a signed object with an invalid preimage, a signed L1 reference that is false, or code on the key; no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing.

What today does and why it fails

Two actions: propose stores blob pointers behind a whitelist, one per L1 block; prove, hours later, verifies an aggregated proof behind a second whitelist, writes the checkpoint and advances finalization (00 §2). Image ids are owner-set booleans, so every prover release invalidates in-flight proofs. Nothing on L1 references the preconfirmed chain; derivation clamps timestamps to the landing time; a non-empty forced-inclusion queue reorgs the preconfirmed suffix; a prover that stops halts finalization until the ring buffer fills. This fails R1, R5, R6 and R7 and leaves TH1, TH2, TH7, TH11, TH13, TH16, TH17, TH20 and TH25 open.

1. There is no sequencer seal; the batch commitment is derived

The sequencer signs every block header (V1). No second signature over a batch exists: the batch-level commitment is a digest every party computes identically from the certified chain, and the committee's view change pins where a view ends. Consequences: no seal message to withhold (W5 has no object), no seal-equivocation offence, and a certified block is landable the instant its certificate exists. A lander may land any certified prefix [lastLanded.height + 1, L] spanning up to MAX_VIEWS_PER_LANDING = 4 consecutive views (one segment per view, each opened by its view change and ended by the next view change's lock, the last ended by the end certificate), so the fixed landing gas is amortized over several terms when gas is dear. proven

2. Canonical blob encoding (the DA commitment)

payload = 0x03 ‖ u32(numBlocks) ‖ Σ_i ( u32(len(rec_i)) ‖ rec_i )
rec_i   = headerCore_i ‖ seqSig_i (65 B) ‖ [viewChangeBytes_i if first block of a view] ‖ txListBytes_i

headerCore is the non-derivable subset of the signed header (timestamp, coinbase, gasLimit, anchor number, anchor hash and state root, carried certificate height and hash, termId, view, vcHash, seat, txListHash; every block carries vcHash and the anchor calldata carries it on every block, so the guest rebuilds PH for every block); txListBytes are the exact compressed bytes whose keccak is txListHash (the sequencer's own zlib stream, copied, never re-compressed). Packing is today's coder: 31 bytes per field element, 130,044 usable bytes per blob, zero-padded, split in order. Every byte is a signed field, a signature, or bytes committed by a signed hash, so the blob versioned hashes are a function of the certified block set and the range with no encoder freedom: two landers of one range produce bit-identical blobs and any prover's proof is usable by any lander. A mis-encoded landing yields an end hash different from the certificate and is unlandable (self-harm only). proven

Batch size per second. The L1 blob budget is about 224 KiB/s at every slot time. Client policy targets 32 KiB/s of compressed output per sequencer (one seventh of L1; about 15 blobs per 60-s term). Ranges are lander-chosen, so no per-term cap exists on-chain; the caps are per landing: 6 blobs (the per-transaction limit) and 256 blocks. An oversized landing is not includable, never slashable. Per block, validity rule V9 (certificate page) bounds the canonical record headerCore ‖ seqSig ‖ VC bytes ‖ txListBytes to 390,123 bytes, so that the framed single-block payload (0x03 ‖ u32(numBlocks) ‖ u32(len) ‖ rec, nine framing bytes) fits the 3 blobs = 390,132 bytes one L1 block carries at 2-s slots; a maximum-size block uses the whole blob capacity of one L1 block there (round 2, H1; round 3, H3 corrected the framing bytes). Landers size multi-block ranges with 4 framing bytes per further record. Per term, rule V10 bounds the sum of the records of the term's sequencer blocks to TERM_BYTES_MAX = 1,950,660 bytes (15 blobs, three landings): a certified term can oblige landers to at most three paid landings, which is what the per-landing reward below is sized for (round 3, H2). Forced-inclusion blocks and forced batches (view 254) are exempt from V10 in the guest and at attestation (round 4, R4H-6): FI blocks are derived from queue entries, bounded by one blob each and by MAX_FI_PER_LANDING, and are never the sequencer's choice, so an exhausted budget can never make a sequencer block invalid through V8 and a MISS can never result from the budget. A transaction that is provable but pushes a block or term over its bound is simply not certifiable; MAX_ENVELOPE on the P2P layer equals the V9 number.

3. The landing transaction

land(LandInput) (anyone; rewardTo in calldata) or landFor(rewardTo, LandInput) (only the gate contract, see Frame transactions), blobs attached in range order. Checks run cheapest first so a race loser exits within about 25k gas in the degraded regime; the proof is verified last, as today, so its revert rolls back every write.

  1. L1 sequence. firstHeight == lastLanded.height + 1, else revert NotNext; at most 256 blocks. Several landings may sit adjacent in one L1 block; there is no one-per-block rule.
  2. L2 deadline (degraded regime only). deadline == 0 || block.timestamp ≤ deadline.
  3. L3 parent link. parentBlockHash == lastLanded.blockHash; the journal's parent hash and parent state root are read from storage, never from calldata.
  4. L4 segments, views and rights. segments[] lists up to four consecutive (termId, view, sequencer, firstHeight, lastHeight, fiCount) entries; viewChanges[] is the VC chain from lastVCHash through every segment boundary, each VC's lock equal to the previous segment's last block, at most V_MAX per term, each equal to the view change first recorded on L1 for that (t, v, closes) if a live one exists (a recorded REPLACE or RESUME VC is dead once lastLanded.height > vc.lockHeight: the first-recorded rule ignores it, so the original view's own TIMEOUT or TERM_END VC can still be recorded or landed for the same (t, v, closes); A3), and each verified against committee(signerTerm) (a FALLBACK VC may be signed by any later committee within the landing horizon). Each TIMEOUT-kind VC applies the absence penalty (MISS) to the timed-out leader once per (t, v); REPLACE and RESUME kinds apply none. Every certificate and VC carries the view's redraw count n (0, 1 or 2; REDRAW_MAX = 2) and is verified against the committee of that count: n = 0 is the term's committee, n ≥ 1 the first K seats of the walk keccak(seed ‖ 0x03 ‖ t ‖ n ‖ m) with the sequencing page's exclusions, and the committee proof justifies n with ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW(t, v, vcHash, n) signatures of the committee at n − 1 (round 4, R4-C1: a redraw keeps the leader, the view and the opening object and only replaces an abstaining committee). Each segment's sequencer must satisfy isHolder(t, v, sequencer), computed as rank-based sortition over the seats eligible at termStart(t): the inbox first applies every mutation-ring entry whose effective time has passed, then reads the rank from the Fenwick tree corrected by the entries effective in (termStart(t), now] (sequencing page §5; round 4, R4H-1), so the holders it computes equal what nodes computed at termStart(t) whatever the registry did since; or, with the sentinel view 255, the term must be open-empty or the chain in dead mode and the sequencer bonded, or the term in no-committee mode and the sequencer its sortition holder (any bonded address only after that term's economic deadline; round 2, S9). Sentinel segments open with the object keccak("ETNA_DEAD" ‖ lastLanded.blockHash ‖ t). The committee of each segment's term is the inbox's own on-chain walk (sequencing page §8), pinned as committeeRoot[t] by recordAssignment(t) or by the first landing that settles t (a landing of an unpinned term inside the horizon walks it in the same transaction and pays for it); the committee proof's key hashes are checked against that root, never accepted from calldata. Rights and committees of a term older than ROLE_HORIZON (34 h) are read from its pinned assignment (every landing pins the terms it settles); a term older than the horizon that was never pinned is unlandable and therefore replaceable.
  5. L5 end certificate. endCert with height == endHeight, phHash equal to the guest's end header hash, popcount ≥ Q(m) for the last segment's committee of size m at the certificate's redraw count, aggregate signature valid against that committee (one pairing). Intermediate segments need no certificate of their own: each is ended by a view change whose lock certificate the VC's signers verified. Sentinel-view exception (dead mode, no-committee mode, open-empty, forced): an empty certificate is accepted, and then the verifier is never degraded (§7). Verified on L1, not in the guest, so the withholding defence never rests on ZK soundness.
  6. L6 blobs. blobhash(i) equals the listed hashes for i < n ≤ 6 and the next one is zero; blobHashesRoot = keccak(hashes). Because V9 bounds every block's canonical record to 3 blobs (390,132 bytes), any single certified block is landable in one transaction at every supported slot time; the lander is paid R_BLOB per blob on top of the per-block ramp.
  7. L7 anchor tip. l1AnchorTipNumber < block.number and ≥ the last landed tip; its hash equals blockhash within 256 blocks, else the EIP-2935 contract (8,191 blocks; 4.55 h at 2-s slots); older is refused (TH17). The guest chains every anchored header to this tip.
  8. L8 forced inclusion. fiHeadBefore equals the queue head; the consumed count meets the anchored-time floor rule of the Forced inclusion page (T_floor is the start of the first segment's term; at most 64 per landing); fiConsumedHash over the consumed entries; fees credited to the ETH pull ledger.
  9. L9 replacement (only if replace is set): §6.
  10. L10 effects. lastLanded = {height, blockHash, stateRoot, phHash, termId, view, at: block.timestamp, anchorTipNumber, anchorTipTimestamp}; the term records (including each settled term's bytesLanded and paid-landing count, §5); lastLandedTerm (read by the sequencing page's dead-mode rule); the assignment digest and committee root of every term the landing settles (≤ SETTLE_MAX per landing); the SignalService checkpoint (§8); lander and attester rewards (§5); the prevrandao mix; emit Landed.
  11. L11 proof. journalHash per §4; verifier.verify(journalHash, proof, kRequired) reverts unless the required number of distinct ZK leaves accept.

Gas: two ZK leaves ≈ 600k (assumed 300k each; unmeasurable from the repository), one aggregate certificate ≈ 120k, up to four VCs ≈ 120k each worst case, rights ≤ 290k worst case (≈ 7k typical), FI walk ≤ 270k under a backlog (≈ 10k typical), about ten overwritten stores ≈ 100k, attester credits ≈ 160k, plus one fresh checkpoint slot ≈ 98k state gas (unavoidable under R2). About 1.5 M typical, 2.5 M worst case: 25 % of a 10 M block at 2-s slots, 15 % of the EIP-7825 cap. assumed

4. What the ZK proof covers

Journal { chainId, protocolParamsHash,                       // TH25: guest constants published on L1
          parentBlockHash, parentStateRoot, firstHeight,     // parent = lastLanded (L1 storage)
          endHeight, endBlockHash, endStateRoot, endPhHash,
          blobHashesRoot,                                    // L6, inbox-computed
          segmentsRoot,                                      // keccak(segments[]) as resolved by L4
          vcHashOfFirstBlock,                                // 0 if the range continues an open view
          l1AnchorTipNumber, l1AnchorTipHash, l1AnchorTipTimestamp,
          fiHeadBefore, fiHeadAfter, fiConsumedHash,
          rewardTo }                                         // bound for every landing kind: proofs are per lander (round 2, S6)
journalHash = keccak256(abi.encode(Journal)); leaf input = hashPublicInputs(journalHash, leaf, address(0), chainId)

The guest proves: decoding the blobs (recomputed versioned hashes match blobHashesRoot) yields blocks firstHeight..endHeight; each header's seqSig recovers to leader and its anchor calldata carries (termId, view); the first block of a view carries vcHashOfFirstBlock; every block executes VALID from the parent state to exactly its blockHash (V6); hashes chain from parentBlockHash to endBlockHash, whose header carries endStateRoot; every anchored L1 header lies on a parent-hash chain ending at the tip, whose timestamp is exported, anchors monotone from the parent's; timestamps obey V4; the forced-inclusion blocks are exactly the consumed queue entries in order, derived from their own blobs, at the placement the forced-inclusion page specifies. Sequence, rights, certificate, blob hashes, anchor tip, queue state and deadlines stay outside the proof; execution, derivation, anchor contents, FI contents and per-block signatures are inside it. proven that no state root is finalized without valid proofs over a journal binding the parent root (storage), blob hashes (blobhash), the anchor tip (blockhash or EIP-2935), the consumed queue entries (storage) and the certified end hash (L5).

5. Sequential landing, pipelined proving, lander economics

Landing is sequential in height; proving is not. A block's proof depends only on its parent state, public on the certified chain, so provers prove blocks as they are certified and aggregate any range. Happy-path "landed" latency ≈ 2 to 3 minutes after a block assumed (block proofs ≤ 20 s pipelined, aggregation ≤ 90 s, one to two L1 blocks). Catch-up: 256 blocks and 6 blobs per landing, several landings per L1 block.

No prover role, registration, assignment or fee pool. The lander is paid in TAIKO on the existing L1 bond ledger from the bond of the sequencer whose blocks it lands: reward = blocks × r(τ, t) per segment, plus R_BLOB per blob, plus one per-landing component r_land(τ) for each landing a term needs (round 3, H2: a fixed 2 M-gas cost per landing cannot be covered by a per-block price when a term needs three landings). r ramps linearly from R_BLK_MIN (0.5 TAIKO) at term end to R_BLK_MAX (4 TAIKO, the marginal proving cost) over LAND_WINDOW; r_land ramps from 0 to R_LAND_MAX (1,300 TAIKO ≈ 0.2 ETH at the assumed rate, a 2 M-gas landing at 100 gwei) over the same window. Per-landing reward rule (round 4, R4H-7): TermRecord tracks bytesLanded; landing k of term t (k counting the landings whose first segment belongs to t) is paid r_land only if k ≤ ⌈bytesLanded_after / (6 × 130,044)⌉, where 6 × 130,044 = 780,264 bytes is one full landing. A one-block landing therefore earns at most one r_land per term, the r_land exposure of a term is exactly 3 × R_LAND_MAX, and splitting a range earns nothing beyond the landings the term's bytes already oblige. The break-even fee at which a third party is paid in full is ≈ 100 gwei per landing whatever the term's size; above it landing waits for the fee to fall or the holder to land at its own cost, and nothing is penalized while it waits (no rule reads the absence of a landing). A landing may cover four views so the fixed gas is amortized. Before term end the price is the minimum and the sequencer lands its own blocks (debit = credit; net cost L1 gas). If its prover fails, the price ramps: a Dutch auction with the sequencer as reserve bidder. A sequencer that knows its prover is down shortcuts the ramp with a signed RewardBoost, capped at R_BLK_MAX.

The landing reserve (invariant I2; round 4, R4H-3, R4-C2, R4-C4). Reward-type debits (ramps, blob reward, boost, per-landing reward, attester share) are drawn from the holder's landing reserve, a ledger amount of LAND_RESERVE = 8,400 TAIKO per seat that registration and reactivation require on top of 1.05 × the seat floor: the registration bond is ≥ 1.05 × seats × B_SEAT + seats × LAND_RESERVE. Four rules make the reward exist at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself (which takes the whole ledger, reserve included) and short of the self-directed reward drain costed in the residual below:

  • Non-withdrawable. The reserve cannot be withdrawn while the owner has any active or pending seat; it leaves the ledger only through requestExit + EVIDENCE_WINDOW, like seat stake. withdraw is limited to the excess over 1.05 × floor + reserve (round 4, R4H-3: round 3 had funded the reward at registration and left the funds withdrawable at S(t) + 1).
  • Ring-fenced. Class-C debits never touch the reserve: MISS is taken from the 5 % buffer above the floor, then from seat stake down to the 50 % hard floor. The reserve is debited only by reward-type payments, so a timeout can never empty it (round 4, R4-C4).
  • Sized for concurrency. The per-term reward exposure is 60 × R_BLK_MAX + 15 × R_BLOB + 3 × R_LAND_MAX + 60 × ATT_REWARD_PER_BLOCK = 240 + 15 + 3,900 + 6 = 4,161 TAIKO, realised only at the maximum ramp; LAND_RESERVE = 8,400 per seat is two terms of it. An owner with CAP = 8 seats holds 67,200 TAIKO of reserve, about 16 terms of worst-case exposure, against an expectation of about 7 terms drawn inside one LAND_WINDOW_MAX (60 terms) at the launch registry (8 of 72 seats); the probability that more than 16 of such an owner's terms fall in one window is below 0.1 % (binomial, 60 draws at 1/9: ≈ 0.02 %). Class-A stake is never touched by rewards (round 2, S5).
  • Lapse. When a reward debit takes the reserve below two terms of exposure the contract emits ReserveLow, one term of exposure before the lapse; a reserve below one term's exposure makes the owner ineligible with a DELAY_REG-dated effect (never DELAY_S) and for at least SUSPEND(3), stored in the same two-interval store as structural suspensions, until topped up and reactivated (round 4, R4-C2: a DELAY_S-dated, owner-triggerable eligibility write was a seed-known grinding lever). The sequencing page states the rule this instance obeys: every eligibility input is either DELAY_REG-dated or not owner-triggerable.

Residual, stated plainly. No L1 rule can guarantee funds for a term drawn before the debits that emptied the reserve, and the owner can trigger those debits at will: it lets its own terms ramp to the maximum and lands them with rewardTo an address it controls, so 4,161 TAIKO per term moves from the reserve to a freely withdrawable balance at no net cost until the reserve lapses. The window is the terms drawn inside DELAY_REG after the lapse plus the terms still unlanded at that moment (up to 34 h of drawn terms); such a term is landed by its holder at gas cost, or replaced under A1 with no penalty to anyone, and the drain costs the owner one SUSPEND(3) (36 h) of lost revenue per cycle. A self-inflicted class-A slash (the owner submits S1 against its own conflicting headers) empties the ledger, reserve included, and unfunds the same window at the price of the whole ledger (limitations L20). The journal binds rewardTo for ordinary landings as for forced ones (round 2, S6: a copied proof with a swapped reward address would otherwise land identically), so proofs are per lander: a prover sells or gives a proof by proving for that lander's address, and the proofs topic carries the lander address. Attesters of the end certificate share ATT_REWARD_PER_BLOCK (0.1 TAIKO) per landed block, debited from each block's holder. open whether to index the minimum to block.basefee through a DAO-set rate constant.

6. Deadline and replacement

deadline(t) = termEnd(t) + LAND_WINDOW (30 min) is the economic deadline: the ramp reaches its maximum. LAND_WINDOW_MAX (60 min) caps the lag that chained late landings can build inside A1. The clock voids nothing by itself, and no rule penalizes a term for not landing (invariants I4 and I5). Round 4 removed the abandonment penalty S4a with its evidence deadline, outage gate, void waiver and escrow (R4H-5, R4-C3): a certified view is landable by anyone and paid from the holder's reserve (§5), so a holder cannot abandon a provable view; the only unlandable certified content is a guest-completeness bug, which is a false positive for any abandonment rule and is handled by replacement plus the client blacklist. Class C is MISS only. The withholding defence (TH5) rests on the worthlessness of uncertified blocks and on "certified implies landable by anyone", not on a penalty.

  • A1 replaceable (inbox predicate). With t' the first unlanded term: replaceableFrom = min(termEnd(t') + LAND_WINDOW_MAX, max(termEnd(t') + LAND_WINDOW, lastLanded.at + LAND_CHAIN_GRACE)) + REPLACE_GRACE (REPLACE_GRACE = 300 s; round 4 renamed it, its only remaining role is this formula). A replacement landing (or a forced batch) is accepted iff block.timestamp > replaceableFrom. Inputs are inbox storage and the block timestamp. The chain grace keeps a late parent from making its child replaceable at once; the maximum caps the lag chained late landings can build. Bonded landing intent (round 2, S3): anyone with a TAIKO ledger balance may call announceLanding(firstHeight, endHeight) with firstHeight = lastLanded.height + 1, locking ANNOUNCE_BOND = 2,000 TAIKO on the ledger (not ETH), while replaceableFrom has not passed; it defers replaceableFrom by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE, once per range (the deferral is keyed on lastLanded). If an ordinary landing covering firstHeight follows, the bond is returned with it; if a forced or replacement landing lands first, the bond is forfeited: 40 % to that lander, 60 % burned. An announcement is refused once the head forced-inclusion entry has been due at the T_floor of the first unlanded term for longer than LAND_WINDOW (round 3, C3), so the forced path is deferred at most once. A fake announcement therefore costs its poster 2,000 TAIKO and can delay the forced path by at most one grace period, and a landable backlog is never reorged while someone bonded is landing it. Nodes label confirmations above lastLanded "replaceable" from replaceableFrom.
  • A2 fork. When A1 holds in a node's L1 view, the current holder may reopen its current view (same term and view number) with a new opening object: a VC of kind REPLACE carrying an L1 reference, whose hash becomes the view's vcHash. Its first block has parentHash = lastLanded.blockHash. The reference is inside every REPLACE lock vote's signed bytes and inside the VC (round 3, S1). Attesters attest it iff A1 holds at that reference in their view, the reference is at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old and canonical, and the first unlanded certified term's deadline has passed. Every equivocation key (S1, S3a, S3c, fork choice, gossip dedup, slashing-protection records) includes the opening object, so the original tail and the fork are never equivocation. Client duty: re-include the void blocks' transactions in order, except transactions the fork's sequencer lists in the REPLACE VC as unprovable (a Q-signed quarantine list), which are dropped.
  • A3 L1 arbitration (landing-based; round 4, R4H-5 and R4-C3). Until a replacement (or a forced batch) lands, landing the original stays valid and pays the ramp; whichever lands first wins, and the loser is invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape. A recorded REPLACE VC has no closing effect: recordViewChange accepts a REPLACE or RESUME VC only if vc.lockHeight == lastLanded.height, vc.lockPhHash == lastLanded.phHash and block.timestamp > replaceableFrom at record time (A1 re-evaluated on inbox state; otherwise the call is refused), and the accepted record serves two purposes only: (i) it is the first-recorded VC for (t, v, closes) that the fork's landing must match under L4, and (ii) it is priority evidence for the forced path, deferring it by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE once, exactly like a landing announcement. A REPLACE or RESUME record is dead once lastLanded.height > vc.lockHeight (round 4 fix, R4H-5 residual): the first-recorded rule ignores a dead record, so when the original view is landed on top of the record's lock, the original's own TIMEOUT or TERM_END VC for the same (t, v, closes) is recorded or landed as if no REPLACE had been recorded, and a dead record is no longer priority evidence; without this, a stale record would have refused the original view's closing VC at the next view boundary and forced a second replacement. A live fork's own later VCs close a different object (round 3, S6), so they never collide with the original's on L1. The lock reset of the certificate page (preconf §6) and the RESET attester state trigger on a landed replacement or forced batch only, never on a record; the RESET-state rules (only RESUME is signable under the old opening object; S3b ignores landed-head locks) are unchanged. Round 3's recorded-REPLACE close was removed in round 4 because a stale record, formed before a backlog landing, closed a live view and voided fresh locked blocks with a single transaction and no proof (R4H-5), and because a record voided a whole certified backlog while proving was down (R4-C3). Dead-mode landings (view 255, no certificate) are replacements and obey A1.
  • Why descendants are void. Every certified block above lastLanded was built on a state L1 will never hold once a replacement lands; its proof asserts a parent root the inbox does not store. Unavoidable when the preconf chain runs ahead of L1; this design bounds when: only when a replacement lands, never by time within the landing horizon. The horizon is the smaller of ROLE_HORIZON (34 h) and the EIP-2935 window (27 h at 12-s slots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots); a range whose first term is older than it can no longer be landed and is replaceable. A global proving outage shorter than the horizon voids nothing.
  • Rarity. A replacement needs A1 (≥ 35 to 65 minutes without any landing) and a landed proof: a replacement needs a working proof system, so a proving outage on its own voids nothing (round 4, R4-C3 restored this). It happens when the head range is unprovable while a replacement is provable (a content-specific guest bug), or when a holder forks after an outage and its fork lands before the backlog. A few events per year, each voiding at most about an hour of blocks, with no penalty to anyone.

7. Verifier composition, degradation, rotation

ZK_K = 2 distinct ZK leaves of ZK_N (RISC0, SP1; target 3). TEE leaves never count (assumption A3, TH1). Each leaf keeps today's staticcall shape plus require(remote.code.length > 0) and {current, previous, previousValidUntil} image sets: a DAO upgrade moves the old set to previous with a two-hour overlap, or zero when flagged emergency (TH20). Single-proof mode: if block.timestamp − lastLanded.at > DEGRADE_AFTER (2 h), one leaf suffices until the next landing, only for landings with a non-empty certificate. Safe under T11: the journal's end block hash must equal the certified hash, whose header commits the state root that Q attesters executed, so a false root needs one ZK bug and a colluding quorum. This is the one place attester execution carries part of state safety, and only after two hours of stalled landing.

8. Races, ePBS timing, finality and the SignalService call

Primary shape, direct to builder: an expiry VERIFY frame, the lander's own nonce, the SENDER frame calling land, and a trailing status VERIFY; any revert (lost race, NotNext, bad proof, rotated image, out of gas) makes the transaction invalid: no gas, no blob fee. Public-mempool fallback: the shared-nonce gate G_LAND calling landFor; deployed only once EIP-8141 is Final, inert while its immutable address is zero. Without EIP-8141: a type-3 transaction from the lander's EOA with deadline; a loser reverts for ≈ 25k gas plus its burned blob fee.

Levels, no slots: landed = in an L1 block the node has (checkpoint written); L1-safe = that block has one descendant (under ePBS the payload of N is canonical in practice once N+1 builds on it); L1-final = finalized per the node's beacon client (reported, never consumed by a contract); void = replaced or removed by an L1 reorg (inbox state reverts with L1; the range returns to CERTIFIED; no penalty). An Empty slot means the transaction was not executed; the expiry frame makes the stale copy free.

12. Attacks defended and not defended

  • TH1 forged proof: defended, bounded. Normal mode needs two distinct leaves; TEE never counts. Single-proof mode also needs a colluding quorum. Not defended: a common-mode bug in the shared guest; only diverse guests resolve it (open).
  • TH2 prover cartel, W6: defended. The sequencer lands itself; otherwise the ramp attracts any newcomer with the certified data (held by ≥ Q attesters); a total outage delays, never voids.
  • TH4 gossiped ≠ landed: defended. L5 binds the end hash to a certificate; the guest chains every signed header from lastLanded to it.
  • TH5 W1/W3/W5: defended. Uncertified blocks are unlandable; provers rebuild blobs from committee-held data; no seal object. Not defended: withholding that still lands within the window costs only latency.
  • TH7 FI starvation: defended. L8 counts due entries at the anchored timestamp, kept fresh by the anchor age rule; a landing skipping a due request reverts, so the blocks are unlandable and replaceable (no penalty: the holder earns nothing from them); the forced batch bypasses rights after escalation.
  • TH8 bond griefing: defended. No penalty is accusation-triggered and none reads the absence of a landing (invariant I5): the only landing-path debit is MISS, applied by a Q-signed TIMEOUT VC against a leader silent to the whole committee.
  • TH11 landing-race waste: defended with frames, degraded without. No third party can make a winner revert after payment: every check depends on readable state and the winner's calldata; FI fees are pull-based.
  • TH12 reorg: defended. A reorged landing returns the range to CERTIFIED with no penalty.
  • TH13 congestion: defended up to the break-even fee. Windows in seconds; ≤ 2.5 M gas; blobs per landing sized to the block; the ramp pays a third party in full up to about 100 gwei per landing, whatever the term size (R_LAND covers the fixed gas; round 4, R4H-8 corrected the earlier per-term figure); above that, landing waits or the holder pays, and the wait costs no bond because no rule reads a missing landing.
  • TH16, TH17, TH20, TH25. Only the two gate shapes touch EIP-8141 and both fail closed; the anchor tip is checked on L1; image overlap in seconds; protocolParamsHash makes a mismatched guest unlandable (a liveness, never a soundness, failure).
  • Bounded, not prevented: reorg of a landable backlog after a stall (round 2, S3; round 4, R4-C3). Once replaceableFrom has passed and proving returns, the current holder may fork a REPLACE view, and any bonded party may land a forced batch, racing the backlog; whichever lands first wins, and if the fork or the forced batch lands first the stall's certified blocks are void. Defences: nobody is penalized (no rule reads a missing landing); a bonded announceLanding, or an accepted REPLACE record, gives the backlog priority for one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE; a REPLACE lock vote with a false L1 reference is class B (S3d); the replacer and forcer earn no penalty share (only 40 % of a forfeited announcement bond); landing the backlog pays the maximum per block; the RESUME view re-includes voided transactions before any forced entry (V8 exception); confirmations are labelled "replaceable" from replaceableFrom. Residual: one reorg of the backlog per stall, bounded by the grace period, no penalty to anyone (limitation L6).
  • Not defended: poison content (a certified block no guest can prove) is handled by replacement plus a client-side blacklist fed by prover error reports.

13. Failure analysis

  • Sequencers. All offline: takeover produces blocks under backup views; certified ranges are landed by anyone; nothing is voided or penalized without a landed replacement; in dead mode any bonded address restarts from the landed head. All malicious: unprovable content (replaced; the term earns nothing, no penalty), refusing to land (pay the ramp), spurious forks (attesters reject a REPLACE failing A1; the inbox refuses a REPLACE record whose lock is not lastLanded); they cannot land uncertified data or a false root.
  • Attesters. All offline: nothing landable, no deadline runs, nobody penalized; dead mode is the floor after 75 minutes. All malicious (≥ Q): they can certify a block only they hold (landable only if provable), refuse certificates (liveness only), or sign a false REPLACE certificate that voids provable blocks (bounded griefing).
  • Provers and landers. All offline: the sequencer lands itself; if it cannot, ranges accumulate CERTIFIED, un-voided; the first returning prover clears the backlog. All malicious: they can only decline or race.
  • L1 builders. All refuse landings: the public-mempool shape needs no builder once the gate exists; before then a total boycott stalls landing but voids nothing. FOCIL does not cover blob transactions. Total L1 censorship is outside T1.
  • DAO never acts. Open-ended image windows never expire; single-proof mode and replacement keep the chain moving unless every ZK system is broken.

14. False positives and false negatives

  • Deadline and replacement. FP: an honest sequencer's blocks hit a guest bug; after ≥ 35 minutes a replacement lands and it loses the term's revenue, nothing else. FN: a sequencer producing provable blocks but never landing pays only the ramp (intended); one producing unprovable blocks loses only the term's revenue, by design, since no rule can tell a guest bug from a refusal to land.
  • Certificate at landing. FP: a sequencer partitioned from every attester for TIMEOUT gets no certificate; its blocks are skipped by a TIMEOUT VC, never landable, it pays MISS (50 TAIKO) and no deadline runs. A leader that keeps gossiping signed headers to some attesters is never timed out; at worst its term is blank (round 4, R4H-4).
  • Reward ramp. FP: in a fee spike a lander is paid up to the maximum per block; bounded by the per-term lock. FN: a lazy cartel waits for the maximum; any defector undercuts.
  • Single-proof mode. FP: an idle chain enters it although nothing failed; harmless under T11. FN: an attacker cannot trigger it while any leaf works.
  • Image overlap. FP: a prover two hours behind a release is refused. FN: a retired image trusted two hours longer; the emergency flag sets zero.

15. Robustness

6/4/2-s slots: no rule uses slots; blobs per block 10/7/3 so landers shrink ranges; a 2 M landing is 20 % of a 10 M block; LAND_WINDOW spans 300/450/900 L1 blocks; the EIP-2935 window (13.6/9.1/4.55 h) always exceeds the hard deadline. Without Frame Transactions: losers pay ≈ 25k gas plus one blob fee; deadline stops stale landings; safety, liveness and latency unchanged. ePBS Empty slots: delay inclusion one slot and consume deadline seconds; cannot cause a replacement (that needs a landed proof). EIP-4788 gaps: not consumed. Partitions: a sequencer cut from attesters is "sequenced" only, no penalty; landers cut from L1 are covered by the window; provers cut from gossip range-sync from any committee member.

16. Claims

  1. proven Blob versioned hashes are a pure function of the certified block set and the range (given txListHash in the signed header).
  2. proven The landed block set equals the certified block set (V7 by construction).
  3. proven No state root is finalized without the required valid proofs over a journal binding parent root, blob hashes, anchor tip, consumed queue entries and the certified end hash.
  4. proven within the landing horizon: nothing is voided without a landed replacement or forced batch accepted under A1 (a recorded VC has no closing effect; round 4, R4H-5), and a proving outage shorter than the horizon (34 h, or the EIP-2935 window if shorter: 27 h at 12-s slots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots) voids and penalizes nothing. Beyond the horizon, unlanded ranges are void by time (limitations L14).
  5. proven No accusation object exists (invariant I5): the only landing-path debit is MISS, applied once per (term, view) by a Q-signed TIMEOUT VC, and no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing.
  6. proven Replacement is locally decidable: A1 is a predicate on inbox state and the block timestamp, re-evaluated at record time for REPLACE and RESUME records; attester disagreements resolve by L1 ordering of landings.
  7. proven Finality lag under sequencer collusion is bounded by LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE after the next unlanded term's end.
  8. proven A drawn term's lander reward exists at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself (invariant I2, qualified): the reserve is non-withdrawable while a seat is active, ring-fenced from MISS, reward debits are its only drain, and any lapse takes effect DELAY_REG later; the residual is the terms drawn inside DELAY_REG after a lapse plus those still unlanded, which the owner can bring about by landing its own late terms to an address it controls at the price of one SUSPEND(3) of revenue per cycle, landed at gas cost or replaced without penalty (§5).
  9. assumed Landing gas 1.5 to 2.0 M (≈ 300k per ZK leaf; unmeasurable from the repository).
  10. assumed A 256-block range is provable on two leaves within LAND_WINDOW / 2; happy-path latency 2 to 3 minutes.
  11. assumed T11 carries single-proof-mode safety; attesters execute before attesting.
  12. assumed TAIKO rewards cover ETH-denominated landing cost up to the published break-even fee (no oracle; a DAO-set rate constant is the open alternative).
  13. open Whether post-payment VERIFY frames stay consensus-legal; fallback is a claim VERIFY in the prefix.
  14. open The rate of backlog reorgs after stalls on an adversarial testnet; would justify a refundable REPLACE bond on top of the landing announcement.

17. Alternatives rejected

  • A quorum-attested explicit seal (a withholdable object and a gossip round on the landing path); a sequencer-signed seal over sequencer-chosen blob bytes (a withheld message voids 30 minutes); a batch grid with dense batch ids; per-batch rewards (invite range splitting); any penalty for not landing, time-based (cascades across an outage) or evidence-based (a guest bug is a false positive for every form of it; §6); a recorded REPLACE VC with a closing effect (a proof-free void; A3); a withdrawable landing reserve (§5); a replacer share of a penalty (griefing incentive); hard 2-of-2 ZK with no degradation (halts until a DAO upgrade); permanent single-proof (puts the ZK-bug case entirely on attesters); certificates verified inside the guest (withholding defence would depend on ZK soundness); FI due-ness by MPT proofs in the guest; per-entry anchor checks on L1; data-only landings during proving outages (reintroduces the propose/prove split); prover assignment or ahead-of-time proving auctions (a liveness dependency on the assignee); one landing per L1 block (slot-coupled); a fee pool from L2 base fees (second treasury); gas-reimbursing rewards measured on-chain (manipulable); deterministic re-compression (no canonical zlib); calldata DA fallback (six times dearer, not FOCIL-protected either); uncertified landing after a delay (recreates the withholding payoff).

18. Open questions

  1. Is one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE of bonded priority (announceLanding) enough against the backlog reorg of L6, or should a REPLACE view itself post a refundable bond forfeited if the original lands within REPLACE_GRACE afterwards?
  2. The landing horizon (34 h, or the EIP-2935 window if shorter) is the one time-based void; is 34 h the right trade against the registry's role-history cost?
  3. Keep single-proof mode after two hours, given June 2026, or accept a longer stall until a third ZK system exists?
  4. Should the ramp be indexed to block.basefee through a DAO-set TAIKO-per-ETH constant, or are the published break-even fees with DAO-tuned constants (upgrade only) acceptable?
  5. Is a client-side unprovable-transaction blacklist fed by prover error reports acceptable as a non-consensus mitigation for poison content?
  6. Measured mainnet gas for the RISC0 and SP1 leaves is needed before freezing the landing budget.
