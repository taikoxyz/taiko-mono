Frame Transactions (EIP-8141) in Etna

Verdict on the "Nonce as a Lock" hypothesis: possible without EIP changes, at consensus level, with high confidence; the operational path (mempool policy, unshipped clients, moving pins) has medium confidence. Etna adopts a two-shape gate and depends on Frame Transactions for nothing except zero-cost races. Source: 03-frame-transactions-research.md.

What the primitive gives

A frame transaction whose sender is a shared, non-upgradeable gate contract G and whose nonce is G's account nonce is a consensus-level mutual-exclusion primitive: of any set of transactions sharing (G, n), at most one can ever be included, and every other is invalid rather than included-and-reverted. It pays nothing, including its blob fee, because the nonce check is the first step of frame-transaction processing and nothing is charged until a payment-scoped APPROVE executes. proven (each load-bearing step is a verbatim spec line: nonce check first, APPROVE increments the nonce, VERIFY failure makes the transaction invalid).

What it does not give: it fixes who pays, never whether the L2 slot advances. Any revert of the winner's action after payment approval still burns the shared nonce for everyone, and under R7 the racing transaction carries a ZK proof that G cannot check in its prefix. So the PDF's single mechanism would hand a third party a nonce-burn lever: outbid the honest tip for one slot and waste it at gas cost. proven

The two-shape gate Etna adopts

  1. Public-mempool shape. A shared-nonce transaction through a per-role gate, an expiry VERIFY frame in front, a minimum tip pinned. It is the only construction whose zero-cost race propagates through the public mempool and therefore the only fallback that depends on no builder. The race it makes free is the one between shape-1 competitors for the same nonce: losers of the same (G, n) in the same block are invalid and free. A (G, n) transaction whose parent was consumed by another landing (a shape-2 landing by any actor, or a direct land) is still valid: frame 3 reverts at the inbox's first check (parent hash and firstHeight, before blob or proof verification, about 30k gas of frame execution plus the transaction's intrinsic cost, 21k plus its calldata, and, since the shape carries blobs, its blob fee, which a frame transaction's payer owes once payment is approved, 03 §3.5), nonce n is burned and P pays that gas. Shape 1 is zero-cost only against other shape-1 competitors for the same nonce; against a shape-2 landing it pays today's early-revert cost, bounded by the first check, because the public-pool rules let G's prefix read no storage but G's own, so G cannot compare the input with lastLanded before payment (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). proven for the qualified statement.
  2. Direct-to-builder shape. The actor's own nonce, an expiry frame in front, a trailing status VERIFY frame behind. Any revert of the landing is invalid and free; pipelining and multi-segment submission are allowed; the inbox needs no new trust root because msg.sender is the actor. proven from the spec text; open whether post-payment VERIFY frames stay allowed (fallback: a claim VERIFY in the prefix).
  3. Both shapes are compatible on one seat: a (G, n) transaction with a trailing VERIFY and a plain (G, n) transaction are alternatives for the same nonce. proven

Load-bearing pins of the gate

Each pin, if missing, lets a third party forge msg.sender == G or burn the nonce at will: exactly four frames with G as frame 1; frame 3 is the only SENDER frame: target INBOX, flags 0, value 0, data the ABI encoding of landFor(address _rewardTo, LandInput _in) with the selector pinned, word 0 (_rewardTo) equal to P, length at least 68 and at most LAND_CALLDATA_MAX bytes (assumed 8,192; sized at implementation from the proof, certificate and segment descriptors: a typical landing, one segment and one view change with two Groth16-wrapped leaves of about 320 bytes each, is about 4.5 KB of ABI data, and the four-view worst case with eight redraw proofs and four boosts about 8 KB; a bound set too small refuses only the gate path, never the direct land); the gate reads only this fixed prefix with FRAMEDATALOAD (the selector, word 0 and the offset word of the dynamic input: 4 + 32 + 32 = 68 bytes), the rest of the data is covered by P's signature over the transaction (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01: the research record's 68-byte pin came from the PDF's proposeFor(address, bytes32), whose data lives in blobs; a landing carries its proof in calldata); the payer, the signer and the named actor are the same address; two signatures with fixed modes and no padding malleability; a minimum tip; no external calls, no SLOAD, no TIMESTAMP or NUMBER in G's bytecode (public-pool eligibility). G is versioned per L1 fork and never deployed on a persistent network before EIP-8141 is Final, because the expiry verifier address, the 8-byte expiry data, the nonce field and opcode numbers are all in motion in open pull requests. assumed that the pins survive to Final; each pin fails closed, and the direct land(...) stays callable by any EOA behind no switch.

What the inbox must offer in both regimes

  • landFor(address _rewardTo, LandInput calldata _in) gated on an immutable gate address (zero = disabled), next to the direct land(...); the SENDER frame's data is the ABI encoding of that call, its length bounded by LAND_CALLDATA_MAX (the pins above), and its hash is what the gate's signature covers. The direct land(...) from any EOA through the public mempool is always available and sits behind no switch; the gate adds only the zero-cost race, and the gate address is an immutable set at deployment (a DAO upgrade changes it), so no governance action is needed to restore the public path when G fails (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).
  • The firstHeight and parentBlockHash fields of the input, so a stale landing reverts in both shapes instead of landing on the wrong parent: shape 2's trailing VERIFY makes the revert invalid and free, shape 1 pays the early revert (the inbox's first check, about 30k gas of frame execution plus the intrinsic cost and the blob fee, with nonce n burned; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01); there is no batch id anywhere (ranges are lander-chosen).
  • Forced inclusions consumed by due time, and their fees, bonds and refunds pull-based, so no third party can make a lander's transaction revert after payment approval (03 §4 claim 36). This includes the refunds of entries that a four-hour stall voids and the escalation surcharge returned to an entry consumed with content, which the landing makes withdrawable per entry by the requester each entry stored at save time, writing the entry's status into its own word for a later withdrawFiRefund(id) instead of sending or crediting a per-requester slot (round 6, R6H-1 and the fix pass): a requester whose address reverts cannot make the landing revert, and no lander can redirect a refund. It also includes the queue beyond the last entry a range consumes: the journal names no entry there, only the latest anchor time at which the range relied on none being due (fiNotDueAt), and the inbox compares the stored save time of the next entry with it by inequality, so an entry saved after the proof was made, whose save time is later than every anchor of the range, cannot make the landing revert either (round 6, R6H-1).
  • No bond check on the gate path (a bond revert would be a burn lever); the ring-buffer bound kept as the per-block spam bound.

Degradation if Frame Transactions slip

Baseline marked as degraded

Every zero-cost property disappears together. The fallback is today's revert model: the inbox's one-per-block revert, private revert-protected builder submission, and the deadline field that already exists in the propose input. Losers pay execution gas plus the burned blob fee; stale landings revert with a deadline error. Nothing in the inbox interface changes between the two regimes: land stays callable directly by anyone, and landFor is gated on an immutable gate address that is zero until EIP-8141 is Final and that only a DAO upgrade changes. Two inbox changes are worth keeping in both regimes because they remove other-triggerable reverts: forced inclusions by due time, and pull-based fees and refunds.

Hazards the design must not lean on

  • Public-mempool rules for type 0x06 are not implemented in any shipped client (one-pending-per-sender, revalidation, MAX_VERIFY_GAS). Treat every mempool property as unavailable until a client ships it. proven at the time of writing.
  • An ePBS builder can pay its bid and reveal an invalid payload; the primitive never guarantees the L2 batch lands.
  • Clients never derive the next landable range from nonce(G): a reverted SENDER frame, an L1 reorg or the direct path decouple them; lastLanded is the only source.
  • EIP-8151 restricts ecrecover for accounts with code: every key that signs attestations, seals or evidence is a code-less EOA or a BLS key.
