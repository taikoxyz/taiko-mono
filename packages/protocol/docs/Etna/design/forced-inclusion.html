Forced inclusion and censorship resistance

A forced-inclusion (FI) request is one L1 transaction and one L2 block. Due-ness is evaluated against the L1 time a block anchors, never against a landing's own timestamp, so no third party can make a proven landing revert and an entry becoming due never reorgs blocks in flight. When every sequencer refuses, anyone lands a forced batch containing exactly the due entries, without rights, certificates or blobs. Source: judge synthesis of the three B7 designs.

What today does and why it fails

One blob, one block, a fee doubling with backlog, due 576 s after the save ("1.5 epochs"), consumed by the next whitelisted proposal up to 10 per proposal ("one per slot"), fees pushed to the proposer before the proposer gate, and nothing that reserves heights in the preconfirmed chain: any non-empty queue at landing reorgs the whole preconfirmed suffix (00 §2.4, §10). The permissionless fallback is dead code; the June 2026 recovery voided the queue by an owner call. Evaluating "due" at a landing's timestamp would let anyone make a proven landing revert (03 §4 claim 36).

1. Request: two shapes (F1)

  • saveForcedInclusion(BlobReference): today's shape, exactly one blob, kind BLOB, dataHash = the versioned hash.
  • saveForcedInclusionCalldata(bytes manifest): at most 4 KiB, kind CALLDATA, dataHash = keccak(manifest); the bytes live in L1 history forever and the request is eligible for FOCIL inclusion lists. A checkpoint pin or reveal fits this shape: pinCurrentOrigin() is a 4-byte call and revealCheckpoint(0, header) at the 1,536-byte header cap is 1,636 bytes of calldata, so a user can force a durable L2 checkpoint through this queue with no deadline (Anchor page §3; adopted from the Astra candidate, PR #22188).
  • Both require msg.value ≥ FI_BOND + FI_BASE_FEE × (FI_FEE_THRESHOLD + pending) / FI_FEE_THRESHOLD × 4^(level + inProgress) (SKIP_ESCALATION; round 3, H1: without it a poison stream cost 0.02 ETH per day to re-arm). The price is set at save time and counts the stall in progress (round 6, R6H-1): inProgress is 1 while a stall is in progress at save time, that is block.timestamp − fiClearTip ≥ LAND_WINDOW while the head entry is due, else 0, and level = max(0, runCount − ⌊(block.timestamp − runLastAt) / ESCALATION_DECAY⌋) is the level of the escalation run, ESCALATION_DECAY = 7 days: each stall (§2) is counted once, at the landing that burns its first voided entry (§5), which sets runCount to the level plus one and runLastAt to its block time, so the level rises by one per stall and falls by one per ESCALATION_DECAY since the last stall. The schedule this covers: an attacker can post entries before any stall is visible, but an entry that survives a stall's void set and can seed the next stall was saved after the cut minus FI_DELAY (§2), while the stall was in progress or after it was counted, so with stalls less than ESCALATION_DECAY apart the k-th stall's seed pays at least 4k−1 times the floor whatever was pre-posted; spacing stalls more closely than that buys nothing, because the level never falls between them, while spacing them ESCALATION_DECAY or more apart returns each to the floor price, so the long-run rate is one cheap stall per week (§12); and splitting a stall's voids over many landings neither lowers nor inflates the count, because a stall is counted once. Bond and surcharge. FI_BOND = 0.05 ETH is refunded to the requester when the entry is consumed, with content, by expiry or by a stall, except that the bond and the whole fee of the first entry a stall voids (the entry at the stored head, the presumed poison) are burned (round 6, R6H-1); every other void-by-stall entry gets its bond and whole fee back. For every entry except a stall's first void the escalation is a refundable deposit (round 6, R6H-1): the kind byte keeps the exponent e = level + inProgress the entry paid (e ≤ 19 fits for any payable fee (FI_FEE × 4^20 exceeds 2^88 wei and the ETH supply, so a save at e ≥ 20 cannot be paid), and an entry consumed with content or voided by expiry refunds its requester exactly the surcharge, FI_FEE × (4^e − 1), FI_FEE being the unescalated fee the formula required at save time, and credits its includer feeWei − that surcharge, so whatever the requester paid above the required price FI_FEE × 4e is the includer's bounty, undiluted (round 6 fix pass: a proportional split of the paid fee gave the includer only a 4−e share of an overpayment). Every refund (the bond, the surcharge, a void-by-stall entry's bond and fee) stays with the entry rather than in a per-requester ledger (round 6 fix pass): the consuming landing or forced batch writes the entry's status into the two status bits of the kind byte of the word it has already read, a warm modify of an existing word with no state growth (an entry at or above the head is pending; after consumption the states are consumed with content, which an expired entry shares, voided and refundable, burned and claimed); the two status bits encode: 00 none (pending, or burned: nothing withdrawable), 01 consumed with content (shared by expiry), 10 voided and refundable, 11 claimed; withdrawFiRefund(id) requires id below the head, status 01 or 10, a new-layout entry and msg.sender equal to the stored requester, then sets 11; a burn writes nothing, so an entry that was never consumed can never be claimed, and the requester withdraws per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id) on the inbox, which checks the status and msg.sender == requester, pays the bond and the refund by the formula above and sets the state to claimed; only the includer's credit goes to the one ETH pull ledger (withdrawFiFees, one slot per includer), so nothing from a voided entry reaches the includer and a self-including attacker recovers only its own refunds. The schedule the per-entry refund covers: a landing or forced batch that consumes 64 entries from 64 fresh requesters writes no fresh slot per requester, where a per-requester ledger would have written 64 fresh slots at ≈ 98k state gas each, ≈ 6.3 M, so the FI walk stays ≤ 128 cold loads ≈ 270k gas plus 64 warm writes and FORCED_LANDING_GAS (§11) is unchanged. The schedule this covers: an attacker gets the surcharge back on every entry except a stall's first void, which is exactly the entry whose price §12 counts, so the refund lowers no stall's cost. Honest-user effect: an honest request never pays the escalation, during a stall, after a run or during a slow landing, but it fronts it: after a run of k stalls a requester deposits about 0.001 × 4k ETH above the unescalated fee (about 4 ETH after six stalls), refunded when its entry is consumed minutes later, and the level falls one step per week without a stall. The rest of the value is recorded as the fee (any excess is a bounty for whoever includes it; today's refund call disappears). An entry is appended to the queue that already lives at Inbox slots 255-256: pending Shasta entries keep the legacy ForcedInclusion{feeInGwei, BlobSlice{blobHashes, offset, timestamp}} layout and read as dataHash = blobHashes[0], savedAt = timestamp, feeWei = feeInGwei × 109, kind BLOB, with no requester and no bond, so a stall that voids one refunds nothing: its fee is burned when it is the stall's first void and otherwise credited to the includer, as on expiry (round 6, R6H-1); an entry that an abandoned Shasta proposal had consumed is requeued at the abandonment with feeInGwei = 0, since its proposer was paid at proposal time, and is due at the fork, its save time plus FI_DELAY lying before the freeze (Anchor page M2; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01), so its includer is credited nothing and a stall that voids it burns or refunds nothing. Every new entry stores its requester: its record in the parallel mapping packs requester (20 bytes), feeWei (uint88) and a kind byte holding the kind (one bit), the entry's status after consumption (two bits; round 6 fix pass) and the exponent e (five bits), the one word a new entry already wrote for its version tag, kind and exact fee (a non-zero requester now marks a new entry), and its bond is the constant FI_BOND, so recording the requester adds no storage; the pending count the entry was priced at (uint24) is kept beside savedAt, so the unescalated fee FI_FEE the surcharge formula needs is recomputed at consumption, not stored in wei (round 6 fix pass); fiEntry(id) normalises both layouts to FiEntry{dataHash, savedAt, feeWei, bondWei, kind, escalation, status, offset, requester}, requester and bondWei being zero for a legacy entry (interfaces page §1; round 2, S8; round 6, R6H-1). The fresh storage a new entry writes (≈ 98k to 196k state gas under EIP-8037, unchanged by the requester) is a second anti-stuffing floor. The queue is unbounded: a full ring would be a censorship lever.

2. One clock: anchored L1 time (F2)

For index i and an L1 timestamp T: due(i, T) := i < tail(T) ∧ savedAt_i + FI_DELAY ≤ T; void(i, T) := (kind_i = BLOB ∧ savedAt_i + FI_EXPIRY ≤ T) ∨ stalled(i, T). Stall clock (round 6, R6H-1): the inbox field fiClearTip holds the clearTip of the last landed block, and clearTip is derived per block like the cursor (§4). For block h with parent p, parent anchor time T_p and the parent's cut cut(p) = clearTip(p) + FI_SKIP (FI_SKIP = 14,400 s, 4 h): clearTip(h) = T_p if no entry at cursor(h) is due at T_p; else cut(p) if T_p ≥ cut(p) and no entry at cursor(h) is due at cut(p) (the stall's void set is used up, so the clock restarts at the cut); else clearTip(p). The clock is read at the parent's anchor, like V8 (§4), so every sequencer block that V8 admits because nothing is due moves it, and what a range assumes about the first entry it does not consume is exactly the inequality the inbox checks (§5, step 4). It is thus the latest parent-anchor time at which a block's cursor had no due entry (the due prefix fully consumed or voided), or the cut of a stall whose void set is used up: a block, and so a landing, that leaves a due entry of the current void set unconsumed does not move it, and because it is a function of the chain, how ranges are split into landings never changes a block's content. At drainedAt the inbox sets fiClearTip to the drain block's timestamp, so the queue is treated as clear at the fork (Anchor page M3), and the escalation run starts at level zero; entries requeued by the abandonment of the Shasta tail (Anchor page M2; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01) are due at drainedAt and run on that clock like any pending entry, so one whose blob has left retention stalls the first Etna landings until the cut, drainedAt + FI_SKIP, and is then voided like any unprovable entry, refunding nothing (no requester, no bond, fee zero). Void by stall: for a block whose parent p has anchor time T_p ≥ cut(p), that is T_p − clearTip(p) ≥ FI_SKIP, stalled(i, T_p) holds for every entry due at cut(p), at any cursor position and not only at the stored head. Such an entry derives to the empty FI block (F3: no transactions) and counts as consumed for L8 and for the cursor; the first entry a stall voids loses its bond and fee, the others are refunded (§1, §5). The void set is fixed at the cut, not at T_p: an entry that becomes due after the cut is served with content, or, if it is itself unprovable, starts the next stall on the restarted clock. Up to MAX_FI_PER_LANDING are consumed per landing as before, and a landing that leaves entries of the void set does not move fiClearTip, so the next landing keeps voiding: a pre-posted stream of N entries costs one FI_SKIP plus ⌈N/64⌉ landings, not N stalls. A forced batch evaluates every block at its anchor T_A (§6). The schedule this covers: before the trigger an adversary can post any number of unprovable entries at any spacing, and every one due at the cut is voided with the first, so pre-posting buys no further stall; an entry that survives a stall was saved after clearTip + FI_SKIP − FI_DELAY, 14,100 s into the stall and so past LAND_WINDOW, and the inbox priced it with the stall in progress, or with the stall already counted (§1); and an entry posted to keep a stall going is not voided by it, so a stuffer cannot hold a stall open with refunded entries: its later entries are served with content and pay their unescalated fees. The judge's trace (two pre-posted entries due together, no landable range) is closed: the first block of a REPLACE, RESUME or dead-mode view may advance the anchor past fiClearTip + FI_SKIP (§4), the blocks after it void both entries, and such a void-by-stall landing caps L8's T_floor at the cut, so it can always end after the void set whatever falls due after the cut (§5). A forced batch voids them too only when its anchor lies before the due time of the first unprovable entry outside the void set, a window an attacker can shrink to one L1 block or none (§6), so the ordinary void landing, not the forced batch, is the dependable path. Round 4 refuted R4H-2 on the premise that the entries behind a skipped head are consumed with content; a pre-posted unprovable stream removes that premise, so voiding the whole due prefix at the cut is now intended, and its victims, honest entries due at a stall's cut, are refunded and must re-post, the 112-minute bound applying to the re-post (§9). The protocol cannot tell a poison entry from an honest entry that sat at the head through a four-hour outage, and burns the bond and fee of either. This is a clock-based rule, added after red-team round 1 (F4) so that an entry whose content no ZK guest can prove cannot halt every landing path forever; the gate is the stall clock, not an entry's own age, so an entry that waited hours in a backlog is never voided while some block catches up with the due prefix at least once every four hours (round 2, F5), and a backlog that never clears for four hours, which only saturating stuffing produces (§12), is a stall too. fiClearTip is inbox storage the guest receives in the journal and nodes read from the last ForcedInclusionsConsumed event. T is always the timestamp of an L1 block whose hash is pinned: at preconfirmation time the parent block's anchor, in the guest the same anchored headers it hash-chains to the tip, at the inbox the landing's verified anchor tip. Entries are FIFO with non-decreasing save times, so due entries form a prefix; under a stall every entry due at the cut is void, otherwise only expired blob entries are. No finality assumption is needed: every entry counted at an anchor sits in an ancestor of that anchor; a reorg that removes the entry removes the anchor, which already invalidates the block. proven

3. FI block content (F3)

An FI block for entry i with parent p: parentHash = p.hash, timestamp = p.timestamp + 1, parentBeaconBlockRoot = p's and PH.anchorNumber = p's (the anchor never advances in an FI block), extraData kind = FI (3), coinbase = the parent's coinbase (includer-independent), gasLimit = FI_GAS_LIMIT, transactions = the decoded manifest's transactions in order with invalid ones skipped, as today (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). An undecodable payload and a void entry (expired or voided by a stall, §2) each yield the empty FI block, with the same header fields and no transactions; a void entry yields it without reading the payload. Term and view are the sequencer's (the seat follows from them), and the sequencer signs it like any block. Its canonical record in a landing (landing page §2) is headerCore ‖ seqSig, with the view change if it opens a view, and no txListBytes: the transactions are derived from the entry's payload witness, which hashes to the entry's dataHash (§5), so the record is about 250 bytes and no FI block adds a blob to any landing (round 6 fix pass). proven that content is includer-independent given the parent.

4. Placement rule V8 (F4; replaces V5(e))

Every block has a cursor, derived and never stored: after a landing, cursor = fiQueue.head; for block h with parent p, cursor(h) = cursor(p) + 1 if kind(h) ∈ {FI, FORCED}, else cursor(p), so nothing about it is stored on L2 (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147); the stall clock clearTip of §2 is derived alongside it, starting at fiClearTip (round 6, R6H-1). With T_p = L1[anchor(p)].timestamp:

  • FI block (kind FI): valid iff the entry at the parent's cursor is due at T_p, the anchor (parentBeaconBlockRoot and anchorNumber) equals the parent's, and the content is per F3; its cursor is the parent's + 1.
  • Sequencer block (kind NORMAL): valid iff no entry is due at the parent's cursor at T_p, or the MAX_FI_RUN = 2 blocks ending at p are all FI blocks. A sequencer block may advance the anchor freely; the obligation the new anchor creates binds from the next block.
  • Genesis child (kind GENESIS_CHILD): the first Etna block is a sequencer block exempt from the obligation, like the first block of a waived view below; its parent's anchor is the drain block recorded at drainedAt (Anchor page M3), and the obligation binds from its child (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147; round-6 verification).
  • First-block waiver (round 2, S3, for RESUME; extended in round 6, R6H-1): the first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change, or of a sentinel dead-mode view, may be a sequencer block even if an entry is due; the obligation binds from its second block. For RESUME (the view that follows a forced or replacement landing) it lets the re-included transactions of the voided range take the state before any forced transaction saved after them; for REPLACE and dead mode it lets a fork from lastLanded advance its anchor past fiClearTip + FI_SKIP before the FI obligation binds, so the blocks after it void a poison prefix (§2) instead of carrying it. The waiver is keyed on the opening object, so it is used at most once per opening, and an honest entry waits at most one block (one second) per opening: the obligation binds again from the second block, and openings are scarce and gated, since a REPLACE needs A1 (35 to 65 minutes without a landing) and Q lock votes, a RESUME follows a landed forced or replacement batch, and a dead-mode opening object changes once per term and only after 75 terms without a landing. L8's count at the inbox (§5) is unchanged, so the 112-minute bound does not move.

Consequences: an entry becoming due while blocks are in flight is a non-event for them (their parents' anchors are older) and binds the first block after the anchor advances past its due time. Nothing is ever reorged by an FI. A backlog drains at two blocks in three while the sequencer keeps one block in three. The obligation crosses term and takeover boundaries with the cursor. FI blocks are exempt from the per-term byte bound V10 (round 4, R4H-6): V10 sums sequencer blocks only, in the guest and at attestation, so a blob-shaped backlog can never exhaust a term's budget, a sequencer block is never made V8-invalid by an exhausted budget, and a MISS can never follow from one; the 40 FI blocks a term can hold are bounded by MAX_FI_PER_LANDING, and they earn no lander reward of their own: their records are about 250 bytes and carry no payload, the entry's own blob or calldata being the payload witness (§3; round 6 fix pass), so they add no blob to a landing and earn no R_BLOB, the per-block ramp counts them among the term's sixty blocks inside 60 × R_BLK_MAX, and the per-term reward exposure is unchanged. proven

5. Inbox check at landing (F5; the landing page's L8)

  1. fiHeadBefore == fiQueue.head; fiHeadAfter ≤ tail; consumed count c ≤ MAX_FI_PER_LANDING = 64.
  2. T_floor = max(lastLanded.anchorTipTimestamp, termStart(termId) − ANCHOR_MAX_AGE − 1), except that a void-by-stall landing, one whose first FI block is evaluated at or after the cut so that the journal marks its first consumed entry void by stall, uses min(that, fiClearTip + FI_SKIP), capping it at the cut (round 6, R6H-1); D = the number of entries due at T_floor (walking at most 64); require c ≥ min(D, ⌈2n/3⌉) for a range of n blocks.
  3. Every consumed entry is due at the landing's anchor tip timestamp.
  4. fiConsumedHash = keccak(chainId ‖ head ‖ c ‖ (i, kind, dataHash, savedAt) for each consumed entry) from storage, exported to the journal; it reads only entries, which never change once saved. For the first entry the range does not consume, the journal exports fiNotDueAt (X): the highest parent anchor time T_p at which a block of the range relied on no entry being due at a cursor equal to fiHeadAfter (V8 admitting a sequencer block, or the stall clock moving, §2), or the cut when the range claims a stall's void set used up with its cursor there, whichever is later, and zero if neither; the inbox requires the stored entry at fiHeadAfter, if any, to have savedAt + FI_DELAY > X, and entries have non-decreasing save times, so no later entry is due at X either (round 6, R6H-1). The schedule this covers: a third party may save an entry after the proof was made and before the landing, but that entry's save time is later than every anchor of the range, so it passes the check and no proven landing can be made to revert by saving an entry; a range that relied on an entry not being due when it was due fails the check, as it should.
  5. Fees of entries consumed with content or voided by expiry are credited, less each entry's surcharge FI_FEE × (4^e − 1) (§1), to the pull ledger of the range's sequencer (claimableEth, one slot per includer, withdrawn with withdrawFiFees); the bond and the surcharge stay with the entry for its requester, who withdraws them per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id) once the landing has written the entry's status, consumed with content, into the two status bits of the kind byte it already read, a warm modify of an existing word (round 6 fix pass). Entries the journal marks void by stall (fiStallMask, bit j for entry fiHeadBefore + j; round 6, R6H-1) are written voided and refundable, bond and whole fee withdrawable by the requester the same way, except the first entry each stall voids: when the journal's fiStallClock (the clearTip under which the marked entries were voided, constant within a stall) differs from burnedFor, the stall clock last burned, the bond and whole fee, surcharge included, of the first marked entry, the entry at the stored head whenever the stall stopped every landing at it, are burned (its status written burned, so nothing is withdrawable), burnedFor is set to that stall clock, and the stall is counted: runCount becomes the escalation level plus one and runLastAt the block time (§1). A stall is therefore burned and counted exactly once, however a lander splits its voids into landings. A legacy Shasta entry has no requester and no bond: voided by a stall, its fee is burned if it is the first marked entry and otherwise credited to the includer, as on expiry; a requeued entry's fee is zero, so nothing is burned or credited for it (Anchor page M2; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).
  6. fiQueue.head = fiHeadAfter; fiClearTip = fiClearTipAfter, the end block's clearTip from the journal, whose reliance on the entry at fiHeadAfter not being due is the check of step 4; lastLanded.anchorTipTimestamp updated.

Why step 2 is exact and satisfiable: every block in the range has a timestamp at or after its term's start (V4), its parent's timestamp is at most one second less, and V5 bounds the parent's anchor to within ANCHOR_MAX_AGE of it; anchors are monotone, so every parent anchor in the range is at least T_floor. Entries due at T_floor are due at every block of the range, and V8 forces at least ⌈2n/3⌉ FI blocks in any valid stretch that starts on an FI block. An entry voided by a stall counts as consumed, so a range that voids a poison prefix meets the count exactly as one that includes it, and a range whose first block is waived (§4) meets it by ending after the FI blocks that follow, as a range that starts after a full FI run already must. The cap at the cut keeps a void-by-stall landing satisfiable (round 6, R6H-1): without it, T_floor at the start of a REPLACE view's term can lie past the cut, so D would also count an entry due after the cut, which the void set does not contain and which, if unprovable, no range can include; with it, D counts only the void set, and the range [the waived first block, the empty FI blocks of the void set] has c = D (64 at most) and meets c ≥ min(D, ⌈2n/3⌉). The schedule this covers: an attacker that saves an unprovable entry just after the void-set boundary, due one second after the cut, cannot block the void landing; that entry is the next landing's obligation and, being unprovable, the next stall, saved during this one and so priced with it in progress (§1). The cap applies only where the guest has proved that the first FI block voids at or after the cut, and V8 still binds every block of the range (step 4 at its end), so no landing can use it to skip an entry that was due. No third party can move the outcome: T_floor is storage plus the chain's own term id, the counted entries were saved at least FI_DELAY before it, the head moves only by a landing on this parent, fees are pull, and the walk is bounded. proven

Guest. From the consumed-hash preimage and the anchored headers it already verifies, the guest checks F3 and F4 for every block of the range with the cursor starting at fiHeadBefore and the stall clock at fiClearTip (both from inbox storage through the journal): each FI block's payload witness hashes to its dataHash (KZG versioned hash for blobs, keccak for calldata; void entries need no witness), in a block whose parent's anchor has reached the parent's cut, every entry due at that cut is voided and marked in fiStallMask, the parent's clearTip being exported as fiStallClock (round 6, R6H-1), each sequencer block satisfies V8 (against the consumed entries' save times while the cursor is below fiHeadAfter), and the journal's queue fields (head before and after, consumed hash, clear time before and after, stall mask, stall clock and fiNotDueAt) equal the recomputed values. Once the cursor reaches fiHeadAfter the guest takes as input whether the entry there is due: every "not due" it relies on enters fiNotDueAt, which the inbox checks against storage (step 4), while a "due" can only end the range there or hold the clock until the next block that V8 admits because nothing is due. A sequencer that omits, reorders or alters an FI's transactions produces an end hash different from the certified one or a journal the inbox rejects: unlandable either way.

6. The forced batch (F7)

A landing through the same land with the sentinel view FORCED = 254, sequencer address zero, empty certificate, no view changes, no blobs. Inbox checks, cheapest first: (1) block.timestamp > replaceableFrom(), the landing page's replacement predicate: the sequencer pipeline is past its deadline machinery, so escalation needs no separate constant; (2) the range starts at lastLanded; (3) the lander-chosen anchor A is verified by blockhash or EIP-2935 and T_A read from it; (4) m = min(due entries at T_A, 64) ≥ 1 and the batch has exactly m blocks, each derived from one due entry per F3, with kind FORCED and view 254; every block is evaluated at T_A, so once T_A ≥ fiClearTip + FI_SKIP every entry due at that cut is void by stall and the rest of the m are included with content (the batch builds on lastLanded, whose clearTip is fiClearTip; round 6, R6H-1); a forced batch therefore ends a stall only when T_A lies in [cut, the due time of the first unprovable entry saved after cut − FI_DELAY), with no first-block waiver needed, and an attacker can shrink that window to the single L1 block at the cut, or to none, by saving such an entry just after the void-set boundary, so the ordinary void landing of §5, whose T_floor is capped at the cut, is the dependable path out of a stall; (5) the consumed hash, the stall mask, the stall clock and fiNotDueAt as above; (6) the journal is domain-separated as a forced journal with rewardTo bound in, and verified with ZK_K = 2 always (no certificate, so single-proof mode never applies: forced-batch safety rests on ZK alone; a forced batch landed on a chain that holds an unfinalized provisional record inherits that record's status, writes no checkpoint of its own, is subsumed by the one written when the record is finalized, and is voided with it by a conflict, landing page §7); (7) effects: lastLanded height, hashes, roots and anchor tip updated, but lastLanded.at and lastLandedTerm untouched (the pipeline is still unlanded: forced batches may chain, dead mode still counts, and any ordinary landing closes the window); a forced-ring record; the head advanced; fiClearTip set by the rule of §2 at the batch's last block (T_A if no entry beyond the m consumed is due at T_A; the cut if the void set is used up; else unchanged, the next landing continuing the void set; round 6 fix pass); fees of entries consumed with content or voided by expiry to rewardTo's pull-ledger slot, less each entry's surcharge FI_FEE × (4e − 1) (§1); each consumed entry's status written into its kind byte as in §5, its requester withdrawing bond and surcharge per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id); void-by-stall entries refunded or burned as in §5; the checkpoint written.

Content is a pure function of (lastLanded, A): block j derives from entry head + j, with ts_0 = max(parent.timestamp + 1, T_A + ANCHOR_MIN_AGE) and ts_j = ts_0 + j, block 0's parentBeaconBlockRoot the hash of A and the rest inheriting it, kind FORCED in every block's extraData (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147), coinbase = address(0), no sequencer signature (the guest derives the PH-equivalent fields as before and skips V1, V2 and V4's upper bound for view 254). Anyone builds it from the queue and the payloads; the requester holds them by construction. Binding rewardTo into the forced journal means a mempool copy of the proof cannot redirect the fees. No rights check, no certificate, no absence penalty; but a forced batch is refused while a bonded landing announcement, or an accepted REPLACE or RESUME record (landing page A3), covers the head. The two share one deferral per range (round 5, R5-C3): a single flag keyed on lastLanded, spent by whichever comes first, defers replaceableFrom by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE once, and the other buys nothing further (landing page A1: announceLanding locks ANNOUNCE_BOND = 2,000 TAIKO, and the bond is forfeited, 40 % to the eventual forced or replacement lander and 60 % burned, if no covering landing follows; an announcement is refused once the head entry has been due at the T_floor of the first unlanded term for longer than LAND_WINDOW, round 3 C3), so a landable backlog has priority over the forced path for exactly one grace period (round 2, S3) and the bound of §9 is 112 minutes as a rule. proven unique and constructible whenever armed.

7. In-flight batches, resumption, races (F8)

A forced landing voids exactly what a replacement voids, and only when replacement is already permitted: the certified blocks above lastLanded whose term passed the deadline machinery without landing. The current holder, or a dead-mode sequencer, resumes on the forced head with a RESUME view change and re-includes voided transactions. The RESUME reference has one definition, on the certificate page (normative for message formats): the l1Ref of a RESUME lock vote is the (number, hash) of the L1 block that contains the forced or replacement landing being resumed from, the same Replace {l1RefNumber, l1RefHash} field a REPLACE vote signs, checked by S3d against blockhash or EIP-2935 and against the forced ring or lastLanded (round 5, R5S-3; this page defers). Races: a late original, a REPLACE fork, a dead-mode landing and a forced batch all target lastLanded; whichever lands first wins, the loser is invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape, else about 25k gas with no blob fee on the forced side. Nobody is penalized by losing such a race: round 4 removed the abandonment penalty and the waiver machinery that exempted resumers from it (R4H-5, R4-C3), because a certified view is landable by anyone from the holder's reserve and no rule reads the absence of a landing. The forced ring survives as the record (first height, L1 block number and time of each forced landing) against which S3d checks a RESUME reference.

8. Fees, blob expiry, FOCIL (F6, F9, F10)

  • Fees: ETH, pull-only. Ordinary landing: to the sequencer of the range holding the FI block (it surrendered a one-second slot and the lander is already paid per block in TAIKO). Forced landing: to rewardTo. In both cases the includer receives the fee less the surcharge FI_FEE × (4e − 1), that is the unescalated fee plus whatever the requester paid above the required price, its bounty undiluted (round 6 fix pass); the only refunds are the requester's (its bond, the escalation surcharge, and bond and fee of an entry a stall voids behind the first; §1, round 6, R6H-1), each withdrawn per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id). No split and no push: the includer pulls its credit from its one ledger slot and a requester pulls each refund from its entry's own record.
  • Proving budget: FI blocks execute under the same zk-gas metering every Etna block inherits from Unzen (a per-block zk-gas limit the execution client enforces at build and import), and additionally under FI_ZK_GAS_LIMIT, a lower per-FI-block budget the guest checks, so the proving cost of an FI block is bounded by construction; a guest completeness bug inside that budget is the residual the stall rule (FI_SKIP, §2) covers.
  • Blob expiry: nodes and provers fetch the payload on the save event and retain it until consumed. Calldata entries never expire (only a stall voids them, §2). A blob entry consumed at anchor a satisfies savedAt + FI_EXPIRY > T(a); its landing follows within ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE, so the proof is generated within savedAt + 7 d + 95 min, inside retention even if retention halves at short slots assumed. An entry can reach FI_EXPIRY only if no landing of two or more blocks happened for seven days; then it derives to the empty FI block (F3), its fee less the surcharge goes to the includer and its bond and surcharge to the requester, per entry by the formula of §1, a rule that takes precedence over a stall for an expired entry (round 6, R6H-1). No owner call replaces today's queue reset.
  • FOCIL (Hegotá): protected legs are calldata requests, the blob-free forced landing (≈ 3 KiB, ≈ 1.2 M gas), slashing evidence and fee withdrawals; not protected are blob-carrying requests and every ordinary landing. A user who fears L1-builder censorship uses the calldata shape, and then the whole escalation leg is inclusion-list eligible. Before Hegotá the floor is T1 and FI_DELAY absorbs ordinary inclusion latency.
  • Attester policy (recommended, not validity): while a node's own L1 view holds a due, unconsumed entry, it withholds attestation from a sequencer block whose anchor timestamp is more than FI_ANCHOR_LAG = 288 s older than its own L1 head (ANCHOR_MIN_AGE + 240 s; stated in seconds so the policy is slot-independent: as 20 L1 blocks it fell below the 48-s minimum anchor age at 2-s slots and withheld from every honest block while an entry was due; round 5 fix pass). An honest attester that withholds under this policy holds no attested block of the view, so after TIMEOUT it signs a timeout (certificate page §1, round 5): the stale holder is taken over within seconds and pays MISS, unless it first gets one block attested by part of the committee and then stops (certificate page §1(d)), in which case its view runs to term end without MISS and the due entry waits one term; either way the next holder inherits the cursor. This turns the 30-minute legal staleness into a lost view within seconds for a single stale sequencer; the hard bound below does not rely on it.

9. Latency bounds and worked timeline

Honest: an entry saved at s is due at every L1 block with timestamp ≥ s + 300; the first such block is anchored by an L2 block at ≥ s + 300 + 48, the FI block follows one second later and is attested within the round trip: attested ≈ s + 6 min, landed ≈ 2 to 3 min later. Compliant adversary (stale anchors, deadline landings, colluding quorum): the first term starting at or after s + FI_DELAY + ANCHOR_MAX_AGE has T_floor ≥ s + FI_DELAY for every landing of its blocks, so its landings must consume the entry, and they must land by its end + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + REPLACE_GRACE, else the forced path opens: landed ≤ s + 300 + 1800 + 120 + 3600 + 300 + 600 = s + 6,720 s (112 min, the last 600 s being the one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE deferral per range that a landing announcement and an accepted REPLACE record share; round 5, R5-C3), or the forced path plus proving and inclusion (≈ 4 min). Absent pipeline: replacement opens about TERM + LAND_WINDOW + REPLACE_GRACE after the last landing, so a forced batch lands about 40 minutes after the stall began. Throughput floor: two entries per three seconds under any chain that lands at all. proven under T1, T3 and one honest prover-lander, for every entry that is not due at a stall's cut (§2), which absent a guest-completeness bug needs saturating stuffing for four hours (§12); under a poison stall, honest entries due at the stall's cut are voided and refunded, and the bound applies to their re-post (round 6, R6H-1).

12. Attacks defended and not defended

  • TH7 starvation by a censoring sequencer with honest attesters: defended. Blocks that skip a due entry are invalid (V8), so no honest attester attests them; with nothing attested and no certificate for TIMEOUT, the honest committee times the censor out (certificate page §1, round 5), the next view's holder sees the same cursor and must include the entry, and the censor pays MISS, unless it first gets one valid block attested by part of the committee and then stops (certificate page §1(d)), which keeps its view to term end without MISS and delays the entry by one term. A censor that goes silent, withholds payloads or gossips bodiless headers is timed out the same way. The 112-minute bound holds in every case.
  • TH7 starvation with a colluding quorum: bounded (112 min). Skipping blocks are unlandable (the T_floor backstop); stale anchoring is legal for 30 minutes; deadline landings add the hard window. Then the forced path. A certified-and-skipped chain earns its holder nothing and is replaced; no penalty attaches (no rule reads a missing landing).
  • TH7 starvation by all sequencers or an empty registry: defended. Nothing lands; replacement opens about 40 minutes after the last landing; anyone with the payload and a prover lands a forced batch; forced batches chain until a sequencer resumes.
  • TH7 / TH11 stuffing to burn a lander's slot: defended. Every lever inventoried: the due count (a function of storage and the chain's own term id), the consumed-entry checks (chain content), the walk (bounded), the payout (pull), void (a function of anchored time and of the stall clock, which is derived from the chain), expiry (no drop function), the forced gate (monotone until an ordinary landing). Nothing works.
  • TH7 stuffing for delay: bounded, priced. N entries ahead of a victim delay it about 1.5 N seconds and cost 0.001 × (N + N(N−1)/100) ETH plus about 196k state gas per entry: one hour of delay ≈ 60 ETH in fees plus L1 gas; six hours ≈ 2,100 ETH. Not defended: a quadratic price buys a bounded delay, and an attacker buying most of L1's calldata gas can keep two of every three L2 blocks an FI block for a few ETH per hour. Stuffing that keeps the due prefix from ever clearing for four hours is a stall (§2; round 6, R6H-1): it needs at least the drain rate, about 2,400 entries an hour without a gap, 9,600 over the four hours, each paying the quadratic fee with at least about 200 entries pending (the FI_DELAY window alone at that rate), so at least 0.005 ETH each and about 50 ETH in all, plus their state gas; the in-progress quadrupling is a deposit these entries get back once consumed with content (§1). It then voids only the entries due at the cut that are still unconsumed, refunded except the first, whose bond and fee are burned, after which the clock restarts at the cut; entries posted to hold the stall open are served with content and pay their unescalated fees, so another stall needs another four hours of stuffing, and its first void is priced as the next stall of the run.
  • TH7 blob expiry: defended. After a week-long outage the head entry voids to an empty block; the requester re-posts, as calldata if it wants permanence.
  • TH5 withholding to postpone an FI: defended. No seal to withhold; a withheld chain does not land; replacement then opens the forced path, which needs no certificate.
  • TH8 bond griefing through FIs: defended. No FI-specific slashing rule, no penalty reads a missing landing, and FI blocks are outside V10, so a backlog can never cause a MISS (round 4, R4H-6).
  • TH1 forged forced batch: bounded as every batch. Two unsound systems needed; no single-proof mode.
  • Unprovable entries (round 1, F4; round 3, H1; round 6, R6H-1): bounded per stall, geometrically priced within a week, one cheap stall per week beyond. A decodable entry that no guest can prove within the FI zk-gas budget stalls every landing path (ordinary, replacement, forced and dead mode all must consume it) until the stall clock is four hours old; then the blocks that follow the first block of a REPLACE, RESUME or dead-mode view, which may advance the anchor past fiClearTip + FI_SKIP (§4), void every entry due at the stall's cut, the poison and whatever was pre-posted behind it (64 per landing, the next landings continuing until the void set is used up), and that void landing is always satisfiable because its T_floor is capped at the cut (§5); a forced batch anchored at or past the cut does the same only before the first unprovable entry outside the void set falls due, a window the attacker can shrink to one L1 block or none (§6). The schedule covered: before the trigger the attacker can post any number of entries at the unescalated price, but each one due at the cut is voided with the poison and refunded, so a pre-posted stream of N entries costs one FI_SKIP plus ⌈N/64⌉ landings, not N stalls; an unprovable entry saved to fall due just after the cut does not block the void landing but becomes the next stall; the only entry that can seed the next stall is one saved after the cut minus FI_DELAY, which the inbox prices with the stall in progress or already counted, and the level falls only by one per ESCALATION_DECAY since the last stall. With stalls less than a week apart, back to back or spaced, the k-th therefore costs its poster at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4^(k−1) ETH (its seed's bond and whole fee, the surcharge included, burned) whatever it pre-posts: six stalls (a day back to back) about 1.7 ETH, nine (36 h) about 88 ETH, twelve (two days) about 5,600 ETH; one stall a day buys nothing more, since each stall restarts the decay clock, the fourteenth costing at least 0.001 × 413 ≈ 67,000 ETH; so within such a run a budget buys about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls of about four hours each, as a rule and not by a DAO action. Spacing stalls ESCALATION_DECAY or more apart returns each to the floor price, and each level of decay needs a week without a stall, so over any period of T days a budget buys at most about T / 7 + 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls: the long-run rate a small budget buys is one four-hour stall per week at about 0.05 ETH each, until the DAO ships a guest fix. Each stall also voids the certified blocks above lastLanded (a locked-block reorg per stall), and honest entries due at the stall's cut are voided, refunded and must re-post (§2); the DAO's guest fix is the cheaper remedy in practice but never a prerequisite for liveness. Listed on the limitations page (L16).
  • Not defended: sandwiching an FI block (ordinary MEV; the payload is public five minutes ahead); L1-level censorship of the blob-shape request beyond T1 (use calldata).

13. Failure analysis

  • Requesters. All offline: empty queue. All malicious: stuffing, bounded and priced; unprovable entries, bounded per stall, geometrically priced within a week and one cheap stall per week beyond (§12); undecodable manifests derive to empty blocks; no landing can be made to revert; the worst a requester loses is its own bond and fee, burned only when its entry is the first a stall voids, and an honest requester fronts the escalation surcharge as a deposit it gets back (§1).
  • Sequencers. Holder offline: takeover; the backup inherits the cursor. All offline or malicious past the replacement predicate: forced batches serve the queue without any sequencing right; dead mode serves ordinary traffic; the first landing wins. They cannot skip a due entry (V8 at attesters, the count at the inbox, V8 in the guest), alter its content, or profit from a stall.
  • Attesters. Fewer than Q live: no certificates, no ordinary landings; forced batches carry no certificate, so FIs keep landing after the predicate holds. All malicious: they can certify skipping or stale chains; skipping chains are unlandable, staleness is bounded; the 112-minute bound holds.
  • Provers and landers. All offline: certified FI blocks are not landed and the forced path also needs a prover, so P5 is conditional on one honest funded participant; the requester, holding the payload and facing at most 64 single-purpose blocks, can be that participant. After seven days blob entries expire; calldata entries never expire (only a stall voids them, §2).
  • L1 builders. Censoring the calldata request or the forced landing: FOCIL after Hegotá, else T1.
  • DAO. Never needed: no queue reset, no parameter on the liveness path; entries no guest can prove are voided with every entry due at the stall's cut once the stall clock is four hours old, instead of by an upgrade, and each further stall within a week of the last is priced geometrically at save time (round 6, R6H-1), so the halt is bounded by about four hours per stall, the number of stalls less than a week apart by about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH), and the long-run rate by one stall per ESCALATION_DECAY at the floor price (a DAO guest fix ends it sooner but is never a prerequisite).

15. Robustness

6/4/2-s slots: every constant is seconds, wei, gas or a count; the attester-policy lag is in seconds too (FI_ANCHOR_LAG = 288 s at every slot time, §8); the forced landing (no blobs) fits a 10 M-gas block; blob-shape requests compete for 3 blobs per block at 2 s but the per-second rate is unchanged; EIP-2935 covers the forced batch's anchor lookup far beyond the replacement predicate. Without Frame Transactions: no property depends on EIP-8141; the forced loser burns no blob fee. ePBS Empty slots: a save or landing in an unrevealed payload is not one; the lander resubmits. L1 EIP-4788: L1's beacon-roots contract is never read; L2's own EIP-4788 contract records each L2 header's anchor hash (an L1 execution block hash) and plays no part in forced inclusion (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). Without FOCIL: all L1 legs rely on builder competition and T1; no design change.

16. Claims

  1. proven No third party can make a landing revert through the FI queue: the consumed-entry hash reads only entries, which never change once saved, and the first unconsumed entry is checked by an inequality that an entry saved after the proof always passes (§5; round 6, R6H-1).
  2. proven The inbox count is satisfied by every V8-conforming chain and cannot be satisfied by a chain that skips an entry due at T_floor early in the range.
  3. proven (under T1, T3, one honest prover-lander) An entry saved at s is in an L1-landed block by about s + 112 min (102 plus the one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE per range that a bonded landing announcement or an accepted REPLACE record, whichever comes first, adds; round 5, R5-C3) against any sequencer and attester behaviour, plus proving and inclusion, unless it is due at a stall's cut (§2), which absent a guest-completeness bug needs four hours of saturating stuffing; an entry due at a stall's cut is voided and refunded, and the bound applies to its re-post (round 6, R6H-1).
  4. proven An FI becoming due never invalidates an in-flight block.
  5. proven The forced batch is unique and constructible from L1 data whenever armed.
  6. proven A forced batch voids only what a replacement voids and only when a replacement is permitted.
  7. proven FI block content is includer-independent given the parent.
  8. proven A void entry never requires its blob; a consumed blob entry's proof is generated within savedAt + 7 d + 95 min.
  9. proven A stall voids entries only once no block has caught up with its due entries for four hours of anchored time (the stall clock, derived per block, so no split of ranges into landings changes it); a chain that keeps consuming its due entries keeps resetting it, so a live chain voids nothing unless its backlog never clears for four hours, and the void set is fixed at the stall's cut, so entries posted to hold a stall open are served with content, not voided. A pre-posted stream of N unprovable entries ends after one FI_SKIP plus ⌈N/64⌉ landings, and the void landing is always satisfiable, its T_floor being capped at the cut. With stalls less than ESCALATION_DECAY apart the k-th costs its poster at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4k−1 ETH whatever it pre-posts, so such a run lasts about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls of about four hours, and beyond it the rate is at most one stall at the floor price per ESCALATION_DECAY; every entry but a stall's first void gets the escalation surcharge back, so no honest request pays it (round 6, R6H-1).
  10. assumed T1: L1 includes a 1.2 M-gas, 3 KiB, blob-free transaction within minutes; FOCIL strengthens this after Hegotá.
  11. assumed Blob retention ≥ 9 days at every slot time.
  12. assumed The stuffing arithmetic with today's fee constants.
  13. open Guest cost of recomputing up to 64 KZG versioned hashes per landing; a raiko benchmark resolves it (a lower forced-batch cap does not change claim 3).
  14. open Whether third-party forced landers appear at 0.001 ETH against about 0.012 ETH of L1 gas, or the requester is always the lander; the bounty overpayment (undiluted since the round 6 fix pass: the includer keeps everything above the required price) and the 40 % share of a forfeited announcement bond are the two levers.

17. Alternatives rejected

  • Due-ness at the landing's timestamp (today; the claim-36 lever); a seal's end timestamp or the term start as the clock (assume one batch per term); FI transactions inside sequencer blocks (breaks the one-block shape and gas accounting); per-anchor MPT proofs of the queue in the guest; trickle detection with a second clock; a separate escalation constant (any value races or wastes); an FI-specific missed-inclusion penalty (it would infer fault from a missing landing, which no rule does since round 4); fee splits; drop-and-refund of expired entries (a third-party head mover); forbidding early inclusion for byte identity (moot under the anchor clock); a 900-s delay justified by finality (unnecessary: the counted entries share the anchor's chain); anchor freshness as a validity rule (validity would depend on each attester's L1 head); a forced batch carrying its own blobs (loses FOCIL eligibility); a bonded FI-lander role; a bounded ring queue; requester bonds beyond the refundable FI_BOND; a blob-only request shape; scaling the caps with queue depth; the Anchor as coinbase for forced blocks; keeping 576 s; a skip of the stored head entry only, priced by skips counted after the fact over a sliding 24 h (R6H-1: entries pre-posted before the first skip paid the unescalated price, and two of them due together left no landable range; a sliding count would also let spaced stalls decay to a fixed price per stall); a bond escalating with queue depth such as FI_BOND × 4pending/8 (it prices an honest burst as an attack, while the stall-in-progress term prices exactly the entries that can seed a stall); an escalation run that resets after a day without a stall (a stall every 28 hours returned to the floor price; the level now falls one step per ESCALATION_DECAY); the escalation kept as a fee (it charged every honest request after an attack; it is now a deposit refunded to every entry but a stall's first void); the save time of the first unconsumed entry in the consumed-entry hash (an entry saved after the proof changed it and made the proven landing revert; replaced by the fiNotDueAt inequality, §5); a per-payer cap (Sybil-able); a provability witness at save time (it narrows the precondition but removes neither need); a proportional split of the paid fee between includer and requester, feeWei / 4^e to the includer (it gave the includer only a 4−e share of an overpayment; the surcharge is now exactly FI_FEE × (4e − 1) and the bounty undiluted, round 6 fix pass); a per-requester refund ledger (a landing consuming 64 entries of 64 fresh requesters would have written 64 fresh slots, ≈ 6.3 M state gas; refunds are now per entry, two status bits in the entry's own word, round 6 fix pass); FI-block records carrying their transactions (a record of up to one blob per FI block; the entry's own blob or calldata is the payload witness, so a record is about 250 bytes and adds no blob, round 6 fix pass).

18. Open questions

  1. MAX_FI_RUN = 2 versus 1: two-thirds drain versus half; trades stuffing-drain speed against ordinary users' share under saturation.
  2. Fee to the range's sequencer adds a guest-verified FI count per range. Acceptable, or should fees go to rewardTo, accepting that FI blocks become a pure cost to the role that must include them?
  3. Binding rewardTo into the forced journal departs from fungible proofs. Keep, or accept mempool front-running of forced-batch bounties?
  4. Adopt the attester anchor-lag policy, or rely on ANCHOR_MAX_AGE alone and accept the 112-minute bound as the honest-path worst case under one stale sequencer?
  5. FI_DELAY = 300 s: confirm against measured blob-fetch latency on a devnet with sparse blob pools.
  6. Resolved in round 6 (R6H-1): whether SKIP_ESCALATION should price honest requests. It no longer does: the escalated part of the fee is refunded to every entry except a stall's first void, so each stall's seed is priced as before and no honest request pays it (§1); what remains for an honest requester is the deposit it fronts while the level is above zero.
