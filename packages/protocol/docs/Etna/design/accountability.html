Accountability and data availability

Draft for adversarial review. Objective service defaults are punishable; historical private withholding is not thereby proved. This page does not establish that R6 passes.

What the labels mean

Assumed — A-IMPLEMENTATION: the interface and state-transition rules below are the normative design to implement, not deployed behavior. Every unlabeled table cell and interface declaration is covered by this assumption. Proven marks a logical consequence of those exact rules; Assumed marks a required environmental or implementation condition; Open marks an unresolved measurement or guarantee. Safety also assumes A-CRYPTO, A-L1 and A-UPGRADE from the threat model.

Proven — limited claim: a signature plus a missed on-chain deadline establishes breach of a voluntarily accepted service obligation. It does not establish whether the signer withheld P2P data intentionally, whether every peer received a broadcast, or which provisional branch deserves canonical priority. Availability certificates remain advisory; they cannot publish a SignalService checkpoint or displace an accepted L1 head.

Finite obligations backed by finite collateral

Assumed — normative registry: a bucket is registered on L1 by its collateral owner before clients accept its signatures. It contains an immutable owner, ECDSA signing key, role kind, protocol revision, canonical base-head digest, registration time and 60 numbered duty positions. Opening a bucket transfers exactly 6,000 TAIKO from that owner's free balance into 60 separate reserves of 100 TAIKO. A position is an obligation identifier, not a CL slot or a distinct human. Bodies larger than 32,768 bytes use separate positions for separately committed fragments; a client must check every fragment and the enclosing ordered commitment before accepting a complete block.

Proven — scope: a signed time is not proof of when a signature was created or first broadcast. “Preexisting reserve” means the client's authenticated L1 view contains the bucket and unspent reserve before the client accepts the bonded promise. A signature produced later with an in-range claimed time is still accountable against that reserve; a client must not confuse it with evidence of timely gossip. Clients need a recent authenticated registry view and must stop treating a reserve as available once spent or released. Reorg risk follows the L1 view they choose.

Assumed — complete admission gates: before accepting any challenge/evidence, the registry checks domain and canonical encoding, signature, registered role/key/revision/context, position range, data bounds and issuance interval; it recomputes subjectHash from revision, baseHeadHash and l2BlockNumber. Historical revision definitions remain available for evidence. Invalid input reverts before reserving any new state or moving value. An availability challenge additionally requires now<=retentionEnd, an unreleased/unspent reserve, no previous challenge for the position, and no prior global revelation of the exact data digest and length. A position cannot be reused for a second promise after discharge; unused capacity requires another position or bucket. A certificate with insufficient or already consumed reserve is unbonded and clients reject its claim of insurance.

The publication challenge

Assumed — normative challenge: anyone presents the complete signed DutyClaim to challengeAvailability, with msg.value>=Q=2×4,000,000×(block.basefee+2 gwei) wei, computed at inclusion. Exactly Q is locked; any excess becomes an immediate pull credit to the opener. Underfunding reverts before allocation. This permits a capped prepaid quote when basefee changes between signing and inclusion. The included call creates one immutable challenge with opener, admitted claim hash, openedAt, deadline=openedAt+600, and Q. Anyone can answer by publishing the complete canonical fragment bytes in calldata. A valid answer must arrive at now<=deadline, have the exact committed byteLength and keccak digest, and fit the published resource bound. The first successful answer records the data key and revelation time globally, credits all Q to the actual responder, and ends that challenge. There is no reserved right to answer.

Assumed — deadline boundary: response is allowed at exactly the deadline; default finalization requires now>deadline. After that point, finalizeDefault is permissionless. It refunds Q to the stored opener and allocates the reserved TAIKO once: 10% to that opener's pull credit and 90% to an irrevocably nonwithdrawable sink ledger. A third party may finalize but cannot replace the opener or steal the report reward. L1 reorgs revert the challenge, revelation and credits together; a receipt is not final until its L1 block is finalized.

Assumed — inclusive availability semantics: full calldata revelation permanently satisfies this digest's publication duty, including when a party other than the signer supplies it. The revealed bytes need not execute successfully to satisfy custody; validity is checked separately by clients and the canonical proof. A claim that promises an invalid execution is not turned into a valid block by revealing it. Public L1 data remains retrievable under A-ARCHIVE; permanent archive loss is not cured by a second paid challenge.

Many signers, one body, no duplicate reward

Assumed — bounded lazy accounting: define dataKey=keccak256(abi.encode(dataDigest,byteLength)). Each reserved position admits at most one challenge during its lifetime. Different signers or positions may have separate challenges for the same dataKey; each receives its own full 600-second deadline and supplies its own Q. There is only one global first-revelation timestamp. A responder selects one still-pending challenge and earns only that challenge's Q. Other challenges are settled individually, without iterating over signers: if the global revelation time is at or before their own deadline, refund their Q in full; otherwise they remain defaults. No additional response reward is created. Once data is revealed, new challenges for it are rejected. Hash collision resistance binds a shared revelation to the same bytes.

Proven — no late-notice slash: if signer B is challenged 590 seconds after signer A, B still has 600 seconds from B's own included notice. A response after A's deadline but within B's deadline does not cure A's objective breach; it satisfies B and earns B's Q. This avoids both an unbounded response-payment multiplication and slashing a newly notified signer after only 10 seconds. Exact duplicate attempts cannot extend a deadline, overwrite the reporter, tie up extra reserve or collect a second reward.

Assumed — races with equivocation: if a position is slashed by equivocation while its availability challenge is still pending, that challenge is canceled and its untouched Q is refunded to its opener. If a timely global revelation already settled it, its prior ETH outcome remains final. A previously slashed position cannot admit a new challenge or produce another TAIKO reward. First L1 order selects the first valid slash outcome; transaction ordering never increases total liability beyond the reserve.

Proven — liability bound: if an equivocator produces n conflicting signatures against one position, the registry pays at most 10 TAIKO total to reporters and moves at most 90 TAIKO to the sink. If two independently funded positions are slashed, the bound is 200 TAIKO total. No promise creates unbounded per-user compensation. Losses from MEV or a soft reorg may exceed the bond; these amounts are deterrence parameters, not insurance valuations.

Assumed — deliberately unslashable actions: submitting an invalid raw proof, declining an unaccepted proving offer, losing a canonical race, revealing a complete body only after privately withholding it, or behaving as an unbonded submitter is not an offense by itself. Ordinary invalid submissions pay Ethereum gas; invalid frame transactions may only impose off-chain builder simulation cost. An unsigned or never-included private action supplies no authenticated on-chain evidence to slash. This gap is a first-class limitation for R6.

Proof jobs: failure without an exclusive proving right

Assumed — exact job terms: a sponsor locks an immutable nonzero TAIKO bounty for taskHash, containing the exact old head and terminal execution output fields (excluding the constructed head hash), data commitment, origin, revision and immutable task bytes. An offer expires 600 seconds after creation; the sponsor may cancel only before acceptance. An accepting worker locks a separate 1,000 TAIKO reserve; acceptance fixes deadline=acceptedAt+300, worker address and expected proof-bound jobId. Acceptance requires the task's old head to equal the current canonical head and origin.timestamp+600 >= acceptedAt+300+120, plus the ordinary task encoding and data bounds. The worker executes/validates the immutable task before voluntarily accepting. This freshness guard prevents an already stale origin from making the agreed deadline impossible; it does not prove the sponsor's output is correct.

Assumed — historic acceptance record: the Inbox stores an immutable receipt for the first successful consumption of each canonical parent: parentConsumption[parentHash]=AcceptedReceipt. Receipts cannot be overwritten or pruned while any job depends on them. The task uses the exact noncircular field list in the core specification; it excludes payment/job assignment, constructed head hash, transaction fee envelope and sender; the proof includes job ID and beneficiary when claiming the job. Job settlement does not merely inspect today's head, because a later transition cannot erase a default that already occurred.

  1. Assumed — success: an exact task/job/worker receipt accepted at or before the deadline pays the locked bounty to the worker and releases its reserved bond. Anyone may submit that proof; copying it preserves its proof-bound beneficiary.
  2. Assumed — competing consumption: if any different action consumed that parent at or before the deadline, refund the sponsor's bounty and release the worker's bond. This includes another prover completing the same execution with different job/beneficiary fields. No worker is punished for an action that the canonical head made unavailable before its deadline. If an upgrade disables the task revision, the Inbox records the first immutable revisionRetirement[revision] timestamp atomically with activation; retirement at or before the job deadline likewise cancels and refunds unless the job already completed on time. A retirement after the deadline cannot erase an earlier default. This is an objective upgrade consequence, not an operational waiver or DAO adjudication.
  3. Assumed — default: after the deadline, if neither timely success, timely competing consumption nor timely revision retirement exists, slash the worker bond and refund the sponsor bounty. A competing consumption first accepted after the deadline does not retrospectively waive this miss. A late exact completion likewise earns no expired bounty.

Proven — no reservation deadlock: accepting a job does not reserve the parent or gate submitSegment; other provers retain the same admission rules. If every assigned worker disappears, a newly entering independent prover can still land a valid segment. If nobody can produce a valid proof, canonical progress stops safely under the acknowledged failure of A-EXEC. A malicious sponsor can offer an impossible output; a worker that knowingly accepts it bears an objectively impossible duty. Local execution before acceptance, not a privileged adjudicator, prevents that bait. Sponsor and worker collusion can self-slash but cannot create value.

Conservation, exit and attack economics

Assumed — accounting invariant: all TAIKO amounts use uint256 token-wei, 1018 per TAIKO. Separate ledgers record free deposits, position/job reserves, sponsor bounties, withdrawable credits and the permanent sink. Their sum never exceeds TAIKO held. Transfers into the ledger are credited by actual balance delta and must match the requested value; no fee-on-transfer or rebasing behavior is assumed for the existing TAIKO token. ETH challenges have a separate invariant: pending deposits plus withdrawable ETH credits never exceed held ETH. No ETH/TAIKO price conversion or oracle is required.

Assumed — release: an exit request prevents opening new duties with the exiting allocation, but not historical evidence. Unspent position collateral becomes releasable only at now>max(evidenceClose,latestAdmittedChallengeDeadline) and after any pending challenge is lazily settled. Evidence remains admissible at exactly evidenceClose. An accepted job's reserve remains locked until one of its three settlement outcomes. All outward transfers use pull credits and state-first accounting with a reentrancy guard. Failed token/ETH delivery leaves the credit claimable; it cannot revert a previously recorded penalty. Ownership or signing-key rotation cannot rewrite historic liabilities.

Proven — no self-report mint: let an actor control signer, reporter and responder. A successful self-challenge moves its own Q from deposit to responder credit and costs L1 gas. A self-default returns its own Q but loses 90% of the slashed TAIKO even after collecting the 10% report share. A self-equivocation has the same loss. Sponsor/worker/report identities do not change the proof-job equation. The sink is not withdrawable by an owner, reporter, worker or fee beneficiary; it is not described as a token burn because tokens physically remain held. A malicious upgrade could rewrite any accounting rule, which remains the explicit A-UPGRADE trust boundary.

Assumed — overflow and denial-of-service bounds: all addition/multiplication uses checked uint256 arithmetic; reject values before narrowing. Bounds are checked before signature recovery or allocation. No unbounded signer/challenge/job loop appears on a settlement path. One evidence call handles at most two positions, one answer at most 32,768 bytes, and one settlement at most one challenge or job. Counter exhaustion reverts rather than reusing an identifier. An address can open many buckets, but each costs real additional collateral and gas; a per-address cap is not represented as a Sybil defense.

Proven — worked example: at a 30-gwei basefee, Q is 8,000,000×32 gwei = 0.256 ETH. Alice challenges two signers of the same missing digest, spending 0.512 ETH in deposits plus gas. Bob publishes the bytes against the first challenge within both deadlines: Bob receives 0.256 ETH, Alice recovers the other 0.256 ETH, and both signers keep their TAIKO. With no answer, Alice recovers both deposits and receives 20 TAIKO; the two signers lose 200 TAIKO, of which 180 becomes permanently nonwithdrawable. Front-running the answer moves the response fee but still reveals the same bytes.

State machines and interface boundary

Position:
  Free balance → Reserved at bucket creation
  Reserved → Challenged by valid signed claim + Q
  Reserved or Challenged → Slashed by valid equivocation
  Challenged → Served by timely matching global revelation
  Challenged → Slashed by deadline default
  Reserved or Served → Released only after evidenceClose and settlement
  Slashed and Released are terminal; Served may still be slashed for equivocation.

Challenge:
  Absent → Pending → AnsweredAndPaid | SatisfiedElsewhereAndRefunded
                   → DefaultedAndRefunded | CanceledByPriorSlashAndRefunded
  Exact duplicate admission does not create a second challenge.

Proof job:
  Offered → Canceled or Expired (bounty refund)
          → Accepted → Completed (worker bounty, bond return)
                     → SupersededOnTime or RevisionRetiredOnTime (sponsor refund, bond return)
                     → Defaulted (sponsor refund, worker slash)

Assumed — interface sketches only: these declarations specify messages and observable transitions; production implementations are out of scope. Every mutating evidence method performs all gates before state effects. Registry ownership authorizes implementation upgrades only and supplies no override, manual slash, appeal, unpause or withdrawal-from-sink selector.

struct DutyClaim {
    uint256 bucketId;
    uint256 position;
    uint256 revision;
    bytes32 baseHeadHash;
    uint256 issuedAt;
    uint256 l2BlockNumber;
    bytes32 subjectHash;
    bytes32 blockHash;
    bytes32 manifestRoot;
    uint256 fragmentIndex;
    uint256 fragmentCount;
    bytes32 dataDigest;
    uint256 byteLength;
}
// EIP-712 type name, field names/order and widths are exactly as above.
// Domain: name="EtnaDuty", version="1", chainId, Registry address.

interface IEtnaAccountability {
    function challengeAvailability(DutyClaim calldata claim, bytes calldata signature)
        external payable returns (bytes32 challengeId);
    function respondAvailability(bytes32 challengeId, bytes calldata completeFragment) external;
    function settleAvailability(bytes32 challengeId) external;
    function finalizeDefault(bytes32 challengeId) external;
    function proveEquivocation(DutyClaim calldata a, bytes calldata signatureA,
        DutyClaim calldata b, bytes calldata signatureB) external;
    function settleProofJob(uint256 jobId) external;
    function releasePosition(uint256 bucketId, uint256 position) external;
    function withdrawCredit(address recipient, uint256 taikoAmount, uint256 ethAmount) external;

    event AvailabilityChallenged(bytes32 indexed challengeId, bytes32 indexed positionId,
        bytes32 indexed dataKey, address opener, uint256 deadline, uint256 ethDeposit);
    event DataRevealed(bytes32 indexed dataKey, bytes32 indexed challengeId,
        address responder, uint256 revealedAt, uint256 byteLength);
    event ChallengeSettled(bytes32 indexed challengeId, uint8 outcome,
        address creditedAccount, uint256 ethCredit);
    event PositionSlashed(bytes32 indexed positionId, uint8 offense,
        bytes32 evidenceHash, address reporter, uint256 reporterAmount, uint256 sinkAmount);
    event JobSlashed(uint256 indexed jobId, address reporter,
        uint256 reporterAmount, uint256 sinkAmount);
    event JobSettled(uint256 indexed jobId, uint8 outcome, address worker,
        uint256 bountyCredit, uint256 bondReleased);
    event PositionReleased(bytes32 indexed positionId, address owner, uint256 amount);
    event CreditWithdrawn(address indexed account, address recipient,
        uint256 taikoAmount, uint256 ethAmount);
}

// 3=Defaulted, 4=CanceledBySlash; offense 1=PositionEquivocation,
// 2=PublisherDoubleSign, 3=AvailabilityDefault, 4=ProofJobDefault.
// Job outcomes 1=Completed, 2=SupersededOnTime, 3=Defaulted,
// 4=RevisionRetiredOnTime. No-job canonical submissions bind jobId=0.

Detection rates, node behavior and the remaining gap

False positives and false negatives
Attack or conditionResult and exact limitation
Persistent refusal after an included, funded valid challengeProven: if no holder publishes matching bytes by the deadline, default is objectively detected with probability 1 in that L1 history. Detection still requires an observer to know the signed claim and choose to fund/report it.
Honest responder under bounded assumptionsAssumed: A-INCLUSION guarantees a conforming, adequately funded response is included within 120 seconds; responder notices and retrieves within the remaining 480 seconds, and gas/state resource limits permit the response. Under these premises the rule has zero false service defaults. This is a conditional argument, not a measured network rate.
Partition, permanent censorship, exhausted gas budget or unbounded basefee increaseProven counterexample: a completely honest signer may be unable to get its answer included. Worst-case false-positive rate for “malicious withholding” is 100%. Q is an incentive, not automatic ETH prepayment; the answering account needs upfront ETH and may be unwilling to subsidize excess gas.
Selective serving or reveal just before the deadlineProven: a withholder can answer once challenged and avoid slashing. False-negative rate for prior withholding is 100% in this strategy. There is no random-sampling claim or assumed independence that removes it.
Entire privately built branch first disclosed with its proof and dataProven: no attributable signed breach need exist before landing. Canonical L1 admission may accept it before a public competitor. Detection of malicious prior withholding is 0%; false negatives are 100%. Advisory certificates do not prevent the resulting soft reorg or wasted competing proof.
All observers collude, refuse to pay, or never obtain a signatureProven: no challenge is filed, so detection can be 0%. A stake count does not create an honest observer.
Equivocation with both signatures disclosed on timeProven under A-CRYPTO: fabricated accusations fail; authentic conflicting promises are deterministically punishable, subject to still-locked reserves. Hidden evidence disclosed only after evidenceClose misses punishment.

Assumed — honest-node response: when a body or parent is missing, label the block UNKNOWN, withhold any locally issued soft confirmation, request the bytes from multiple peers, and stop extending that unknown branch. If an authenticated, adequately reserved custody claim exists and its deadline permits, submit the paid challenge. Switch to a locally executable alternative provisional branch or wait for canonical derivation; never synthesize the missing parent. When a conflicting canonical proof lands, roll back the provisional branch, re-execute the accepted data and notify applications that depended on soft state. Neither a challenge nor a custodian vote freezes the canonical Inbox.

Open — strict brief: the combination of strict open L1 sequencing, minute-long private proving, no required public ordering commitment, and advisory preconfirmations leaves the historical withholding/private-fork attack intact. This page therefore makes no assertion that R6 or the brief's stronger withholding requirement is satisfied. To remove this exact gap, a design must restrict late competing branches through an enforceable public commitment/order rule, accept a separately justified canonical ordering mechanism, or explicitly relax that guarantee. Each alternative needs its own liveness and based-sequencing analysis; timeouts alone are not a proof.

What this mechanism does not build

Proven — rejected inference: stake-weighted or randomly sampled availability attestations do not by themselves prove historic P2P publication, honest membership, or a unique canonical branch. A private notarization service would add a permissioned trust boundary. Per-address caps do not prevent one actor buying many addresses. Paying the whole slash to a challenger would erase deterrence under self-reporting. Unbounded calldata recovery would make a response economically or mechanically impossible. Automatic punishment for a reverting proof would let an attacker impersonate someone else's failure without a signed accepted duty. These alternatives are excluded for those concrete reasons.
