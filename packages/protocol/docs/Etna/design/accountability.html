Accountability and data availability

Reviewed specification — mandatory fragment publication. Every bonded early fragment must be publicly posted with its exact ordered manifest within 240 seconds. Mandatory segment staging removes first-reveal-at-landing; owner-authorized conflicts remain separately punishable. Prior private computation and every soft reorg are not thereby punishable.

What the labels mean

Assumed — A-IMPLEMENTATION: the interface and state-transition rules below are the normative design to implement, not deployed behavior. Every unlabeled table cell and interface declaration is covered by this assumption. Proven marks a logical consequence of those exact rules; Assumed marks a required environmental or implementation condition; Open marks an unresolved measurement or guarantee. Safety also assumes A-CRYPTO, A-L1 and A-UPGRADE from the threat model.

Proven — limited claim: a signature plus a missed on-chain deadline establishes breach of a voluntarily accepted service obligation. It does not establish whether the signer withheld P2P data intentionally, whether every peer received a broadcast, or which provisional branch deserves canonical priority. Availability certificates remain advisory; they cannot publish a SignalService checkpoint or displace an accepted L1 head. The independent mandatory staging rule now requires the exact canonical data/context to have been public for 360 seconds before acceptance. Every version-2 DutyClaim already has an absolute fragment deadline. An optional signed sealed-publication covenant adds a separate whole-segment deadline; neither replaces the other.

Finite obligations backed by finite collateral

Assumed — normative registry: a bucket is registered on L1 by its collateral owner before clients accept its signatures. It contains an immutable owner, ECDSA signing key, role kind, protocol revision, canonical base-head digest, registration time, immutable claimDomainVersion=2 and 60 numbered duty positions. Opening a bucket transfers exactly 600 TAIKO from that owner's free balance into 60 separate reserves of 10 TAIKO. A position is an obligation identifier, not a CL slot or a distinct human. Bodies larger than 32,768 bytes use separate positions for separately committed fragments; a client must check every fragment and the enclosing ordered commitment before accepting a complete block.

A future migration with actual old obligations must retain their original domain/version and historic rules; it cannot retroactively slash weaker promises. New version-2 buckets reject version-1 signatures. The publication digest fixture must use the version-2 domain.

Mandatory actual publication for every early bonded fragment

Assumed — exact public object: publishFragment receives the full fragment-manifest preimage, a fragment index and the complete selected bytes. The manifest contains revision, L2 block number, block hash, recordLength, ordered uint32 lengths and ordered bytes32 hashes. Recompute FD and FR using exactly the codec, its fixed chain IDs/Inbox/Anchor/revision domain, and canonical ABI. Require 1…4 equal-length arrays, positive recordLength within the codec block-record cap, count=ceil(recordLength/32768), every intermediate length 32768, last length equal to recordLength-32768×(count-1) (1…32768), and lengths summing to recordLength. Require index<count; selected bytes have that exact length and keccak digest. Bounds precede allocation/hashing. No execution proof, signer approval, private witness or head reservation is needed to publish.

FR = keccak256(abi.encode(FD, uint48(number), bytes32(blockHash),
    uint32(recordLength), uint32[] lengths, bytes32[] hashes));
FRAGMENT_PUBLICATION_TAG = keccak256(bytes("ETNA_FRAGMENT_PUBLICATION_V1"));

    uint256(lengths.length), hashes[index], uint256(lengths[index])));

Assumed — immutable receipt: only actual matching calldata publication creates the first receipt, using current L1 timestamp and block number. The full FR preimage is public in that same transaction, so a private ordering manifest cannot conceal how the chunks reconstruct the block. A duplicate still checks actual bytes and manifest, then returns the unchanged receipt without a second publication reward. The binding hash authenticates the signed block number/hash/count/digest/length even when a default reporter supplies only its signed claim and no manifest preimage. FR already binds the execution domain and revision; its V1 tag denotes the unchanged codec format, not the upgraded EIP-712 duty version.

Assumed — fulfillment: for a DutyClaim C, compute its key from C.manifestRoot and C.fragmentIndex and its binding from its signed fields. The obligation is satisfied exactly when a matching receipt exists with that binding and publishedAt<=C.issuedAt+240. Every displayed bonded block requires valid reserved claims for all exact FR fragments under the codec; one missing fragment leaves the block unreconstructible and its corresponding promise independently defaultable. Honest clients reject internally inconsistent claimed metadata. A signer who authenticated an unconstructible or contradictory promise cannot replace it with an unrelated receipt. Full execution correctness remains a separate client/proof check.

Proven — no implicit discharge: a blob commitment, a sealed-stage receipt, an arbitrary hash or a caller assertion that a block was included does not create this fragment receipt. Segment staging and final republication remain independently mandatory for canonical acceptance. Every early bonded fragment therefore incurs actual bounded calldata publication, even if the segment also uses blobs. Anyone holding bytes can publish them once for every matching publisher/custodian claim. One public fragment with another FR/index is not silently substituted. Historic publication receipts are never pruned or overwritten; archive retrieval remains A-ARCHIVE. L1 reorgs revert receipts and dependent settlements together.

Assumed — direct objective default: at now>publishBy and now<=bucket.evidenceClose, anyone calls proveFragmentPublicationDefault(claim,signature). After all original domain/key/context/range checks, Registry itself looks up the exact first receipt. Absence, a mismatched binding, or a first publication after publishBy establishes the signed default. A matching timely receipt rejects the accusation. Slash the one reserved 10 TAIKO once: 1 to the first valid reporter, 9 to the permanent sink. Invalid evidence pays revert gas; no ETH challenge or new response period is required. Late publication cannot erase an earlier miss. A pending challenge is refunded if this direct proof consumes its reserve first; all offenses share the existing once-only position limit.

Proven — timing example: a bucket opens at t=1000; C is signed with issuedAt=1010, so publishBy=1250. A funding challenge at t=1050 has deadline min(1650,1250)=1250. Publication with its exact FR preimage at t=1249 satisfies C and can earn that targeted challenge's Q. First publication at t=1251 is late: its bytes are useful, but C remains slashable, Q returns to the challenger and no responder earns Q. A malicious holder cannot wait until t=1649 and avoid the miss. A complete competing segment may still win once its own 360-second staging requirement is met; this rule punishes the specified missed disclosure, not every soft rollback.

A funded challenge cannot extend the signed deadline

Assumed — admission: anyone presents the complete signed DutyClaim while now<publishBy, its reserve is live, its position has never been challenged and its key has no publication receipt. Require msg.value>=Q=2×4,000,000×(block.basefee+2 gwei) wei at inclusion; exactly Q is locked and excess becomes the opener's pull credit. Underfunding rejects before allocation. Store opener, position, complete authenticated claim/binding/key, openedAt, Q and deadline=min(openedAt+600,publishBy). For the allowed 60-second issuance window and 240-second covenant this is the absolute publishBy. No later challenge can refresh it or extend the fixed evidence lock.

Assumed — paid response: respondAvailability receives challenge ID, full FR preimage, index and complete fragment. It checks the same actual-publication gates and exact correspondence to the admitted claim. If it creates that key's first matching receipt at or before this challenge's deadline, credit exactly this challenge's Q to the actual responder. No role has a reserved response right. If the receipt already existed from a standalone publication or another response, settle this challenge from its immutable time: a timely matching publication refunds Q to the opener; a late or mismatching one leaves a default. A late response may publish useful bytes and finalize default, but earns zero Q. Standalone publishFragment pays no protocol reward.

Assumed — boundary and settlement: publication/response at exactly deadline is timely; default needs now>deadline. A pending challenged default refunds Q and pays its once-only 1-TAIKO reporter share to the stored opener, even if another caller finalizes. Direct default/equivocation/conflict that slashes first can select its normal first reporter and cancels the pending challenge with Q refunded. First L1 order resolves that bounded competition, never multiple payouts. A malformed or duplicate accusation cannot move another reserve. Already admitted challenges remain settleable after evidenceClose and are settled before release; undisclosed new evidence is rejected after evidenceClose. No valid v2 challenge deadline reaches beyond issuanceEnd+240, strictly before evidenceClose=issuanceEnd+3600.

Many signers, one exact fragment publication

Assumed — bounded lazy accounting: different positions can promise the same FR/index with different issuedAt and deadlines. They share one immutable first-publication receipt, but each challenge supplies its own Q and retains its own absolute deadline. One actual first publication earns at most one selected challenge's Q; other challenges are settled individually: timely for that claim means its opener gets Q back, late means its default persists. No publication iterates over signers. For example A has publishBy=1250 and B has publishBy=1300; first publication at 1270 defaults A but can satisfy B. A challenge opened at1249 against A does not create a new 600-second grace period: A already accepted1250 when signing. No paid challenge is admitted once any receipt exists for its key; malformed contradictory metadata is handled by direct evidence, not a second publication market.

Assumed — other offenses: slashing a position for equivocation, sealed default or owner-authorized canonical conflict cancels any still-pending challenge and refunds its untouched Q. A timely already settled ETH outcome remains final. Previously slashed/released positions cannot admit a new challenge or produce another TAIKO reward. A position is never reused after publication. Durable public data discharges its publication duty permanently, while applicable equivocation/conflict liability lasts until evidenceClose; there is no ongoing private-server retention test.

A sealed publication deadline that late disclosure cannot erase

Assumed — exact covenant: early one-second DutyClaims commit individual known fragments. After a segment is sealed, its complete DA mode, manifest, execution-affecting context and actual blob versioned hash or calldata body hash are known. The service owner may then sign a PublicationClaim using a different reserved position. The immutable StageContext encoding yields contextHash=taskHash, the exact DA/context key signed by this covenant. Clients verify the full context against that hash before treating the covenant as meaningful. The covenant intentionally excludes stageId and stageSalt: any actual matching publication must discharge it, independently of who chose a staging envelope. An early fragment keccak is never compared with a blob versioned hash or substituted for this complete stage key.

Assumed — objective evidence: DARegistry records a stage receipt and, on the first actual publication of that context, permanent firstContextPublication[contextHash]={exists,publishedAt,publishedL1Number}, only after checking actual current-transaction BLOBHASH or hashing the full bounded calldata body under the staging specification. getFirstPublication(contextHash) exposes this immutable historic fact. Repeating or restaging that context never refreshes its first-publication timestamp. Historical receipts needed for this evidence are not pruned. Any party can discharge the covenant by staging the exact matching context with any salt at publishedAt<=publishBy, without signing or proving on behalf of the producer. No checkpoint, head reservation or ordering priority results.

Assumed — direct default proof: anyone may call provePublicationDefault(claim,signature) when now>publishBy and now<=bucket.evidenceClose. The contract authenticates the original signature, bucket and unspent position, then reads DARegistry. A matching first-context receipt with exists=true and publishedAt<=publishBy rejects the accusation. exists=false or first publication after publishBy proves the missed signed duty; the contract performs the registry lookup itself instead of accepting a caller assertion of absence. Slash that position's 10 TAIKO once: 1 TAIKO to the reporter and 9 to the permanent sink. No ETH challenge deposit or additional response period is necessary because the publication deadline was part of the signed promise. Late disclosure cannot change the immutable first-publication timestamp or cure the default.

Proven — example: a bucket opens at t=1,000; the owner seals exact context C and signs its separate position 12 at t=1,030, promising publication of C by t=1,270. C first appears under stage ID S at t=1,300. A report at t=1,301 slashes 10 TAIKO even though the bytes are now public; eligibility at t=1,660 still does not erase that missed covenant. If any peer had staged C under any salt at t=1,260, the covenant would have been satisfied and acceptance could first occur at t=1,620. An invalid alleged signature cannot move collateral. A signer who voluntarily signs an unconstructible key remains responsible for that explicit promise; clients reject such a claim before relying on it.

Assumed — one position, one claim kind: a DutyClaim and a PublicationClaim for the same bucket/position are incompatible promises and provide mixed-kind equivocation evidence. Two PublicationClaims are incompatible if any signed field differs, including issuedAt because it changes publishBy. This prevents silently extending a covenant deadline. Two DutyClaims with the same position but different issuedAt are likewise incompatible: time now changes the mandatory absolute publication deadline. No signer can extend liability by re-signing identical data with a later time. Cross-kind and publication-equivocation proofs require both original signatures and slash the shared reserve only once.

Proven — scope: data first revealed in its canonical proof transaction fails the 360-second public-age gate, even without a signed service covenant. Timely public staging defeats that specific surprise-disclosure trace; it does not show who computed the branch privately or make the public branch uniquely canonical. A producer who stages on time and never signs a broken covenant incurs no withholding penalty merely for having private computation or winning a mature public race. Optional publication claims add liability; they are not an allowlist for canonical production.

A signed soft branch versus the same owner's canonical branch

Assumed — authenticated owner evidence: proveCanonicalConflict accepts publisher DutyClaim X, its original signature, the exact accepted SegmentStatement preimage S, one block index/hash, and exactly six Merkle siblings. It retrieves accepted receipt Y itself using Inbox.getAcceptedReceipt(X.baseHeadHash) and requires the preimage hash to equal Y.statementHash. The Inbox's receipt must attest a successfully checked ProducerAuthorization over the complete accepted statement hash, stageId and immutable keyId; it stores the authenticated producer identity. This owner assent is mandatory even when another address relays or funds the transaction. Merely naming the publisher as beneficiary, coinbase or producer in unauthenticated calldata is not evidence of its authorization. The slashing path reads the stored successful authorization result; it does not re-evaluate a mutable ERC-1271 policy or today's registered key.

Assumed — bounded predicate: require Y.producer==bucket.owner, the same revision, Y.oldHeadHash==X.baseHeadHash, a real leaf whose number equals X.l2BlockNumber, and acceptedBlockHash!=X.blockHash. The X evidence window must still be open and its position still reserved. Slash only X's 10-TAIKO reserve once, paying 1 TAIKO reporter/9 sink. No reserve is invented for an unbonded Y and no honest losing publisher is punished when a different owner wins.

Assumed — receipt authentication: The supplied exact canonical statement preimage must hash to the immutable accepted receipt. The membership verifier uses the receipt-bound domain, revision, old head, number range and blockHashesRoot; caller-supplied versions of these fields cannot replace them. Reject padding indices, overflow before uint48 conversion, non-six-level paths, roots not accepted by the Inbox and non-publisher X claims before touching collateral. Reorgs roll back acceptance and its derivative evidence together.

Proven — explicit false negatives: this rule deliberately requires the same immediate accepted parent context. A signed descendant whose bucket still references an earlier base than Y.oldHeadHash cannot be slashed through an unbounded receipt-ancestry walk. Clients refresh promises against a newly accepted base if they want this protection for later segments. Private economic control across different owner addresses is also unobservable. These pipeline/cross-segment and Sybil cases remain false negatives for this particular offense; mandatory stage age still applies to their canonical data. The rule proves an authorized conflicting act within its defined scope, not global economic identity or irreversible soft finality.

Proof jobs: failure without an exclusive proving right

Assumed — exact job terms: a sponsor locks an immutable nonzero TAIKO bounty for taskHash, containing the exact old head and terminal execution output fields (excluding the constructed head hash), data commitment, origin, revision and immutable task bytes. An offer expires 600 seconds after creation; the sponsor may cancel only before acceptance. An accepting worker locks a separate 1,000 TAIKO reserve; acceptance fixes deadline=max(acceptedAt+300,stage.publishedAt+360+120), worker address and expected proof-bound jobId. Acceptance requires the task's old head to equal the current canonical head, an exact live staging receipt/context, origin.timestamp+900 >= deadline+120 and stage.publishedAt+900 >= deadline+120, plus ordinary task encoding and data bounds. The job separately binds that receipt's stageId. taskHash equals the exact StageContext contextHash and excludes stageId/payment/job assignment; changing the salt gives a different stage for the same execution task, but cannot replace the stageId accepted for this job. The worker executes/validates the immutable task before voluntarily accepting. This freshness guard prevents an already stale origin from making the agreed deadline impossible; it does not prove the sponsor's output is correct.

Assumed — historic acceptance record: the Inbox stores an immutable receipt for the first successful consumption of each canonical parent: parentConsumption[parentHash]=AcceptedReceipt. Receipts cannot be overwritten or pruned while any job depends on them. The task uses the exact noncircular field list in the core specification; it excludes payment/job assignment, constructed head hash, transaction fee envelope and sender; the proof includes job ID and beneficiary when claiming the job. Job settlement does not merely inspect today's head, because a later transition cannot erase a default that already occurred.

  1. Assumed — success: an exact task/job/stageId/worker receipt accepted at or before the deadline pays the locked bounty to the worker and releases its reserved bond. Anyone may submit that proof; copying it preserves its proof-bound beneficiary.
  2. Assumed — competing consumption: if any different action consumed that parent at or before the deadline, refund the sponsor's bounty and release the worker's bond. This includes another prover completing the same execution with different job/beneficiary fields. No worker is punished for an action that the canonical head made unavailable before its deadline. If an upgrade disables the task revision, the Inbox records the first immutable revisionRetirement[revision] timestamp atomically with activation; retirement at or before the job deadline likewise cancels and refunds unless the job already completed on time. A retirement after the deadline cannot erase an earlier default. This is an objective upgrade consequence, not an operational waiver or DAO adjudication.
  3. Assumed — default: after the deadline, if neither timely success, timely competing consumption nor timely revision retirement exists, slash the worker bond and refund the sponsor bounty. A competing consumption first accepted after the deadline does not retrospectively waive this miss. A late exact completion likewise earns no expired bounty.

Proven — no reservation deadlock: accepting a job does not reserve the parent or gate submitSegment; other provers retain the same admission rules. If every assigned worker disappears, a newly entering independent prover can still land a valid segment. If nobody can produce a valid proof, canonical progress stops safely under the acknowledged failure of A-EXEC. A malicious sponsor can offer an impossible output; a worker that knowingly accepts it bears an objectively impossible duty. Local execution before acceptance, not a privileged adjudicator, prevents that bait. Sponsor and worker collusion can self-slash but cannot create value.

Conservation, exit and attack economics

Assumed — accounting invariant: all TAIKO amounts use uint256 token-wei, 1018 per TAIKO. Separate ledgers record free deposits, position/job reserves, sponsor bounties, withdrawable credits and the permanent sink. Their sum never exceeds TAIKO held. Transfers into the ledger are credited by actual balance delta and must match the requested value; no fee-on-transfer or rebasing behavior is assumed for the existing TAIKO token. ETH challenges have a separate invariant: pending deposits plus withdrawable ETH credits never exceed held ETH. No ETH/TAIKO price conversion or oracle is required. Unsolicited donations are uncredited surplus; they neither create a withdrawal claim nor block legitimate withdrawals. Exact conservation of internal credits is distinct from the liabilities≤held-balance solvency inequality.

Assumed — release: buckets stop advertised issuance automatically after 60 seconds; free, unreserved balances may be withdrawn without an exit request. Unspent position collateral becomes releasable only at now>evidenceClose and after any pending challenge is lazily settled. All version-2 challenge deadlines are earlier than evidenceClose, so no challenge extends the lock. A previously admitted default is resolved during release even after evidenceClose; an expired undisclosed signature cannot revive liability. Evidence remains admissible at exactly evidenceClose. An accepted job's reserve remains locked until one of its four settlement outcomes. All outward transfers use pull credits and state-first accounting with a reentrancy guard. Failed token/ETH delivery leaves the credit claimable; it cannot revert a previously recorded penalty. Ownership or signing-key rotation cannot rewrite historic liabilities.

Proven — no self-report mint: let an actor control signer, reporter and responder. A successful self-challenge moves its own Q from deposit to responder credit and costs L1 gas. A self-default returns its own Q but loses 90% of the slashed TAIKO even after collecting the 10% report share. A self-equivocation has the same loss. Sponsor/worker/report identities do not change the proof-job equation. The sink is not withdrawable by an owner, reporter, worker or fee beneficiary; it is not described as a token burn because tokens physically remain held. A malicious upgrade could rewrite any accounting rule, which remains the explicit A-UPGRADE trust boundary.

Assumed — overflow and denial-of-service bounds: all addition/multiplication uses checked uint256 arithmetic; reject values before narrowing. Bounds are checked before signature recovery or allocation. No unbounded signer/challenge/job loop appears on a settlement path. One evidence call handles at most two positions, a canonical-conflict proof has exactly six sibling hashes, one answer has at most 32,768 bytes, and one settlement handles at most one challenge or job. Counter exhaustion reverts rather than reusing an identifier. An address can open many buckets, but each costs real additional collateral and gas; a per-address cap is not represented as a Sybil defense.

Proven — bounded entry cost: at one promise per second, continuous fully funded service locks roughly 36,000 TAIKO plus live bucket capacity. Accounting for the full 60-second issuance interval, 3,600-second evidence interval and strict-after release, one new full bucket every 60 seconds peaks at 37,200 TAIKO at the release boundary. Four promises per second, one bucket every 15 seconds, peaks at 147,000 TAIKO. A new participant can enter with one 600-TAIKO bucket; no minimum continuous throughput is imposed. More capital buys more concurrent obligations but no exclusive admission or sequencing right.

Proven — publication cost: mandatory early-fragment calldata is additional to segment staging and final republication. At the surveyed 64-gas-per-nonzero-byte calldata floor, 32,768 nonzero data bytes alone account for 2,097,152 gas, before ABI/manifest metadata, transaction overhead, hashing, receipt storage and events. At 20 gwei per gas that component alone is 0.04194304 ETH. This is an arithmetic resource bound, not a measured total transaction charge. Full FR preimages contain at most four lengths/digests; each actual receipt adds bounded state, but lifetime paid state growth is not constant. Different signers of the same FR/index share publication cost. No v2 claim receives an automatic subsidy; Q is optional third-party funding and cannot justify waiting past publishBy. A provider must price and finance its own promise. The 4-million-gas response reference remains a launch measurement obligation; worst-case total publication/state cost must be checked against the active fork.

Open — economic deterrence: a 10-TAIKO position is a bounded service penalty, not cover for arbitrary MEV or a guarantee that a 1-TAIKO report reward exceeds L1 evidence gas. A-ECON includes an adequately funded observer or subsidy where needed. The independent mandatory DA age gate does not depend on such an observer. The forced-inclusion queue separately prices spam with a 50% irreversible ETH sink; service collateral and queue prices constrain costs without proving distinct owners or fair race outcomes.

Proven — worked example: at a 30-gwei basefee, Q is 8,000,000×32 gwei = 0.256 ETH. Alice challenges two signers of the same missing FR/index key, spending 0.512 ETH in deposits plus gas. Bob publishes the bytes and exact FR preimage against the first challenge within both absolute deadlines: Bob receives 0.256 ETH, Alice recovers the other 0.256 ETH, and both signers keep their TAIKO. With no answer, Alice recovers both deposits and receives 2 TAIKO; the two signers lose 20 TAIKO, of which 18 becomes permanently nonwithdrawable. Front-running the answer moves the response fee but still reveals the same bytes.

State machines and interface boundary

Position:
  Free balance → Reserved at bucket creation
  Reserved → Challenged by valid signed claim + Q
  Any unreleased/unspent position → Slashed by its valid equivocation/conflict/default evidence
  Reserved → FragmentPublished by exact on-time calldata bytes + FR preimage
  Reserved → PublicationCovenantSatisfied by exact on-time stage
  Reserved → Slashed by missed signed publication deadline
  Challenged → Served by matching first publication no later than publishBy
  Challenged → Slashed by absolute deadline default; late response refunds Q
  Reserved, FragmentPublished, Served or PublicationCovenantSatisfied → Released after evidenceClose and settlement
  Slashed and Released are terminal; satisfied duties retain applicable equivocation and owner-conflict liability.

Challenge:
  Absent → Pending → AnsweredAndPaid | SatisfiedElsewhereAndRefunded
                   → DefaultedAndRefunded | CanceledByPriorSlashAndRefunded
  Exact duplicate admission does not create a second challenge.

Proof job:
  Offered → Canceled or Expired (bounty refund)
          → Accepted → Completed (worker bounty, bond return)
                     → SupersededOnTime or RevisionRetiredOnTime (sponsor refund, bond return)
                     → Defaulted (sponsor refund, worker slash)

Assumed — interface sketches only: these declarations specify messages and observable transitions; production implementations are out of scope. Every mutating evidence method performs all gates before state effects. Registry ownership authorizes implementation upgrades only and supplies no override, manual slash, appeal, unpause or withdrawal-from-sink selector.

Detection rates, node behavior and remaining limits

Predicate-specific false positives and false negatives; no empirical rate is asserted
Attack or conditionResult and exact limitation
A signed bonded fragment misses issuedAt+240, then appears lateProven: given the authentic version-2 claim, live reserve, funded timely reporter and correct receipt state, absent/late/mismatching first publication deterministically proves default. No prior challenge or sealed covenant is required. Late data cannot cure it and earns no Q for that expired challenge. False negatives are zero for this precise missed-duty predicate under those premises.
Signer delays after a challenge hoping for 600 additional secondsProven: responseDeadline is capped by the signed publishBy. A late response may recover useful bytes but Q returns to the opener and the position remains slashable. An optional sealed claim cannot waive or replace the early-fragment duty.
Honest publisher/custodian under bounded assumptionsAssumed: the signer knows its signed deadline, possesses data and manifest, has upfront ETH, and submits sufficiently early for A-INCLUSION's 120-second bound to fit its 240-second promise. If relying on another holder, actual notice/retrieval plus funding/inclusion must fit the same deadline. A late funding challenge grants no new period, because the original signature already accepted that deadline. Under these premises there are zero false service defaults. No 480-second response allowance remains.
Partition, reorg, censorship, exhausted gas budget or unbounded fee increaseProven counterexample: an honest actor may miss the canonical publication deadline despite trying. False accusations of malicious intent can reach 100%; the contract penalizes a recorded service default, not proved intent. A reverted L1 publication supplies no surviving receipt. Q is an incentive, not ETH prepayment to a responder.
Selective P2P withholding followed by timely actual publicationProven limitation: publication by the signed deadline satisfies the duty. Private silence before then can still create delay, and historical-intent false negatives can be 100%. An issuedAt constrained to the 60-second bucket window bounds the promised deadline but does not prove actual signing time. The rule caps a measurable disclosure delay; it does not prove one-second global broadcast or eliminate every strategically timed soft reorg.
Every exact FR fragment is public but a server refuses later requestsProven: its duty is discharged; anyone can reconstruct the block from the ordered manifest and calldata under A-ARCHIVE. There is no repeated paid server-retention test. An archive failure does not manufacture a new slash of an already timely publisher.
Signed sealed covenant missed, followed by late stagingProven: original signature plus timely report and firstContextPublication lookup still prove this independent optional duty. Timely fragment publication does not implicitly satisfy complete segment staging; nor does a segment blob automatically create early-fragment receipts.
Entire private candidate first disclosed in its canonical acceptanceProven under A-L1/A-DA: no qualifying 360-second-old complete stage exists, so acceptance rejects independently of reporter activity. This guard applies to unbonded canonical producers as well.
Mature public competitor wins; signer uses Sybil or pipelined owner contextsProven: ordinary soft rollback is possible. Same-owner canonical-conflict evidence retains its exact immediate-parent/height scope and cross-owner/cross-segment false negatives. Switching owners cannot erase an already signed version-2 fragment default, but an actor can choose explicitly unbonded soft service; it must still satisfy mandatory canonical staging. Address counts do not establish independent ownership.
Hidden signatures, no reporter, or discovery after evidenceCloseProven: economic detection/punishment can be zero. Admitted challenges settle after close, but undisclosed new evidence cannot revive released exposure. This finite service liability is distinct from permanent canonical proof/DA requirements.
Fabricated or repeated evidenceProven under A-CRYPTO: invalid signatures/domains/contexts reject before balance effects and cost accuser gas. A matching timely receipt defeats default allegations. One reserved position pays once across all offenses; changed version-2 issuedAt is a genuinely different signed promise, not a harmless way to defer publication.

Assumed — honest-node response: require every exact FR fragment and its valid reserved version-2 claim before displaying a complete bonded block. Missing body/parent/origin remains UNKNOWN. Re-gossip complete bytes, manifests and signatures; monitor each absolute deadline. A holder may publish directly, or accept a funded challenge before that deadline. After a miss, retain the original signature and report it before evidenceClose; a late response cannot remove the evidence. Stop extending unavailable dependencies, reconstruct from public receipts and canonical history, and independently observe staged alternatives throughout their public-data interval. If another mature branch wins, roll back the soft suffix and notify applications. No publication, challenge or service vote freezes the Inbox.

Open — economic limits: this revision strengthens every early bonded claim rather than relying on optional sealed duties to catch delayed disclosure. The final independent reviews accept the bounded R6 guarantee, while retaining publication costs and finite accountability as explicit risks. Ten TAIKO is not unlimited MEV insurance or a token/ETH price guarantee. L1 censorship, capital concentration, bounded pre-deadline withholding, unbonded services and provisional reorgs remain explicit. Design convergence does not establish launch readiness or universal historical availability.

What this mechanism does not build

Proven — rejected alternatives: a bare digest cannot establish actual publication; a private FR preimage would conceal reconstruction context; an early block keccak is not a later blob versioned hash. Publishing complete bounded calldata plus the exact ordered FR preimage closes those specific gaps. Paying Q after a missed absolute deadline would reward delayed recovery without enforcing the agreed deadline, so late responders receive no Q. Repeating a challenge cannot extend liability. Quorum address counts do not prove independent custodians, beneficiary naming does not prove producer assent, and slashing every losing branch would punish honest competition. The selected duties remain objective, finite and separate from canonical ordering.
