Liveness and safety arguments

This page assembles the per-block arguments into two end-to-end sketches. Each step names the page that carries it and the assumption it consumes (T1 to T13 in 01-threat-model.md). Nothing here is a formal proof; the red-team rounds in iterations/ attack exactly these chains.

1. The properties

PropertyStatementLevel
P1 state safetyNo L1 checkpoint is ever written for a state root that is not the result of executing the certified chain from the previous checkpoint.landed
P2 preconf safetyA locked block is reverted only by ≥ 2Q(m) − m provably slashable attesters (12 at a full committee), by a landed replacement or forced batch after the deadline machinery, or by an L1 reorg. No L1 record, no clock and no accusation voids a locked block (invariant I4; round 4, R4H-5).locked
P3 block livenessWhile one honest bonded owner exists, the chain produces certified blocks within seconds of any silence, or landed blocks within about 75 minutes of any committee collapse.attested / landed
P4 landing livenessEvery certified block is landed while one honest party can prove and one L1 builder includes blob transactions.landed
P5 censorship boundA forced-inclusion request is included by a bounded L1 time regardless of sequencer behaviour (Forced inclusion).landed
P6 accountabilityEvery deviation that harms P1 to P4 is either impossible, worthless, or objectively slashable; no honest party can be slashed by accusation.L1
P7 localityEvery consensus decision an L2 node makes is a pure function of signed objects and L1 data older than finality.node

Round 4 restated the design property-first. The five invariants the rules are derived from, each owned by the page that states it: I1 sortition is a pure function of state at S(t): the holders and committee the inbox computes at landing time equal what nodes computed at termStart(t), whatever registry mutations became effective in between (Sequencing); I2 a drawn term's lander reward exists at landing time whatever the owner does, short of a class-A slash of itself: no other owner action and no liveness event removes the funds, reward debits are the only drain and the reserve holds two terms of them, with the owner-triggerable drain costed as a residual (Landing §5); I3 no minority takes a view from a producing leader: a view changes hands only when the leader is provably silent to the whole committee or the term ends, and an abstaining minority can at most withhold certification (Certificates); I4 nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement after the deadline machinery or 2Q(m) − m slashings (Landing §6); I5 no accusation object exists, and no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing (Slashing §3).

2. Safety argument (P1, P2)

  1. A landing writes a checkpoint only after the verifier accepts a proof over a journal whose parent root and parent hash are read from inbox storage, whose blob hashes are read from blobhash, whose anchor tip is checked against blockhash or EIP-2935, whose consumed queue entries are read from storage, and whose end header hash is bound by an aggregate certificate verified on L1 (Landing L3, L5, L6, L7, L8, L11). proven
  2. The guest proves execution from that parent to that end, that every header's signature recovers to the term's leader, that every anchored L1 header chains to the tip, and that forced-inclusion blocks are exactly the consumed entries. So a false root needs an unsound proof (T3) accepted by ZK_K = 2 distinct systems, or one system in single-proof mode plus a colluding quorum that certified a header committing to the false root (T11). TEE never counts (A3). assumed T3 and T11; the common-mode guest bug is the stated residual.
  3. What lands is what was certified (V7): the end certificate binds the end header hash, the guest chains signed headers from the last landed block, and view changes pin where views end; a block without a certificate on itself or a descendant cannot be landed. proven (Certificates §6).
  4. A locked block cannot be reverted cheaply. A certified block that carries C(h) has Q signers who verified C(h); a view change with a lock below h has Q signers; the intersection is ≥ 2Q − K = 12 when both are signed by the committee of one redraw count, each of whom signed a lock below a certificate they had verified, which is the objective lock-lie offence S3b (scoped to the opening object, at any redraw count). Cost ≥ 12 × B_SEAT = 240,000 TAIKO plus, for a certificate pair, the leader. Across a redraw the two committees differ and the intersection can be empty: reverting a locked block then needs ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers of the certifying committee plus Q of the redrawn one, slashable only for keys in both, with probability about 6·10−7 per view at one third of the seats (certificate page §14; limitations L16). proven within one redraw count, given the certificate formats; assumed T11 across a redraw.
  5. Handoff ambush (W1) needs the same 12 double-signers: the next leader's first block carries the recorded term-end view change; landing withheld blocks above its lock needs a second view change for the same view. The honest view change is recorded on L1 within seconds, long before any landing of withheld blocks can be proven. proven
  6. Replacement voids but never falsifies: a replacement landing starts from lastLanded with a certified REPLACE view and a valid proof, so P1 holds through it; P2 is weakened only for blocks that were certified but unlanded past the deadline machinery, which is announced by an L1 predicate every node evaluates identically. A recorded REPLACE VC voids nothing by itself: it is accepted only with lockHeight == lastLanded.height, lockPhHash == lastLanded.phHash and block.timestamp > replaceableFrom, and until the replacement or a forced batch lands, landing the original stays valid and whichever lands first wins (round 4, R4H-5, R4-C3). proven from the landing rules.
  7. Randomness and rights cannot be bent into safety violations: a biased seed changes who sequences, never what is valid; a holder under a wrong assignment cannot land (rights are checked at landing from the same function nodes use).

3. Liveness argument (P3, P4)

  1. Block production. While one honest eligible seat exists, the hash walk finds it as primary or backup with probability approaching 1 over a few terms (open-empty terms admit any bonded address). A view whose leader is silent to the whole committee (no header of the view with a valid leader signature above the attesters' locks for TIMEOUT = 5 s) is closed by Q = 22 of 32 committee signatures (T9: gossip reaches honest nodes within about a second; T11: fewer than m − Q(m) + 1 = 11 of 32 abstainers in the committee and its redraws). assumed T9, T11.
  2. Certification. With ≥ Q(m) of the term's m committee seats honest and online, every valid block is certified within about 0.7 s and every honest view change forms within one extra round trip. If fewer are online, no block is certified: the chain degrades to "sequenced" only and a later committee's fallback closes the term (any depth). An abstaining minority of ≥ m − Q(m) + 1 makes the term blank: the leader keeps its view (it keeps gossiping signed headers, so honest attesters never time it out), nothing of it lands and nobody is penalized; attesters that attested but saw no certificate for TIMEOUT seconds may vote a committee redraw for the same leader and view (⌊m/2⌋ + 1 signatures, at most REDRAW_MAX = 2 redraws), so blanking needs a blocking minority in the first draw and in every redraw (round 4, R4H-4, R4-C1; invariant I3); if the committee walk finds fewer than 8 seats, no-committee mode makes landings certificate-free. The quorum scales with the committee size, so a small honest registry certifies normally with a published, weaker collusion bound.
  3. Landing. Any certified prefix is landable by anyone holding the data, which ≥ Q attesters hold and serve for two hours; blob bytes are deterministic so proofs are fungible; the lander reward ramps to a level that exceeds proving and landing cost in a 100-gwei hour assumed. Two distinct ZK systems are needed in normal mode; if none lands for two hours, one suffices. Landing needs an L1 builder to include a blob transaction (T1); FOCIL does not protect blobs, so a total builder boycott stalls landing but voids nothing.
  4. Nothing voids by clock within the landing horizon. A proving or L1 outage shorter than the horizon (34 h, or the EIP-2935 window if shorter: 27 h at 12-s slots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots) leaves every certified block certified and penalizes nobody (there is no abandonment rule: S4a was removed in round 4, R4H-5 and R4-C3); the first landing after the outage clears the backlog. Replacement needs a landed proof, so an outage alone cannot trigger it: a REPLACE VC is recordable only at lastLanded after replaceableFrom, the record has no closing effect, and landing the original stays valid until a replacement or forced batch lands. Beyond the horizon, unlanded ranges are lost without a slash (limitations L14); a due forced-inclusion head entry unconsumed for four hours is voided (FI_SKIP). These are the design's two clock rules. proven within the horizon.
  5. Recovery floor. If every registered owner and every committee is dark, after 75 unlanded terms dead mode lets any address with B_SEAT of bond sequence from the last landed head and land without a certificate; the first L1 landing wins. Recovery needs one honest bonded party and two working ZK systems, no DAO (certificate-free landings never degrade to one system, limitation L19). proven
  6. Bridging resumes with the first landing, since the checkpoint is written in the same transaction; L1→L2 messages resume with the first anchor at most 30 minutes stale (ANCHOR_MAX_AGE).

4. Accountability argument (P6)

Every harmful deviation falls into one of three bins. Impossible: finalizing a false root, landing uncertified data, landing beyond a lock, excluding a registrant, pausing the chain. Worthless: withholding (unextendable, unlandable, times out in 5 s), producing unprovable content (replaced after the deadline machinery, no penalty), refusing to attest (loses income; a minority below Q can blank a term but never take it, bleed its leader or stall a term with an honest majority), not landing a certified view (anyone lands it, paid from the holder's reserve). Slashable: every signature pair over conflicting messages, every signed object naming an invalid preimage, a signed L1 reference that is false (S3d), a registered key that acquired code. No rule accepts an unsigned claim and no rule infers fault from the absence of a landing (invariant I5), so no honest party can be slashed by accusation; silence is never slashed because a colluding committee could manufacture it. proven (Slashing §3).

5. Locality argument (P7)

Rights, committees and seeds are functions of L1 data at least 20 minutes old (LOOKBACK) and of registry writes future-dated by at least 22 minutes, so every node below finality computes the same answers as the inbox does at landing: the inbox evaluates a term as ranks among the seats eligible at termStart(t), from its Fenwick prefix-count tree corrected by the mutation-ring entries effective after termStart(t), so registry churn between term start and landing changes no assignment (invariant I1; round 4, R4H-1), and every eligibility input is either DELAY_REG-dated or not owner-triggerable (round 4, R4-C2). Validity rules read only signed objects and L1 blocks at least 48 s old, expressing disagreement as pending, never invalid. Takeover is decided by the existence of a view change, a signed object; a node's clock only decides whether it signs a timeout. Dead mode is the single rule that reads current L1 state, only adds rights, and is arbitrated by the landing contract. proven (Sequencing claims 1, 2; Certificates claim 1).
