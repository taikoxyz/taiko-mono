1. How to read the claims

Proven below means a deduction from the specified rules and named assumptions, or a cited source observation. It does not mean mechanically verified Solidity, a completed circuit audit, or an empirical performance result. Assumed identifies premises the mechanism cannot supply for itself. Open identifies an unresolved obligation and the evidence or decision needed to close it. A-IMPLEMENTATION makes the safety proofs conditional on faithful implementation; it does not prevent an interface-complete design from being reviewed before production code exists. Production implementation is expressly outside this research task. Specification gaps block design readiness; deployment verification and performance benchmarks are separate launch gates. These labels apply to the grouped table rows and numbered arguments as well as their introductions.

Proven distinction: an individual block can be locally execution-valid and later lose canonical selection. Local validation answers “does this signed candidate correctly extend this known parent in this named L1 view?” Canonical acceptance answers “did Ethereum accept a valid proof and available data extending the unique Inbox head?” Ethereum finality answers a third question about the containing L1 block. None implies either of the others in advance. One-second issuance and minute-level proof landing therefore do not alone prove an impossibility.

3. Safety proof sketches

S1. Only a proven execution transition advances the accepted head

Proven under A-CRYPTO, A-IMPLEMENTATION, A-L1, A-DA and A-UPGRADE: start with the authenticated migration head. Assume accepted head H is correct. The next successful Inbox call requires the statement's old-head digest to equal H, authenticates a mature matching actual-data stage, verifies its proof and the exact freshly republished data relation, checks producer authorization and the frozen forced prefix, then installs one derived successor. The proof binds chain IDs, Inbox and Anchor addresses, revision, complete parent and terminal commitments, every execution/anchor transition, origin, data mode and commitments, stage ID, producer, ordered block-hashes root, beneficiary and authorized reward terms. A different parent or altered data cannot satisfy that statement without a cryptographic/implementation failure. Induction therefore establishes execution correctness of every accepted head in the chosen L1 history. Staging alone changes no canonical head or checkpoint.

Proven scope: sequential Ethereum execution lets at most one incompatible successor consume H. Another sound proof of a different branch from H becomes stale after that consumption; it does not become a proof of an invalid execution. An L1 reorg can replace an accepted history before finality. Users must not infer finality from a frame acknowledgment or a valid local candidate.

S2. Pinned origins authenticate L1 facts without trusting an operator

Proven under A-L1, A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION: a successful HeaderStore pin checks a canonically parsed header's hash against the nonzero EVM BLOCKHASH for an earlier L1 block while the lookup is available. Thus its number, state root and timestamp belong to that ancestor in this history. A chain of header hashes proved from an older anchor checkpoint to that pinned origin authenticates the older header too. A reorg replacing the pinned ancestor necessarily replaces its descendant pin transaction; a valid pin cannot float onto an unrelated history merely because a client cached it.

Open implementation obligation: exact header schemas must match the active fork, reject ambiguous RLP and bind number/hash/timestamp/state root consistently. Pinning only a supplied root, trusting an RPC's claim of finality, or omitting ancestry verification would invalidate this proof. A shorter L1 production interval shrinks BLOCKHASH's wall-clock retrieval opportunity; permissionless timely pinning must be operationally available under A-INCLUDE. No consensus-layer lookahead or fixed slots-to-seconds conversion is used.

S3. The bridge consumes authenticated checkpoints, never soft claims

Proven under S1, S2, A-IMPLEMENTATION and A-UPGRADE: only the authenticated Inbox-to-SignalService path publishes an accepted L2 checkpoint on L1. Only the proved Anchor path publishes an authenticated L1 checkpoint on L2. Existing Bridge and Vault message authentication, replay protection and storage must be preserved by the in-place upgrades described in migration. A publisher signature, availability receipt or job acceptance cannot write either checkpoint. Even complete capture of those soft-service roles therefore supplies no separate path to forge a withdrawal root.

Open launch obligation: authenticate actual proxy addresses, implementation code hashes, owners, resolvers, paused state, storage layouts and historical message/checkpoint formats, then rehearse the migration against that manifest. The source review is not an authenticated live-deployment inventory. No custody-safety conclusion covers an incorrect migration or a later malicious DAO upgrade.

S4. Liability cannot exceed escrow or produce an unbacked payout

Proven under the proposed accounting and A-IMPLEMENTATION: every accepted bonded promise consumes a distinct reserved position; each position can be slashed at most once and pays at most its reserve. Invalid, duplicate and expired evidence cannot allocate another reserve. A challenge receives its own ETH deposit; a timely publication pays that deposit once, while default refunds it and separately allocates the reserved TAIKO. Shared-digest revelation is settled lazily per challenge without creating duplicate publication rewards. Withdrawal follows settlement of admitted expired obligations. Internal accounting conserves recorded balances across free balances, reserves, pending escrows, unpaid credits and irreversible sink balances, separately for each asset. Their total is at most the held asset balance; unsolicited donations are uncredited surplus. An exact raw-balance equality check would let donations block withdrawals and is forbidden. Surplus creates no claimant entitlement or owner sweep.

Proven limitation: this is an accounting invariant, not an economic security valuation. Ten TAIKO per position can be less than an attack's MEV or a customer's loss. A 10% reporter/90% sink split makes self-slashing lose 90% of the slashed amount before gas, but does not bound profits outside the contract. TAIKO collateral backs finite service penalties; proof soundness and checkpoint provenance protect custody. Separately, 50% of each forced-request ETH fee becomes an irreversible sink and 50% a processing credit, so self-enqueue/self-process cannot refund the entire queue price.

S5. What a soft confirmation proves

Proven under A-CRYPTO, A-EXEC, A-IMPLEMENTATION and a correct named L1 view: when a node has the complete parent state and bounded candidate body, validates signature/domain/role claims, verifies anchor and forced-prefix rules, and reexecutes the block, its VALID_IN_VIEW result establishes that individual candidate's execution correctness in that context. A missing dependency is UNKNOWN. Optional custodians do not replace execution. Two honest nodes given the same complete inputs agree on validity; they may have different available provisional branches.

Proven limitation: this construction supplies no global first-seen ordering, guaranteed economic-owner diversity, or pre-L1 finality. An honest soft branch may lose to a different fully staged, mature branch at L1. Applications accepting provisional balances or trading against a soft head bear that rollback risk; bridge final settlement does not use those balances.

S6. Every accepted execution payload had an objective public-data lead

Proven under A-L1, A-DA, A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION: the DARegistry records a real current-transaction blob hash or hashes the complete bounded calldata, together with all execution-affecting context. Canonical acceptance proves the exact staged body/context matches its execution statement, requires the selected immutable receipt to be at least 360 and no more than 900 seconds old, and republishes full matching data with its valid proof. Therefore a payload first disclosed only in the canonical action cannot be accepted. A private proof prepared earlier supplies no exception to the age predicate.

Proven scope: a stage proves prior public L1 availability, not execution correctness, ordering priority, universal peer receipt, or when someone began private computation. Multiple conflicting candidates may be staged. A newer stage cannot reset another usable receipt; expired material can be restaged for a new public-data interval. Canonical parent, revision and proof checks remain mandatory. Reorgs roll back stage receipts and dependent acceptance within their affected history.

Proven under A-RETRIEVE and A-PROVE: 60 seconds for retrieval plus 180 seconds for proving fits within the 360-second notice interval, leaving 120 seconds of margin for a same-branch proof to be ready. Inclusion can only be requested once the transaction is eligible; A-INCLUDE does not promise a transaction is included before maturity. Neither this opportunity nor a faster private proof determines which eligible candidate Ethereum selects.

4. Liveness proof sketches and their limits

L1. Incumbents cannot reserve all canonical admission rights

Proven under the admission rules: any address can stage actual data and submit an eligible valid segment without a publisher ticket, custodian certificate, job acceptance, private relay or administrator action. Producer assent is an objective self-authorization, not someone else's approval; relay and fee payer may be different parties. The DAO has no routine operation to perform. Assigned workers have no exclusive claim, and their deadlines do not delay a replacement proof. A malicious incumbent cannot squat a finite global registry capacity because there is no such capacity or canonical lease. Staging supplies no head reservation and does not obligate others to use its candidate.

Proven conditional progress: suppose a reconstructible accepted head remains unchanged long enough for a funded honest entrant to acquire bounded work, stage it, reach the 360-second age threshold, prove it and have its still-valid submission included within both 900-second freshness limits. A-DA, A-PROVE and A-INCLUDE yield a successor. If a competing eligible action instead consumes the parent first, canonical progress already occurred, although the honest worker's computation and paid stage may be wasted. Repeated competing progress does not prove honest ordinary transactions will be selected; forced FIFO supplies that separate guarantee.

Assumed timing example: seal a 60-second segment at t=60, stage promptly and complete a 180-second proof by t=240. The stage matures at t=420; prompt final inclusion gives roughly seven to eight minutes from the first block. Taking the full 120-second inclusion allowance for staging and again for canonical acceptance instead yields 60+120+360+120=660 seconds, plus header-acquisition time. Proving can run during the public-data interval. These examples fit 900-second freshness when the origin/context are selected consistently; they are not measured throughput or a universal Δ. The 900-block unsafe horizon bounds soft exposure through that healthy interval. Once origin or stage freshness expires, restage/reseal/reprove as required; no stale receipt silently waives either rule.

Proven revision effect: origins may be reused, with nondecreasing number/timestamp and identical header on equal number. A byte/gas-bound segment need not wait for the next L1 header merely to seal. Within its freshness period, the same origin can support multiple canonical successors. The 900-second age ceiling still forces origins and their exact queue snapshots eventually to advance on an indefinitely progressing chain.

Proposed job feasibility rule: a worker accepts only a known live exact staging receipt/context. Its objective deadline is max(acceptedAt+300, stage.publishedAt+360+120), and both origin.timestamp+900 and stage.publishedAt+900 must be at least deadline+120. Thus a freshly staged job is not slashable merely because its protocol maturity exceeds 300 seconds. Historic parent-consumption and revision-retirement receipts still decide innocent cancellation versus an already missed deadline; no job grants exclusive proof rights.

L2. A fixed forced request cannot be overtaken by later arrivals

Proven under the queue rules and canonical progress: enqueue creates an immutable index and timestamp. A parent's snapshot fixes the exact queue tail at its authenticated origin; a child cannot change that obligation by delaying its landing. At most four due requests at the head of that frozen prefix must be consumed before discretionary transactions, by execution or deterministic proved rejection. New arrivals have larger indices and never increase the number of predecessors of an existing request. Once that request is in a mature snapshot, every accepted successor that has not yet consumed it removes four earlier due requests, or consumes it when fewer remain.

Proven finite-delay bound under A-LIVE(Δ): let a request enqueue at L1 timestamp t, and let p be an upper bound on its still-unconsumed predecessors. Its maturity is 120 seconds. At any acceptance at or after t+120+900, origin freshness forces the selected origin timestamp to be at least t+120. The origin's exact snapshot therefore contains the request and every earlier request is also mature. Within Δ seconds there is such an accepted parent; within a further ceil((p+1)/4) accepted successors, the request is processed. Origin reuse does not weaken this age argument. A conservative bound is:

processedAt ≤ t + 1020 seconds + (1 + ceil((p + 1) / 4)) × Δ
where Δ bounds accepted-successor spacing, not Ethereum slot duration.

Assumed numerical example: with p=9 and Δ=660 seconds, the bound is 1020+4×660=3660 seconds, or 61 minutes after enqueue. This is deliberately conservative and conditional, not an advertised universal forced-inclusion SLA or a claim that a 660-second Δ was measured. A lightly loaded pipelined chain can process much sooner. There is no uniform delay bound across an arbitrarily large paid backlog or a total prover outage.

Proven scope: “processed” means a validity-checked execution attempt, or a proved reason the raw transaction is malformed or invalid in its canonical execution state. It cannot guarantee application success, restore an obsolete nonce, force an oversized transaction, or guarantee the ordering outcome an unforced soft transaction would have had. The 2048-byte and one-million-gas request bounds make each required item finite; launch measurements must establish the affordability of four worst-case requests plus proof verification.

Proven limited deterrent: the fee remains 0.001 ETH×(1+floor(pending/50)), but half is irreversibly excluded from withdrawals. Starting empty, 1000 enqueues without intermediate processing pay 50×0.001×(1+…+20)=10.5 ETH, of which 5.25 ETH is a net sink even if the attacker earns every processing credit. Gas, staging, proof and republication costs are additional. Intermediate processing can lower the next admission fee but must consume existing FIFO work; it cannot cheaply hold a fixed victim behind the same unprocessed prefix forever. This is a concrete resource cost, not a proof that all censorship is unprofitable.

5. Withholding: prevention, attribution and residual limits

Proven revision-0 counterexample W1: an attacker could privately build Y from accepted H, first reveal Y with a valid proof before public X landed, and leave no violated signed duty. That made honest X's proof stale and could extract MEV. Proposed revision-1 repair: Y must now have complete actual L1 data and execution context staged for at least 360 seconds, and must republish that data in its proof-bearing acceptance. W1's first-disclosure-at-landing step fails the eligibility predicate. The attacker may privately compute earlier, but it must expose Y and give public peers the same-branch proving opportunity before Y can win.

Proven three-part guarantee under the named assumptions: mandatory staging prevents first disclosure at acceptance; signed serving/publication covenants make specified missed duties objectively punishable; authenticated canonical owner assent makes one additional class of conflicting endorsements provable. Advisory custodians alone supply none of the first guarantee. The complete byte/context and receipt rules are normative in staging, and evidence settlement is normative in accountability.

Authenticated same-owner conflict

Proven attributable predicate: an accepted receipt records a producer that authorized the exact statement and stage ID using EIP-712 and an immutable authenticated key. A bonded publisher's DutyClaim X and a bounded Merkle proof for a different accepted block Y can establish a conflict only when collateral owner equals that authenticated producer, revision/base accepted head/L2 number match exactly, and block hashes differ. The execution proof binds the accepted block-hashes root; the membership proof uses the fixed encoding in the block tree. Slash X's reserved position at most once. Do not slash a beneficiary merely because someone named it; do not repeatedly evaluate a mutable generic EIP-1271 policy as historical authorization.

Proven scope: someone else's winning branch cannot automatically slash an honest publisher. Distinct owners may share economic control without a provable link. This rule deliberately matches Y's immediate accepted old-head hash to X's explicit base-head context; it does not infer cross-segment ancestral equivalence. A conflicting later segment outside that exact context may escape this specific slash. Ordinary signed-equivocation and publication duties remain independent. This bounded predicate is not a global promise that one publisher can never change any future soft history.

Sealed publication-by covenant

Proposed objective duty: after sealing, the complete DA key and execution context are known. A publisher uses a separate reserved position to sign them with an issuedAt inside its preexisting bucket window and publishBy=issuedAt+240 seconds. Any matching actual stage recorded by publishBy discharges the duty, regardless of who paid for staging. At now>publishBy, a valid signature and the immutable first qualifying stage timestamp or its absence establish default. A late stage cannot cure it; anyone can report and receive 10% of the position's slash while 90% enters the sink.

Proven distinction: a one-second block fragment's keccak digest is not a later unknown segment blob versioned hash. Early block claims retain their funded serving challenges; only the sealed exact key carries this absolute stage deadline. Neither bucket registration nor a signer-chosen issuedAt proves earlier P2P dissemination. The duty is the objectively enforceable publication deadline it says, not a global gossip timestamp.

7. Known limitations and risks requiring explicit acceptance

  • Proven: fully staged, mature competing branches can displace honest soft branches; soft confirmations are revocable. Mandatory prior DA rejects first-disclosure-at-landing, not every MEV opportunity or branch conflict.
  • Proven: faster minimal proofs can repeatedly beat transaction-rich discretionary branches. Frozen FIFO protects eligible forced transactions, but ordinary mempool fairness and honest prover profitability are not guaranteed.
  • Open: no exclusive canonical lease, capital-proportional liability and an irreversible queue price deter specific monopoly tools; they do not prevent a rich prover/builder cartel from winning every race. Ten TAIKO per position is a bounded service penalty, not insurance calibrated against unlimited extractable value.
  • Proven: every accepted payload needs prior staging and full republication at acceptance, normally two blob inclusions or duplicate calldata. Losers may also pay staging cost. The 360-second lead increases soft rollback exposure; it does not strengthen soft finality.
  • Proven under the role schedule: the steady exposure baseline at one 10-TAIKO claim per second for 3600 seconds is 36,000 TAIKO; at four fragments per second it is 144,000 TAIKO. The exact peak for just-in-time full 60-position buckets, including their overlapping 60-second issuance windows and strict release boundary, is 37,200 TAIKO at one claim/second and 147,000 TAIKO at four. Separate sealed covenants add positions. See roles for the derivation; these smaller exposures still impose meaningful capital requirements.
  • Accepted bounded-service scope, subject to review: retention is 1200 seconds and evidence lasts 3600 seconds; discovery after the evidence horizon may be unpunishable. It does not waive the accepted-data age predicate, recover a released bond, or permit a false custody root.
  • Assumed: timeouts operate under finite inclusion/observation/funding bounds. Arbitrary censorship, fee spikes and network partitions can cause honest service defaults or proof expiry.
  • Proven: larger transactions cannot use the bounded force path, and an unlimited earlier paid backlog defeats a uniform short inclusion SLA. The user's operation must fit the stated admissible form.
  • Assumed: public archives and execution state remain reconstructible; neither KZG commitments nor execution proofs guarantee perpetual off-chain storage.
  • Open: chosen gas, body, proof-size and deadline values require measurement on the launch engine, prover and active L1 fork. No benchmark or production capacity result is claimed.
  • Proven: optional invalid-on-failure frame transactions incur builder simulation cost even when excluded from L1. Public-mempool support for the terminal guard is not supplied by the analyzed draft; ordinary stale losers can pay reverting gas.
  • Assumed: upgrades preserve safety and historical obligations. An inactive DAO permits routine operation; a malicious DAO remains outside the safety assumption, and permanent compatibility with all future L1 forks is not promised.

9. Decisions and evidence needed to close the design

Open review decisions: adjudicate whether mandatory prior actual DA plus exact signed publication/consistency duties addresses the specified withholding attack, and whether the concrete collateral and irreversible queue price supply sufficient practical monopoly deterrence. The revision adopts the literal R7 form: one canonical action republishes complete data and proof, with earlier nonexclusive generic DA publication allowed. If “first-ever publication only with proof” is a separate intended condition, that conflicts with this staging choice and needs an explicit architectural decision; it must not be silently inferred or silently waived. No relaxation is claimed merely by documenting a limitation. The user-provided nonce-as-lock PDF is still absent in the input register; the research verifies the hypothesis independently and cannot claim to have reviewed that artifact.

Open design-review evidence: fresh red teams must close any remaining ambiguity in staging keys and byte/context binding, block-tree attribution, historical deadlines, surplus accounting, frozen FIFO and shared-interface migration. A conditional design proof may assume a correctly implemented specified circuit; an unspecified proof statement or admission safeguard must be completed now. No production contracts are required or authorized during this research task.

Open launch-validation evidence: implement and independently verify the specified circuit and canonical blob/KZG relation after design approval; benchmark maximum block execution, sustained proof throughput, verifier and response resources; authenticate live proxy/storage/deployment facts; rehearse old checkpoint/message/bond compatibility; test removal of operational owner powers; and recheck the launch EIP-8141 clients and public fallback. These are future tests of a complete design, not fabricated pass results and not all prerequisites to finishing this research document. The 900-second staging eligibility plus retrieval margin must fit the actual launch DA-retention guarantee.

Proven minimum dependency statement: Etna needs sound Ethereum execution/consensus, actual available bounded L1 data with sufficient retention for staging and retrieval, an executable sound proof verifier, reconstructible accepted state and some funded capable participant for progress. Without safety prerequisites it cannot launch safely. If blob retention or cost is unsuitable, bounded full calldata staging and republication remains available; proof-bound bodies that do not fit must be rebuilt smaller rather than changing bytes beneath a proof. Frames are assumed by the brief but have an ordinary-transaction fallback preserving atomic propose-with-proof; ePBS, block-level access lists, FOCIL, larger blob capacity, keyed nonces and shorter L1 intervals are optional benefits or compatibility facts, never role-assignment inputs. The dated roadmap survey separates current EIP status and target-fork selection from these dependencies.

Open final disposition: a later revision may claim convergence only with the required independent judge-reviewed rounds and passing gates. If a precise subset is instead proven incompatible, publish that argument, the smallest proposed relaxation and the best design under it. Revision 1 supplies a new observable fact—actual prior public data—so the revision-0 indistinguishability counterexample cannot be carried over unchanged. The residual inability to infer every historical gossip decision is not a blanket impossibility theorem for R1–R7.
