1. How to read the claims

Proven below means a deduction from the specified rules and named assumptions, or a cited source observation. It does not mean mechanically verified Solidity, a completed circuit audit, or an empirical performance result. Assumed identifies premises the mechanism cannot supply for itself. Open identifies an unresolved obligation and the evidence or decision needed to close it. All interface sketches and proposed transitions are covered by A-IMPLEMENTATION until built and verified. These labels apply to the grouped table rows and numbered arguments as well as their introductions.

Proven distinction: an individual block can be locally execution-valid and later lose canonical selection. Local validation answers “does this signed candidate correctly extend this known parent in this named L1 view?” Canonical acceptance answers “did Ethereum accept a valid proof and available data extending the unique Inbox head?” Ethereum finality answers a third question about the containing L1 block. None implies either of the others in advance. One-second issuance and minute-level proof landing therefore do not alone prove an impossibility.

3. Safety proof sketches

S1. Only a proven execution transition advances the accepted head

Proven under A-CRYPTO, A-IMPLEMENTATION, A-L1 and A-UPGRADE: start with the authenticated migration head. Assume accepted head H is correct. The next successful Inbox call requires the statement's old-head digest to equal H, verifies its proof and exact data relation, checks the frozen forced prefix, then installs one derived successor. The proof binds chain IDs, Inbox and Anchor addresses, revision, complete parent and terminal commitments, every execution/anchor transition, origin, data mode and commitments, beneficiary and authorized reward terms. A different parent or altered data cannot satisfy that statement without a cryptographic/implementation failure. Induction therefore establishes execution correctness of every accepted head in the chosen L1 history.

Proven scope: sequential Ethereum execution lets at most one incompatible successor consume H. Another sound proof of a different branch from H becomes stale after that consumption; it does not become a proof of an invalid execution. An L1 reorg can replace an accepted history before finality. Users must not infer finality from a frame acknowledgment or a valid local candidate.

S2. Pinned origins authenticate L1 facts without trusting an operator

Proven under A-L1, A-CRYPTO and A-IMPLEMENTATION: a successful HeaderStore pin checks a canonically parsed header's hash against the nonzero EVM BLOCKHASH for an earlier L1 block while the lookup is available. Thus its number, state root and timestamp belong to that ancestor in this history. A chain of header hashes proved from an older anchor checkpoint to that pinned origin authenticates the older header too. A reorg replacing the pinned ancestor necessarily replaces its descendant pin transaction; a valid pin cannot float onto an unrelated history merely because a client cached it.

Open implementation obligation: exact header schemas must match the active fork, reject ambiguous RLP and bind number/hash/timestamp/state root consistently. Pinning only a supplied root, trusting an RPC's claim of finality, or omitting ancestry verification would invalidate this proof. A shorter L1 production interval shrinks BLOCKHASH's wall-clock retrieval opportunity; permissionless timely pinning must be operationally available under A-INCLUDE. No consensus-layer lookahead or fixed slots-to-seconds conversion is used.

S3. The bridge consumes authenticated checkpoints, never soft claims

Proven under S1, S2, A-IMPLEMENTATION and A-UPGRADE: only the authenticated Inbox-to-SignalService path publishes an accepted L2 checkpoint on L1. Only the proved Anchor path publishes an authenticated L1 checkpoint on L2. Existing Bridge and Vault message authentication, replay protection and storage must be preserved by the in-place upgrades described in migration. A publisher signature, availability receipt or job acceptance cannot write either checkpoint. Even complete capture of those soft-service roles therefore supplies no separate path to forge a withdrawal root.

Open launch obligation: authenticate actual proxy addresses, implementation code hashes, owners, resolvers, paused state, storage layouts and historical message/checkpoint formats, then rehearse the migration against that manifest. The source review is not an authenticated live-deployment inventory. No custody-safety conclusion covers an incorrect migration or a later malicious DAO upgrade.

S4. Liability cannot exceed escrow or produce an unbacked payout

Proven under the proposed accounting and A-IMPLEMENTATION: every accepted bonded promise consumes a distinct reserved position; each position can be slashed at most once and pays at most its reserve. Invalid, duplicate and expired evidence cannot allocate another reserve. A challenge receives its own ETH deposit; a timely publication pays that deposit once, while default refunds it and separately allocates the reserved TAIKO. Shared-digest revelation is settled lazily per challenge without creating duplicate publication rewards. Withdrawal follows settlement of admitted expired obligations. These transitions preserve held-assets = free balances + reserves + pending escrows + unpaid credits + sink balances, separately for each asset.

Proven limitation: this is an accounting invariant, not an economic security valuation. One hundred TAIKO can be less than an attack's MEV or a customer's loss. A 10% reporter/90% sink split makes self-slashing lose 90% of the slashed amount before gas, but does not bound profits outside the contract. TAIKO collateral backs finite service penalties; proof soundness and checkpoint provenance protect custody.

S5. What a soft confirmation proves

Proven under A-CRYPTO, A-EXEC, A-IMPLEMENTATION and a correct named L1 view: when a node has the complete parent state and bounded candidate body, validates signature/domain/role claims, verifies anchor and forced-prefix rules, and reexecutes the block, its VALID_IN_VIEW result establishes that individual candidate's execution correctness in that context. A missing dependency is UNKNOWN. Optional custodians do not replace execution. Two honest nodes given the same complete inputs agree on validity; they may have different available provisional branches.

Proven limitation: this construction supplies no global first-seen ordering, guaranteed economic-owner diversity, or pre-L1 finality. An honest soft branch may lose to a complete private branch at L1. Applications accepting provisional balances or trading against a soft head bear that rollback risk; bridge final settlement does not use those balances.

4. Liveness proof sketches and their limits

L1. Incumbents cannot reserve all canonical admission rights

Proven under the admission rules: any address can submit a valid segment without a publisher ticket, custodian certificate, job acceptance, private relay or administrator action. The DAO has no routine operation to perform. Assigned workers have no exclusive claim, and their deadlines do not delay a replacement proof. A malicious incumbent cannot squat a finite global registry capacity because there is no such capacity or canonical lease.

Proven conditional progress: suppose a reconstructible accepted head remains unchanged long enough for a funded honest entrant to acquire the bounded work, prove it and have its still-valid submission included, with origin age below 600 seconds. A-PROVE and A-INCLUDE yield a successor. If a competing action instead consumes the parent first, canonical progress already occurred, although the honest worker's work may be wasted. Repeated competing progress does not prove honest ordinary transactions will be selected; forced FIFO supplies that separate guarantee.

Open timing evidence: the proposed 60-second formation interval, 180-second proof target and 120-second inclusion model sum to an illustrative 360 seconds. This fits inside 600 seconds when the origin and work are chosen consistently, but does not establish measured throughput, arbitrary backup-failure latency, or a guaranteed Δ. Once the proof-input context is fixed, its expiry is real. Repeated origin expiry under congestion invalidates the finite-time argument rather than silently extending the rule.

L2. A fixed forced request cannot be overtaken by later arrivals

Proven under the queue rules and canonical progress: enqueue creates an immutable index and timestamp. A parent's snapshot fixes the exact queue tail at its authenticated origin; a child cannot change that obligation by delaying its landing. At most four due requests at the head of that frozen prefix must be consumed before discretionary transactions, by execution or deterministic proved rejection. New arrivals have larger indices and never increase the number of predecessors of an existing request. Once that request is in a mature snapshot, every accepted successor that has not yet consumed it removes four earlier due requests, or consumes it when fewer remain.

Proven finite-delay bound under A-LIVE(Δ): let a request enqueue at L1 timestamp t, and let p be an upper bound on its still-unconsumed predecessors. Its maturity is 120 seconds. At any acceptance at or after t+120+600, origin freshness forces the selected origin timestamp to be at least t+120. The origin's exact snapshot therefore contains the request and every earlier request is also mature. Within Δ seconds there is such an accepted parent; within a further ceil((p+1)/4) accepted successors, the request is processed. A conservative bound is:

processedAt ≤ t + 720 seconds + (1 + ceil((p + 1) / 4)) × Δ
where Δ bounds accepted-successor spacing, not Ethereum slot duration.

Assumed numerical example: with p=9 and Δ=360 seconds, the bound is 720+4×360=2160 seconds, or 36 minutes after enqueue. This is deliberately conservative and conditional, not an advertised universal forced-inclusion SLA. A lightly loaded chain can process much sooner. There is no uniform delay bound across an arbitrarily large paid backlog or a total prover outage.

Proven scope: “processed” means a validity-checked execution attempt, or a proved reason the raw transaction is malformed or invalid in its canonical execution state. It cannot guarantee application success, restore an obsolete nonce, force an oversized transaction, or guarantee the ordering outcome an unforced soft transaction would have had. The 2048-byte and one-million-gas request bounds make each required item finite; launch measurements must establish the affordability of four worst-case requests plus proof verification.

5. Withholding: the exact unresolved boundary

Proven attributable guarantee: signed incompatible obligations establish equivocation; a funded on-chain challenge followed by no matching response before its recorded deadline establishes a publication-service default. Any observer can submit that evidence. No central observer decides whether peers “really saw” a broadcast. The accountability specification defines evidence formats, reserves, deposits, deadlines, replay rejection and payouts.

Proven counterexample W1: at t=0, honest publisher P builds and gossips branch X from accepted H. Active economic participant M privately builds branch Y from H and funds its own 180-second proof. M first reveals Y with complete DA and valid proof on L1 before X lands. X becomes stale, its prover loses work and its users see a soft reorg. M need never sign a conflicting or unsatisfied duty for Y; if challenged after landing, Y is already public. The attack costs a proof, DA and L1 fees, and may earn MEV or censor X's unforced transactions. A distinct submitting address can belong to M without an objective on-chain ownership link.

Proven implication: the candidate does not necessarily punish W1. Advisory witnesses for X may all be honest; they cannot restrict an always-open canonical proof race. A contract seeing identical eventual data, proof and duty records cannot distinguish earlier private withholding from earlier public dissemination followed by network delays. The signed-duty mechanism therefore does not establish the user's full historical-withholding requirement. R6 remains an unresolved pass/fail gate.

Proposed honest-node response: retain signed claims and conflicting envelopes, request complete data from multiple peers, expose UNKNOWN rather than a confirmation while dependencies are missing, stop extending a suspect service branch, and open a funded challenge for a valid reserved claim. Track the accepted L1 head independently. If another valid branch wins, roll back the unsafe suffix and notify the user; do not manufacture slash evidence from an unprovable first-seen timestamp.

Open requirement decision: no relaxation is authorized by merely documenting W1. If the brief requires every harmful private-before-landing branch by an economically active participant to be penalized, this candidate fails that claim. Possible directions are a prior canonical ordering commitment, an independently certified canonical sequence, or a narrower objective-service guarantee with explicit rollback. Each changes a material property and requires adjudication. The first can reintroduce proposal before proof; the second creates a sequencing dependency outside L1; the third relaxes the intended withholding guarantee. These tradeoffs do not prove that no other construction can meet the literal R1–R7.

7. Known limitations and risks requiring explicit acceptance

  • Proven: sound private branches can displace honest public soft branches; soft confirmations are revocable. W1 is a requirement gate, not an already accepted limitation.
  • Proven: faster minimal proofs can repeatedly beat transaction-rich discretionary branches. Frozen FIFO protects eligible forced transactions, but ordinary mempool fairness and honest prover profitability are not guaranteed.
  • Open: no exclusive canonical lease and capital-proportional liability remove certain entry barriers; they do not prevent a rich prover/builder cartel from winning every race. No chosen token bond has been calibrated against extractable value.
  • Assumed: timeouts operate under finite inclusion/observation/funding bounds. Arbitrary censorship, fee spikes and network partitions can cause honest service defaults or proof expiry.
  • Proven: larger transactions cannot use the bounded force path, and an unlimited earlier paid backlog defeats a uniform short inclusion SLA. The user's operation must fit the stated admissible form.
  • Assumed: public archives and execution state remain reconstructible; neither KZG commitments nor execution proofs guarantee perpetual off-chain storage.
  • Open: chosen gas, body, proof-size and deadline values require measurement on the launch engine, prover and active L1 fork. No benchmark or production capacity result is claimed.
  • Proven: optional invalid-on-failure frame transactions incur builder simulation cost even when excluded from L1. Public-mempool support for the terminal guard is not supplied by the analyzed draft; ordinary stale losers can pay reverting gas.
  • Assumed: upgrades preserve safety and historical obligations. An inactive DAO permits routine operation; a malicious DAO remains outside the safety assumption, and permanent compatibility with all future L1 forks is not promised.

9. Decisions and evidence needed to close the design

Open human decisions: adjudicate the historical-withholding guarantee against W1; confirm whether strict always-open L1 sequencing is mandatory or whether independent canonical ordering is acceptable; define what concrete economic monopoly deterrence R6 must establish; approve any explicitly proposed relaxation before it is called a pass. The user-provided nonce-as-lock PDF is still absent in the input register; the current research verifies the hypothesis independently and cannot claim to have reviewed that artifact.

Open engineering evidence: specify and review the complete circuit and canonical blob/KZG byte relation; measure maximum block execution, sustained proof throughput, proof verification, calldata publication and active-fork resource budgets; authenticate live proxy/storage/deployment facts; rehearse old checkpoint/message/bond compatibility; verify permissionless removal of every operational owner power; recheck finalized EIP-8141 clients and public fallback equivalence. These are concrete evidence requests, not fabricated pass results or a request to write production code during this research task.

Proven minimum dependency statement: Etna needs sound Ethereum execution/consensus, available bounded L1 data, an executable sound proof verifier, reconstructible accepted state and some funded capable participant for progress. Without the safety prerequisites it cannot launch safely. Frames are assumed by the brief but have an ordinary-transaction fallback preserving atomic propose-with-proof; ePBS, block-level access lists, FOCIL, larger blob capacity, keyed nonces and shorter L1 intervals are optional benefits or compatibility facts, never role-assignment inputs. The dated roadmap survey separates current EIP status and target-fork selection from these design dependencies.

Open final disposition: a later revision may claim convergence only with the required independent judge-reviewed rounds and passing gates. If a precise subset is instead proven incompatible, publish that argument, the smallest proposed relaxation and the best design under it. The indistinguishability result limits what this transcript can prove about prior gossip; it is not a blanket theorem that all permissionless based-rollup designs are impossible.
