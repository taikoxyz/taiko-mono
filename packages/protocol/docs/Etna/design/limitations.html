1. Known limitations

#LimitationOwner pageWhy accepted
L1The assignment is public 20 to 80 minutes ahead (TH6): a targeted DoS or front-running preparation window against a known holder.SequencingMitigation is replacement within 5 s, not secrecy; secret leader election costs about 100k gas per landing and is listed below as not built.
L2An abstaining minority of m − Q(m) + 1 committee seats (11 of 32) in the first draw, and a blocking minority in each of up to two redraws (a redraw needs ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 = 17 votes from attesters that attested but saw no certificate), can withhold certification from a term (a blank term at the sequenced level); non-receipt is unprovable. They cannot time out, bleed or take a producing leader's view (round 4, R4H-4; since the round-5 fix pass the rule reads the attester's own state: an attester that attested the leader's blocks, which the abstainers keep uncertified, never signs a timeout), and a stakeless network flood at a public term start costs its target one view and 50 TAIKO per burst. A cartel majority of ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 (17 of 32) can also redraw a committee that has already certified, since the REDRAW precondition is not verifiable on L1; what it buys is a re-roll toward Q (L16). The revenue consequence, from simulation (slashing §5; round 4, R4-C1; rows for the concentrated honest set added in round 5, R5-C2): below about 25 % of the seats a cartel gains nothing; at the T11 boundary (one third) it gains about 5 points of productive revenue against a dispersed honest set (40 single seats: 38 %) and about 11 points against the eight 8-seat honest owners the launch condition produces (44 %, whether the cartel holds 32 single seats, 4 × 8 or 2 × 8 + 16 × 1), because excluding an 8-seat primary by owner (and up to four backup seats) leaves a pool of about 84 to 88 seats in which the 32 cartel seats are about 36 to 38 %; a single-seat entrant against such a cartel is excluded only by its own seat (the cartel stays at 33 to 35 % of the pool), has about 16 % of its primary terms blanked with two redraws and earns about 84 % of its fair revenue. These shares count certified terms, the fees of delayed seconds carrying over to later blocks; a blocking minority also delays every handoff out of a committee it blocks by 12 to 22 s (the next view needs committee(t)'s closing VC, or a FALLBACK from +12 s and +10 s per further dark committee), and weighted by seconds the one-third cartel's share against the eight 8-seat owners is 50.8 % instead of 44.0 % (56.9 % instead of 48.9 % against six of them at 72 seats), the extra 7 to 8 points needing TERM_END withheld after certified terms too, else 2.4 to 3.5 (round 6, R6-C2). Withholding TERM_END also makes the honest members of committee(t) record the view's highest certificate on L1 (recordCertificate, about 165k gas per record for a term already pinned, assumed, plus the committee walk, about 1 M gas at 128 seats and 5.4 M at 4,096, when the term is not yet pinned, since the call pins it first if needed, which at termEnd + 2.5 s is the usual case unless a recordAssignment(t) pin preceded it; normally one or two records per withheld term, since the view's leader and the member whose bit is the lowest set bit of the highest certificate's bitmap of the view send first and the others only after one L1 block without a record; round 6, R6S-3 and the fix pass), a gas cost the cartel imposes at no cost to itself. Above one third the gain is superlinear (40 % of seats yields about 70 to 74 %, 50 % yields 97 %) and the design does not defend it.Sequencing, SlashingT11 bounds malicious seats below one third; the launch condition (8 owners, 72 seats) is a heuristic that keeps blanking rare for small cartels at launch (a single 8-seat cartel blanks 0 % there) and nothing enforces it; inside T11 the loss is the term's revenue, never eligibility, the term or the leader's stake.
L3Q + 1 silent colluders (leader plus a quorum) can certify data only they hold: an unslashable stall until the replacement window opens (35 to 65 minutes after the last landing, up to 75 with a landing announcement), after which the holder reopens from the last landed block; the private blocks land only if the colluders publish them before that. The exception is shown beside the "locked" label (certificate page §4; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01).CertificatesOutside the stated collusion bound T11. Rounds 1 and 2 showed no L1 rule can distinguish unavailable from available-but-unprovable data without slashing honest committees, so the earlier availability-escalation rule was removed; the cost is a bounded stall, not a safety loss.
L4A premature view change by Q colluders orphans at most two sequenced blocks at zero provable cost.CertificatesTime is local and unprovable; harm is one view; rotation bounds repetition (a timeout never strikes).
L5Fallback race, both directions. (a) A colluding committee(t) that abstains all term and records its own term-end view change just before committee(t+1)'s fallback can invalidate up to about a minute of the honest fallback chain. (b) A Q cartel in committee(t+1) can sign a FALLBACK VC for an honest term t at a lock below its honest TERM_END lock; since round 5 (R5S-5) L1 records and lands such a VC only from termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK (12 s), only while no VC of committee(t) is recorded for the view, only with a certificate at its lock (so it never lands uncertified blocks), and committee(t)'s own VC at a higher lock supersedes it until a landing consumes it. Round 6 (R6S-3) covers the schedule in which a blocking minority of committee(t) also withholds TERM_END, so that no committee(t) VC ever forms: anyone may record the view's highest certificate of committee(t) with recordCertificate, a FALLBACK whose lock is below the recorded certificate is refused, and one recorded earlier is dead, so a later committee closes the view at the certified lock. The certificate exists before term end, and recording it is a staggered duty once no TERM_END VC has formed by termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + 0.5 s: the view's leader and the member whose bit is the lowest set bit of the highest certificate's bitmap of the view send it then, every other attester of committee(t) only after one L1 block without a record, so it reaches L1 within about one L1 block, or two if both first senders withhold, before a landing can consume a FALLBACK (that needs a proof of blocks built on it). What remains is a FALLBACK consumed by a landing in the same L1 block as, or before, the certificate record, which needs Q of a later committee plus L1 ordering against the record (T1); it orphans every descendant of the FALLBACK lock until that landing (minutes), with no slashable key. The same holds for a view closed mid-term by a TIMEOUT VC that has not reached L1, which is why the next leader and every signer must record committee(t)'s view changes promptly (roles page; round 5 fix pass).CertificatesBoth need ≥ Q colluders in one committee, outside T11: about 4·10−5 per term at one third of the seats, 6·10−3 at 45 % (certificate page §14), and (b) also needs L1 ordering against the certificate record; first-recorded-wins and the certificate record keep the outcome deterministic, and a landing that consumes a FALLBACK is itself a landing, so the harm is the FALLBACK lock's descendants until that landing and never a proof-free void.
L6Replacement or forced-batch reorg of a landable backlog: once the replaceable window opens and proving returns, the current holder may fork and race the backlog, and any bonded party may land a forced batch; if either lands first the outage's certified blocks are void.LandingRevised after round 2 (S3) and round 4 (R4H-5, R4-C3). Nobody pays anything: there is no abandonment rule (S4a and its void waiver were removed in round 4); a recorded REPLACE VC voids nothing by itself (it is accepted only with lock = lastLanded after replaceableFrom, and serves as the VC the fork's landing must match and as priority evidence for the forced path), so until the replacement lands, landing the original stays valid and whichever lands first wins; a landable backlog gets bonded priority through announceLanding, and the announcement and the record share one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE deferral per range, from whichever comes first, never two (round 5, R5-C3: two separate deferrals had put the forced-inclusion bound ten minutes above the published 112); a REPLACE lock vote with a false L1 reference is class B (S3d); confirmations are labelled "replaceable" from replaceableFrom. Residual: one reorg of the backlog per stall, bounded by the grace period, unprofitable to the forcer beyond its own transaction's ordering, with no penalty to anyone.
L7A common-mode bug in the shared guest code defeats the two-system rule.LandingOnly diverse guest implementations resolve it; a third ZK system is the stated target.
L8Forced-inclusion stuffing with a quadratic fee buys a bounded delay; an attacker buying most of L1's calldata can keep two of every three L2 blocks an FI block for a few ETH per hour. FI blocks and forced batches are exempt from the per-term byte budget V10 (which counts sequencer blocks only; round 4, R4H-6), so a blob-shaped backlog never exhausts a term or bleeds its leader, and 40 FI blocks per term still drain.Forced inclusionOrdinary users keep one block in three; the drain rate is a tunable constant.
L9An entity's seat share is only priced, never bounded: no owner-keyed rule can distinguish one entity's many owners from many entities.SlashingProven impossibility; the price of primary share is linear in seats and unbounded at exclusion, but productive revenue is not linear: blank terms give a cartel above one third of the seats a superlinear revenue share (L2; round 4, R4-C1); the collusion bound is T11 in its single form (fewer than one third of the seats malicious; fewer than m − Q(m) + 1 abstainers in a committee and its redraws; fewer than Q colluders), an assumption, not a rule.
L10Key theft: a thief can destroy the victim's bond (equivocate and self-report), netting at most 2,000 TAIKO.SlashingThe capped challenger share bounds the thief's gain; custody is the operator's problem.
L11An L1 reorg deeper than 48 s makes an honest holder's certified range unlandable; the range is replaced after the deadline machinery and the holder loses its revenue, with no penalty, for an event it did not cause.AnchorNeeds a > 20 %-stake payload reorg; the loss is one range's revenue and nothing from the bond.
L12L1-level censorship of a registration or of a blob-carrying request is outside the design (T1); FOCIL protects only calldata legs after Hegotá.Slashing, Forced inclusionAn L1 property; the calldata request shape exists for exactly this case.
L13Landing needs an L1 builder to include a blob transaction; a total builder boycott stalls landing (but voids nothing).LandingT1; the public-mempool gate removes the builder dependency once EIP-8141 is Final.
L14The landing horizon is the one time-based void. A range whose first term is older than 34 h (ROLE_HORIZON) and was never pinned, or whose anchor tip has left the EIP-2935 window (27 h at 12-s slots, 4.55 h at 2-s slots), can no longer be landed and is replaceable; certified and locked blocks in it are lost without a slash. The level table's "locked" promise therefore holds only while the chain is within the horizon; nodes label confirmations "replaceable" from replaceableFrom (the moment a forced or replacement landing may void them; round 2, S3) and "outage" once no landing has happened for DEGRADE_AFTER, and publish beside "locked" that its 39-ETH credit bound holds only while landing keeps up within LAND_WINDOW and at a full committee (the bound scales with the committee size, which falls with the suspended share of the domain, L16).Landing, AnchorRound 1 (F7) found the original "nothing voids by clock" claim overstated. Unbounded role history would cost state on every term, and the EIP-2935 window is an L1 fact; the horizon is stated everywhere the claim was made.
L15Every TAIKO amount is a formula instantiated with assumed inputs (term revenue, exchange rate).ParametersMeasurement is a listed prerequisite; ratios, not amounts, are the design intent.
L16Small registries certify with small committees: the quorum scales with the committee size, so the number of provably slashable double-signers behind a "locked" block is 12 at 32 seats but only 4 at 8, and below 8 no certificates exist (no-committee mode); nodes publish the committee size beside the confirmation level, with this and the other exceptions to "locked" shown beside the label (certificate page §4; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Suspended seats shrink committees the same way, since a suspended seat stays in the rank domain and is an empty slot when drawn (round 5, R5S-2): the 12 double-signers and the 13 × B (39-ETH) credit bound hold at a full committee, and the expected committee size falls with the suspended share of the domain (with at most a third of the seats suspended, inside T11, m < 8 has probability about 3·10−7 and the mean bound is about 8.4 double-signers; a parked majority beyond T11 can force no-committee terms; round 6, R6-C1). An open-ended suspension therefore has a permissionless exit since round 6 (R6-C1): exitStale(owner, seats) exits the seats of an owner continuously suspended or lapsed for longer than STALE_EXIT = 7 days without reactivating, effective τ + DELAY_REG under the tenure rule, a domain write like exitSlashed, so it opens no post-seed lever (R5S-2) and a parked seat leaves the domain about a week after it parks. That bound holds within one redraw count: across a committee redraw (⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers of the certifying committee, then Q of the redrawn one, up to twice) a locked block can be reverted with only the keys sitting in both committees slashable, a path with probability about 6·10−7 per view at one third of the seats and 2·10−3 at 45 % (certificate page §14; round 4 fix). S3b spans redraw counts within an opening object, so a key in both committees that locks below a certificate it attested is still slashed, and it exempts no lock (round 5, R5S-1). An entry that no guest can prove stalls every landing path until the stall clock is four hours old (FI_SKIP); then every entry due at the stall's cut (the stall clock plus four hours) is voided with it, the poison and anything pre-posted behind it (64 per landing, the next landings continuing), by a void landing whose T_floor is capped at the cut so that it is always satisfiable; a pre-posted stream therefore costs one stall, not one per entry, and entries due after the cut are served with content on a restarted clock or, if unprovable, are the next stall (round 6, R6H-1). A forced batch also ends a stall, but only inside a window the attacker can shrink to one L1 block or none, so the ordinary void landing is the dependable path. The first entry a stall voids loses its bond and fee, and honest entries due at the cut are voided, refunded and must re-post; each further stall needs an entry saved after the previous stall's cut minus FI_DELAY, which the inbox prices with that stall in progress or already counted, and the escalation level falls only by one per ESCALATION_DECAY = 7 days since the last stall, so with stalls less than a week apart the k-th costs its poster at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4k−1 ETH (six back to back, a day, about 1.7 ETH; twelve, two days, about 5,600 ETH; a daily drip gains nothing, its fourteenth costing at least 0.001 × 413 ≈ 67,000 ETH), about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls in all. What remains: spaced a week or more apart, each stall returns to the floor price, so over T days a budget buys at most about T / 7 + 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls, and until the DAO ships a guest fix a poster can buy one four-hour landing stall per week for about 0.05 ETH, and each stall voids the certified blocks above lastLanded once. The escalation is a deposit refunded to every entry but a stall's first void, so no honest request pays it, but while the level is above zero every requester fronts it for the minutes until its entry is consumed, about 0.001 × 4level ETH (about 4 ETH after six stalls). No-committee operation gives users the sequenced level only; the 1-s block guarantee is then a rewarded duty bounded by the 5-s timeout, not a slashable rule (README assumption A7 revised accordingly).CertificatesSilence is deliberately unslashable because a colluding committee could manufacture it.
L17The migration pauses L2 block production for about an hour, at most a day if the outgoing whitelisted provers stall. It also depends on an execution-client fix: L2's EIP-4788 contract (which on L2 holds anchored L1 execution block hashes) may be deployed only after the taiko-geth sealer fix (taiko-geth#601, L2's EIP-4788 system call on the build path as on import) runs on every geth node, because deployed earlier it splits those nodes from the chain, a liveness failure, never a safety one (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). The abandonment of the unproven Shasta tail after DRAIN_DEADLINE (Anchor page M2; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01) loses the ordinary content of the abandoned proposals, announced in advance (M4), and requeues the forced-inclusion entries they consumed with fee zero: the fees the Shasta proposer was paid at proposal time are not recovered, a leak of at most MAX_FORCED_INCLUSIONS_PER_PROPOSAL = 10 entries per abandoned proposal (Inbox.sol:65) at the fee paid at save time (0.001 ETH at an empty queue, rising linearly with the backlog), so an hour-long tail of at most 300 proposals at 12-s slots leaks at most about 3 ETH at the base fee, and in practice the fees of the few entries the queue held.AnchorSequencing from an unfinalized tail would need a drain-time exception to the parent rule. The deployment order (sealer fix, then EIP-2935, then EIP-4788 on L2, then the fork) is a pre-T0 checklist item on the Anchor page §5. The fee leak is accepted over a proof-backed drain, which would let a whitelisted prover hold the migration (R1); no forced request accepted by Shasta is discarded by the migration except by the stall rule.
L18The ordinary MEV of a single sequencer within its public, attested block stream (including sandwiching a forced-inclusion block whose payload is public five minutes ahead).allOut of scope (01 §8); Etna removes only the private-state amplification.
L19Certificate-free landings (dead mode, no-committee terms, open-empty terms, forced batches) always need two distinct ZK systems: single-proof mode applies only where a quorum executed the state, and its landings are provisional (L-PF). One ZK back-end outage during such a regime stalls it until the DAO rotates images or adds a leaf.Landing, RolesDeliberate: without an attester-executed state root, one proof system would carry P1 alone. R1's "DAO never needed for liveness" therefore holds for certified operation and fails in the compound case of an uncertified regime plus a back-end outage (round 3, H4), and in a proof conflict after a provisional landing (L-PF).
L20No L1 rule can guarantee funds at landing time for a term drawn before the reward debits that emptied its holder's reserve. The reserve (14,400 TAIKO per seat, two terms of the 7,161-TAIKO worst-case exposure per seat, five paid landings and the pin reward included; round 6, R6H-3) is non-withdrawable down to one term per seat and never touched by liveness debits; since round 5 (R5S-4) it is drained only by rewards paid to other owners' landers, attesters and pinners, a self-landing skips the lander's debit and credit, and every reward-type credit to an owner with seats refills that owner's reserve before its withdrawable balance, so the honest path (self-landing 2 to 3 minutes after term end, which round 4's sizing had left out and which drained a single-seat owner in about 24 own terms) moves only the attester share, offset in expectation by the owner's own attester income. Since round 6 an owner whose reserve falls below seats × 7,161 lapses, DELAY_S-dated and, like every suspension, changing no rank, for at least SUSPEND(3) and until it tops up to seats × 14,400 and reactivates, so every draw it keeps is funded by at least one term per seat (R6S-2); ReserveLow fires once per crossing below (seats + 1) × 7,161, with hysteresis, and the client tops up on it; and the ramps run from landability, so an outage of any length charges the maximum ramp only to the terms the first landing after it reaches, at most four (R6H-2): an owner at its required level with three seats or fewer can still lapse if the first post-recovery range reaches more of its terms than it has seats (s × 14,400 − j × 7,161 falls below s × 7,161 only for j > 1.01 s, and a first range reaches at most j = 4 of the owner's terms), and one with four or more cannot from the outage alone. An 8-seat owner holds about 16 terms of worst-case exposure, 8 of them above its line; about 7 of its terms fall in one LAND_WINDOW_MAX at the launch registry and more than 8 in about 22 % of windows, so the top-up matters when its own prover stays down through fees above break-even. The residual window is the terms drawn inside DELAY_S after the crossing plus the terms still unlanded at it. The owner can still drain at will, by letting its own terms ramp and landing them with rewardTo an address that owns no seats (up to 7,161 TAIKO per term moves from the reserve to a withdrawable balance; the refill rule cannot tell such an address from a genuine third party), which frees at most 7,239 TAIKO per seat while it keeps its draws and lapses it 22 minutes after any further crossing for at least 36 h, or by a self-inflicted class-A slash that takes the whole ledger and, since round 5 (R5S-2), changes no draw; invariant I2 is therefore stated with those two exceptions.Landing, SlashingRound 4 (R4H-3, R4-C4), re-sized in round 5 with the honest drain included and in round 6 per seat, for horizon-length outages and for the 2-s landing unit (R6S-2, R6H-2, R6H-3). Such a term is landed by its holder at gas cost or replaced under A1 with no penalty; a drain below one term per seat costs the owner its draws for at least SUSPEND(3) (36 h), and the exposure is realised only at the maximum ramp, which runs from landability.
L21Three L2 lookups shrink or disappear (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147): the Anchor's permanent blockHashes map of past L2 block hashes shrinks to EIP-2935's window of the last 8,191 L2 blocks (about 2.3 h at 1-s blocks), and getBlockState().anchorBlockNumber and the Anchored event disappear; consumers read the latest synced L1 block as the L2 head's parentBeaconBlockRoot resolved through L1. open whether any L2 application relies on the permanent lookup; the issue's review found no other on-chain consumer.AnchorKeeping them would need a Taiko-specific write in every block, which is exactly the per-block cost the change removes; recent L2 hashes stay readable through EIP-2935.
L22L2 receives L1 checkpoints only through reveals (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147): a recorded anchor hash becomes a checkpoint only when someone calls revealCheckpoint (about 100k gas, assumed until measured), and the record in L2's EIP-4788 contract (an L1 execution block hash keyed by L2 timestamp, where Ethereum's holds beacon roots) is guaranteed for 8,191 s only, after which a later block may overwrite it and a reveal by timestamp then fails; a pinned origin (pinCurrentOrigin, adopted from the Astra candidate, PR #22188) stays revealable with no deadline. An L2 contract that reads that contract gets L1 execution block hashes, not beacon roots.Anchor, RolesA missed or expired reveal is harmless: a newer L1 state root proves every signal an older one did, the sequencer reveals each new anchor by client policy, anyone else may, bridge clients retry against the newest checkpoint, and a queued pinCurrentOrigin() pins whatever anchor its processing block carries, so a censoring sequencer can delay a durable checkpoint by the forced-inclusion bound but never prevent one; the reveal is paid only for checkpoints actually used, and the semantic difference is documented.
L-OPNo emergency brake (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): with the pause and owner powers removed from the shared contracts (the L1 and L2 SignalService, the Bridge, the Vaults and the quota manager, upgraded in place before T0 with addresses and storage unchanged), a live exploit of a custody contract can only be stopped by a DAO upgrade; the Bridge quota (immutable after the upgrade, since its setters are frozen) bounds the loss rate in the meantime. The schedule this covers: an attacker that finds a bug in a custody contract drains it at the quota rate until the DAO's upgrade executes, a period the DAO's own process sets.Anchor, RolesR1 as written forbids an emergency brake faster than the DAO, and a brake as slow as a DAO upgrade adds nothing over the upgrade; the cost is an audit of the custody contracts and the brake itself (working assumption A5 revised). After the upgrades no address other than the DAO can stop bridging, remap a token or mint a bridged token. Residual DAO operational powers (verifier X4, 2026-10-01): the DAO keeps two DAO-only entry points that are not upgrades, DefaultResolver.registerAddress (the Bridge and the Vaults resolve their remote peers by name through it) and the owner branch of BridgedERC20 mint and burn (every bridged-token proxy was initialized with the vault's owner), each as slow as an upgrade because it passes the same governance; both are recorded in limitation L-OP as residual DAO operational powers; they are kept because upgrading every bridged-token proxy is out of proportion to a power the DAO already holds by upgrade, and the resolver is DAO-only already.
L-PFProvisional finality (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): a landing accepted with one ZK leaf in single-proof mode advances the chain and pays its rewards but gets its L1 checkpoint only after a second-leaf proof of the same journal or DEGRADED_FINALITY = 24 h with no conflict, and every later landing on its chain writes no checkpoint of its own and is subsumed by the record's finalization checkpoint, so in degraded mode bridge withdrawals and every message that needs an L1 checkpoint wait up to 24 h; a conflict (a valid proof from the other leaf over the same inputs with a different end state) needs a DAO resolution: until the DAO names the sound leaf, no landing of either mode, the forced batch included, and no checkpoint write except by finalizeProvisional of records preceding the conflicting one (which writes at most that record's start) proceeds, and the provisional landing and everything built on it can be voided by that resolution: the rollback restores lastLanded and the forced-inclusion head to the start of the oldest unfinalized record (at most one DEGRADED_FINALITY of landings, each record storing the start it extended), the entries those landings consumed are due again on the stall clock from the restart, and the fees already credited for them, the rewards paid for the voided landings and the term records they settled are not clawed back, a bounded leak (fix pass of 2026-10-01). Elapsed time is not evidence of soundness: the window adds the chance of a second-leaf proof or of a conflict, not safety, which rests on T11 as before.Landing, CertificatesThe alternative, a checkpoint written at once for a one-leaf landing, made a one-leaf root as final as a two-leaf one; a conflict between two valid proofs is the one event no rule can resolve objectively, so it is the one state that waits for the DAO, beside L19. The label "landed (provisional)" is shown with its exception (certificate page §4).
