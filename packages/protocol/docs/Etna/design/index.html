Etna: a fully permissionless based rollup

A research design, not a description of any deployed system. Dated facts cite their sources at the time of writing (2026-09-30 to 2026-10-01).

Etna replaces the whitelisted operator and prover with bonded seats anyone can buy, replaces 384-second epochs with 60-second terms computed from L1 state, certifies every 1-second block with a bonded attester committee, and lands data and a ZK proof in one permissionless L1 action that finalizes immediately. The DAO keeps upgradeability and nothing else. This site is the design; the research behind it is in the repository documents. Status: draft under red-team review; see the iteration log and the readiness verdict in the README.

How to read claims

Every claim is tagged. proven means proven by the written argument on the page and nothing more; assumed means taken from a cited source or stated as a premise; open means unresolved, and what would resolve it is named. Nothing is machine-checked or audited: no formal model of the protocol has been built (arguments claim 6) and no implementation exists (after the DeepSeek review, 2026-10-01). The red-team attack reports and the verification reports in the iteration log are written by independent agents, not by the pages' authors. Gas figures are estimates; TAIKO amounts are formulas instantiated with assumed inputs.

3. The architect's decisions

The three independent designs per building block disagreed on these points; the consistency critic listed 30 contradictions, 27 missing interfaces and 25 parameter conflicts. Decisions, with the losing option and the reason:

  1. Committee of up to 32 seats with a size-scaled quorum ⌊2m/3⌋ + 1 and BLS attester keys (over 16 seats with ECDSA lists, and over a constant quorum: round 1, F1). Twelve provable double-signers behind every locked block at full size, published per term (a seat drawn while its owner is suspended or lapsed is an empty slot, so the size, and with it the count, falls with the suspended share of the domain; round 6, R6-C1); one pairing verifies any size; no ecrecover on the attester path under EIP-8151. Cost: proof-of-possession registration and about 165k gas per certificate verified on L1, one 32-key aggregate verification (the EIP-2537 pairing plus key aggregation and calldata; assumed until measured).
  2. The committee's view change is the batch seal (over a sequencer-signed or quorum-attested seal). Nothing exists for a sequencer to withhold; landers land any certified prefix inside one view.
  3. A term-end view change closes every term (over "no object at the boundary"). The recorded lock is what makes the handoff ambush cost twelve slashed seats; the cost is one aggregate object per minute.
  4. Up to four consecutive views per landing (revised after round 1, F6: one view per landing made the fixed landing gas unamortizable at high fees). Each view is a segment bounded by view changes; the end certificate binds the last.
  5. A landed replacement is the only void; the clock does not void within the landing horizon; no abandonment rule. The landing horizon is the smaller of the 34-hour role horizon (ROLE_HORIZON, for which roles stay computable on L1) and L1's EIP-2935 window, 27 h at 12-s slots and 4.55 h at 2-s slots, a different quantity from the role horizon. Nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement or forced batch after the deadline machinery, or 2Q(m) − m slashings, or, after a landing accepted with one leaf in single-proof mode, a DAO resolution of a proof conflict that voids that provisional landing and every block built on it (landing page §7; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01) (invariant I4); a proving outage shorter than the landing horizon voids and penalizes nothing. Round 5 closed the two objects that had been left wider than I4 allows: the lock-lie rule S3b exempts no lock (round 3 had exempted every lock that was a landed block, which let a Q cartel revert locked blocks with a timeout at a landed lock; the RESET state already makes every mandatory post-reset vote a RESUME, which S3b never covers; R5S-1), and a FALLBACK VC by a later committee is recorded and landed only from termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK × (signerTerm − t) of L1 time, only while no VC of the term's own committee is recorded, only with a certificate at its lock, and is superseded by the own committee's VC at a higher lock until consumed (R5S-5). That supersession needs a quorum of the term's own committee, which a blocking minority of it can deny by withholding TERM_END, so round 6 adds a gate that needs none: anyone records the view's highest certificate of the term's own committee on L1, a FALLBACK whose lock is below it is refused and one recorded earlier is dead, and when no TERM_END VC has formed by termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + 0.5 s the term's last leader and the member whose bit is the lowest set bit of the highest certificate's bitmap of the view must record it at once and every other member one L1 block later if no record has appeared; the certificate exists before term end, so the record reaches L1 within about one L1 block, or one more if a cartel holds both first senders, before any landing can consume a FALLBACK, which needs a proof, and the stagger holds the gas a withholding cartel imposes on honest members to about 165k per record for a term already pinned, plus the committee walk (about 1 M gas at 128 seats, 5.4 M at 4,096) when it is not, since recordCertificate pins the term first if needed, normally one or two records per withheld term (round 6, R6S-3 and the fix pass; the residual, a FALLBACK consumed in the same L1 block as or before the record, is limitation L5). The abandonment offence of rounds 1 to 3 (S4a, with its outage gate and void waiver) was removed in round 4 (R4H-5, R4-C3): a certified view is landable by anyone and paid from the holder's reserve, so a holder cannot abandon a provable view, and the only unlandable certified content is a guest-completeness bug, a false positive for any abandonment rule, handled by replacement plus the client blacklist. The landing horizon and the four-hour forced-inclusion stall rule are the two clock rules, added or made explicit after round 1 (F4, F7); the stall rule voids every entry due at the stall's cut, not only the stored head, once no block has consumed the due entries for four hours of anchored time (round 6, R6H-1; decision 22).
  6. Anchored L1 time is the forced-inclusion clock (over term start or landing time). No third party can make a proven landing revert; an entry becoming due never reorgs in-flight blocks. Nor can a third party do it by saving an entry: the journal names no entry beyond the last one the range consumed, only fiNotDueAt, the latest anchor time at which the range relied on no entry being due at its new head, and the inbox checks the stored save time of the entry there, if any, against it by inequality; an entry saved after the proof was made has a save time later than every anchor of the range, so it always passes (round 6, R6H-1).
  7. Absence penalty 50 TAIKO, burned, with no eligibility consequence; challenger share 10 % capped (over 200 TAIKO with strikes and 50/50 splits). Round 1 (F3) showed that a timeout must never drive suspension; strikes come from structural offences only. Since round 4 (R4H-4) the bleed applies only to a leader silent to the whole committee, so an honest online leader is never bled, and it is paid from the 5 % buffer above the floor and then from seat stake, never from the landing reserve (R4-C4); since round 5 (R5H-1 and the fix pass) "silent" means that for 5 s no certificate appeared and no honest attester holds an attested block of the view that is still uncertified, so a holder that gossips headers without payloads is bled like a dead one.
  8. Registry index of 65,535 seats (over 4,096). Exclusion by a full array becomes unpriceable (above the token supply).
  9. Landing windows 30 min economic, 60 min hard; dead mode after 75 terms (over 35 min hard and 40 terms). Fewer honest false positives; the forced-inclusion bound becomes 112 minutes (102 plus the one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE per range that a landing announcement and an accepted REPLACE record share, whichever comes first; round 5, R5-C3) instead of 77. It holds for every entry that is not due at the cut of a four-hour stall of the queue; an entry a stall voids is refunded unless it is the stall's first void (the stored head), and its re-post gets the same bound (round 6, R6H-1).
  10. The owner is separate from its keys and may be a contract; only the sequencer key must be code-less.
  11. Sentinel views 254 (forced) and 255 (open-empty, dead, no-committee); at most four takeovers per term. A term is open-empty when its domain is empty or every holder view the walk draws for it is void (every drawn owner suspended or lapsed), the two cases treated alike (round 6, R6H-2); before the term an owner can void only its own views, so a term opens only when all its drawn owners are suspended, and opening it adds rights without removing any.
  12. No-committee mode replaces a separate committee-degraded mode: below K_MIN_CERT = 8 non-empty committee slots (a seat drawn while its owner is suspended or lapsed is an empty slot), sortition still picks holders and landings are certificate-free, reserved to the holder until its economic deadline.
  13. Roles stay resolvable for the 34-hour role horizon and every landing pins the terms it settles; a term older than the role horizon that was never pinned is unlandable and replaceable (revised after round 1, F7). The landing horizon of decision 5 never exceeds it.
  14. The public-mempool Frame-Transaction gate is not deployed until EIP-8141 is Final; the direct trailing-VERIFY shape is the primary path and plain transactions the fallback.
  15. No availability-escalation rule (revised after round 2, S4/H2: the earlier demandData game could not be verified by the inbox and either slashed honest committees or priced nothing). A private certificate is an unslashable stall until the replacement window opens (limitation L3); the opening object of every view is now in every signed attester message, so replacement and resumption never create equivocation.
  16. A landable backlog has bonded priority over the forced and replacement paths (round 2, S3): announceLanding (bond ANNOUNCE_BOND = 2,000 TAIKO, forfeited 40 % to the eventual forced or replacement lander and 60 % burned if no covering landing follows; refused once the head forced-inclusion entry has been due for longer than LAND_WINDOW, round 3, C3) defers the replaceable window once per range, and a recorded REPLACE VC is priority evidence and nothing more (round 4, R4H-5) that shares that one deferral: one LAND_CHAIN_GRACE per range, from whichever of the announcement and the record comes first, never two (round 5, R5-C3).
  17. The committee excludes only the primary holder by owner (round 2, F1: excluding every holder by owner emptied committees of the honest set); the published launch condition (eight owners, 72 seats) is a heuristic over a Sybil-able, revocable count that no rule enforces or protects, chosen so that a small cartel blanks few terms at launch; the DAO arms T0 with a second upgrade-class call armEtna(T0) after reading the domain count, and launch may begin in no-committee mode if the condition fails (round 4, R4-C5). The sequencing page owns every selection rule (other pages defer to it).
  18. A timeout is signed only on leader silence, and the lock travels in a separate monotone lock vote (round 3, C1 and S5: with "no certificate for 5 s" as the trigger, 11 abstaining seats made the honest majority hand a live primary's term to a cartel backup, breaking the cost curve; and re-voting at a higher lock was itself equivocation). Round 4 (R4H-4) fixed the silence signal itself and round 5 (R5H-1, R5-C1) bounded it; after the round-5 verification it reads the attester's own state rather than message arrival: an attester signs TIMEOUT for (t, v) only when no new certificate of the view has appeared for TIMEOUT seconds, counted from the moment it first holds the view's opening object and restarted on each certificate (round 6, R6H-4), and it holds no block of the view that it attested and that is still uncertified. What that covers: a leader that publishes nothing at all is timed out TIMEOUT after its view opened, since the window runs before any block exists, and no attester can time out a view before it holds the object that opened it; a leader held by an abstaining minority has its two real blocks attested by the honest attesters, who then never time it out; a dead leader is timed out; a leader that withholds payloads or gossips bodiless, invalid or future-stamped headers gets nothing attested, is timed out at 5 s and bled unless a blocking minority of the committee colludes with it (certificate page §1(d)), and cannot later re-sign lock + 1 with other content (S1). A redrawn committee can certify the held blocks because the staleness bound is measured at receipt: a block every honest node received on time stays attestable, a late or private one never does (found in the fix pass: measured at signing, the bound let no redrawn committee certify anything). An abstaining minority can only make a term blank (invariant I3), and a bounded committee redraw vote (⌊m/2⌋ + 1 attesters that attested but saw no certificate, REDRAW_MAX = 2) raises the blanking threshold from 11 to 16 of 32.
  19. A REPLACE or RESUME VC is recorded only if its lock equals lastLanded and replaceableFrom has passed at record time, and a record has no closing effect: until a replacement or forced batch lands, landing the original stays valid and whichever lands first wins (round 4, R4H-5 and R4-C3, replacing round 3's rule under which a recorded REPLACE VC closed the original tail and one L1 transaction could void fresh locked blocks). The L1 reference is inside the signed bytes (for RESUME, the L1 block that contains the forced or replacement landing being resumed from; the certificate page owns the definition, round 5, R5S-3) and a reset attester may sign only RESUME (round 3, S1, S2, S6); the lock reset triggers on a landed replacement only, and because the reset makes RESUME the only signable kind, S3b needs no exemption for reset locks (round 5, R5S-1).
  20. Sortition is a rank among the seats in the domain at termStart(t), read from a persistent (versioned) segment tree over the append-only seat array: every entry or exit writes a new version tagged with its effective time τ + DELAY_REG, monotone because every domain write uses the same delay and an exit is accepted only after MIN_TENURE, and term t reads the version effective at its start (invariant I1; round 4, R4H-1: round 3's dense eligible list with swap-and-pop moved positions under in-flight terms; round 5 fix pass: round 4's Fenwick tree with a mutation ring made the stalest pin's gas grow with the number of writes, and the per-slot cap meant to bound it either failed to or let an incumbent delay entrants). MIN_TENURE = 7 days keeps every seat drawn and bled for at least a week, and every domain write pays for its own version. Entry and exit are the only inputs that change E_t or a rank; a suspension of any kind, class A or B evidence included, changes neither: a suspended seat drawn as a holder is a void view and drawn as a committee slot an empty slot, so no owner write, self-submitted evidence included, changes a committee after the seed is fixed (round 5, R5S-2; round 4's premise that an evidence-driven suspension could not be owner-triggered was false, since the offender can be its own challenger). A reserve lapse is such a suspension and is DELAY_S-dated since round 6 (R6S-2). The walk reads each drawn owner's attester key valid at termStart(t), from a per-key validity interval, so a rotation that takes effect after a term starts, up to 34 h before the term lands, changes nothing for that term (round 6, R6S-4). The seats of an owner continuously suspended or lapsed for longer than STALE_EXIT = 7 days without reactivating can be exited by anyone with exitStale, effective τ + DELAY_REG under the tenure rule like exitSlashed (round 6, R6-C1): an owner that parks seats in an open-ended suspension before a seed is fixed shrinks every committee they are drawn into for about a week at most, and the exit is a DELAY_REG-dated domain write, so whoever calls it moves no rank after a seed is fixed, and an owner that tops up and reactivates within the week is never exited.
  21. A per-term byte budget (V10), a per-landing reward and a landing reserve at registration (round 3, H2: a per-block price could not cover a term that needs several landings, and a holder at its buffer could certify a term nobody was paid to land). V10 counts sequencer blocks only (round 4, R4H-6) and caps them at TERM_BYTES_MAX = 5 × 254,000 = 1,270,000 bytes, about 9.8 blobs; the per-landing reward is paid only to the landings a term needs (round 4, R4H-7): landing k of a term earns it only if k ≤ ⌈bytes landed so far / LANDING_UNIT⌉, LANDING_UNIT = 254,000 bytes, a constant at every slot time (round 6, R6H-3). The schedule this covers: the sequencer chooses its block sizes and the lander its range boundaries; V9 holds every block's record to one blob (130,035 bytes), so a greedily packed landing that is not its term's last carries more than 260,088 bytes of payload even in the three blobs (LANDING_BYTES = 390,132 bytes) one L1 block carries at 2-s slots, at most about 12,500 bytes of a term go uncounted, and bytesLanded after k greedy landings exceeds (k − 1) × 254,000 (landing page §5), so a term at the cap needs at most five landings at every slot time and each of them is paid however the blocks pack, and one-block landings earn at most one per term. The bound sets throughput: client policy targets about 20 KiB/s of compressed output per sequencer (1,270,000 bytes per 60-s term; 32 KiB/s under the earlier 15-blob bound), which the DAO can raise only with the exposure and the reserve re-derived. The per-term exposure is therefore 60 × 4 + 15 × 1 + 5 × 1,300 + 60 × 0.1 + 400 = 7,161 TAIKO, pin reward included (R_BLOB, 1 TAIKO per blob, is paid for at most 15 blobs per term in landing order, the five paid landings of a cap term at three blobs each, so the sum is a hard bound for every landing pattern: sixty one-block landings would otherwise earn 60 R_BLOB, 7,206, and 2 × 7,206 = 14,412 > 14,400; round 6 fix pass), and the reserve is LAND_RESERVE = 14,400 TAIKO per seat, two terms of it (14,322) and at most B_SEAT, ring-fenced from liveness debits and, while any seat is active or pending, non-withdrawable down to one term per seat (invariant I2; round 4, R4H-3, R4-C4; the per-seat line below). Since round 5 (R5S-4) a self-landing skips the lander's reserve debit and credit (the attester share still leaves, offset in expectation), and every reward-type credit to an owner with seats refills that owner's reserve before its withdrawable balance, so only rewards paid to seatless addresses leave the reserve system. Round 6 holds the sizing against the adversary's schedule. The guarantee is per seat and read at every draw: an owner whose reserve falls below seats × 7,161, one term per seat, lapses for at least SUSPEND(3) until it tops up to seats × 14,400 and calls reactivate(), so an owner that launders its reserve through a seatless rewardTo before a draw still moves its own money but loses its draws 22 minutes after the crossing; the lapse is DELAY_S-dated because, like every suspension, it changes no rank, and voiding its own draws or emptying its own committee slots after a seed is fixed never adds a cartel member to a committee or moves another owner's rank (round 6, R6S-2). The ramps r, r_land and PIN_REWARD run from landableFrom(t) = max(termEnd(t), lastLanded.at), fixed in the term record by the first landing that reaches the term: nobody can land during an outage, so no term is charged for that time, and a chain-wide outage of any length costs the maximum ramp only for the terms the first landing after it reaches, at most four, not for the whole backlog, so an owner at its required level keeps its draws from four seats up, while one with three seats or fewer can still lapse if that landing reaches more of its terms than it has seats (round 6, R6H-2). ReserveLow fires once per crossing below (seats + 1) × 7,161, one term of headroom above the line (14,322 for one seat, 64,449 for eight), is re-armed only at seats × 14,400, and the client tops up to that level on it, at most seats × 7,239 TAIKO (round 5, R5S-4; round 6, R6S-2).
  22. Forced-inclusion entries carry a bond and a stall-escalating fee floor, and a four-hour stall voids every entry due at its cut (round 3, H1: a poison stream was re-armable for 0.02 ETH a day; round 6, R6H-1). The stall clock fiClearTip is the latest parent-anchor time at which a block's cursor had no due entry, derived per block like the cursor and stored at each landing: a block whose cursor has no due entry at its parent's anchor moves it to that parent anchor, a block that uses up a stall's void set restarts it at the stall's cut, and any other block leaves it (round 6 fix pass); once a block's parent anchor reaches the cut, fiClearTip + FI_SKIP (4 h), every entry due at the cut derives to the empty FI block, at any queue position, up to 64 per landing and the next landings continuing until that void set is used up, when the clock restarts at the cut; an entry that became due after the cut is served with content. The first block of a view opened by a REPLACE or RESUME view change, or of a dead-mode view, may be a sequencer block although an entry is due, once per opening, so a fork from lastLanded can advance its anchor to the cut (an honest entry waits at most one block per opening), and the count such a void-by-stall landing must meet is taken no later than the cut, so it asks only for the void set, and the waived block plus the void set's empty FI blocks always meet it. That ordinary landing is the dependable way out of a stall: a forced batch ends one only if its anchor lies between the cut and the due time of the first unprovable entry not yet due at the cut, which the batch would have to serve with content, a window an attacker can shrink to one L1 block or none. The first entry a stall voids, the stored head and the presumed poison, loses its bond and fee, burned, since no rule can tell it from an honest entry stuck at the head through a four-hour outage; every other voided entry is refunded, bond and fee, to the requester the entry stores, which withdraws per entry with withdrawFiRefund(id) against the status the landing writes into the entry's own word (round 6 fix pass), and its requester re-posts (a legacy Shasta entry, pending at the fork or requeued with fee zero from an abandoned Shasta proposal (Anchor page M2; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01), has no requester and no bond, so a stall that voids it refunds nothing: its remaining fee is burned if it is the first void and otherwise credited to the includer, as on expiry). The fee floor is set at save time and multiplied by 4level, where the escalation level rises by one at each stall, counted once however many landings void it, and falls by one per ESCALATION_DECAY = 7 days since the last stall, and by four more while a stall is in progress; an entry consumed with content gets the surcharge above the unescalated fee back, withdrawable per entry, the includer keeping the rest of what was paid, and one a stall voids behind the first gets its whole fee back, so an honest request posted during a stall, after one or while landing is merely slow pays no escalation unless it is a stall's first void, though it must front the surcharge at save time, and after an attack that surcharge falls only fourfold per week. The schedule this covers: before the trigger an attacker can pre-post any number of unprovable entries at the unescalated price, but every one due at the cut is voided with the first, so pre-posting buys one stall, not one per entry; the only entry that can seed the next stall is one saved during this one, which pays the next stall's price; so with stalls less than a week apart, back to back or spaced, the k-th costs its poster at least 0.05 + 0.001 × 4k−1 ETH, six back to back (a day) about 1.7 ETH and twelve (two days) about 5,600 ETH; a daily drip gains nothing, since each stall restarts the decay clock, its fourteenth costing at least 0.001 × 413 ≈ 67,000 ETH, so such a run buys about 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls of about four hours each, with no DAO action. Stalls a week or more apart return to the floor price, so over any period of T days a budget buys at most about T / 7 + 1 + log4(budget / 0.001 ETH) stalls: the long-run rate the escalation lets through is one cheap stall a week, at about 0.05 ETH and four hours of halted landing each. Round 3's rule skipped only the stored head and priced only skips already made, which left no landable range at all once two pre-posted unprovable entries were due together.
  23. Certificate-free landings never degrade to one proof, stated as limitation L19 (round 3, H4) rather than hidden behind a "proven" DAO-independence claim.
  24. No anchor transaction in L2 blocks (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147; over keeping a per-block protocol transaction, and over a Taiko-specific pre-execution call writing {number, hash, stateRoot}, which needs custom execution logic in four implementations and a 70-byte value beyond Ethereum's header limits). Each L2 header's parentBeaconBlockRoot holds the hash of the anchored L1 block and its 7-byte extraData the fee-sharing byte, term, view and kind; the signed header binds the rest and the blob record carries it. The only pre-execution calls are the standard EIP-4788 and EIP-2935 ones: L2's 4788 contract records each anchor hash (an L1 execution block hash) for at least 8,191 s, about 2.3 h, and anyone turns a recorded hash into a checkpoint with a permissionless reveal on the L2 SignalService, upgraded in place behind the same proxy; fee sharing and zk-gas metering stay Taiko-specific as today. The fork is safe because the bridged L2 upgrade installs a current-root guard: the legacy anchorV4 and the L2 syncer path run only in blocks whose header records a zero root, so the first anchor-free block cannot save a forged checkpoint through the legacy path. Cost: one reveal of about 100k gas per checkpoint actually used (assumed until measured), and no per-block protocol execution or gas reserve.
  25. Compared with the Astra candidate (PR #22188, 2026-10-01). Adopted from it: the current-root guard on the legacy L2 writers in place of a stored fork time, permanent origin pins with a deadline-free reveal, the reveal parser bounds and oracle code-hash check, and the 100 % coinbase fee split main had already adopted. Rejected, with the arguments posted on that PR: revocable preconfirmations with no fork choice, mandatory 360-s public staging with double DA, a per-landing admission rent, 2-KB forced requests, and proof races as the sequencing rule.
  26. Operational powers removed from the shared contracts by compatible upgrades (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01; this replaces the open question AD6, which had kept the Bridge's and Vaults' pause and owner powers out of the design's scope). R1 as written, fully permissionless with DAO upgrades only, forbids an emergency brake faster than the DAO, and a brake as slow as a DAO upgrade adds nothing over the upgrade. So the Bridge, the L1 and L2 SignalService, the ERC20, ERC721 and ERC1155 Vaults and the quota manager they call (if it keeps owner setters) are upgraded in place, bridged through the DelegateController before T0 and independent of the fork, to compatible implementations that remove pause/unpause (the _authorizePause override reverts unconditionally, or the functions are removed), remove or freeze every owner-only operational function (changeBridgedToken and any owner setter: a frozen configuration changes only by a DAO upgrade), keep every storage layout, message format, selector used by clients and address (R2), add no storage, and pass a selector audit of the inherited EssentialContract surface; upgrade authority stays with the DAO, which R1 allows. Today these are operational powers, not upgrade powers: Bridge._authorizePause and SignalService._authorizePause admit the owner or an immutable pauser address, and ERC20Vault.changeBridgedToken is owner-only (main at 31df8fe, verified 2026-10-01). The schedule this covers: a pauser key, or the DAO's operational key short of an upgrade vote, that halts bridging or remaps a bridged token; after the upgrades no such action exists, and no address other than the DAO can stop bridging, remap a token or mint a bridged token; the DAO keeps two DAO-only entry points that are not upgrades, DefaultResolver.registerAddress (the Bridge and the Vaults resolve their remote peers by name through it) and the owner branch of BridgedERC20 mint and burn (every bridged-token proxy was initialized with the vault's owner), each as slow as an upgrade because it passes the same governance; both are recorded in limitation L-OP as residual DAO operational powers (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01; verifier X4). The cost is an audit of the custody contracts and the loss of an emergency brake: a live exploit of a custody contract can then be stopped only by a DAO upgrade, while the Bridge quota, immutable after the upgrade, bounds the loss rate in the meantime (limitation L-OP). Working assumption A5 is revised accordingly; this is a human decision the user may reverse.
  27. Single-proof landings are provisional for 24 h unless a second leaf confirms them (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01; this resolves the landing page's open question on keeping single-proof mode after two hours). A landing accepted with one ZK leaf (DEGRADE_AFTER = 2 h unchanged, a non-empty certificate required as before) advances lastLanded, pays rewards and runs every deadline as a normal landing does, but writes no checkpoint to the L1 SignalService; the inbox stores the provisional record (the start it extended with the forced-inclusion head at that point, end height, end block hash, state root, leaf id, landedAt) in a bounded list, and the record becomes final, with its checkpoint written, when anyone presents a proof of the same journal (same start state, same input commitments, same end state) from a different leaf (confirmProvisional) or when DEGRADED_FINALITY = 86,400 s elapse after landedAt with no conflict (finalizeProvisional, callable by anyone and done by the next landing that passes it). A valid proof from a different leaf over the same start state and input commitments with a different end state is a conflict: the inbox records it, emits ProofConflict and enters CONFLICT, in which no landing of either mode and no checkpoint write except by finalizeProvisional of records preceding the conflicting one, which stays callable and writes at most the conflicting record's start, until a DAO upgrade names the sound leaf and rolls lastLanded and the forced-inclusion head back to the start of the oldest unfinalized record if needed (the entries the voided landings consumed are due again from the restart; fees credited and rewards paid for those landings are not clawed back, limitation L-PF), after which the chain restarts from there, dead-mode style, and the preconfirmed blocks built on the voided landing are replaced. A later landing that starts from a provisional end state, with one leaf or two, finalizes nothing: a sound leaf proving that from S these blocks lead to S' says nothing about whether S was the true result of the blocks before it (a fabricated state trie is still a trie), so confirmation needs a proof of the same journal, checkpoints are written in order and each finalization writes the start of the oldest record still unfinalized, so a two-leaf landing writes its checkpoint in its own transaction unless an unfinalized provisional record precedes it on its chain, in which case it writes none and is subsumed by the one checkpoint written when the newest record it follows is finalized, at most 24 h later; a conflict at record k voids k and everything landed after it, at most 24 h of landings, and the DAO's resolution restores the newest L1 checkpoint, so the two-leaf landings between that checkpoint and the conflicting record are replaced with it (fix pass of 2026-10-01). The schedule this covers: a false root in degraded mode needs one ZK bug, a colluding quorum (as before) and, in addition, that no honest prover of the other leaf produces a proof over the same journal for 24 h; when the other leaf is unusable for that long, the premise of the mode, the window adds delay, not evidence, and the mode's safety rests on T11 as before: elapsed time is not evidence of soundness, which is why these landings are provisional and not why they are safe. Cost: in degraded mode, bridge withdrawals and every message that needs an L1 checkpoint wait up to 24 h; limitation L19 stands, since certificate-free regimes always need two leaves.
