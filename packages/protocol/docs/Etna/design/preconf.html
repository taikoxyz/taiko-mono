Preconfirmation validity, certificates and the withholding defence

Every node decides locally and identically whether a 1-second block is valid. A committee of 32 bonded attesters certifies each block with one aggregate BLS signature; a block the committee never received cannot be certified, extended or landed. A term's batch is sealed not by the sequencer but by the committee's view change, so there is nothing for a sequencer to withhold. The 1-s grid runs within a term and across a handoff whose last block is certified: an attester signs the term-end timeout as soon as it holds the certificate of the block stamped termEnd − 1 that extends its lock, so the next term's first block, stamped termEnd, follows the view change by about half a second; a handoff without a certified last block falls back to the clock at termEnd + END_GRACE and empties at most four grid slots, once per term; the grid is a rewarded duty, not a validity rule (V4) (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Sources: judge synthesis of the three B3 designs, aligned with Sequencing rights and Propose-with-proof.

What today does and why it fails

A preconfirmed block is a sequencer-signed envelope rebuilt by every node from its attributes; only key membership, parent presence and executability are checked locally, and the produced hash is never compared with the signed one (00 §9.2). Sequencing rights read the whitelist at "latest", the anchored L1 checkpoint is never checked against the node's own L1 view, and what lands may differ from what was gossiped. Withholding is free and pays at handoff (01 §6). Non-receipt is unprovable, so the defence must combine bonded attestations, timeouts on L1-observable facts and making withheld data worthless.

1. Objects and keys

  • Term t (60 s) and view v within it: view 0 is the primary holder, view v ≥ 1 the v-th backup, v ≤ V_MAX = 4. leader(t, v) and committee(t) (up to K = 32 seats drawn by the sequencing page's §8 walk, which excludes the primary holder by owner and the backups by seat only; that page owns every selection rule) are pure functions of the registry at the snapshot S(t) and the cycle seed. With m = |committee(t)| the quorum is Q(m) = ⌊2m/3⌋ + 1 (22 at m = 32); a term with m < K_MIN_CERT = 8 has no committee and no certificates (no-committee mode). Every security statement below is written for m = 32 and scales as 2Q(m) − m: the number of provably slashable double-signers behind a locked block is 12 at m = 32, 6 at m = 16, 4 at m = 8, and nodes publish m beside the confirmation level. The m = 32 figures are conditional on the suspended share of the domain (round 6, R6-C1): m counts non-empty slots, a drawn seat whose owner is suspended or lapsed at termStart(t) is an empty slot, and with a share s of the domain's seats suspended the expected m is about 32(1 − s), so "12 at a full committee" holds only for a committee in which no suspended seat was drawn. The schedule this covers is owners that park seats in an open-ended suspension before a seed is fixed, to shrink every committee they are drawn into: parked seats sit in no committee, so parking lowers the parkers' own chance of a quorum or a blocking minority; inside T11 (at most a third of the seats parked) m falls below K_MIN_CERT with probability about 3·10−7 per term and the expected slashable count is about 8.4 rather than 12; and seats suspended or lapsed for more than STALE_EXIT = 7 days can be exited by anyone (exitStale, sequencing page §5).
  • Sequencer key: secp256k1, code-less at registration (TH18). Attester key: BLS12-381 (G1 public key, G2 signature) with a proof of possession, verified on L1 through EIP-2537. Both are bound to one owner, the slashable identity.
  • Signed header PH = {version, chainId, termId, view, vcHash, height, timestamp, parentHash, blockHash, txListHash, carriedCertHash, anchorNumber}, where vcHash is the hash of the object that opened the view and is carried in the signed header of every block of the view (the opening object itself travels only with the first block); phHash = keccak(SSZ(PH)); seqSig = ECDSA(keccak(DOMAIN_BLOCK ‖ chainId ‖ phHash)). blockHash is the execution block hash the sequencer's engine produced; txListHash is the keccak of the exact compressed transaction bytes (needed so blob encoding is a pure function of the certified chain). The block contains no Taiko-specific transaction; its L2 header carries the two Etna fields that blockHash commits to (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147): parentBeaconBlockRoot, the hash of the anchored L1 block whose number is anchorNumber, non-zero in every Etna block; and extraData, 7 bytes basefeeSharingPctg(1) ‖ termId(4) ‖ view(1) ‖ kind(1) with kind 0 NORMAL (a sequencer block), 1 FORCED (a forced-batch block, view 254), 2 GENESIS_CHILD (the first Etna block) or 3 FI (a forced-inclusion block). The seat is carried nowhere: (termId, view) and the holder list imply it.
  • Attestation A_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_ATTEST ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ redraw ‖ height ‖ phHash)); the struct carries vcHash explicitly so every L1 rule can read the opening object without a header preimage, and redraw: u8 (the view's committee redraw count, §5; 0 for the first draw), which also enters Certificate, LockVote, ViewChange and every equivocation key (round 4, R4-C1). Attesting over phHash rather than blockHash is load-bearing: it commits to carriedCertHash, which makes the lock-lie slash objective.
  • Certificate C(h) = {t, v, vcHash, redraw, height, phHash, bitmap:u32, aggSig} with popcount(bitmap) ≥ Q over the committee of that redraw count; anyone holding Q attestations builds it (about 130 bytes). Two valid certificates over the same header are ordinary (any two Q-subsets of the attestations) and are never an offence. Anyone may record a certificate on L1 with recordCertificate, which takes the certificate and its committee proof, and the inbox keeps the highest per (t, v, vcHash), which bounds the lock of any FALLBACK for that view (§5 (iv); round 6, R6S-3).
  • Timeout TO_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_TIMEOUT ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ redraw ‖ kind)), kind ∈ {TIMEOUT, TERM_END, FALLBACK, REPLACE, RESUME}, with redraw: u8 in the signed bytes like every other attester message (round 5, R5-C4): a statement that view (t, v, vcHash) should close, carrying no lock. Leader liveness signal (round 4, R4H-4; bounded in round 5, R5H-1 and R5-C1; stated on the attester's own state after the round-5 verification; this page is normative for the trigger): an attester signs kind TIMEOUT for (t, v, vcHash, redraw) iff both hold: (1) for TIMEOUT seconds by its clock no new certificate of the view has appeared, counted in a window that starts when the attester first holds the view's opening object (for view 0 at termStart(t) once it holds the closing VC of term t − 1; for view v ≥ 1, or a view reopened by a REPLACE or RESUME VC, when it holds the VC that opened it; for the members of a redrawn committee when the redraw forms, §5) and restarts on each new certificate (round 6, R6H-4), and (2) it holds no block of the view, under the current redraw count, that it attested and that is still uncertified. The window origin covers the leader that publishes nothing at all: the attester is counting before any block exists, so such a leader is timed out TIMEOUT after its view opened, and no attester can time out a view before it holds the object that opened it. Condition (2) is a fact about the attester's own attestations, not about message arrival: gossip deduplicates a re-sent header, so a rule that restarts a timer on arrival cannot tell a holding leader from a dead one, and a signed header alone is free to emit. An attester attests a block only when V1 to V6, V8, V9 and V10 pass and the signing policy of §4 holds, so "attested" already excludes a bodiless header, an unavailable payload, a forged anchor (V5), a skipped forced inclusion (V8), an oversized block and a future or stale timestamp. What the rule covers and why invariant I3 holds for each: (a) a leader held by an abstaining minority produced its blocks at lock + 1 and lock + 2, the honest attesters attested them and they stay uncertified, so no honest attester times it out and the term is blank, never transferred (the round-4 property); (b) a dead leader leaves every block it produced either certified or unattested, so every honest attester times it out after 5 s and it pays MISS; (c) a leader that withholds payloads, gossips bodiless, invalid or future-stamped headers, or squats without producing gets nothing attested, so it is timed out at 5 s and bled, and it cannot later re-sign lock + 1 with different content, because two headers at one (t, v, vcHash, height) are S1; (d) in a real partition, or when a leader gets a block attested by only part of the committee (published at the edge of the staleness window, or anchored at the edge of the forced-inclusion anchor-lag policy) and then stops, the attesters that attested do not time out, fewer than Q sign, and the view runs to TERM_END without certificates and without MISS; with c colluding committee members who also withhold their timeouts, a VC forms only if the honest attesters that attested nothing reach Q, so the split needs only m − Q(m) + 1 − c honest attesters to have attested (11 − c at m = 32), and at c ≥ 11 a colluding leader can produce nothing at all and keep its view. What this buys the leader: its own term without serving and without MISS, and one term's delay of any due forced-inclusion entry (V8 binds only blocks that are certified and landed; the 112-minute bound still holds). The cost is the term's revenue; MISS deters an absent holder that does not collude, not one that does, and users lose at most one term of blocks per such term. Consequence: an abstaining minority (≥ m − Q(m) + 1) can only make a term blank (no certificates, the leader keeps its view, nothing of it lands, no penalty to anyone) and, by withholding its TERM_END lock votes as well, delay every handoff out of a committee it blocks by 12 to 22 s, after certified terms too: the next leader then waits for a FALLBACK of committee(t + 1) from termEnd(t) + 12 s, or of committee(t + 2) from + 22 s if it blocks committee(t + 1) as well (§5; round 6, R6-C2), and each term whose TERM_END it withholds costs an honest member about 165k gas per record to record the view's highest certificate when the term is already pinned, plus the committee walk (about 1 M gas at 128 seats, 5.4 M at 4,096) when it is not, since recordCertificate pins the term first if needed, normally one or two records per withheld term (§5; round 6, R6S-3 and the fix pass); it can never take the view or apply MISS (invariant I3), and a non-serving holder cannot keep a view past TIMEOUT unless it splits the committee as in (d) or colludes with at least m − Q(m) + 1 members. Term-end signal (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): an attester of committee(t) signs kind TERM_END for view (t, v), and then its lock vote, at the earlier of (a) the moment it holds a certificate C(h) under the same opening object (t, v, vcHash) for a block whose timestamp is termEnd(t) − 1, the term's last possible block under V4 (timestamps strictly increase and stay below termEnd), and that block extends its lock (h ≥ lock_i, on the lock's chain); otherwise it waits for (b) now ≥ termEnd(t) + END_GRACE by its clock, as before. The lock vote is at max(lock_i, h), which is h whenever (a) fires. (a) is clock-free and never a lock lie for a block extending the key's lock: every header the key attested under that opening object, at any redraw count (the comparison S3b makes), carried a certificate at or below lock_i ≤ h, so no attestation of the key lies above its vote. The condition is on the key's own state, as the round-5 liveness signal is, and not on the certificate alone, because heights on two branches of one view are not tied to timestamps: an equivocating leader (S1) can build a sparse branch whose block stamped termEnd − 1 sits at a height below the lock of keys that attested a denser branch, so a certificate of that block fires (a) only for keys whose lock it extends, and a certificate on another branch, or under another vcHash of a REPLACE-forked object, fires nothing: those keys vote their own monotone lock under (b) (restated in the fix pass of 2026-10-01). The schedule this covers: a leader that publishes the block stamped termEnd − 1 on its one chain, which every honest lock lies on, hands over on the grid (§5); one that does not, or whose last block is not certified, is closed by (b) at most END_GRACE + 2 = 4 grid slots later, and no leader can delay the handoff beyond (b) by withholding that block, because (b) runs on the attesters' clocks whether or not any block of the view arrived. FALLBACK is signed by a later committee; REPLACE and RESUME after a reset (§6).
  • Redraw vote RD_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_REDRAW ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ n)), n ∈ {1, 2}: signed by an attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for TIMEOUT seconds (§5). One statement per (t, v, vcHash, n) with no conflicting form, so it needs no slashing rule. (The fix pass briefly had the vote name a block whose staleness bound the redrawn committee would waive; a second verification showed that a cartel majority could then name a block the leader never signed, or one built privately, so the vote names none and the redrawn committee relies on the receipt-time staleness bound of §4.)
  • Lock vote LV_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_LOCK ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ redraw ‖ kind ‖ lockHeight ‖ lockPhHash ‖ l1RefNumber ‖ l1RefHash)): the attester's vote for where the view's batch ends. l1Ref is zero except for kinds REPLACE and RESUME, where it is an L1 block (number, hash), compared on L1 by blockhash or EIP-2935; this page is normative for it (round 5, R5S-3): for REPLACE, an L1 block at which the landing page's predicate A1 held, evaluated on the inbox state before that block's landings; for RESUME, the L1 block that contains the forced or replacement landing being resumed from (A1 held there on the state before that landing, which is what S3d recomputes). It is inside the signed bytes (round 3, S1). Lock votes under one (t, v, vcHash, kind) are monotone: a key may re-vote for a strictly higher lock that extends its previous one; two votes at one height with different phHash, or a later vote below an earlier one, are equivocation (S3c).
  • View change VC(t, v) = {t, v, closes: vcHash, redraw, kind, signerTerm, lockHeight, lockPhHash, l1Ref, bitmap, aggSig}: Q lock votes over one lock by the committee of the recorded redraw count, naming the opening object it closes (round 3, S6: a REPLACE or RESUME VC closes the original object and its hash opens a new one, so the fork's own later VC closes a different object and never collides with it on L1). It closes view (t, v), opens the next, and is the seal of the view's batch, which ends exactly at the lock. No sequencer-signed seal exists. A view is identified by (t, v, vcHash), the hash of the object that opened it; a REPLACE or RESUME reopens the same view number with a new opening object, so every equivocation key below includes the opening object. A FALLBACK VC (signerTerm > t) is accepted by recordViewChange and by landing L4 only under the L1-time gate of §5 (round 5, R5S-5), and never with a lock below the certificate of committee(t) recorded on L1 for the view it closes (§5 (iv); round 6, R6S-3).
  • Lock (per attester): the highest certificate it holds together with the block chain under it, or the highest height landed on L1 in its own view, whichever is higher; monotone within a term.

2. Produced hash equals signed hash

Enforced three times. (i) The node submits the received payload unchanged, with the envelope's parentBeaconBlockRoot, through engine_newPayload, never a rebuild, and requires VALID and engine hash equal to PH.blockHash; a mismatch rejects the block. (ii) Attesters sign phHash, which contains blockHash, only after (i). (iii) The ZK guest recomputes blockHash from blob data, rebuilds PH from the blob record (headerCore, landing page §2) and the block header, checks that extraData's (termId, view, kind) equal PH's (termId, view) and the block's kind and that its first byte equals the protocol constant BASEFEE_SHARING_PCTG, checks that the witnessed L1 header at PH.anchorNumber hashes to the header's parentBeaconBlockRoot, and verifies ecrecover(seqSig) == leader(t, v) as a public input; an irreproducible signed header is unlandable (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147). blockHash commits to the whole L2 header, including parentBeaconBlockRoot and extraData, so PH binds the anchor hash through blockHash and its number through anchorNumber; vcHash and the carried certificate are bound by PH itself and travel in the blob record, not in the block. Equivocation evidence is two signed PHs, as before, and extraData's (termId, view) prevents one block from being presented under another view's PH. proven

4. Attesting and confirmation levels

Attester i attests b iff V1 to V6, V8, V9 and V10 pass, b.height > lock_i, b extends the lock's chain, no timeout was signed for (t, v), and the policy bounds hold: PH.timestamp ≥ receivedAt − STALE_MAX and PH.timestamp ≤ now + CLOCK_SKEW, where receivedAt is when this node first received the block. Policy gates signing only, so clocks cannot desynchronise validity (T13). The lower bound is measured at receipt, not at signing (round 5 fix pass): every honest node receives gossiped blocks whether or not it sits in the committee, so a block published on time stays attestable later, which is what lets a redrawn committee certify a held leader's blocks, while a block published late, or privately to a few nodes, never becomes attestable to the others (W2 and TH6 hold; private certification still needs Q colluders in one committee, limitation L3). Measured at signing, as before the fix pass, the bound let no redrawn committee attest anything, because a redraw forms at least TIMEOUT after the held blocks' timestamps. One attestation per (t, v, vcHash, redraw, height) per key, so a key that sits in both draws attests a held block again under the new count (round 5 fix pass). Attestations gossip on their own topic; anyone aggregates, so the sequencer never holds anything the network does not. Attestation dedup, the gossip dedup key and the client's slashing-protection record all key on (t, v, vcHash, redraw, height).

5. View change: timeout, handoff, fallback

Attesters sign a timeout when, by local clock, for TIMEOUT = 5 s no new certificate of (t, v) has appeared, counted from the moment they first hold the view's opening object and restarted on each new certificate (§1; round 6, R6H-4), and they hold no block of the view that they attested and that is still uncertified (kind TIMEOUT, the leader liveness signal of §1, only while v < V_MAX), or, for kind TERM_END, at the earlier of holding a certificate under the same opening object (t, v, vcHash) of the block stamped termEnd(t) − 1 that extends its lock and now ≥ termEnd(t) + END_GRACE (§1; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01), in both kinds whether or not any block of the view ever arrived. Clocks decide only whether to sign; the decision to switch is the existence of a VC, a signed object, so two honest nodes holding it switch identically and a node lacking it learns it from the next view's first block (V2). Recording is a duty: the next leader records on L1 every view change of committee(t) that it carries, and every attester that signed a view change records it within TO_REBROADCAST of its formation if no record exists yet, because the FALLBACK gates below rest on committee(t)'s own records reaching L1 first; a FALLBACK can target every view of term t, including a view closed mid-term by a TIMEOUT view change, not only the term's last view (round 5 fix pass). If no TERM_END VC of committee(t) has formed by termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + 0.5 s, that view's highest certificate is recorded with recordCertificate (gate (iv) below; round 6, R6S-3), staggered so that one record normally suffices: the leader of the term's last view and the member whose bit is the lowest set bit of the highest certificate's bitmap of the view (a signer, so it holds the certificate) send it at that moment, and every other member sends only after one L1 block has passed without a record, each reading the record first and sending only a higher certificate. The schedule this covers: a blocking minority that withholds TERM_END makes the record necessary; the gas it imposes on honest members is about 165k per record for a term already pinned, normally one or two records per withheld term (limitation L2), plus the committee walk (about 1 M gas at 128 seats, 5.4 M at 4,096) when the term is not yet pinned, since recordCertificate pins the term first if needed, which at termEnd + 2.5 s is the usual case unless a recordAssignment(t) pin preceded it (round 6 fix pass), more only when late senders race into one L1 block, where a certificate not above the stored height is refused before its verification; and if the cartel also holds both first senders and stays silent, the honest record comes one L1 block later (by termEnd + 26.5 s at 12-s slots, + 6.5 s at 2-s), still before any landing can consume a FALLBACK, which needs blocks built on its lock to be certified and proven (tens of seconds at the assumed proving latency, landing page §5). On the happy path the TERM_END VC forms at about termEnd, on the certificate of the block stamped termEnd − 1, and the duty costs nothing. Two steps:

  1. Broadcast TO_i; rebroadcast every TO_REBROADCAST while the condition holds.
  2. Any node holding ≥ Q timeouts of one (t, v, vcHash, kind) proposes VC(t, v, kind, lock*) with lock* the highest certified block it holds, attaching C(lock*). Attester j casts a lock vote iff it holds C(lock*) and every block up to it (range-fetching if needed) and lock* ≥ its own lock. A higher honest lock arrives as a higher vote and the proposer re-proposes at it; every attester may re-vote upward (lock votes are monotone, §1), so with ≥ Q honest online attesters the VC forms at the honest maximum in one extra round trip (≈ 0.4 s) without any key signing two conflicting locks (round 3, S5).

After VC(t, v, TIMEOUT), leader(t, v+1) starts at lock* + 1 carrying the VC; after VC(t, v, TERM_END), leader(t+1, 0) likewise. A view without certificates yields a VC at the previous lock (an empty batch). After V_MAX view changes the term idles to its end. Pipelined handoff (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). Leader policy: a leader issues the block stamped termEnd − 1 even when it is empty, so its term closes by §1 (a). Happy path: that block is published at termEnd − 1; C(h) forms within the 0.7-s certification window, by about termEnd − 0.3 s; TERM_END timeouts and lock votes at h gossip at once and the VC forms within one round trip, at about termEnd; leader(t+1, 0) publishes PH(h+1) with timestamp termEnd about 0.5 s after it, carrying the VC (a block stamped termEnd and received at termEnd + 0.5 s sits 2.5 s inside STALE_MAX = 3 s, and V2 admits the timestamp because termStart(t+1) = termEnd(t)), and continues on the 1-s grid: no grid slot is empty at the handoff. Clock fallback: when the leader does not issue that block, or C(h) does not form, (b) applies: attesters sign TERM_END at termEnd + 2 s, the VC forms at about termEnd + 2.5 s and the first block of term t + 1 is published at about termEnd + 3 s with a timestamp of at most that time, so at most END_GRACE + 2 = 4 grid slots (termEnd − 1 to termEnd + 2) are empty, once per term. The schedule this covers: a leader that withholds its last block gains nothing but those four slots, since (b) runs on the attesters' clocks; one that publishes a block stamped termEnd − 1 later than termEnd − 1 + STALE_MAX gets it attested by nobody (§4) and is closed by (b) as well. The recordCertificate duty (no VC by termEnd + 2.5 s) and the FALLBACK gate (termEnd + 12 s) are unchanged. Fallback, any depth, gated by L1 time (round 5, R5S-5). If no VC closing term t exists by termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK, the committee of a later term t' within the landing horizon may sign a FALLBACK VC for t with signerTerm = t' for the highest (v, lock) it holds, once termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK · (t' − t) has passed by its clock and by the timestamp of its latest L1 block (so nearer committees have priority), only while it holds no VC of committee(t) for that view and sees none recorded, and never below a recorded certificate of committee(t) for the view (§10; round 6, R6H-4 and R6S-3); the landing verifies the VC against committee(signerTerm). Which signers and schedules the FALLBACK kind covers and why invariant I4 holds for each: every committee(t') with t' > t inside the horizon may sign it, and none of them attested under (t, v), so S3b never applies to them, and a FALLBACK lock below committee(t)'s highest certificate is not an objective lie either (its signers attested nothing under (t, v), and receipt of a certificate is unprovable), so no slashing rule can see it; what protects committee(t)'s locked blocks is L1, and since round 6 it no longer needs committee(t) to form a view change. If committee(t) has Q honest members, its TERM_END VC forms at about termEnd on the certificate of the block stamped termEnd − 1, or at termEnd + 2 s on the clock fallback when that block is missing or uncertified (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01), is sent for recording at once (by + 2.5 s at the latest) and supersedes any FALLBACK (iii). If a blocking minority of committee(t) withholds TERM_END, so that no VC of committee(t) can form (R6S-3), every certificate the term formed still exists before term end: at termEnd + 2.5 s the leader and the member whose bit is the lowest set bit of the highest certificate's bitmap of the view record the highest one, and the other honest members one L1 block later if neither did, so it reaches L1 within one or two L1 blocks, and from then on a FALLBACK below it is refused, or dead if recorded earlier; consuming a FALLBACK needs a landing that proves blocks built on its lock, which an honest next leader refuses to build (V2) and which takes tens of seconds to prove, so such a landing precedes the record only if L1 orders it first. The residual (limitation L5) is a FALLBACK consumed by a landing in the same L1 block as, or before, the honest certificate or view-change record: it needs Q of a later committee, a next leader that builds on the FALLBACK lock (a colluding one, or one that never received the higher certificate), a proof ready by then and L1 ordering against the honest record (outside T1), and it orphans every descendant of the FALLBACK lock until the consuming landing (minutes of blocks, not one term); its probability is at most that of Q in one committee in the §14 table (4·10−5 per term at one third of the seats), multiplied down by the L1 ordering it needs. A certificate record can be turned against an honest FALLBACK only by Q colluders of committee(t) certifying a block only they hold, which is the stall of limitation L3 (§6) that the same Q + 1 colluders can cause without it. Two consecutive dark committees delay a term's closure by one more fallback interval, never wedge it. A conflicting late VC is resolved on L1 by recordViewChange (first live record per (t, v, closes) wins; a REPLACE or RESUME record is accepted only under the landing page's A3 condition, §6). The REPLACE and RESUME kinds serve the landing page's replacement rule.

Committee redraw (round 4, R4-C1). An attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for TIMEOUT seconds may sign REDRAW(t, v, vcHash, n), n = 1, 2 (REDRAW_MAX = 2). With ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 such signatures (17 of 32) the committee of (t, v) is redrawn as the first K seats of the walk keccak(seed ‖ 0x03 ‖ t ‖ n ‖ m), with the same exclusions as the sequencing page's §8, for the same leader, the same view and the same opening object; its members attest the view's valid blocks that they received on time, under the receipt-time staleness bound of §4 (a block received within STALE_MAX of its timestamp stays attestable after the redraw; a late or privately held one never does), and the leader continues once a certificate forms; for its members the TIMEOUT window of §1 starts when the redraw forms; attestations, certificates and lock votes of the redrawn committee carry n in their signed bytes, and the landing verifies certificates against the committee of the recorded redraw count for that view (the committee proof carries n). A REDRAW message has no conflicting form (one statement per (t, v, vcHash, n)), so it needs no slashing rule, and it changes no holder and applies no penalty. Rationale: it raises the blanking threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 in the first draw and requires the cartel to hold a blocking minority in every redraw. What a cartel majority (≥ 17 of 32) gains from redrawing a committee that has already certified is stated in §8 and §14: a re-roll toward Q in a fresh committee and, if it finds Q there, a lock reset whose only slashable keys are those sitting in both committees; at one third of the seats this path has probability about 6·10−7 per view against 4·10−5 for the slashable path, so the redraw is not the cheaper reversion in probability, only in provable cost.

6. Landing binding (V7) and certified-but-private data

The inbox keeps lastLanded = (t, v, height, phHash) and lastVCHash. A landing covers a contiguous range of up to four consecutive views (segments, landing page L4); for one segment, either it continues the open view from lastLanded.height + 1 with an end certificate whose phHash equals the guest's end phHash, so any certified prefix lands in blob-sized pieces; or it opens a view by supplying the VC chain from lastVCHash, each VC's lock equal to lastLanded when applied, the first block's vcHash (a guest public input) equal to the last VC's hash. recordViewChange(vc) may be called by anyone and stores the first VC per (t, v, closes); later landings must match it while it is live. For kind FALLBACK the record is accepted only under the L1-time gate of §5 (i) and only at or above the certificate of committee(t) recorded for that (t, v, closes), is superseded by a committee(t) VC at a higher lock until a landing has consumed it, §5 (iii), and is dead once a higher certificate is recorded, §5 (iv) (round 6, R6S-3). For kinds REPLACE and RESUME the record is accepted only if vc.lockHeight == lastLanded.height, vc.lockPhHash == lastLanded.phHash and block.timestamp > replaceableFrom at record time, else refused, and it is dead once lastLanded.height > vc.lockHeight (ignored by the first-recorded rule, so the original view's own TIMEOUT or TERM_END VC for the same (t, v, closes) is then recorded or landed in its place, and no longer priority evidence; the fork's own later VCs close a different object, round 3 S6, and never collide with it); an accepted record has no closing effect (round 4, R4H-5, R4-C3): it is the first-recorded VC the fork's landing must match and priority evidence that defers the forced path by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE once, like a landing announcement. Until a replacement or forced batch lands, landing the original stays valid; whichever lands first wins, and the loser is invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape (landing page A3). The record puts a term's end on L1 within seconds although its landing takes minutes, which is what wins the handoff race below.

Consequences: a block with no certificate on itself or a descendant is unlandable; the landed set equals the certified set every node holds (V7); blocks above a view's lock never land. proven given the landing page adopts this binding. Carve-out: ranges landed with the sentinel views 254 (forced batch) and 255 (open-empty, dead mode, no-committee mode) carry no certificate, reach only the sequenced level before landing, and always require two ZK systems (single-proof mode never applies to them); the claims above are about certified ranges. The landing also refuses any view change that differs from the one first recorded on L1.

Lock reset. When a node observes a landed replacement or a landed forced batch (never a record: a recorded REPLACE VC resets nothing, round 4, R4H-5; invariant I4: nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement after the deadline machinery or 2Q(m) − m slashings, or, after a landing accepted with one leaf in single-proof mode, a DAO resolution of a proof conflict that voids that provisional landing and every block built on it (landing page §7; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01)), every certificate above lastLanded is void for it, every attester's lock resets to lastLanded, and the attester enters the explicit RESET state (§10): under the old opening object it may sign only a RESUME timeout and RESUME lock vote (also at term end, where the RESUME VC doubles as the handoff), never TIMEOUT or TERM_END, so no mandatory message after a reset can be a lock lie (round 3, S2). S3b exempts no lock, and the inbox keeps no ring of already-landed locks (round 5, R5S-1: an exemption over every already-landed lock covered every head a lander chooses to land, so a Q cartel could revert locked blocks with a TIMEOUT VC at a lock it had just landed, and no evidence verified). Why no honest signer needs an exemption: an honest lock vote of kind TIMEOUT, TERM_END or FALLBACK is at least the carried certificate the key verified at V3 when it last attested, and lock votes are monotone, so no honest vote of those kinds can sit below a certificate the same key attested outside a reset; inside a reset the key signs only RESUME, which S3b never reads. A TIMEOUT VC at a landed lock below a certified block is therefore a lock lie like any other, and its signers are slashed. The REPLACE and RESUME kinds are attested only when the landing page's replacement predicate holds at the signed L1 reference in the attester's own view, and that reference must be at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old and canonical. Every equivocation key (attestations, timeouts, headers, fork choice, gossip dedup, the slashing-protection record) is (term, view, opening object, redraw, height): a REPLACE fork by the same holder opens a new opening object, so nothing signed under the original one conflicts with it, and the lock-lie rule S3b compares locks only within one opening object.

Certified-but-private data (no escalation rule). Q + 1 colluders (leader plus Q attesters) can certify a block only they hold and lock a VC on it; honest attesters cannot sign that lock and the next leader cannot extend it: the one case where "certified" does not imply "available". Rounds 1 and 2 of the red team showed that no L1 rule can distinguish "unavailable" from "available but unprovable", and that any data-post answer the inbox cannot verify either slashes honest committees or prices nothing; the availability escalation of the earlier draft (a demand answered only by a landing) was therefore removed. The consequence of a private certificate is a stall until the landing page's replacement predicate opens (35 to 65 minutes after the last landing), after which the current holder reopens from lastLanded; the private blocks never land and their signers earn nothing, but they are not slashed. This is the same class of harm as Q colluders simply refusing to certify, extended from one term to the replacement delay; it is listed as limitation L3 and is the price of having no accusation object anywhere in the design.

7. The withholding defence, variant by variant

  • Unextendable. A block the committee never received has no certificate (V6 needs its execution). One block can be built on it (carrying the previous certificate) but cannot be certified, so nothing carries a certificate above it: private state is at most 2 blocks and dies within TIMEOUT.
  • Unlandable. §6.
  • W1 handoff ambush: impossible without slashing. The next leader's first block carries VC(t, v_last) with lock (h, H); honest nodes accept nothing else at h+1 in term t+1 (the VC may now form before termEnd, on the certificate of the block stamped termEnd − 1; nothing below depends on when it forms, only on its existence, its lock and its record; after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01). For A's withheld h+1..h+m to land, term t must close at a lock ≥ h+m, a second VC for (t, v_last): 2Q − K = 12 keys sign both (S3c), each fully slashed, and the recorded honest VC beats A's landing (minutes of proving) to L1. The only other object that can close (t, v_last) is a FALLBACK VC by a later committee, which L1 accepts only from termEnd + 12 s, only while no live VC of committee(t) is recorded, never below a recorded certificate of committee(t), only with a certificate at its lock, and which committee(t)'s own VC at a higher lock supersedes until a landing has consumed it (§5; round 5, R5S-5; round 6, R6S-3); a colluding leader(t+1) with Q of committee(t+1) therefore cannot land a private h + m above the honest lock (no certificate of committee(t) exists there), and it can void the honest blocks between a lower FALLBACK lock and h only by consuming that FALLBACK in the same L1 block as, or before, the honest record, the L1-ordering residual of limitation L5. If A releases just before term end and Q attesters certify, the TERM_END locks reflect it and B extends h+m; if fewer saw it, the lock stays at h and A's blocks are worthless. proven
  • W2 private MEV. No block reaches the committee, so no attester holds anything attested → timeout after 5 s; private blocks never land; STALE_MAX refuses blocks published more than 3 s after their timestamp. Residual: the ≤ 0.7-s certification window plus ordinary ordering power.
  • W3 prover grief. Landing needs no live sequencer: Q attesters hold every block below any lock and serve ranges for RETAIN_SECONDS; anyone lands. If all Q withhold, the range never lands and is replaced (limitation L3). Bytes availability, stated once (after the #22188 review, 2026-10-01): every attester that attested a block executed it (V6) and holds its bytes; attesters serve ranges for RETAIN_SECONDS; the design adds no L1-accountable publication duty, because a failure to serve is unprovable on L1, and an L1 publication duty costs what the bonded-fragment path of #22188 costs (the argument posted there).
  • W4 selective withholding. A missing block stops certification there; a node holding the next block range-requests from any bitmap member of the carried certificate, not from the withholder.
  • W5 seal withholding. No seal exists; certificates and VCs are assembled by anyone from gossiped signatures.
  • W6, W7. Proofs: anyone proves given availability. Blobs: impossible after landing (same-transaction hashes).

12. Attacks defended and not defended

  • TH3 silent sequencer: defended. Gap ≤ TIMEOUT + 1.5 s; cost is forfeited rewards and the 50-TAIKO bleed (silence is unprovable, so no bond and no eligibility effect). A holder that gossips headers without attestable payloads is silent for this purpose and is timed out at 5 s outside the cases of §1(d) (round 5, R5H-1).
  • TH4 equivocation: defended. Both headers kept by every node; anyone submits S1. Gossiped ≠ landed is impossible.
  • TH5 W1 to W7: defended (§7). W1 costs 12 slashed seats inside one committee; its cross-committee FALLBACK form is gated by L1 time, first record and the recorded certificate of committee(t) (§5; round 5, R5S-5; round 6, R6S-3), with the L1-ordering residual in L5.
  • TH6 stale-state MEV: reduced. Private state ≤ 2 blocks and ≤ TIMEOUT; a holder cannot stretch the window by gossiping unattestable headers (round 5, R5-C1).
  • TH8 bond griefing: defended. Every rule is a signature pair or an L1 timestamp; the accused always had a way out; silence is never slashed.
  • TH12 L1 reorg: fail closed. V1/V2 read LOOKBACK-old state; V5 reads ≥ 48-s-old blocks; a deeper reorg invalidates identically everywhere and the view times out.
  • TH14 P2P DoS: defended. Only leader and committee keys accepted before decompression; per-key dedupe and mute; bounded, identity-rated range requests; L1 Landed events are ground truth.
  • TH17 false anchor: defended locally by V5 and on L1 by EIP-2935 against the guest's anchored hashes. On L2 a checkpoint exists only through a reveal whose header RLP must hash to the recorded parentBeaconBlockRoot, so a fake anchor (attestable only if ≥ Q attesters skipped V5) can be revealed only on an unlanded chain, which cannot land and is replaced; bridge withdrawals wait for "landed" (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147).
  • Not defended: silent Q+1 collusion. A stall until the replacement predicate opens (35 to 65 minutes after the last landing, up to 75 with a landing announcement), unslashed; the private blocks land only if the colluders publish them; they cannot revert a locked block without 2Q(m) − m further provable slashings, forge state, or land beyond a lock (limitation L3).
  • Not defended: premature view change. Q colluders time out an honest leader early; time is local and unprovable; harm one view and ≤ 2 sequenced blocks; cost 0, bounded by rotation.
  • Not defended: blank terms. A minority holding ≥ m − Q(m) + 1 seats of the committee and of each of its two redraws (16 of 32 in the first draw) withholds certification of an honest primary's term; nothing lands, nobody is penalized, the term is not transferred; the revenue effect per owner structure is on the slashing page §5 (limitation L2).
  • Not defended: fallback race (limitation L5). A colluding committee(t) that abstains all term and records its own TERM_END VC just before committee(t+1)'s fallback can invalidate up to ANCHOR_MIN_AGE + L1 inclusion of the honest fallback chain; in the other direction, a Q cartel in committee(t+1), with a next leader that builds on its FALLBACK lock, whose FALLBACK below committee(t)'s highest certificate is consumed by a landing in the same L1 block as, or before, the honest certificate or view-change record, orphans every descendant of the FALLBACK lock until that landing, minutes of blocks (round 6, R6S-3). Both need ≥ Q colluders in one committee, outside T11, and the second also L1 ordering against the honest record, outside T1.

13. Failure analysis

  • Leaders (primary and backups) offline. Each view costs TIMEOUT + 0.5 s; after V_MAX the term idles; t+1 starts from the last VC. Nobody slashed; a view with certified blocks is landable by anyone, paid from the holder's landing reserve, and the design has no abandonment rule since round 4 (R4H-5: certified implies landable by anyone).
  • Leaders malicious. Equivocate (S1); withhold (worthless); future timestamps (policy refuses, timeout); wrong anchor (V5 rejects, unlandable); invalid payload (V6 rejects, treated as silence).
  • committee(t) all offline (or ≥ m − Q(m) + 1 abstaining in the committee and in both redraws). No certificates, no VC; the leader produces ≤ 2 blocks and holds (an honest attester that attested them never times it out, §1); at termEnd + 12 s committee(t+1) signs the FALLBACK VC at the last lock, which L1 accepts from that time, never below the certificate the leader recorded at + 2.5 s, and lands only with a certificate at its lock (§5), and if it is dark too, committee(t+2) after another 10 s, and so on within the landing horizon; loss ≈ one term per dark committee, no slash. If every registered attester is offline, nothing certifies or lands: the chain fails closed at the preconfirmation layer and state moves only through forced batches until attesters return or the sequencing page's dead mode restores landing. No-committee mode (a committee walk finding fewer than K_MIN_CERT = 8 seats, which the published launch condition of the Anchor page §5 is meant to avoid): no certificates or VCs exist, takeover only at term end, landings are certificate-free under the sentinel rules, the API exposes only sequenced and landed, and property P2 does not hold.
  • committee(t) all malicious. Certify whatever the leader signs, orphan sequenced or attested blocks by premature VC (cost 0), stall behind a private lock (unslashable; L3), revert a locked block (12 provable slashings), run a private chain until landing. They cannot land beyond a lock, forge a proof, or touch a term with an honest committee, except, as a later term's committee with a colluding next leader, through the L1-ordering residual of limitation L5 (§5); reach is otherwise bounded by one term.
  • Nodes, RPC, landers, provers. No validity role; data is guaranteed from any honest bitmap member for RETAIN_SECONDS.
  • Challengers all offline. Slashing is delayed, not lost (evidence windows outlive bond locks); structural defences need no challenger.

15. Robustness

Slot time 12 → 2 s: no rule uses slots; ANCHOR_MIN_AGE = 48 s covers 4 to 24 slots, always beyond ePBS's N+1 confirmation; ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + TERM ≈ 1.5 h stays inside EIP-2935 at every slot time (4.55 h at 2 s); per-landing verification (one 32-key aggregate verification per certificate or VC, at most 5, ≈ 165k each) ≈ 0.8 M gas worst case. Without Frame Transactions: V1 to V6, certificates, view changes and unlandability never touch L1; recordViewChange and slashing become ordinary transactions whose race losers pay gas. ePBS Empty slots: V5 anchors execution blocks by number and hash; an Empty slot adds none; if the L1 head stalls, leaders keep anchoring the last block (V5 is monotone, not "must advance"). L1 EIP-4788 gaps: L1's beacon-roots contract is never read; L2's own EIP-4788 contract is fed by each L2 header with an L1 execution block hash and keyed by L2 timestamp, so L1 slot gaps never reach it (anchor-free blocks, issue #22147).

16. Claims

  1. proven V1 to V6 are identical across honest nodes; no rule reads wall clock or "latest".
  2. proven A block the committee never received cannot be certified, so nothing above it carries a certificate; it cannot be the end of a landing nor lie under one.
  3. proven A locked block at height h cannot be below the lock of any later VC of the same redraw count unless ≥ 2Q(m) − m attesters are slashable (intersection argument, evidence S3b); the bound is 12 at full committees and published per term. Across a redraw the intersection can be empty: reverting a locked block then needs ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers plus Q of the redrawn committee, unslashable except for keys in both committees, with the probability given in §14 (round 4 fix; T11 in its single form is what bounds it). No lock is exempt: a TIMEOUT, TERM_END or FALLBACK vote by a key of committee(t) below a certificate that key attested is a lock lie whether or not that lock was already landed (round 5, R5S-1).
  4. proven given the landing binding: the landed set equals the certified set (V7); "gossiped ≠ landed" is impossible.
  5. proven W1 requires two VCs for one (t, v), hence 12 S3c-slashable keys, and the honest VC reaches L1 minutes before any landing of withheld blocks can; a FALLBACK by a later committee is not a second VC for this purpose, because L1 refuses it while the honest VC is live, before termEnd + 12 s and below a recorded certificate of committee(t), and lands it only with a certificate at its lock (round 5, R5S-5; round 6, R6S-3); given T1, the certificate record that committee(t)'s leader and attesters owe from termEnd + 2.5 s (staggered, §5) reaches L1 within one or two L1 blocks, before any landing can consume a lower FALLBACK, whether or not committee(t) forms a view change, and the L1-ordering residual is limitation L5.

17. Alternatives rejected

  • ECDSA attestation lists at K = 16 (1.4 KiB per envelope, keys under the EIP-8151 hazard, only 6 double-signers behind a locked block); mandatory parent certificate in every block (cadence becomes the round trip); a long provisional window of unattested progress (an unslashable reorg tail); majority quorum (two certificates then need ≈ 2 double-signers); Q = 3K/4 (17.5 % of terms fail at 20 % downtime); single-step certificates with a "max high" rule (one private certificate stalls every view); a fallback that may skip certified blocks; committee = backup permutation; batch = whole view landable only after its VC (exceeds one L1 block's blobs); availability response as a DA post (the inbox cannot check derivation);
