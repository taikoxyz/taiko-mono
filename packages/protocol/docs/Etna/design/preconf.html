Preconfirmation validity, certificates and the withholding defence

Every node decides locally and identically whether a 1-second block is valid. A committee of 32 bonded attesters certifies each block with one aggregate BLS signature; a block the committee never received cannot be certified, extended or landed. A term's batch is sealed not by the sequencer but by the committee's view change, so there is nothing for a sequencer to withhold. Sources: judge synthesis of the three B3 designs, aligned with Sequencing rights and Propose-with-proof.

What today does and why it fails

A preconfirmed block is a sequencer-signed envelope rebuilt by every node from its attributes; only key membership, parent presence and executability are checked locally, and the produced hash is never compared with the signed one (00 §9.2). Sequencing rights read the whitelist at "latest", the anchor calldata is never decoded, and what lands may differ from what was gossiped. Withholding is free and pays at handoff (01 §6). Non-receipt is unprovable, so the defence must combine bonded attestations, timeouts on L1-observable facts and making withheld data worthless.

1. Objects and keys

  • Term t (60 s) and view v within it: view 0 is the primary holder, view v ≥ 1 the v-th backup, v ≤ V_MAX = 4. leader(t, v) and committee(t) (up to K = 32 seats drawn by the sequencing page's §8 walk, which excludes the primary holder by owner and the backups by seat only; that page owns every selection rule) are pure functions of the registry at the snapshot S(t) and the cycle seed. With m = |committee(t)| the quorum is Q(m) = ⌊2m/3⌋ + 1 (22 at m = 32); a term with m < K_MIN_CERT = 8 has no committee and no certificates (no-committee mode). Every security statement below is written for m = 32 and scales as 2Q(m) − m: the number of provably slashable double-signers behind a locked block is 12 at m = 32, 6 at m = 16, 4 at m = 8, and nodes publish m beside the confirmation level.
  • Sequencer key: secp256k1, code-less at registration (TH18). Attester key: BLS12-381 (G1 public key, G2 signature) with a proof of possession, verified on L1 through EIP-2537. Both are bound to one owner, the slashable identity.
  • Signed header PH = {version, chainId, termId, view, vcHash, height, timestamp, parentHash, blockHash, txListHash, carriedCertHash, anchorNumber}, where vcHash is the hash of the object that opened the view and is carried by every block of the view (the opening object itself travels only with the first block); phHash = keccak(SSZ(PH)); seqSig = ECDSA(keccak(DOMAIN_BLOCK ‖ chainId ‖ phHash)). blockHash is the execution block hash the sequencer's engine produced; txListHash is the keccak of the exact compressed transaction bytes (needed so blob encoding is a pure function of the certified chain).
  • Attestation A_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_ATTEST ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ redraw ‖ height ‖ phHash)); the struct carries vcHash explicitly so every L1 rule can read the opening object without a header preimage, and redraw: u8 (the view's committee redraw count, §5; 0 for the first draw), which also enters Certificate, LockVote, ViewChange and every equivocation key (round 4, R4-C1). Attesting over phHash rather than blockHash is load-bearing: it commits to carriedCertHash, which makes the lock-lie slash objective.
  • Certificate C(h) = {t, v, vcHash, redraw, height, phHash, bitmap:u32, aggSig} with popcount(bitmap) ≥ Q over the committee of that redraw count; anyone holding Q attestations builds it (about 130 bytes). Two valid certificates over the same header are ordinary (any two Q-subsets of the attestations) and are never an offence.
  • Timeout TO_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_TIMEOUT ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ kind)), kind ∈ {TIMEOUT, TERM_END, FALLBACK, REPLACE, RESUME}: a statement that view (t, v, vcHash) should close, carrying no lock. Leader liveness signal (round 4, R4H-4; this page is normative for the trigger): an attester signs kind TIMEOUT for (t, v) iff, for TIMEOUT seconds by its clock, it has received no header of (t, v) with a valid leader signature (V1, V2) at a height above its lock, and no new certificate. Headers that fail V3 (carried-certificate lag) still count as liveness: a leader that is HOLDing because a minority abstains keeps gossiping signed headers (provisional, unextendable, not attested), so honest attesters never time it out; a dead leader sends nothing and every honest attester times it out. Consequence: an abstaining minority (≥ m − Q(m) + 1) can only make a term blank (no certificates, the leader keeps its view, nothing of it lands, no penalty to anyone); it can never take the view or apply MISS (invariant I3). TERM_END is signed at term end; FALLBACK by a later committee; REPLACE and RESUME after a reset (§6).
  • Redraw vote RD_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_REDRAW ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ n)), n ∈ {1, 2}: signed by an attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for TIMEOUT seconds (§5). One statement per (t, v, vcHash, n) with no conflicting form, so it needs no slashing rule.
  • Lock vote LV_i = BLS(sk_i, keccak(DOMAIN_LOCK ‖ chainId ‖ t ‖ v ‖ vcHash ‖ redraw ‖ kind ‖ lockHeight ‖ lockPhHash ‖ l1RefNumber ‖ l1RefHash)): the attester's vote for where the view's batch ends. l1Ref is zero except for kinds REPLACE and RESUME, where it is the L1 block (number, hash) at which the replacement predicate held, or the forced or replacement landing being resumed from; it is inside the signed bytes (round 3, S1). Lock votes under one (t, v, vcHash, kind) are monotone: a key may re-vote for a strictly higher lock that extends its previous one; two votes at one height with different phHash, or a later vote below an earlier one, are equivocation (S3c).
  • View change VC(t, v) = {t, v, closes: vcHash, redraw, kind, signerTerm, lockHeight, lockPhHash, l1Ref, bitmap, aggSig}: Q lock votes over one lock by the committee of the recorded redraw count, naming the opening object it closes (round 3, S6: a REPLACE or RESUME VC closes the original object and its hash opens a new one, so the fork's own later VC closes a different object and never collides with it on L1). It closes view (t, v), opens the next, and is the seal of the view's batch, which ends exactly at the lock. No sequencer-signed seal exists. A view is identified by (t, v, vcHash), the hash of the object that opened it; a REPLACE or RESUME reopens the same view number with a new opening object, so every equivocation key below includes the opening object.
  • Lock (per attester): the highest certificate it holds together with the block chain under it, or the highest height landed on L1 in its own view, whichever is higher; monotone within a term.

2. Produced hash equals signed hash

Enforced three times. (i) The node submits the received payload unchanged through engine_newPayload, never a rebuild, and requires VALID and engine hash equal to PH.blockHash; a mismatch rejects the block. (ii) Attesters sign phHash, which contains blockHash, only after (i). (iii) The ZK guest recomputes blockHash from blob data, rebuilds PH, and verifies ecrecover(seqSig) == leader(t, v) as a public input; an irreproducible signed header is unlandable. The Etna fields of PH are also the calldata of the first transaction anchorV5, so they sit inside blockHash and the guest can reconstruct PH from blobs. proven

3. The validity predicate

A node holding PH, seqSig, the payload, its message set and its L1 view accepts block b at height n (rules V1 to V6 below, plus V8 on the forced-inclusion page and V9 and V10 below) iff V1 to V6 hold. Failure is silence (the block is dropped, seqSig retained as evidence), except the two pending states, which are held and re-evaluated.

4. Attesting and confirmation levels

Attester i attests b iff V1 to V6, V8, V9 and V10 pass, b.height > lock_i, b extends the lock's chain, no timeout was signed for (t, v), and the policy bounds hold: PH.timestamp ∈ [now − STALE_MAX, now + CLOCK_SKEW]. Policy gates signing only, so clocks cannot desynchronise validity (T13). One attestation per (t, v, height) per key. Attestations gossip on their own topic; anyone aggregates, so the sequencer never holds anything the network does not. Attestation dedup, the gossip dedup key and the client's slashing-protection record all key on (t, v, vcHash, redraw, height).

5. View change: timeout, handoff, fallback

Attesters sign a timeout when, by local clock, for TIMEOUT = 5 s they have received no header of (t, v) with a valid leader signature above their lock and no new certificate (kind TIMEOUT, the leader liveness signal of §1, only while v < V_MAX) or when now ≥ termEnd(t) + END_GRACE (kind TERM_END). Clocks decide only whether to sign; the decision to switch is the existence of a VC, a signed object, so two honest nodes holding it switch identically and a node lacking it learns it from the next view's first block (V2). Two steps:

  1. Broadcast TO_i; rebroadcast every TO_REBROADCAST while the condition holds.
  2. Any node holding ≥ Q timeouts of one (t, v, vcHash, kind) proposes VC(t, v, kind, lock*) with lock* the highest certified block it holds, attaching C(lock*). Attester j casts a lock vote iff it holds C(lock*) and every block up to it (range-fetching if needed) and lock* ≥ its own lock. A higher honest lock arrives as a higher vote and the proposer re-proposes at it; every attester may re-vote upward (lock votes are monotone, §1), so with ≥ Q honest online attesters the VC forms at the honest maximum in one extra round trip (≈ 0.4 s) without any key signing two conflicting locks (round 3, S5).

After VC(t, v, TIMEOUT), leader(t, v+1) starts at lock* + 1 carrying the VC; after VC(t, v, TERM_END), leader(t+1, 0) likewise. A view without certificates yields a VC at the previous lock (an empty batch). After V_MAX view changes the term idles to its end. Fallback, any depth: if no VC closing term t exists by termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK, the committee of any later term t' within the landing horizon may sign a FALLBACK VC for t with signerTerm = t' for the highest (v, lock) it holds, once termEnd(t) + END_GRACE + VC_FALLBACK · (t' − t) has passed (so nearer committees have priority); the landing verifies the VC against committee(signerTerm). Two consecutive dark committees therefore delay a term's closure by one more fallback interval, never wedge it. A conflicting late VC is resolved on L1 by recordViewChange (first recorded per (t, v, closes) wins; a REPLACE or RESUME record is accepted only under the landing page's A3 condition, §6). The REPLACE and RESUME kinds serve the landing page's replacement rule.

Committee redraw (round 4, R4-C1). An attester that attested a block of (t, v) but has seen no certificate for TIMEOUT seconds may sign REDRAW(t, v, vcHash, n), n = 1, 2 (REDRAW_MAX = 2). With ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 such signatures (17 of 32) the committee of (t, v) is redrawn as the first K seats of the walk keccak(seed ‖ 0x03 ‖ t ‖ n ‖ m), with the same exclusions as the sequencing page's §8, for the same leader, the same view and the same opening object; attestations, certificates and lock votes of the redrawn committee carry n in their signed bytes, and the landing verifies certificates against the committee of the recorded redraw count for that view (the committee proof carries n). A REDRAW message has no conflicting form (one statement per (t, v, vcHash, n)), so it needs no slashing rule, and it changes no holder and applies no penalty. Rationale: it raises the blanking threshold from 11 to 16 of 32 in the first draw and requires the cartel to hold a blocking minority in every redraw. What a cartel majority (≥ 17 of 32) gains from redrawing a committee that has already certified is stated in §8 and §14: a re-roll toward Q in a fresh committee and, if it finds Q there, a lock reset whose only slashable keys are those sitting in both committees; at one third of the seats this path has probability about 6·10−7 per view against 4·10−5 for the slashable path, so the redraw is not the cheaper reversion in probability, only in provable cost.

6. Landing binding (V7) and certified-but-private data

The inbox keeps lastLanded = (t, v, height, phHash) and lastVCHash. A landing covers a contiguous range of up to four consecutive views (segments, landing page L4); for one segment, either it continues the open view from lastLanded.height + 1 with an end certificate whose phHash equals the guest's end phHash, so any certified prefix lands in blob-sized pieces; or it opens a view by supplying the VC chain from lastVCHash, each VC's lock equal to lastLanded when applied, the first block's vcHash (a guest public input) equal to the last VC's hash. recordViewChange(vc) may be called by anyone and stores the first VC per (t, v, closes); later landings must match it while it is live. For kinds REPLACE and RESUME the record is accepted only if vc.lockHeight == lastLanded.height, vc.lockPhHash == lastLanded.phHash and block.timestamp > replaceableFrom at record time, else refused, and it is dead once lastLanded.height > vc.lockHeight (ignored by the first-recorded rule, so the original view's own TIMEOUT or TERM_END VC for the same (t, v, closes) is then recorded or landed in its place, and no longer priority evidence; the fork's own later VCs close a different object, round 3 S6, and never collide with it); an accepted record has no closing effect (round 4, R4H-5, R4-C3): it is the first-recorded VC the fork's landing must match and priority evidence that defers the forced path by LAND_CHAIN_GRACE once, like a landing announcement. Until a replacement or forced batch lands, landing the original stays valid; whichever lands first wins, and the loser is invalid and free under the trailing-VERIFY shape (landing page A3). The record puts a term's end on L1 within seconds although its landing takes minutes, which is what wins the handoff race below.

Consequences: a block with no certificate on itself or a descendant is unlandable; the landed set equals the certified set every node holds (V7); blocks above a view's lock never land. proven given the landing page adopts this binding. Carve-out: ranges landed with the sentinel views 254 (forced batch) and 255 (open-empty, dead mode, no-committee mode) carry no certificate, reach only the sequenced level before landing, and always require two ZK systems (single-proof mode never applies to them); the claims above are about certified ranges. The landing also refuses any view change that differs from the one first recorded on L1.

Lock reset. When a node observes a landed replacement or a landed forced batch (never a record: a recorded REPLACE VC resets nothing, round 4, R4H-5; invariant I4: nothing voids a locked block except a landed replacement after the deadline machinery or 2Q(m) − m slashings), every certificate above lastLanded is void for it, every attester's lock resets to lastLanded, and the attester enters the explicit RESET state (§10): under the old opening object it may sign only a RESUME timeout and RESUME lock vote (also at term end, where the RESUME VC doubles as the handoff), never TIMEOUT or TERM_END, so no mandatory message after a reset can be a lock lie (round 3, S2); S3b additionally ignores any lock vote whose lock equals a landed head in the inbox's landing ring. The REPLACE and RESUME kinds are attested only when the landing page's replacement predicate holds at the signed L1 reference in the attester's own view, and that reference must be at least ANCHOR_MIN_AGE old and canonical. Every equivocation key (attestations, timeouts, headers, fork choice, gossip dedup, the slashing-protection record) is (term, view, opening object, redraw, height): a REPLACE fork by the same holder opens a new opening object, so nothing signed under the original one conflicts with it, and the lock-lie rule S3b compares locks only within one opening object.

Certified-but-private data (no escalation rule). Q + 1 colluders (leader plus Q attesters) can certify a block only they hold and lock a VC on it; honest attesters cannot sign that lock and the next leader cannot extend it: the one case where "certified" does not imply "available". Rounds 1 and 2 of the red team showed that no L1 rule can distinguish "unavailable" from "available but unprovable", and that any data-post answer the inbox cannot verify either slashes honest committees or prices nothing; the availability escalation of the earlier draft (a demand answered only by a landing) was therefore removed. The consequence of a private certificate is a stall until the landing page's replacement predicate opens (35 to 65 minutes after the last landing), after which the current holder reopens from lastLanded; the private blocks never land and their signers earn nothing, but they are not slashed. This is the same class of harm as Q colluders simply refusing to certify, extended from one term to the replacement delay; it is listed as limitation L3 and is the price of having no accusation object anywhere in the design.

7. The withholding defence, variant by variant

  • Unextendable. A block the committee never received has no certificate (V6 needs its execution). One block can be built on it (carrying the previous certificate) but cannot be certified, so nothing carries a certificate above it: private state is at most 2 blocks and dies within TIMEOUT.
  • Unlandable. §6.
  • W1 handoff ambush: impossible without slashing. The next leader's first block carries VC(t, v_last) with lock (h, H); honest nodes accept nothing else at h+1 in term t+1. For A's withheld h+1..h+m to land, term t must close at a lock ≥ h+m, a second VC for (t, v_last): 2Q − K = 12 keys sign both (S3c), each fully slashed, and the recorded honest VC beats A's landing (minutes of proving) to L1. If A releases just before term end and Q attesters certify, the TERM_END locks reflect it and B extends h+m; if fewer saw it, the lock stays at h and A's blocks are worthless. proven
  • W2 private MEV. No signed header reaches the committee → timeout after 5 s; private blocks never land; STALE_MAX refuses blocks published more than 3 s after their timestamp. Residual: the ≤ 0.7-s certification window plus ordinary ordering power.
  • W3 prover grief. Landing needs no live sequencer: Q attesters hold every block below any lock and serve ranges for RETAIN_SECONDS; anyone lands. If all Q withhold, the range never lands and is replaced (limitation L3).
  • W4 selective withholding. A missing block stops certification there; a node holding the next block range-requests from any bitmap member of the carried certificate, not from the withholder.
  • W5 seal withholding. No seal exists; certificates and VCs are assembled by anyone from gossiped signatures.
  • W6, W7. Proofs: anyone proves given availability. Blobs: impossible after landing (same-transaction hashes).

8. Slashing conditions this block contributes

Evidence is always signatures or one L1 fact; full formats and payouts are on the Slashing page. S1 sequencer equivocation (two seqSig on different headers at one (t, v, vcHash, height)); S3a attester equivocation (two attestations or two valid certificates at one (t, v, vcHash, height)); S3b lock lie (a lock vote with lock L plus an attestation by the same key on a block whose carried certificate is above L; timeouts carry no lock); S3c lock-vote equivocation; S3d false replacement (a REPLACE or RESUME lock vote whose signed L1 reference did not satisfy the replacement predicate: an L1 fact); S6 invalid named object; S8 coded sequencer key. S3b compares locks only within one opening object, at any redraw count (a lock vote under any n of an opening object below a certificate the same key attested under any n of that object is a lock lie; the redraw count stays in the equivocation keys of S3a and S3c only), and never applies to REPLACE or RESUME kinds. Stated honestly (round 4 fix): the 2Q(m) − m provable-slashing bound behind a locked block holds within one redraw count; reverting a locked block across a redraw needs ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers of the committee at n − 1 plus Q of the redrawn committee, and only the keys that sit in both committees are slashable, because the REDRAW precondition (no certificate seen) is not verifiable on L1. Its probability under T11 is in the §14 table. REDRAW votes have no conflicting form and no rule. The interfaces page is normative for evidence types. Silence is deliberately not slashable and never changes eligibility: a colluding committee could manufacture it against an honest leader at zero cost; silence forfeits rewards and costs the 50-TAIKO absence bleed, nothing else.

9. Gossip and range sync (TH22, TH23)

Topics /etna/{chainId}/1/{blocks, attestations, views}. Pre-relay validation: signer ∈ leader(t, v) or committee(t) for t within one term of current; height within [lock − 64, lock + 512]; one message per (signer, t, v, height, type), a second relayed once as evidence then the signer muted; envelopes ≤ MAX_ENVELOPE (390,123 bytes, the V9 bound). Streams status, blocks_by_range (start, count ≤ 64, certifiedOnly) returning (envelope, certificate) pairs with at most CERT_LAG_MAX + 1 trailing uncertified items, and views_by_term. Requests signed by a registered key are served at RANGE_RATE, unsigned at a quarter of it, one in flight per peer. Serving is a duty of bitmap members and RPC nodes for RETAIN_SECONDS (2 h); older blocks come from L1 blobs. This replaces today's unauthenticated per-hash request topics and 768-entry cache.

12. Attacks defended and not defended

  • TH3 silent sequencer: defended. Gap ≤ TIMEOUT + 1.5 s; cost is forfeited rewards and the 50-TAIKO bleed (silence is unprovable, so no bond and no eligibility effect).
  • TH4 equivocation: defended. Both headers kept by every node; anyone submits S1. Gossiped ≠ landed is impossible.
  • TH5 W1 to W7: defended (§7). W1 costs 12 slashed seats.
  • TH6 stale-state MEV: reduced. Private state ≤ 2 blocks and ≤ TIMEOUT.
  • TH8 bond griefing: defended. Every rule is a signature pair or an L1 timestamp; the accused always had a way out; silence is never slashed.
  • TH12 L1 reorg: fail closed. V1/V2 read LOOKBACK-old state; V5 reads ≥ 48-s-old blocks; a deeper reorg invalidates identically everywhere and the view times out.
  • TH14 P2P DoS: defended. Only leader and committee keys accepted before decompression; per-key dedupe and mute; bounded, identity-rated range requests; L1 Landed events are ground truth.
  • TH17 false anchor: defended locally by V5 and on L1 by EIP-2935 against the guest's anchored hashes.
  • Not defended: silent Q+1 collusion. A stall until the replacement predicate opens (35 to 65 minutes after the last landing, up to 75 with a landing announcement), unslashed; the private blocks land only if the colluders publish them; they cannot revert a locked block without 2Q(m) − m further provable slashings, forge state, or land beyond a lock (limitation L3).
  • Not defended: premature view change. Q colluders time out an honest leader early; time is local and unprovable; harm one view and ≤ 2 sequenced blocks; cost 0, bounded by rotation.
  • Not defended: blank terms. A minority holding ≥ m − Q(m) + 1 seats of the committee and of each of its two redraws (16 of 32 in the first draw) withholds certification of an honest primary's term; nothing lands, nobody is penalized, the term is not transferred; the revenue effect per owner structure is on the slashing page §5 (limitation L2).
  • Not defended: fallback race. A colluding committee(t) that abstains all term and records its own TERM_END VC just before committee(t+1)'s fallback can invalidate up to ANCHOR_MIN_AGE + L1 inclusion of the honest fallback chain; needs ≥ Q colluders in one committee, outside T11.

13. Failure analysis

  • Leaders (primary and backups) offline. Each view costs TIMEOUT + 0.5 s; after V_MAX the term idles; t+1 starts from the last VC. Nobody slashed; a view with certified blocks is landable by anyone, paid from the holder's landing reserve, and the design has no abandonment rule since round 4 (R4H-5: certified implies landable by anyone).
  • Leaders malicious. Equivocate (S1); withhold (worthless); future timestamps (policy refuses, timeout); wrong anchor (V5 rejects, unlandable); invalid payload (V6 rejects, treated as silence).
  • committee(t) all offline (or ≥ m − Q(m) + 1 abstaining in the committee and in both redraws). No certificates, no VC; the leader produces ≤ 2 blocks and holds, gossiping signed provisional headers so it is never timed out; at termEnd + 12 s committee(t+1) signs the FALLBACK VC at the last lock, and if it is dark too, committee(t+2) after another 10 s, and so on within the landing horizon; loss ≈ one term per dark committee, no slash. If every registered attester is offline, nothing certifies or lands: the chain fails closed at the preconfirmation layer and state moves only through forced batches until attesters return or the sequencing page's dead mode restores landing. No-committee mode (a committee walk finding fewer than K_MIN_CERT = 8 seats, which the published launch condition of the Anchor page §5 is meant to avoid): no certificates or VCs exist, takeover only at term end, landings are certificate-free under the sentinel rules, the API exposes only sequenced and landed, and property P2 does not hold.
  • committee(t) all malicious. Certify whatever the leader signs, orphan sequenced or attested blocks by premature VC (cost 0), stall behind a private lock (unslashable; L3), revert a locked block (12 provable slashings), run a private chain until landing. They cannot land beyond a lock, forge a proof, or touch a term with an honest committee; reach is bounded by one term.
  • Nodes, RPC, landers, provers. No validity role; data is guaranteed from any honest bitmap member for RETAIN_SECONDS.
  • Challengers all offline. Slashing is delayed, not lost (evidence windows outlive bond locks); structural defences need no challenger.

15. Robustness

Slot time 12 → 2 s: no rule uses slots; ANCHOR_MIN_AGE = 48 s covers 4 to 24 slots, always beyond ePBS's N+1 confirmation; ANCHOR_MAX_AGE + LAND_WINDOW_MAX + TERM ≈ 1.5 h stays inside EIP-2935 at every slot time (4.55 h at 2 s); per-landing verification (one pairing per certificate or VC, ≤ 5) ≈ 0.6 M gas worst case. Without Frame Transactions: V1 to V6, certificates, view changes and unlandability never touch L1; recordViewChange and slashing become ordinary transactions whose race losers pay gas. ePBS Empty slots: V5 anchors execution blocks by number and hash; an Empty slot adds none; if the L1 head stalls, leaders keep anchoring the last block (V5 is monotone, not "must advance"). EIP-4788 gaps: not consumed.

16. Claims

  1. proven V1 to V6 are identical across honest nodes; no rule reads wall clock or "latest".
  2. proven A block the committee never received cannot be certified, so nothing above it carries a certificate; it cannot be the end of a landing nor lie under one.
  3. proven A locked block at height h cannot be below the lock of any later VC of the same redraw count unless ≥ 2Q(m) − m attesters are slashable (intersection argument, evidence S3b); the bound is 12 at full committees and published per term. Across a redraw the intersection can be empty: reverting a locked block then needs ⌊m/2⌋ + 1 REDRAW signers plus Q of the redrawn committee, unslashable except for keys in both committees, with the probability given in §14 (round 4 fix; T11 in its single form is what bounds it).
  4. proven given the landing binding: the landed set equals the certified set (V7); "gossiped ≠ landed" is impossible.
  5. proven W1 requires two VCs for one (t, v), hence 12 S3c-slashable keys, and the honest VC reaches L1 minutes before any landing of withheld blocks can.
  6. proven Every slashing condition is a signature pair, a code-size check, or an L1 timestamp; silence is never slashed.
  7. proven Two honest nodes reach the same takeover decision from the same messages.
  8. assumed T9 (hop ≤ 300 ms, newPayload ≤ 200 ms): certification ≈ 0.7 s, locking ≈ 1.7 s. Resolved by a 32-attester devnet.
  9. assumed T11, in its single form (round 4, R4-C1): fewer than one third of the seats are malicious; for liveness, fewer than m − Q(m) + 1 abstainers in a committee and its redraws; for safety, fewer than Q colluders.
  10. assumed BLS12-381 with proof of possession; 32-key aggregate verification ≈ 115k gas under EIP-2537; to be measured.
  11. open Whether the guest should verify VCs (gas saved per VC versus circuit surface).
  12. open The fallback race if anti-monopoly cannot keep p ≤ 0.2 for every entity.

17. Alternatives rejected

  • ECDSA attestation lists at K = 16 (1.4 KiB per envelope, keys under the EIP-8151 hazard, only 6 double-signers behind a locked block); mandatory parent certificate in every block (cadence becomes the round trip); a long provisional window of unattested progress (an unslashable reorg tail); majority quorum (two certificates then need ≈ 2 double-signers); Q = 3K/4 (17.5 % of terms fail at 20 % downtime); single-step certificates with a "max high" rule (one private certificate stalls every view); a fallback that may skip certified blocks; committee = backup permutation; batch = whole view landable only after its VC (exceeds one L1 block's blobs); availability response as a DA post (the inbox cannot check derivation); a separate sequencer-signed seal (a second withholdable object); timeout-versus-attestation equivocation as a rule (not objective); anchoring via EIP-4788 roots; rebuild-from-attributes hash checks (today's shape); timestamp upper bound = L1 landing time (the cause of today's retroactive invalidity).

18. Open questions

  1. Should a HOLDing leader's provisional headers be rate-limited or bounded in height above the tip, so the liveness signal cannot be used to keep a view alive for a full term without certified progress?
  2. Is a size-scaled quorum acceptable at launch (a committee of 8 gives a 4-key credit bound at the locked level), or should the sentinel no-committee mode extend up to m = 16 so that small registries never advertise a weak "locked" level?
  3. Is a 7-day evidence window after exit sufficient, given that locked-block safety is a deterrent? A permanent evidence registry costs one slot per slash.
  4. Should VC verification move into the guest to keep landings under ~300k gas of verification at 2-s slots?
  5. Whether to expose a TEE fast-lane signal at the sequenced level (assumption A3); nothing above changes.
