What must remain true,
and what still needs evidence

Seven review properties connect the protocol rules to concrete ways of breaking them. Resource and financial budgets then separate a specified bound from a demonstrated operating limit.

Core specification Public staging Roles and economics Checkpoints Arguments and verdict Learning course
Current deployment verdict: OPEN. The specified interfaces and conditional arguments do not establish a sustainable one-second product, affordable participation, complete forced-Bridge coverage or implementation security. This page consolidates existing rules and their evidence obligations; it adds no role, fee, timeout, proof mode or checkpoint writer. A failed resource or product gate requires a reviewed revision, not an unrestricted pass label.

Proven means a deduction under the stated rules and assumptions. Assumed identifies a premise about execution, cryptography, inclusion or economic participation. Open means evidence or a human decision is still needed. A proposed parameter is not a measured capacity result.

The presentation adopts the property-first approach and explicit dependency chain in the competing design’s arguments and parameter dependencies. Those links pin the reviewed version; they do not import its committee, reserve or degradation rules. See the comparison and changes.

Seven properties to try to falsify

Assumed throughout: the eventual implementation follows the specification, the required proof predicate is sound, Ethereum authenticates canonical history, and upgrades preserve the named safety boundary. Each row identifies a specific counterexample rather than substituting “permissionless” or “ZK” for an argument.

P1 · Open canonical admission.
Roles §1; core admission.		 Proven under the rules: a matching proof, exact parent, eligible data, forced work, producer authorization and public rent suffice. Optional service holders neither reserve nor veto the head. Take every current publisher, custodian and worker offline. Produce a valid successor from a fresh address. A required signature, allowlist entry or reservation held by an absent party would falsify P1. Open: inspect all deployed admission selectors and rehearse recovery with a new funded participant. Eventual proof production and L1 inclusion remain assumptions.
P2 · The landed data and proved execution are identical.
Staging; codec.		 Proven conditional binding: the complete staged context, final actual DA, body encoding, KZG relation, execution outputs and parent share the prescribed commitments. A stage chooses no winner. Stage body A; present a proof of body B or a different origin; land a matching-looking descriptor. Alternatively reveal the complete body for the first time at landing. Any accepted example falsifies P2. Open: independent circuit/parser vectors, exact blob reconstruction, malformed-boundary cases and cross-client execution. A digest alone is not data availability.
P3 · Every usable checkpoint has authenticated provenance.
Checkpoint persistence; migration.		 Proven under the specified gates: L1 writes follow accepted proofs. L2 reads the exact standard hash-recording code and binds a full header to that origin or a permanent pin. Legacy writers cannot remain enabled in Etna mode. Enter the first Etna block, invoke the retained legacy root writer, and inject a forged root before any ordinary pin. Also try a noncanonical header sharing only selected fields with a pinned header. Any successful checkpoint is a custody failure. Open: live proxy selectors, runtime-code hashes, storage layout and first-block build/import/prover parity. Pins authenticate hashes; they do not guarantee an archive still has the header.
P4 · A liability cannot spend another promise’s reserve.
Roles §4–6; accountability.		 Proven by reservation: each position locks its own 10 TAIKO; a worker separately reserves 1,000 TAIKO; accepted bounties are escrowed. A settled position is slashed at most once and pending defaults settle before release. Use one position for two incompatible promises, self-report one, then collect a second payout or withdraw funds backing another live duty. Changing reward beneficiaries must not release a reserved obligation. Open: ledger conservation across every transition, overflow handling, duplicate evidence and delayed release. Solvency does not establish operating liquidity or profitable service.
P5 · New requests cannot change already frozen proof work.
Forced inclusion; forced prefix.		 Proven under the snapshot rule: the accepted parent fixes the next FIFO cut and time. Later enqueues affect later snapshots. Freshness and cursor progress prevent a permanently frozen queue under the liveness assumptions. Build a correct proof for parent H, then enqueue a request immediately before submission. If the added tail alone makes that proof invalid, P5 fails. Separately test repeated fresh accepted heads that never process an eligible old request. Open: exact queue proofs/outcomes, bootstrap boundary and supported Bridge transaction sizes. Unlimited paid backlog has no uniform short service guarantee.
P6 · Penalty follows a bounded signed obligation, not an accusation of intent.
Evidence predicates.		 Proven under the rules: an authenticated conflict or missed fixed publication/job deadline supplies the predicate. Historical timestamps prevent a late reveal or later parent consumption from erasing an existing default. Reveal after the absolute publication deadline and try to cure the miss. Slash an honest lost-race worker despite parent consumption before its deadline. Or submit evidence after collateral release and claim an unlimited historical guarantee. Open: detection and reporter incentives. Global nonreceipt is not objectively provable; hidden evidence may expire and congestion can cause honest contractual defaults.
P7 · Migration preserves custody and never accepts unserviceable pre-launch escrow.
Migration state machine; writer modes.		 Proven under the transition rules: frozen custody addresses, old messages and obligations survive. H0 is exact and unique; new force intake starts only in ACTIVE. The first Etna block uses standard system calls and needs no anchor transaction. Enqueue in PREPARED or DRAINING and strand funds; activate with an undrained old obligation; choose a different H0; or overwrite old checkpoint/message replay state. Any successful trace falsifies P7. Open: authenticated production layout/deployment comparison and a full legacy-to-Etna rehearsal. DAO upgrade honesty remains a named assumption.

Solvent does not mean affordable

Proven accounting distinction: reserved TAIKO pays specified liabilities; it is not automatic ETH funding for publication, rent, proving or gas. Our proof jobs are voluntary and our service market has no demonstrated profitable equilibrium. The competing design’s reserve/recovery analysis makes this distinction especially useful: even correctly separated balances need a cash-flow stress schedule.

A sustained-rate rent lower bound

Proven from the unchanged rent rule: let P=0.05 ETH and d=900 seconds. Start at an activation or acceptance that sets the rent reference. Let the next N acceptances have nonnegative time gaps a₁,…,aₙ, ending T=Σaᵢ seconds after that reference. Rounding the quote upward cannot reduce its charge. For each gap, q(aᵢ) ≥P(1−aᵢ/d): if the gap exceeds d the right side is negative and the actual quote is zero. Summing and using nonnegative total rent gives:

totalRent ≥ P × max(0, N − T/d)
long-run rent/day ≥ 86,400 × P × max(0, r − 1/d)
where r is the sustained accepted-segment rate, in segments/second.

Endpoint convention: the finite inequality starts exactly at a reset and includes the charges of its N successors. An arbitrary reporting interval can omit an already elapsed rent decay; discarding its first acceptance gives the conservative bound P×max(0,N−1−T/d). This loses at most one ceiling P and disappears in the long-run average. The daily rates below are long-run daily equivalents, not a claim that every short calendar-day window has the exact same charge. Same-timestamp acceptances have aᵢ=0 and are included.

Proven necessary capacity condition: let b be sustained accepted L2 blocks/second, x the total encoded bytes/second in their segment bodies, and f the required forced-entry processing rate/second. The current counts and blob-mode cap require:

r ≥ max(b/60, x/122880, f/4)
rent/day ≥ 86,400 ×0.05 × max(0, max(b/60,x/122880,f/4) −1/900) ETH.

The data rate includes headers, forced records, all transaction bytes and RLP overhead. Calldata mode is stricter. The force term applies when that work is admitted and must be processed at the stated sustained rate; it is not a uniform bound for an arbitrary backlog. These are optimistic necessary conditions: gas limits, actual proof throughput, L1 capacity, origin freshness and competing discarded work can make the feasible rate lower or cost higher.

This strengthens the response to points 2–3 of the reciprocal review; it does not establish what actual participants can afford.

R4 scope: a one-second soft issuance interval alone does not mathematically require one accepted block/second: soft branches can lose. The sustained product analyzed here is stronger and useful to state explicitly—a continuously issued branch that keeps bounded landing lag needs its accepted block rate to catch up. Issuing and repeatedly discarding speculative blocks satisfies no demonstrated application demand. Whether revocable previews are the intended product remains open; the calculation is not an impossibility proof for R1–R7.

Parameter choices have a security cost

Proven necessary budget frontier: for an allowed rent-only budget E ETH/day and target r>1/900, require P ≤E/[86,400×(r−1/900)]. For one accepted block/second at the minimum r=1/60, a hypothetical 1-ETH/day budget requires P ≤1/1344 ETH ≈0.000744047619 ETH, about 67.2 times below the current ceiling. That is a conditional calculation, not a new chosen budget or parameter. Lowering P proportionally lowers the bounded capture cost q(D); raising capacity requires rechecking execution, data, proving and transaction fit. Raising only the block-count cap would not remove the existing common-origin timestamp bound of origin.timestamp+60.

Open funding gate: supply authenticated demand and revenue scenarios, total winner and loser costs, and an entrant cash-flow schedule. Count only external fees, external MEV and actually funded service payments as revenue; a producer’s own fees returned through its coinbase are internal transfers. Deduct rent, both DA publications, both proof backends, L1 gas, fragment publication, collateral opportunity cost and operations. Optional proof sharing helps only identical full public inputs; Frame invalidation cannot refund computation or prior staging. A positive balance in one scenario does not establish independent participation or prevent a better-funded cartel.

Open production-profile feasibility: choose the minimum useful transaction workload, supported forced operations, sustained block rate and affordable participant/user budgets, then demonstrate the complete profile with measured proving and L1 resources. The current constants have no demonstrated operating point meeting those goals. If the selected workload exceeds these bounds or revenues cannot fund it, that is a parameter/profile failure requiring a revised, reviewed design; “permissionless in principle” does not cure it. The stronger statement that every rational producer must wait 900 seconds is also unproved: delay preferences, fees, MEV and subsidies can change willingness to pay. None of those revenues may be assumed without evidence.

Full fragment publication is an additional capacity and funding demand

Proven arithmetic under an assumed price scenario: publishing 10,000 nonzero fragment-data bytes per second at a modeled 64 gas per byte and 20 gwei costs 10,000 ×64 =640,000 gas/s, or 640,000 ×20×10⁻⁹ ×86,400 =1,105.92 ETH/day. A 32,768-byte fragment alone contributes 2,097,152 data-floor gas, or approximately 0.04194304 ETH at that price. This byte-only model omits metadata and the rest of the operation. Compute the whole charge using the active fork’s floor/execution/state-gas rules rather than blindly adding quantities already covered by a floor. Staging and final republication are separate transactions; the surveyed EIP-7976 64-gas floor treats zero and nonzero bytes equally. Recheck the active fork before applying any byte-price distinction. The assumed gas price and traffic rate are not measured usage or a forecast.

Open economic conclusion: this is a serious constraint on a high-data-rate bonded soft service. It does not prove publication is physically impossible at every workload or that no customer will pay, but it gives no basis for claiming the intended workload is affordable. Multiple signers can share the same exact fragment publication; there is no need to multiply these data bytes by signer count. Removing or replacing the mandatory calldata duty would change the reviewed withholding mechanism and requires new analysis; this comparison makes no such change.

A paid backlog can delay processing for days

Proven conditional bound: the existing FIFO argument gives processedAt ≤t +1,020 +(1 +ceil((p+1)/4))×Δ. With 1,000 still-unconsumed predecessors and Δ=660 seconds, this is 1,020 +252×660 =167,340 seconds, or 46 hours 29 minutes after enqueue. This is the conservative bound under A-LIVE(Δ), not a measured delay or a promise that every adversary can force the exact upper limit. A higher processing rate can improve it; a violated progress assumption invalidates the wall-clock bound.

Proven attack-budget example: posting 1,000 requests into an initially empty queue without intervening processing costs 10.5 ETH in queue fees, of which 5.25 ETH remains an irreversible sink even if the requester recovers every processing credit. Gas, data publication, proving and opportunity costs remain additional. That is concrete friction, not an adequate short-latency guarantee for every user. Requests above 2,048 bytes or 1M gas have no mandatory path in this revision. Open human decision: determine acceptable waiting times and the required Bridge-operation coverage before choosing production constants.

Proven sustained-service limit: at one segment per 60 seconds, at most four forced entries/minute—5,760/day—can be processed, even if every required entry is eligible. A higher persistent arrival rate grows backlog until fees or another constraint suppress demand, or accepted segment frequency increases. This rate ceiling and the conditional per-request delay proof answer different questions; neither certifies a short delay under arbitrary paid load.

Proven assurance boundary: local execution tells a user whether a soft block is valid in its observed view; it supplies neither a unique network-wide soft chain nor a promise that its branch will win. Mature competing branches, shallow origin reorgs and proving advantages remain real limitations. Open product decision: decide whether this revocable preview is sufficient for the intended applications. If irrevocable or compensated preconfirmation is required, that is additional mechanism work, not a change of terminology or a benchmark task.

Bounded calls can still create unbounded history

Proven from the design: permanent origin membership in SignalService, accepted receipts, data-stage records and publication/evidence records grow with distinct accepted writes. Repeated pins of the same hash add no new membership, but there is no fixed lifetime cap on distinct pins. The EIP-4788 ring is bounded; our permanent membership mapping deliberately removes its expiry from later checkpoint reveal.

Proven canonical persistence bound: with N accepted Etna segments after activation in one canonical history, the one-origin-per-segment rule permits at most N distinct recorded origins. Each can add at most one pin value word and two checkpoint value words: at most 3N new 32-byte storage values attributable to these paths. See the derivation and exclusions. At one acceptance with a distinct origin every 60 seconds for 365 days, N=525,600: pin values alone use at most 16,819,200 raw bytes; pin plus checkpoint values at most 50,457,600. These are scenario bounds, not measured database sizes, an L1-slot assumption, a fixed lifetime cap or a bound on the whole protocol.

Open · storage/resource engineers: measure fresh-slot costs and include trie keys, nodes, database amplification, witness and synchronization costs, logs, receipts and archives. Exclude the fixed legacy baseline when reporting this incremental bound, but include it in total node requirements. Accepted-state persistence excludes discarded branches; their stage, receipt and archival costs still belong to other parts of the complete resource model. Include the integrated-coinbase recovery above: L2 gas payment alone does not establish an irreversible storage price.

Open human decision: accept measured persistence costs or request a separately specified and reviewed pricing/retention change. A pin-only fee would leave the positive-timestamp reveal path able to create two persistent checkpoint words. Pricing both paths needs exact fee/sink, duplicate/refund and queued-funding semantics. Pruning a permanent pin would invalidate the nonexpiring force-and-reveal path. Neither an invented fee nor pruning is adopted by this dashboard.

How to close an open launch item

Proposed review workflow, with no contract authority: the engineer responsible for each row supplies reproducible inputs, implementation/circuit versions, the complete operation envelope, observed results and the comparison with the owning rule. Human protocol stakeholders decide whether economic and service-risk assumptions match the intended product. They cannot label a failed hard bound “accepted” without revising its specification and reviewing the affected safety/liveness arguments.

Open launch gate: demonstrate every mandatory proof, parser, root writer, migration transition and forced operation claimed as supported. Demonstrate that the stated maximum resources fit the intended L1 fork and that the chosen latency workload can be sustained. Establish plausible willing, funded participation without implying perpetual profitability. Where only a bounded subset is supported, expose that subset to users instead of presenting a universal guarantee.
