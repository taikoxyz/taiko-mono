01 — State of the union

We won the protocol. We have not yet won a business.

Credit where due: over the last twelve months this team shipped the most credible based-rollup stack in production. Shasta (mainnet April 2026) rewrote the protocol ground-up — 22× cheaper proposing, 8× cheaper proving, guardian provers deleted, ETH bonds and forced inclusion built in. Preconfirmations have run on mainnet since August 2025 at ~2-second confirmation. We hit 100% ZK coverage of mainnet blocks in December 2025. And when the June bridge exploit hit — a leaked SGX signing key in a public repo plus a debug-attestation gap, $1.7M drained — we halted, recapitalized 1:1, recovered in ten days with no user losses, and shipped the Unzen hardfork through the DAO in five weeks. Unzen makes at least one ZK proof mandatory on every finalized batch. The two-TEE combination that finalized forged proofs can never finalize again.

02 — The market we actually face

Four hard truths, one open window

T1Generic L2 scaling is over as a category.

Fusaka (Dec 2025) took blobs to target 14 / max 21 — running at 20–30% utilization — and L1 gas to 60M, heading "toward and beyond 100M." Post-Fusaka, every major L2 charges under a cent, and all L2s combined now earn less in daily fees than Ethereum L1 itself (~$221K/day). ENS cancelled Namechain in February and moved ENSv2 back to L1 because registrations got 99% cheaper — the first announced L2 killed by L1 scaling, and it was our stack's flagship deal. Vitalik said it plainly in February:

"You are not scaling Ethereum." — Vitalik Buterin, Feb 2026, declaring the rollup-centric roadmap over; the EF's March guidance tells L2s to pursue synchronous composability with L1 instead.

T2Our differentiators are being commoditized — on schedule.

“Based” is no longer only ours: RISE launched mainnet in May 2026 with based sequencing and a first-party perp DEX, and is already #2 among all L2s by activity. “Type-1” is no longer only ours: Linea — with ConsenSys capital and a SWIFT pilot — is executing a type-1 transition. And Ethereum-equivalent ZK proving is now a commodity: the EF declared real-time proving achieved in December; anyone can prove a mainnet block in ~12 seconds on a $100K GPU cluster.

The honest conclusion: equivalence and proving speed are table stakes. What is not commoditized: L1 sequencing with live preconfirmations at mainnet scale (only us), a ZK-mandatory multiproof that survived a real incident (only us), and a credible path to the first Stage-2, operator-less rollup (open to us first).

T3Demand follows apps and distribution — never infrastructure.

2026's activity leaders are all app-led or distribution-led: Lighter (perp chain), RISE (first-party RISEx), Robinhood Chain (19.6M tx/day, brokerage distribution), Base (Coinbase). Revenue concentrates in four verticals: perps ($1T/month, structurally lost to sub-100ms chains — we concede it), prediction markets ($111B in Q2, +1,764% YoY — and Polymarket is publicly leaving Polygon with no chain chosen), RWA/tokenized equities ($33.5B, +184% YoY, ~60% on Ethereum), and stablecoin settlement ($307B supply; Stripe's Tempo and Circle's Arc attacking with fiat distribution). Meanwhile every incentive-led launch — Blast (−97% TVL), Monad, our own Trailblazers era — decayed on the same curve. We will not run another points program. The winning sequence, demonstrated by RISE and MegaETH: secure one flagship app, prove organic volume gated, then scale.

T4Macro: capital demands revenue, not narrative.

BTC ~$65K (−49% from the October 2025 peak), ETH ~$1,900, and AI absorbing ~80% of global venture capital. The crypto stories that still get funded are the ones with cash flows: stablecoins, RWA, and — with CLARITY facing a September Senate vote — regulated market infrastructure. Chains earn an order of magnitude less than apps (Solana, the best chain business, ~$27M/month; Tether earns that in 36 hours). Two consequences for us: our economics must capture sequencing value we currently leak to gateways, and our token's ~2%/month unlock schedule into a $14M float is a board-level problem no R&D mechanism can outrun.

The open window

Ethereum is reorganizing around exactly what we already are. The EF's L1-zkEVM program (EIP-8025) will have validators verify ZK proofs of Ethereum-equivalent execution — our proof stack, enshrined. ePBS ships in Glamsterdam (~Q4 2026); FOCIL follows in Hegotá — both strengthen based sequencing. Native rollups (the EXECUTE precompile) reached proof-of-concept in March; a type-1, based, ZK-mandatory rollup is the closest thing to a native rollup that exists today. And the demand side for synchronous L1↔L2 composability has organized itself into the Ethereum Economic Zone — Aave, CoW Swap, Safe, Flashbots, Titan, Beaver, Monerium, xStocks — with pilots starting now. On that last one we take a deliberate position: the demand is real, but the technology is unproven, and it is not ours to de-risk this cycle. We watch it, we stay compatible with it, and we compete on what is live today — L1 sequencing, real-time proofs, and the Stage-2 trust arc.

03 — Thesis

Stop competing as an L2. Become real-time Ethereum.

In twelve months, Taiko is the execution layer that is Ethereum: sequenced by L1, proven in real time, and the first rollup to reach Stage 2 — with no operator anyone must trust.

Every deliverable below serves one of four claims, each verifiable by outsiders: trust (Stage 2, ZK-first multiproof), speed (sub-second preconfs, ~1-slot ZK finality), capacity (a production-complete Rust client and proven gigagas headroom), and economics (sequencing value captured, publicly dashboarded). Proven technology only — nothing on this roadmap depends on a primitive that hasn't run in production somewhere. We are not selling blockspace. We are selling Ethereum, faster.

We explicitly stop / concede
  • Sub-100ms CLOB perps — Lighter, RISEx, MegaETH own that latency class. We settle and collateralize; we don't match orders.
  • Points-driven growth. Dead playbook; we have the scar tissue.
  • TEE as a security pillar — SGX demoted to optional auxiliary, never sufficient, on a path to retirement.
  • Gwyneth and synchronous composability — out of scope entirely. The demand signal (EF guidance, EEZ) is real, but the technology is unproven and no live network has implemented the booster model. We commit nothing to it this cycle and revisit only when someone proves the primitive in production.
  • The "ENS ecosystem" narrative — Namechain is cancelled.
  • Leading RWA sales with "no sequencer = regulatory advantage" — no legal evidence exists yet. We sell credible neutrality to crypto-native issuers and keep the regulatory claim in reserve for post-CLARITY.
We double down on
  • Based sequencing + preconfirmations — the only L2 architecture that gets stronger every time L1 improves.
  • ZK-first multiproof aligned with the EF's L1-zkEVM standards — our proof stack becomes literally the L1 proof stack.
  • The Stage-2 arc — the one headline no distribution-rich competitor can copy quickly.
  • Client & throughput engineering — the Rust client as the production client, proving cost/latency budgets, and DA efficiency that compounds forever at the EIP-7918 floor.
  • Stack productization with Nethermind/Surge — managed offering, transparent license, shared audits.
  • Rust client as the strategic client; AI-augmented engineering and audit pipelines as a formal capability.

04 — Program structure

Four pillars

P0 · PROVABLE TRUST

From incident to first Stage-2 rollup

Finish the arc the exploit interrupted. Restore everything we switched off — hardened — then remove ourselves from the trust equation entirely.

  • Permissionless proposing & bonded permissionless proving restored (bond machinery is built; it's parked at zero)
  • ZK-first: 2-of-N multi-zkVM, third zkVM lane, SGX auxiliary-only
  • EF zkVM-standards alignment: soundcalc, 128-bit, ≤300KB proofs
  • L2Beat Stage 1 → Stage 2; security program as standing function
P1 · REAL-TIME UX

Sub-second preconfs, ~1-slot finality

The one UX metric that matters. Preconfs get faster and permissionless; ZK finality collapses from hours to slots.

  • Sub-1s preconfs GA, then 200–400ms via streaming/frags
  • Permissionless preconf registry (URC-style), bonded + slashable
  • Real-time proving: batch proofs ≤ ~12s; withdrawals in minutes
  • Glamsterdam/ePBS readiness certified on both clients before the fork lands
P2 · CLIENTS & PERFORMANCE

The Rust client era

Proven engineering only. The strategic client reaches production across the full stack, and throughput and DA efficiency stay permanently ahead of anything an anchor app can throw at us.

  • Rust driver/proposer to production parity, then GA as the default operator client
  • Proving path completed: Rust prover, or a deliberate Rust-sequencing / Go-proving split — decided, shipped, documented
  • DA efficiency: batch compression & blob packing (permanent ROI at the EIP-7918 fee floor)
  • Gigagas capability benchmark published — a qualifier, not a marketing lead
P3 · DEMAND & ECONOMICS

Anchor apps, stack product, captured value

No more generic ecosystem spend. Land named anchors, productize the stack, and stop leaking sequencing value.

  • Anchor verticals: prediction markets (Polymarket is shopping), RWA/tokenized-equity settlement, DeFi money markets (Aave V4 spoke / Morpho) sold on real-time finality and fast exits
  • "Based markets" primitive — stake-gated builder markets settling against L1 collateral (our HIP-3 answer)
  • Preconf fee-split spec + public gateway revenue dashboard → sequencing auction with protocol take
  • Taiko Stack managed offering with transparent license; token work: bonds utility now, vesting restructure at board level

08 — Risks & kill criteria

What breaks this plan, and what we do then

Another security incident
Existential — this is why P0 is pillar zero. Every permissionless re-enable ships with registration delays, rate limits, and circuit breakers; verifier-registration paths and key custody are treated as bridge-critical; secret-scanning CI (already live) plus annual external audits per zkVM guest program. The June response playbook — halt, recapitalize, DAO fork — stays rehearsed.
Glamsterdam slips into 2027
Likely, partially. All FY27 plans assume 12s slots and current blob limits; ePBS work is devnet-gated, not date-gated. Real-time proving and sub-second preconfs do not depend on Glamsterdam. No milestone above waits on an L1 fork date.
Synchronous composability proves out elsewhere (EEZ / native rollups)
We deliberately sat this cycle out — Gwyneth is out of scope as unproven technology, by decision. Nothing in P0/P1/P3 depends on it, and our EF-aligned proof stack keeps us compatible with whatever wins. Re-entry criterion for FY28 planning: a production deployment with real users demonstrating same-slot L1+L2 value — then we adopt a de-risked design rather than pioneer an unproven one.
No anchor app by mid-2027
Shift the BD budget entirely to the based-markets primitive (permissionless deployment converts BD into protocol design) and stack deals. Explicit non-response: another points program.
Token unlocks overwhelm everything
~2.08%/month unlocks into a ~$14M float statistically swamps any mechanism-level accrual. This is a board/investor problem, not an R&D one: vesting restructure (Worldcoin 3→5yr, Story deferral precedents), unlock-to-OTC conversion, timed with Stage-2 comms. R&D's job is to make the revenue line real enough that the token has something honest to accrue.
Team concentration
Four engineers produced ~85% of recent commits. Mitigation: hire against P0/P1 critical paths, formalize the AI-augmented engineering and audit pipeline that already co-authors much of our output, and keep the Nethermind relationship warm as surge capacity in both directions.
Narrative capture by RISE / Linea
They market faster than we do; they cannot ship our milestones. The counter is cadence: Stage 1 in Q1, real-time finality in Q2, Stage 2 in Q3 — each externally verified, each a press moment. We stop announcing intentions and start announcing verifications.
